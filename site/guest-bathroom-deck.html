This is the first Guest Bathroom deck on the site: built straight from the same Manual as the Kitchen, Entryway, Laundry Room, Home Office, Primary Bathroom, Garage, Stair Landing, Pantry, Hall Closet, Dining Room and Guest Bedroom decks, with the same rules.

Built from the Manual, not around it

Five zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The guest bathroom is the one room a visitor spends time in alone, with the door shut and nothing to look at but your standards. It is also the room nobody in the house uses, which is its own problem: a dry drain trap, a towel that smells of the closet, a bottle of shampoo left behind two summers ago. Start at the vanity counter, because it is the first thing anyone sees and the fastest thing to put right.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

60 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE GUEST BATHROOM

FIVE ZONES. START WITH THE COUNTER THE VISITOR SEES FIRST.

●○○○○

The guest bathroom is the one room a visitor spends time in alone, with the door shut and nothing to look at but your standards. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. GHZ-001 Guest Vanity Counter. Do the vanity counter first and the linen shelf last. The counter takes one trip and changes how the whole room reads; the linen shelf needs a wash cycle to finish, so put the stored towels in the machine before you touch anything else and let them run while you work the rest of the room.

The five zones, in order

  1. GHZ-001 Guest Vanity Counter
  2. GHZ-002 Guest Vanity Storage
  3. GHZ-003 Shower or Tub
  4. GHZ-004 Toilet Area
  5. GHZ-005 Guest Linen Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your bathroom. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do GHA-011 The Full Guest Bathroom Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the cord beside the basin, the chemicals under the cabinet, the tub floor, and the water heater in the linen closet.

GUEST VANITY COUNTER

Zone 1 of 5 · Guest Bathroom

THREE THINGS LIVE HERE: SOAP, TISSUES, A TOWEL. NOTHING ELSE.

●●○○○

It gives a visitor somewhere to wash their hands and set a phone down, with nothing of yours in the way.

How it works

Done looks like: A soap pump above half full, one folded hand towel on the ring where you can see it from the doorway, a box of tissues at the back corner, and enough bare counter to set down a phone and a pair of glasses. No toothbrush cup holding your family's toothbrushes, no hair clips behind the tap, no dried soap ring under the dispenser.

Six things to check

  1. A soap pump standing above half full on the counter
  2. One folded hand towel hanging on a ring visible from the doorway
  3. A box of tissues sitting at the back corner clear of splash
  4. A clear stretch of bare counter wide enough for a phone and a pair of glasses
  5. No toothbrush cup or hair clips anywhere on the counter
  6. A dry, ring-free patch of counter under the soap dispenser

Water and electricity Anything with a cord left plugged in next to a basin that a guest will fill. Unplug and store after every use, and keep the cord off the counter entirely.

Fall, cut, or crush Glass tumblers and glass decorative dishes on a wet counter. When one goes over in a small tiled room, someone is standing there barefoot.

Whose bathroom is this really. If a child or a housemate genuinely brushes their teeth in here, the hotel counter standard loses every time and you end up resetting it twice a week. The decision you have been dodging is which room this actually is. Pick out loud: either it is a used bathroom, in which case one named person gets a labeled caddy or drawer and the counter holds their two items alongside the guest three, or it is a guest bathroom, in which case that toothbrush moves today and nothing personal comes back. Holding a hotel standard against a resident who lives here is not a discipline problem, it is a standard aimed at the wrong room.

What it uses

  • Bathroom grime and soap-film cleaner
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Streak-free glass and mirror cleaner (not in this zone's list, and the mirror needs it)
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy

▴ActionGHA-001

CLEAR THE COUNTER TO THREE THINGS

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Take everything off the counter and put back only what a visitor would use, so nothing of the household's is left in view.

How it works

A counter holding a housemate's toothbrush or a stray hair clip stops reading as a guest counter and starts reading as an argument about whose bathroom this is.

You need

  • a small box for anything going back to another room
  • a bin bag

Steps

  1. Take everything off the counter and put back only what a visitor would use: soap, tissues, hand towel. The travel conditioner, the spare contact lens case, and the razor that ended up here because the main bathroom ran out of room all go back where they belong or into the trash.
  2. Set the soap, tissues and towel back in their usual spots, clear of the basin.

Victory. Only soap, tissues and a folded hand towel remain on the counter, with bare space enough for a phone and glasses, and nothing of the household's left behind.

If this keeps happening

  • NO ASSIGNED HOME

TWO BINS. ONE LABELED GUEST, ONE LABELED CLEANING. THE BASE STAYS BARE.

How it works

Six things to check

Poison, choke, or strangle Bowl cleaner, bleach, and drain opener stored under the sink of the bathroom where a visiting child gets sent alone. Latch the door or move the chemicals out of the room.

Fall A slow weep from the trap that nobody sees because nobody opens this cabinet. It reaches the tile as a thin invisible film long before it shows as a puddle.

The bag of hotel miniatures. The collection of tiny shampoos is the hardest thing in this cabinet, because throwing out free soap feels like waste and keeping it feels like hospitality, so it has sat there for years doing neither. Settle it with arithmetic: a guest staying two nights uses one shampoo, one conditioner, one soap, and one lotion. So keep four, unopened, the four with the least battered labels, and put them in a small dish where they read as an offering rather than a hoard. The rest go today. If binning them is genuinely beyond you, they go into a shelter donation box this week, in the car, not back on the shelf.

What it uses

SHOWER OR TUB

Zone 3 of 5 · Guest Bathroom

THREE BOTTLES. ONE MAT. ONE THING BOLTED IN TO GRAB.

●●●●○

It has to be ready for someone to shower in tonight, even though nobody has used it since spring.

How it works

Done looks like: Three bottles in the caddy, all recognizable and more than a third full, a curtain liner or glass screen with no pink film along the bottom edge, a bath mat folded over the tub rim rather than lying wet on the floor, and grout that is the same color in the corners as it is in the middle. A non-slip surface underfoot inside the tub.

Six things to check

  1. Three bottles in a hanging caddy, shampoo, conditioner and body wash, each more than a third full
  2. A curtain liner or glass screen with no pink film along its bottom edge
  3. A bath mat folded over the tub rim rather than lying wet on the floor
  4. Grout the same even color in the corners as in the middle
  5. A non-slip surface visible on the tub floor
  6. One securely anchored grab point fixed to the wall, not a towel bar

Fall An unfamiliar tub with a slick glazed floor, used at night by someone who does not know the room. A non-slip mat or strips are the fix, not a warning.

Fall, cut, or crush A towel bar or a suction rail being used as a grab handle. Both let go under load, and the person comes down against the tub rim on the way.

The grout you cannot clean. You have scrubbed one grout line three times, it stayed gray, and you have quietly started calling that clean. It is not dirt. It is mold that has grown into porous grout or a silicone bead that has failed underneath, and no product will bring it back, so every week you spend part of the clean on a job that cannot succeed. The rule: give a stained line one proper treatment with full dwell time and a stiff brush. If it darkens again within two weeks, stop cleaning it and move it to the repair list, which means raking out and regrouting, or cutting out the old silicone and running a fresh bead. Calling it a repair is the decision. It is a Saturday afternoon once, instead of a chore forever.

What it uses

  • Bathroom grime and soap-film cleaner
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Dedicated mildew remover (not in this zone's list, for grout the soap-film cleaner will not shift)
  • Stiff grout or detail brush (not in this zone's list, and the grout and the non-slip floor need bristles)

TREAT THE GROUT, HANG THE MAT, ANCHOR A GRAB BAR

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Give a stained grout line one proper treatment with full dwell time, fit a non-slip mat, and install one solidly anchored grab point.

How it works

A guest steps into an unfamiliar tub at night on a surface they cannot judge, and a towel bar screwed into drywall tears out the moment it takes a person's weight.

You need

  • dedicated mildew remover
  • a stiff grout brush
  • a non-slip mat or adhesive strips
  • a wall-anchored grab bar

Steps

  2. If it darkens again within two weeks, stop cleaning it and move it to the repair list instead.
  3. Fit a non-slip mat or adhesive strips to the tub floor, and install one securely anchored grab bar, never relying on a towel bar.

Victory. The treated grout line holds its color for two weeks, a non-slip mat sits on the tub floor, and one solidly anchored grab bar is fixed to the wall.

  • SAFETY CONSTRAINT

ONE ROLL IN SIGHT. A DRY BRUSH. A LINER IN THE CAN.

●●○○○

It has to be clean and stocked in a way that is obvious to someone who will never ask you where anything is.

How it works

Done looks like: One spare roll standing in plain sight on the tank or in a holder a seated person can reach without standing up. A brush that is dry, resting in a holder with no liquid pooled in the bottom. A small trash can with a liner actually in it. No ring at the water line, and a dry, dust-free floor behind the pedestal.

Six things to check

  1. One spare toilet roll standing in a holder within reach of a seated person
  2. A dry toilet brush resting in its holder with no liquid pooled in the bottom
  3. A small trash can with a liner actually fitted inside it
  4. No ring visible at the bowl's water line
  5. A dry, dust-free floor behind the pedestal
  6. A bare tank top with nothing standing on it

Poison, choke, or strangle Bleach and an acid bowl or limescale cleaner used one after the other in a small room with the door closed and no fan running. Use one product, ventilate, and never store the two together.

Poison, choke, or strangle A bowl cleaner bottle left standing behind the pedestal after cleaning. It is at floor level, in reach, and the cap is rarely back on properly.

The brush nobody wants to touch. The toilet brush in a guest bathroom is the object your household has silently agreed not to look at. It has splayed bristles, it stands in an inch of gray water in the bottom of its holder, and it has been there for years, spreading more than it removes. The decision you are avoiding is that a brush is a consumable, not a fixture. The rule: splayed bristles or liquid in the holder means both go out today and a fresh set comes in, and from then on the brush gets rinsed in the flush and left propped under the closed seat to drip before it goes back in the holder. If that much handling is more than you will realistically do, buy the kind with a disposable head and stop negotiating with yourself about it.

What it uses

  • Bathroom grime and soap-film cleaner
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Toilet bowl cleaner and a dedicated bowl brush (not in this zone's list, and the bowl needs both)

GUEST LINEN ZONE

Zone 5 of 5 · Guest Bathroom

TWO BUNDLES. ONE LIFT EACH. THE MAT UNDERNEATH.

●●●○○

It holds one complete fresh set of towels so hosting never starts with a search.

How it works

Done looks like: One bundle per expected guest, each holding a bath towel, a hand towel, and a washcloth folded together, matching in color, smelling of nothing at all. A clean bath mat folded flat beneath the bundles. A hand's width of air above the stack. No thin gray towel kept for the dog sharing the shelf.

Six things to check

  1. Two complete towel bundles, each holding a bath towel, hand towel and washcloth folded together
  2. Both bundles matching in color with neither carrying a musty smell
  3. A clean bath mat folded flat beneath the bundles
  4. A hand's width of clear air above the stack
  5. No thin gray towel or dog towel sharing the shelf
  6. A hand's width of clearance kept around anything on the shelf that runs hot

Burn or fire Textiles stacked against a water heater, a hot pipe, or its power cable in a shared closet. Keep a hand's width of clear air on every hot side.

Fall A bath mat with a curled or hardened rubber backing. It stops gripping the tile and slides out from under someone stepping off wet.

The good towels. Every house has a set of nice towels that guests are not quite allowed to use and a set of worn ones that guests actually get, and nobody ever says this out loud. Towels thin whether they are used or not, so holding the good ones back means they will age untouched in a closet while your visitors dry off on something gray. The rule: the guest bundle is built from the best towels you own, put into service today, and the ones they replace get demoted to rags on the same trip so they cannot creep back. And if a towel is genuinely too good for a guest to use, then it is not linen, it is a keepsake, and it belongs anywhere except the towel shelf.

What it uses

  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Washing machine (not a listed product, but the stored linens need a hot wash)

RETIRE THE THIN AND GRAY TOWELS

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Unfold every towel in daylight and pull anything thin, gray, stiff or stained out of the guest stack for good.

How it works

A towel that looks fine folded in closet light shows its wear the moment it is unfolded in daylight, and a guest is the one who finds out first.

You need

  • daylight or a bright lamp
  • a rag bin

Steps

  1. Unfold every towel and look at it in daylight rather than closet light. Anything thin along the fold line, permanently gray, stiff from too much softener, or carrying a hair dye or nosebleed stain comes out of the guest stack. It becomes a rag or a car towel today. It does not go back just in case.
  2. Move the household's best remaining towels into the guest stack to replace what left.

Victory. Every towel remaining in the guest stack is free of thinning, graying, stiffness or stains, and none of it went back in just in case.

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Walk every zone checking for a plugged-in cord beside water, a chemical within a child's reach, a slick tub floor, and any textile stacked against the water heater.

How it works

You need
