This is the first Hall Closet deck on the site: built straight from the same Manual as the Kitchen, Entryway, Laundry Room, Home Office, Primary Bathroom, Garage, Stair Landing and Pantry decks, with the same rules.

Built from the Manual, not around it

Five zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The hall closet is the darkest and deepest storage in the house, and the only one nobody owns. Everything that had to go somewhere ended up behind this door, stacked to the depth of your arm. Start with the shelf you cannot see the back of, because that is where the closet is lying to you about what it holds.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

58 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

⌂RoomHCR-001
⌂

THE HALL CLOSET

FIVE SHELVES OF EVERYTHING THAT HAD NOWHERE ELSE TO GO.

●○○○○

The hall closet is where everything that had to go somewhere ended up. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. HCZ-001 Linen Shelf Zone. Empty one shelf onto the hallway floor before you touch anything else. A closet this deep cannot be sorted in place; you have to see the back wall to know what you own.

The five zones, in order

  1. HCZ-001 Linen Shelf Zone
  2. HCZ-002 Cleaning Equipment Zone
  3. HCZ-003 Cleaning Supply Zone
  4. HCZ-004 Paper and Household Backstock
  5. HCZ-005 Seasonal and Guest Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your closet. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do HCA-011 The Full Closet Safety And Height Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers every shelf's height, the stool, and whether bleach and ammonia are kept apart.

LINEN SHELF ZONE

Zone 1 of 5 · Hall Closet

THREE SETS PER BED. ONE PILLOWCASE EACH. THE SIZE ON THE EDGE.

●●●○○

Holds the sheets, towels, and blankets the beds in this house actually rotate through.

How it works

Done looks like: Three sheet sets per bed, each nested inside one of its own pillowcases, with the bed size written on the shelf edge underneath. Everyday towels folded to one width in a stack no taller than your forearm. Two spare blankets on the top shelf with nothing balanced on them.

Six things to check

  1. Sheet sets nested inside their own matching pillowcases
  2. A bed-size label fixed to the shelf edge under each stack
  3. A stack of folded everyday towels no taller than a forearm
  4. Two spare blankets standing on the top shelf
  5. Clear space above the blankets with nothing stacked on them
  6. Queen and king sets sitting on a lower shelf than the guest blankets

Fall Pulling a towel from the bottom of a chest-high stack brings the whole pile down onto your face and the hallway floor.

Fall, cut, or crush Damp-weight blankets and a full shelf of folded cotton are heavier than they look; check the shelf brackets are screwed into studs, not just into plasterboard.

The good set you never put on a bed. There is a sheet set here that is too nice to use and too nice to give away, so it has sat at the front of the shelf for years while you sleep on the worn ones. Settle it with a date: put it on a bed this month, or accept that it is a keepsake rather than a linen. Keepsakes can live in a memory box. They cannot live on the shelf you reach into every week.

CLEAR THE SHELF AND CHECK EVERY SET

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Unfold every set and check it is complete and fits a bed you still own, so nothing half-usable survives on a technicality.

How it works

A set with a missing half or slack elastic only reveals itself at three in the morning, the worst possible time to find out.

You need

  • a bin bag

Steps

  1. Unfold every set and check it is complete and fits a bed you still own. The flat sheet whose fitted half vanished, fitted sheets with slack elastic that pop off a corner at three in the morning, and towels that smell sour an hour after drying all leave.
  2. Set aside anything still good but unlikely to get used, to give away rather than bin.
  3. Re-nest each surviving set inside its own matching pillowcase before it goes back.

Victory. Every set on the shelf is complete, fits a bed you still own, and no towel carries a sour smell.

If this keeps happening

  • SENTIMENTAL ATTACHMENT
  • UNRESOLVED DECISION

LABEL EVERY STACK AND SET THE HEIGHT LIMIT

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Write each bed size on the shelf edge under its stack, and keep every stack no taller than a forearm.

How it works

CLEANING EQUIPMENT ZONE

Zone 2 of 5 · Hall Closet

EVERYTHING HANGS. NOTHING TOUCHES THE FLOOR. THE DOOR SWINGS CLEAR.

●●○○○

Parks the vacuum, broom, and mop so they come out in one motion and go back without a fight.

How it works

Done looks like: The vacuum standing inside a taped floor outline with its cord wrapped and every crevice tool and brush head zipped in one bag clipped to its handle. Broom, mop, and duster hanging handle-down from wall clips with the heads clear of the floor. The closet door swinging fully open without touching anything.

Six things to check

  1. A vacuum standing inside a taped floor outline
  2. Its cord wrapped neatly around the body
  3. A zipped bag of attachments clipped to the vacuum's handle
  4. A broom hanging handle-down from a wall clip, head clear of the floor
  5. A mop and a duster hanging the same way, heads clear of the floor
  6. The closet door swinging fully open without touching anything

Fall, cut, or crush Unclipped handles slide down the wall and drop across the doorway or onto whoever opens the door.

Water and electricity A damp mop head or a wet bucket stored against a vacuum cord in a closed closet is the one combination to refuse; check the cord for cracked insulation near the plug while you have it out.

The vacuum you keep meaning to fix. It has a split hose or a dead brush roll, and it sits here because throwing out a machine that mostly works feels wasteful. Give it a repair date written on masking tape stuck to the body. If that date passes and you have not ordered the part, you have your answer: the repair was never going to happen, and the broken machine has been standing where the working one needs to stand. Recycle it and keep the one that runs.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Scissors

CLEANING SUPPLY ZONE

Zone 3 of 5 · Hall Closet

ONE CADDY PER ROOM. BLEACH AND AMMONIA APART. OUT OF SMALL HANDS.

●●●○○

Holds the products you clean with, grouped so you carry the right handful to the right room.

How it works

Done looks like: Two or three caddies, each labeled on the end with the room it serves, every bottle wearing its original label, bleach products and ammonia products on separate shelves, and the whole zone up out of a small child's reach or behind a latch.

Six things to check

  1. Two or three caddies, each labeled with the room it serves
  2. Every bottle wearing its own original label
  3. Bleach products standing on one shelf
  4. Ammonia products standing on a separate shelf
  5. A latch or height barrier keeping the zone out of a small child's reach
  6. Gloves and cloths riding inside each caddy

Poison, choke, or strangle Bleach stored beside an ammonia glass cleaner, and any product in an unlabeled bottle at a height a child can reach from the hallway floor.

Burn or fire Aerosol tins and solvent-based sprays sitting against a closet wall that backs onto a boiler or a hot water pipe; move them to the coolest shelf.

Eleven bottles, each a third full. You buy a new one because you cannot see what is left in the old one, and the shelf now carries four glass cleaners, three floor sprays, and a scatter of part-used others. The rule that settles it: one open bottle per job. Pour identical products together, and for the rest, accept that finishing a half bottle down the drain costs you a little while keeping it costs you the shelf you need. You did not save money by keeping them. You paid twice and stored the evidence.

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Line every bottle up on the hallway floor and only pour identical products together, so nothing unmarked survives unidentified.

How it works

A decanted, unmarked bottle is a real hazard the moment somebody other than you reaches for it.

You need

  • a bin bag

Steps
