THE WORKSHOP

SIX ZONES. START AT SAFETY, EVEN THOUGH IT IS THE SMALLEST.

●○○○○

The workshop is the room 6S came from, and the only one in the house where a bad standard can cost you a finger rather than an afternoon. This card is the map.

How it works

Start here. WSZ-006 Safety and PPE Station. Take the Safety and PPE Station first even though it is the smallest zone in the room. It sets the terms for the other five: once the glasses hang by the door and the extinguisher gauge reads green, you can work through the blades and the solvents without improvising.

The six zones

  1. WSZ-001 Main Workbench
  2. WSZ-002 Power Tool Storage
  3. WSZ-003 Fastener and Hardware Zone
  4. WSZ-004 Material Rack
  5. WSZ-005 Finishing and Chemical Zone
  6. WSZ-006 Safety and PPE Station

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Start at Safety and PPE Station, or take the zone that is annoying you today.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your workshop. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 2. A shared shop runs on the same standards for everyone who uses it: the visitor rule on the Safety and PPE Station card exists precisely because two people improvising two different rules is how someone gets hurt.

Safety first. Do WSA-013 The Full Workshop Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers every stuck guard, the unanchored small-parts cabinet and material rack, a shallow-leaning sheet good, the extinguisher gauge, and any loose oily rag.

MAIN WORKBENCH

Zone 1 of 6 · Workshop

ONE PROJECT ON THE BENCH. THE VISE AND THE LAMP, NOTHING ELSE.

●●●○○

The one surface where a project actually gets built, cut, glued, and repaired.

How it works

Done looks like: A bare bench top except the vise and the task lamp, one project tray holding the current job's parts, and a clear run of bench in front of the vise long enough to lay a board across. Every chisel and square back on its traced outline, the dog holes empty, and no offcuts or glue squeeze-out under the bench.

Six things to check

  1. A bare bench top holding only the vise and the task lamp
  2. One project tray holding the current job's parts
  3. A clear run of bench in front of the vise long enough to lay a board across
  4. Every chisel and square back on its traced outline on the tool wall
  5. The dog holes sitting empty
  6. No offcuts or glue squeeze-out under the bench

Fall, cut, or crush Chisels and marking knives lying edge up in an open tray, and a vise whose jaws have worn smooth enough to release a workpiece mid-cut.

Water and electricity The task lamp or a tool cord running across the bench or down onto a damp concrete floor where you stand to work.

The project that has been on the bench since spring. You have a half-assembled something occupying your best surface, and you have not touched it in months because the next step is one you are unsure how to do. Here is the rule that settles it: say out loud what the next physical action is and what date this month you will do it. If you cannot name both in one breath, the project is finished. Take it apart, put the hardware back in the fastener drawers, put the stock back on the rack, and let the bench go back to being a bench. Keeping it assembled is not keeping the project alive, it is only keeping the surface unusable.

  • Portable cleaning caddy

15 MINUTES. 1.

How it works

You need

GIVE THE STALLED PROJECT ITS VERDICT AND SET THE CLEAR DOWN RULE

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Decide the fate of any project that has sat on the bench for months, and agree the ten-minute clear down happens before the glasses come off.

How it works

A project with no next action and no date is not a project in progress, it is the reason the bench stops being usable, and a clear down with nobody assigned to it just does not happen on a bad night.

You need

  2. Agree, out loud, whose job the ten-minute clear down is when more than one of you uses the bench in one evening.

Victory. The bench carries either one project with a named next action and a date, or no project at all, and everyone who uses the bench knows whose job the clear down is that night.

POWER TOOL STORAGE

Zone 2 of 6 · Workshop

GUARDED, UNPLUGGED, AND STORED WITH WHAT IT NEEDS.

●●●○○

Keep every corded and cordless tool guarded, complete with its own accessories, and ready to pick up and use.

How it works

Done looks like: Each tool in a labeled home with its guard closed and its blade wrench or chuck key attached to it, not floating in a drawer. Batteries sitting on a shelf off the chargers, no bare circular saw blade loose in a drawer, and no cord repaired with electrical tape anywhere in the zone.

Six things to check

  1. Each tool in a labeled home with its guard closed
  2. A blade wrench or chuck key attached to its own tool, not floating in a drawer
  3. Battery packs sitting on a shelf, off the chargers
  4. No bare circular saw blade loose in a drawer
  5. No cord repaired with electrical tape anywhere in the zone
  6. Every corded tool unplugged rather than left running on the charger

Fall, cut, or crush A blade guard that has stopped springing shut on its own, and loose blades stored flat in a drawer where your hand goes in first.

Burn or fire Lithium battery packs left on chargers indefinitely, especially sitting on a wooden shelf under a bench full of sawdust.

The expensive tool you never use. Somewhere in this zone is a tool that cost real money and has not cut anything in two years, and you keep it because selling it would be admitting the purchase was wrong. Two questions settle it. Has it done work in the last twenty-four months? And if the job came up tomorrow, would you happily hire one for a day instead? If the answer is no and yes, sell it now, while it still fetches something and while the batteries in it are still worth having. A tool losing value on your shelf is not an asset you are holding, it is a decision you are storing.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Resin and pitch remover
  • Compressed air or a blower

FASTENER AND HARDWARE ZONE

Zone 3 of 6 · Workshop

ONE SIZE PER COMPARTMENT. A SAMPLE SCREW GLUED ON FRONT.

●●○○○

Let you put your hand on the right screw straight away, so a project never stops for a hardware run.

Poison, choke, or strangle Loose screws, washers, and wall anchors on the floor at the height where a toddler or a dog finds them first.

The inherited jar of mixed hardware. There is a jar or a tin, probably from a relative's garage, holding a few kilograms of unsorted screws, nuts, and washers, and it has followed you through two houses because there is obviously good hardware in there. Price the alternative first: a full box of the screws you actually drive costs less than the evening you would spend sorting. So give it one sitting. Pick out only the sizes you already have a compartment for, and when the sitting ends, whatever is left in the jar goes to metal recycling that same day. Do not tip the remainder back into a smaller jar. That is exactly how it survived the last two moves.

THE NIGHT YOU NEED THE BENCH FOR A QUICK REPAIR

THE DAY THAT TESTS IT.

●●●○○

Something breaks at 9pm and you need the bench right now for a five-minute fix, not a whole project.

How it works

Tests: MAIN WORKBENCH

It held if: The bench top is bare except the vise and the lamp, so you set to work immediately with a clear surface and every tool exactly where its outline says it lives.

If it did not: If you had to clear someone else's project off the bench first, or hunt for a tool missing from its outline, the clear down did not hold. Draw WSA-001.

THE SATURDAY MORNING YOU GRAB THE SAW WITHOUT LOOKING

THE DAY THAT TESTS IT.

●●●○○

You are already running late for a job and grab the circular saw off its hook without checking it first.

How it works

Tests: POWER TOOL STORAGE

It held if: The guard snaps back clean under your thumb, the blade wrench is right there on the saw, and the battery is already charged and waiting on its shelf.

If it did not: If the guard stuck, the wrench was missing, or the battery was flat, the storage and safety checks have slipped. Draw WSA-003.

THE JOB THAT NEEDS ONE SPECIFIC SCREW RIGHT NOW

THE DAY THAT TESTS IT.

●●●○○

You are mid-assembly and need one specific size of deck screw, and you need it without stopping the job to go looking.

How it works

Tests: FASTENER AND HARDWARE ZONE

It held if: You match the sample screw glued to the drawer face by eye, pull the compartment, and it is not below its own marked minimum line.

If it did not: If you had to open three drawers or found the compartment nearly empty with no warning, the labels or the minimum lines have slipped. Draw WSA-005.

THE DAY YOU NEED A SPECIFIC LENGTH OF BOARD FAST

THE DAY THAT TESTS IT.

●●●○○

A project calls for one specific length and thickness of board, and you need to find it without unstacking half the rack.

How it works

Tests: MATERIAL RACK

It held if: You pull the board you need from its own grouped bay without moving three others first, and nothing is stored on the floor in your way.

If it did not: If you had to unstack several boards to reach the one you needed, or step over something on the floor to get there, the rack has drifted. Draw WSA-007.

THE TOUCH-UP JOB THAT NEEDS ONE SPECIFIC COLOR

Tests: FINISHING AND CHEMICAL ZONE
