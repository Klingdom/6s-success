This is the first Guest Bedroom deck on the site: built straight from the same Manual as the Kitchen, Entryway, Laundry Room, Home Office, Primary Bathroom, Garage, Stair Landing, Pantry, Hall Closet and Dining Room decks, with the same rules.

Built from the Manual, not around it

Five zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The guest bedroom is the only room in the house with no daily user, which is exactly why it fills up. Nothing here objects when you set a box down, so the room quietly absorbs whatever the rest of the house could not decide about, and then you excavate it every time someone visits. Start with the closet rather than the bed, because the closet is where the overflow actually lives, and the bed goes quickly once the room around it is honest.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-colored panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

57 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE GUEST BEDROOM

THE ROOM WITH NO DAILY USER IS WHERE EVERYTHING UNDECIDED HIDES.

●○○○○

The guest bedroom is the only room in the house with no daily user, which is why it quietly absorbs whatever the rest of the house could not decide about. This card is the map.

How it works

Start here. GBZ-004 Guest Closet. Open the closet first and pull everything onto the bed. You cannot judge how much room a visitor actually has while half of it is hidden behind a closet door, and working on the bed forces you to finish before anyone can sleep in here.

The five zones

  1. GBZ-001 Guest Bed and Linens
  2. GBZ-002 Guest Nightstand
  3. GBZ-003 Guest Dresser
  4. GBZ-004 Guest Closet
  5. GBZ-005 Guest Welcome and Work Surface

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your guest room. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do GBA-011 The Full Room Safety And Access Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the dark path, every cord, and the dresser strap.

GUEST BED AND LINENS

Zone 1 of 5 · Guest Bedroom

ONE SET MADE UP. ONE SET TAGGED. NOTHING ELSE UNDER THE BED.

●●○○○

A bed a visitor can get into tonight without you touching anything first.

How it works

Done looks like: Mattress protector on, one complete set made up on the bed, a second complete set folded inside its own pillowcase on the shelf, two pillows that spring back when you fold them in half, and nothing under the bed but one empty suitcase lying flat.

Six things to check

  1. A mattress protector fitted under the bottom sheet
  2. One complete sheet set made up on the bed
  3. A second complete set folded inside its own pillowcase, standing on the shelf
  4. Two pillows that spring back into shape when folded in half
  5. One empty suitcase lying flat under the bed
  6. A tag naming the bed size visible on the folded spare set

Fall A guest crossing an unfamiliar room in the dark to find the bathroom, with a suitcase or a footstool in the path between the bed and the door.

Poison, choke, or strangle A looped blind or curtain cord hanging within reach of the bed, which matters the moment a visiting family puts a small child in this room.

The demoted sheets. The linen in this room got here because it stopped being good enough for your own bed. Thin sheets, the pilled set, the flat pillows, all quietly reassigned to people who will never say anything about it. The rule that settles it: if you would not sleep on it yourself for a week, it is not guest linen, it is cleaning cloth. Keep two complete sets that pass that test and let the rest go, even if it means buying one set. Two good sets beat six tired ones, and the shelf finally holds a decision instead of a backlog.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Upholstery tool for the vacuum
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Laundry detergent

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE. THEN WHY.

●○○○○

TEST EVERY GUEST SET, RAG THE FAILURES

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Take every sheet set that has been demoted to this room and try each fitted sheet on the actual mattress, so nothing that fails the test stays in the guest rotation.

How it works

A set that pops a corner mid-make is a rag, not a spare, and a guest has no way to know which one they were handed.

You need

  • a bin bag or rag pile

Steps

  1. Take every sheet set that has been demoted to this room and try each fitted sheet on the actual mattress. If a corner pops off while you are making the bed, it is a rag, not a spare. Fold each pillow in half and let go, and anything that stays folded goes out.
  2. Count what survives: you need exactly two complete sets for this bed, not however many happen to fit on the shelf.

Victory. Every remaining sheet set has passed the mattress test, and every remaining pillow springs back when folded in half.

If this keeps happening

  • UNRESOLVED DECISION
  • POOR VISIBILITY

CLEAR UNDER THE BED AND WALK THE DARK ROUTE

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Empty anything that is not the guest suitcase out from under the bed, tie up any loose blind cord, and walk the route from pillow to door with the lights off.

How it works

A guest crossing this room in the dark has never memorised where the footstool or the box lives; you have, which is exactly why you cannot see the hazard anymore.

You need

  • nothing beyond your own two feet and the lights switch

Steps

  1. Walk from the pillow to the door with the lights off, the way a guest will in the small hours in a room they do not know, and clear anything their shin will find.
  2. If the bed sits under a blind, tie the cord up out of reach of a visiting toddler.
  3. Remove anything from under the bed that is not the one folded-flat suitcase, and give it a real home elsewhere in the house.

Victory. Nothing but one empty suitcase remains under the bed, and the dark walk from pillow to door meets no obstacle.

If this keeps happening

  • MISSING TRIGGER
  • SAFETY CONSTRAINT

GUEST NIGHTSTAND

Zone 2 of 5 · Guest Bedroom

TWO CABLES, TESTED TONIGHT. NOTHING OF YOURS IN THE DRAWER.

●○○○○

The handful of things a guest needs in the middle of the night without knocking on your door.

How it works

Done looks like: A lamp whose switch you can find by feel in the dark, one charging cable per common connector plugged in and reaching the pillow, a coaster, a box of tissues at least half full, and a drawer that is genuinely empty apart from a card with the wifi name and password.

Six things to check

  1. A bedside lamp with its switch easy to find by feel
  2. One charging cable per common connector, plugged in and reaching the pillow
  3. A coaster sitting on the nightstand surface
  4. A box of tissues at least half full
  5. A drawer standing open and empty apart from one card
  6. A card printed with the wifi name and password sitting alone in the drawer

Water and electricity A water glass sitting beside a lamp base and a charger head on a small surface, with the cord crossing the top where a sleepy arm sweeps it.

Poison, choke, or strangle Your own prescriptions or painkillers left in the nightstand drawer, at exactly the height a visiting toddler explores first.

Two cables, tested. There are probably six or seven charging cables in this house that nobody can account for, and a few have drifted into this drawer on the theory that a guest might need one. They will not. A guest will find a cable, trust it because it is offered, plug in, and wake to a phone that never charged. That is worse than offering nothing. The rule: keep exactly two here, one of each connector your visitors actually carry, and prove each one tonight by charging a real device with it. Anything you cannot prove goes to the household charging drawer or into electronics recycling. An untested cable is not hospitality, it is a trap with a bow on it.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy

GUEST DRESSER

Zone 3 of 5 · Guest Bedroom

TWO DRAWERS, ALWAYS EMPTY. THE STRAP FIXED. NOTHING BORROWED.

●●●○○

Somewhere a guest can unpack, so they stop living out of a half open suitcase on the floor.

How it works

Done looks like: The top two drawers completely empty and lined, the lower drawers holding spare linens folded to one uniform footprint with a label on the inside front edge, an anti-tip strap fixed to the wall, and the dresser top carrying a lamp or a mirror and nothing else.

Six things to check

  1. The top two drawers standing completely empty and lined
  2. Spare linens in the lower drawers folded to one uniform footprint
  3. A label fixed to the inside front edge of a lower drawer
  4. An anti-tip strap fixed between the dresser and the wall
  5. A lamp or a mirror standing alone on the dresser top
  6. No second object crowding the dresser top beside the lamp or mirror

Fall, cut, or crush An unstrapped dresser with heavy linens low and empty drawers high, in a room where a visiting child is often left to play while the adults talk downstairs.

Poison, choke, or strangle Dry cleaning film or the plastic bags spare bedding came sealed in, left inside a low drawer a small guest can open on their own.

The overflow you told yourself was free. You have been storing things in here on the reasoning that the room stands empty most of the year, so the space costs nothing. It costs the whole point of the room. The rule: these two drawers belong to the guest and are never available, not for a week, not for the box you are about to sort properly, not for the winter coats until spring. When something in the house needs a home and this dresser is the only candidate, that is not a reason to take the drawer, it is a signal that you have not yet decided about the thing. Make the decision instead. This is the drawer that turns a storage room with a bed in it back into a guest room.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner

GUEST CLOSET

Zone 4 of 5 · Guest Bedroom

SIX HANGERS PAST THE TAPE. ONE CLEAR SQUARE. NOTHING SEALED SINCE THE MOVE.

●●●●○

Hanging space for someone else's clothes, plus whatever household storage this room has honestly agreed to carry.

How it works

Done looks like: At least a forearm's width of clear rod with six matching empty hangers on it, a clear floor square big enough for an open suitcase, household bins on the shelf in labeled lidded boxes with nothing loose above head height, and a strip of tape on the rod marking where the guest section ends.

Six things to check

  1. A forearm's width of clear closet rod
  2. Six matching empty hangers on the clear rod
  3. A clear floor square big enough for an open suitcase
  4. Household bins on the shelf inside labeled lidded boxes
  5. Nothing loose sitting above head height on the shelf
  6. A strip of tape on the rod marking where the guest section ends

Fall, cut, or crush Heavy boxes stored above the rod, directly over the head and hands of anyone reaching in for a hanger, in a closet nobody uses often enough to notice a bin creeping toward the shelf edge.

Fall Suitcases and loose bins on the closet floor, in the dark stretch of room a guest crosses barefoot on the way to the light switch.
