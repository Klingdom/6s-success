THE FAMILY ROOM

SIX ZONES. START WHERE THE CABLES CROSS THE FLOOR.

●○○○○

The family room carries more jobs than any other room in the house: screens, floor play, games, blankets, charging, and craft all compete for the same square meters. This card is the map.

How it works

Start here. FRZ-001 Primary Media Zone. Run the Primary Media Zone first. Its cables cross the floor into the other zones, and its cabinet is where stray controllers, chargers, and disc cases quietly collect, so clearing it lets the other five zones tell you the truth about themselves.

The six zones

  1. FRZ-001 Primary Media Zone
  2. FRZ-002 Toy and Play Zone
  3. FRZ-003 Board Game and Puzzle Zone
  4. FRZ-004 Blanket and Comfort Zone
  5. FRZ-005 Charging and Device Zone
  6. FRZ-006 Craft and Activity Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your family room. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do FRA-013 The Full Family Room Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the television's anti-tip strap, the vent heat behind the media unit, the cables crossing the floor, the charging station's cable loop, and the hot glue gun on the craft table.

PRIMARY MEDIA ZONE

Zone 1 of 6 · Family Room

ONE CONSOLE PER SHELF. CONTROLLERS DOCKED. NOTHING ON THE VENTS.

●●●○○

Hold the television, consoles, and controllers so anyone in the house can start watching or playing without hunting for a remote.

How it works

Done looks like: One console per shelf with its own controllers docked beside it, disc cases standing spine out in a single row, every cable labeled at both ends, the power strip off the carpet, and nothing resting on top of a vent slot.

Six things to check

  1. One console standing alone on its own shelf with its controllers docked beside it
  2. Disc cases standing spine out in a single row
  3. Every visible cable wearing a label at both ends
  4. The power strip sitting off the carpet, clear of the walking path
  5. A completely clear vent slot on the top of the console
  6. The television standing solid when nudged, with no forward rock

Fall, cut, or crush A flat screen on a low unit tips forward when a child pulls on the frame or climbs the shelf underneath it. Check the strap or the mount bolts with your hand, not your eye.

Burn or fire Consoles and streaming boxes vent through slots on the top and back. A blanket laid over one, a stack of disc cases on top, or a cabinet door kept shut during play traps that heat against the board.

The console with the save files. You are keeping a previous-generation console because someone's save file lives on it, and that is a genuine reason. It is also exactly why it has sat unplugged behind the newer machine for two years. Settle it in this session rather than the next one. Plug it in now, and either move the save data to a card or to the current console, or hand a controller to the child and sit through one real session to find out whether anyone still cares. If it will not power on, or nobody asks for it again after that evening, you are storing a box of plastic guarding a memory it can no longer play. Photograph the save screen if it matters to someone, then route the console, its controllers, and its cables out together in one trip. Splitting them up is how half of it ends up back in the cabinet.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Soft detail brush (not in this zone's list, needed for vent grooves)

CLEAR THE CABINET AND FIND THE LOOSE CONTROLLER

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Clear the cabinet down to what pairs with a console or television the house still owns, and track down any controller missing from its dock.

How it works

A remote kept in case and a controller nobody feels responsible for both cost the same shelf space as the console people actually use.

You need

  • a bin bag
  • a box for anything to donate

Steps

  1. Pull every disc case, spare remote, headset, and loose cable out of the cabinet onto the floor. Anything that pairs with a console or a television you no longer own leaves today, including any remote you have been keeping in case. Check the dock: if a cradle is empty, that controller is loose in the house, and you go find it now rather than at the start of the next game.
  2. Return the console cabinet to one shelf per console, controllers docked beside it.

Victory. Every remaining disc case, controller, and cable in the cabinet pairs with a console or television the house still owns, and every dock cradle holds the controller that belongs in it.

If this keeps happening

  • CONFLICTING USERS
  • MISSING TRIGGER

TOY AND PLAY ZONE

Zone 2 of 6 · Family Room

FIVE OR SIX OPEN BINS. PICTURE LABELS. THE OVERFLOW LIVES IN THE CLOSET.

●●○○○

Hold the toys that are in active rotation, at a height and in a form where a child can put them away without an adult.

How it works

Done looks like: Five or six open bins with no lids on the lowest shelf, one category each, a photo of the contents taped to the front face where a child looking down sees it, the play mat rolled against the wall, and enough clear floor that two children can build without moving anything first.

Six things to check

  1. Five or six open bins with no lids sitting on the lowest shelf
  2. A photo of each bin's contents taped to its front face at a child's eye level
  3. The play mat rolled and standing against the wall
  4. A clear stretch of floor wide enough for two children to build without moving anything first
  5. Nothing stacked above shoulder height on the shelf
  6. No cracked plastic or loose small part visible in any open bin

Poison, choke, or strangle Marbles, small figure accessories, and the wheels that snap off toy vehicles land on a floor a crawling child also uses. Anything that fits through a toilet paper tube does not belong in a floor-height bin here.

Fall, cut, or crush Cracked hard plastic makes edges sharp enough to cut a knee, and a low shelf loaded heavy at the top will come over when a toddler pulls themselves up on it.

The gift nobody plays with. There is a large, loud, expensive thing in this room that a relative gave, and no child has chosen it unprompted in months. You keep it because of the giver, and every time you sort this zone you move it two feet and put it back. The rule that settles this: the shelf belongs to the child, not to the giver. Run one honest week, touch nothing, and watch whether any child reaches for it without being pointed at it. If none does, it goes. If you are genuinely worried about being asked, take one photograph of the child playing with it, send that photo to the person who gave it, then donate it while the good will is intact. Keeping an unloved toy on the lowest shelf does not honor anybody. It costs a bin that a favorite toy currently does not have.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Mesh wash bag (not in this zone's list, needed for soft toys in the machine)

BOARD GAME AND PUZZLE ZONE

Zone 3 of 6 · Family Room

STACKS NO MORE THAN FIVE HIGH. A NAME ON EVERY SHORT EDGE.

●●●○○

Store games and puzzles so that anything you pull off the shelf is complete and playable tonight.

How it works

Done looks like: Boxes lying flat in stacks no more than five high, each with a strip of tape on the short edge naming the game so you can read it from the doorway, every loose piece bagged inside its own lid, and not one box on the shelf that failed its count.

Six things to check

  1. Boxes lying flat in stacks no more than five high
  2. A tape strip naming the game readable on the short edge from the doorway
  3. No stack sitting high enough to require moving five boxes to reach the bottom one
  4. A loose piece bagged and visible inside its own box lid
  5. No box wearing an undated or old sticky note
  6. A clear shelf face with no dice or pawns scattered on the floor beneath it

Fall, cut, or crush A stack of five or six game boxes pulled from a high shelf comes down as one weight, usually onto whoever reached for the bottom one.

Poison, choke, or strangle Dice, pawns, and the small parts that scatter out of an open box during play are exactly the size a young child puts in their mouth, and this floor is shared with the play zone.

The game with one piece missing. This is the decision the shelf has been avoiding for years, and the answer is not the same for every box. Split missing pieces into two kinds. Substitutable: a lost meeple, a token, a die, a bill of play money. Those you can replace with a coin, a button, a printed sheet, or a request to the publisher. Structural: a lost rules booklet, a board section, a card from a deck that must be complete, or a single puzzle piece. Those cannot be substituted, and the game is over no matter how good the box looks on the shelf. Here is the mechanism that makes it stick. Write the missing item and today's date on a sticky note and stick it to the lid. Any box still wearing that note when you next reset this shelf leaves the house. That turns a vague guilt you have carried for years into a deadline you can either meet or honestly fail.
