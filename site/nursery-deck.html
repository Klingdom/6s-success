Built from the Manual, not around it

Six zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The nursery is the room where safety outranks everything else, and where the person using it is usually exhausted and working one-handed. Every standard here is built for that person at 2 a.m.: a bare crib, supplies inside one arm's sweep of the changing pad, cords shortened and heavy cartons moved off the shelf above the crib so nobody has to keep an eye on them. Start with the crib, because it is the quickest zone in the room and the one where getting it wrong matters most.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

66 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE NURSERY

SIX ZONES. START WITH THE CRIB BEFORE YOU TOUCH ANYTHING ELSE.

●○○○○

The nursery is the room where safety outranks everything else, and where the person using it is usually exhausted and working one-handed. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. NUZ-001 Crib and Sleep Zone. Empty the crib before you touch anything else in the room. It is one armful of work, and the standard is a number you cannot negotiate down: one fitted sheet, and nothing else over the rail.

The six zones, in order

  1. NUZ-001 Crib and Sleep Zone
  2. NUZ-002 Changing Station
  3. NUZ-003 Baby Clothing Zone
  4. NUZ-004 Feeding Station
  5. NUZ-005 Diaper and Care Backstock
  6. NUZ-006 Books and Quiet Play Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your nursery. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do NUA-013 The Full Nursery Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the cord beside the crib, the unanchored furniture across every zone, and the chemicals and medicine on the backstock shelf.

CRIB AND SLEEP ZONE

Zone 1 of 6 · Nursery

ONE SHEET. ONE SLEEP SACK. NOTHING CROSSES THE RAIL.

●●○○○

The one surface in the house where your baby is left alone, which is why it holds a fitted sheet and nothing more.

How it works

Done looks like: A bare mattress with one fitted sheet pulled tight to the corners, the sleep sack folded on the dresser, the monitor mounted on the wall with its cable clipped high and at least an arm's length back from the rails, and nothing draped over the top rail.

Six things to check

  1. A bare mattress holding one fitted sheet pulled tight to all four corners
  2. A folded sleep sack sitting on top of the dresser
  3. A monitor mounted on the wall with its cable clipped up and back from the rails
  4. A clear top rail with nothing draped over it
  5. No quilt, plush toy, or bumper anywhere inside the crib
  6. A clear arm's length of space between the mounted cable and the rails

Poison, choke, or strangle Loose bedding, bumpers, and plush in the crib are a suffocation risk to an infant who cannot roll off them, and a blind or monitor cord reachable through the slats is a strangling risk.

Fall A mattress left at the high setting after your baby can pull to stand puts the rail below chest height, and the fall is over the side onto a hard floor.

The quilt your mother made. Somebody who loves you has given you bedding for this crib. It is beautiful, it took them weeks, and it cannot go in there. The rule that settles it: sentimental bedding gets hung on the wall above the crib or folded into the keepsake box, never inside the rails, and you tell the giver once, plainly, in the same breath as showing them where you hung it. Say it early rather than letting it sit in the crib until the first time you notice it pushed up against your baby's face.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Washing machine on a hot wash
  • Baby-safe rinse or plain warm water for the mouthed rail

STRIP THE CRIB TO ONE SHEET

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Strip the crib down to the mattress and one fitted sheet, sending everything else crossing the rail to its real place.

How it works

A crib loaded with a quilt, a plush toy, or a muslin is not decorated, it is a suffocation risk to a baby who cannot roll off any of them.

You need

  • the toy basket
  • a laundry basket

  2. Pull the fitted sheet tight to all four corners so nothing sits loose in the sleep space.

DROP THE MATTRESS AND CLEAR THE CORD PATH

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Drop the mattress to its lowest setting and clear every cord reachable from inside the rails.

How it works

The day your baby can push up onto hands and knees, a high mattress puts the rail below chest height, and a blind or monitor cord within reach of the slats is a strangling risk that does not wait for a convenient time to fix.

You need

  • a screwdriver if the frame needs it
  • cable clips
  • a step stool

Steps

  1. Drop the mattress to its lowest setting the day your baby can push up onto hands and knees, because the next thing they learn is pulling to stand.
  2. Move the crib clear of the blind cord, the lamp flex, and the monitor cable; find any cord reachable from inside the rails and shorten or clip it out of reach today.
  3. Recheck the gap between the mattress top and the rail with your own hand, not by eye alone.

Victory. The mattress sits at the lowest setting once your baby can push up onto hands and knees, and no cord is reachable from inside the rails.

If this keeps happening

  • SAFETY CONSTRAINT
  • MISSING TRIGGER
  • UNCLEAR OWNERSHIP
  • WRONG LOCATION

CHANGING STATION

Zone 2 of 6 · Nursery

EVERYTHING YOUR HAND TOUCHES IS INSIDE ONE ARM'S SWEEP.

●●●○○

A pad you can work at with one hand staying on your baby the entire time.

How it works

Done looks like: Diapers, wipes, and cream all touchable without moving your feet or lifting your hand off your baby, a lined pail within one step, two spare outfits in the drawer directly beneath the pad, and a pad surface with nothing stacked on it.

Six things to check

  1. Diapers, wipes and cream all standing within arm's reach of the pad
  2. A lined pail sitting within one step of the pad
  3. Two spare outfits inside the drawer directly beneath the pad
  4. A pad surface holding nothing stacked on it
  5. No lamp, framed photo, or lotion bottle sitting on the dresser top
  6. No loose wipe refill bag or pail liner roll sitting out on the dresser

Fall A baby rolling off the changing pad while you turn to reach a shelf, which is why nothing needed during a change may sit outside your arm's sweep.

Poison, choke, or strangle Loose wipe refill bags and the diaper pail liner roll left on the dresser top are thin plastic within reach of small hands, and diaper cream tubes are a swallowing risk once your baby can grab the surface edge.

The second station downstairs. You have half a changing setup on the living room floor, because carrying the baby upstairs eight times a day is unbearable. The rule: run it as a full station or do not run it at all. A half-stocked spot is where you discover you have wipes and no diapers with a bare baby on a sofa. Either mirror the card exactly, same counts, same restock trigger, or replace it with a single caddy you carry down in the morning and back up at night. One and a half stations is the option that fails you.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Washing machine on a hot wash

CLEAR THE DRESSER TOP TO WHAT A CHANGE NEEDS

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Clear the dresser top down to what your hand actually uses during a change, and shut away every loose bag or roll.

How it works

A lamp or a lotion bottle sitting near the pad is not decor, it is one more thing to knock into a full basin or a bare baby mid-change.

You need

  • a small box for items that belong elsewhere
  • a drawer to shut loose plastic into

Steps

Victory. The pad surface and the dresser top around it hold only what a change needs, and nothing loose plastic is left where a hand could reach it.

BABY CLOTHING ZONE

Zone 3 of 6 · Nursery

ONE SIZE PER DRAWER. THE OUTGOING BAG ALREADY HAS SOMETHING IN IT.

●●●●○

Holds the sizes your baby fits this month and moves everything else out of the house fast.

How it works

Done looks like: Each drawer holds one size only with the size written on the drawer front, sleepers and bodysuits filed upright so you read every collar from above, a next-size bin on the closet shelf, and an outgoing bag on the door hook with something already in it.

Six things to check

  1. A drawer front carrying one size written large enough to read from a step back
  2. Sleepers and bodysuits filed upright so every collar reads from above
  3. A next-size bin standing on the closet shelf
  4. An outgoing bag hanging on the door hook with something already inside it
  5. No drawer holding two different sizes at once
  6. No tags-still-on outfit sitting in a drawer your baby has outgrown

Fall, cut, or crush An unanchored chest of drawers with two drawers open will tip forward onto a child using it to climb; anchor it to the wall and close each drawer before opening the next.

Poison, choke, or strangle Decorative buttons, ribbon ties, and hood drawstrings on older hand-me-down clothing come off in a mouth or wrap around a neck; remove them before the garment enters the drawer.

The outfit she never wore. There is a 0 to 3 month outfit in that drawer with the tags still on, given by someone who mattered, now three sizes too small. Keeping it feels like keeping the gift; giving it away feels like a small betrayal. The rule that settles it: keep two garments per size, and choose them by what your baby actually lived in, the sleeper from the first night home, the one in every photograph. The unworn gift is not a memory, it is stock, and it should go to someone else while it is still new enough to delight them. Photograph it on the way out if that helps you close the bag.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
