Floor, bed, surfaces, then drawers and closet

How to declutter a bedroom in one day, step by step

Declutter a bedroom in the order you can see the result: clear the floor first, then make the bed, then the surfaces, the nightstands and the dresser top. That takes about two to three hours and changes how the room feels tonight. Leave the drawers and the closet, about an hour or more each, for their own sessions.

The short answer. Bring three bags or boxes, for things that belong in another room, things that leave the house, and trash. Work in a fixed order, floor, bed, surfaces, nightstands, dresser top, and finish each one before starting the next. Do not open the closet until everything else is done. A clear floor and a made bed are most of what makes a bedroom feel calm.

Before you start: three bags and a time limit

Set yourself up so the work only moves in one direction. You need:

  • A "goes elsewhere" basket. For anything that belongs in another room: mugs, the kids' things, the mail. Do not walk it there one item at a time; that is how a session ends in the kitchen. Empty the basket once, at the end.
  • A "leaves the house" bag. Clothes that no longer fit or get worn, duplicates, things to donate or pass on.
  • A trash bag, and a second for recycling.
  • A laundry basket, if the hamper is already full.

Then decide how long you are working. If one day is the plan, the first part below takes about two to three hours. If you only have thirty minutes, do step one and step two and stop. A bedroom with a clear floor and a made bed is already better, and you can come back.

Step 1: Clear the floor

Start with the floor because it is the biggest visible change and the easiest decisions. Pick up everything, working around the room in one direction, and put each thing in exactly one place: the hamper, a bag, or a pile on the bed of things that live in this room.

Clothes are usually most of it. Worn clothes go in the hamper. Clean ones go on the bed to be put away in step 3. If you are not sure whether something is clean, it goes in the wash; deciding costs more time than washing.

When the floor is clear, run the vacuum, even quickly. It is the moment the room starts to look different, and it is worth having before you get tired.

Step 2: Reset the bed

Clear everything off the bed except the pile you just made, then strip it if the sheets are due, and make it. A made bed becomes the flat surface you sort on for the rest of the day, and it is the single thing that makes the room look finished. The bed and bedding zone has the full standard, including how many pillows and throws actually earn their place.

Step 3: Surfaces, one at a time

Now the chair, the top of the dresser, the window sill, and anything else flat. Do one surface completely before starting the next. For each thing, ask one question: does this get used in the bedroom? If yes, it stays and gets a place. If it belongs in another room, it goes in the basket. If nobody uses it, it goes in the leave bag.

The chair that holds clothes deserves its own word. It is usually doing a real job, holding clothes that are worn but not dirty, so give that job a proper home instead of fighting it: a hook on the back of the door or a single hanger rail, with a limit of one outfit. The pile on the chair is a symptom of things with no assigned home, not of laziness.

Put away the clean clothes from the bed as you go. If they do not fit in the drawers, that is the sign the drawers need their own session; put them in one bag labeled "to fold" for now and move on.

Step 4: The nightstands and the dresser top

Empty one nightstand completely onto the bed. Wipe it. Put back only what you reach for between getting into bed and falling asleep: a lamp, the book you are actually reading, a glass of water, the charger. Everything else goes back to where it is used or into a bag. The nightstand zone covers the drawer too.

If you share the room, the other nightstand belongs to the other person. Clear only what is clearly yours, and leave their side for them. A bedroom shared by two people is a shared zone, and it stays tidy only when both people agree the standard.

Then the dresser top. It is the landing spot for pockets: keys, coins, receipts, jewelry. Give each of those one small dish or tray and clear the rest. A dresser top that holds a tray and a lamp stays clear far longer than one that holds everything.

Step 5: Empty the "goes elsewhere" basket

Walk the basket around the house once and put everything where it lives. Take the leave bags straight to the car, not to the front door, and put the trash out. This is the step that makes the day count; a bedroom that is clear because its clutter moved to the hallway has not been decluttered.

At this point you have done the part most people mean by "declutter my bedroom". It takes about two to three hours for most bedrooms, and the room will look and feel different tonight.

Another day: the drawers and the closet

The dresser drawers and the closet are each about an hour to an hour and a half, almost all of it deciding about clothes. Doing them on the same day as the rest is how a one-day declutter turns into a bed covered in clothes at midnight. Give each its own session, one drawer or one section of the closet at a time.

The question for clothes is simple: have you worn it in the last year, and does it fit? Keep what you wear. Let go of what you do not, and for the few things that are hard to decide, tag each one with a decide-by date and let the date make the call, as in how long to keep a maybe. Fold drawers upright so you can see every item, and hang the closet so everything you wear this season faces you.

Your bedroom declutter checklist

  • Three bags and a basket ready: elsewhere, leaves, trash, laundry
  • Floor cleared, all clothes in the hamper or on the bed, floor vacuumed
  • Bed stripped if due, and made
  • Chair cleared, with a hook or rail for worn-but-clean clothes
  • Every surface cleared, one at a time
  • Your nightstand holds only what you use in bed
  • One tray on the dresser top for pockets
  • The elsewhere basket emptied, leave bags in the car, trash out
  • Drawers and closet booked as their own sessions

A very messy room, or a child's room

If the room is so full you cannot see the floor, the order still works; it just takes longer. Do step 1 in timed rounds of twenty minutes with a break between, and stop when you are tired, because decisions made exhausted are the ones you undo. Start at the door and work inward, so the path to the bed is clear first. If the room feels too much to face at all, where to start decluttering is written for that moment.

For a child's or a teenager's room, the same steps work with the child doing them, not an adult doing them while they are out. Keep the choices small and concrete, and make the homes easy for them to use: open bins, hooks at their height, a hamper where they actually undress. A room that is hard for its owner to use comes back messy within a week, which is the root cause covered in why your family won't put things back. For the toys, see how to organize toys.

Keeping it that way

Two minutes at night does more than a big tidy every month: clothes in the hamper or on the hook, pockets into the tray, anything else back where it lives. The bedroom gets messy again for the same few reasons every time, a missing hamper, no place for worn clothes, a surface everything lands on, and the full list is in why is my house always messy. Fix the reason, and the room stays clear without the effort.

Common questions

How do I declutter my bedroom step by step?

Get three bags ready, for things that belong elsewhere, things that leave the house, and trash. Then clear the floor, make the bed, clear each surface one at a time, reset the nightstands and the dresser top, and finally take the elsewhere basket around the house once. Leave the drawers and the closet for their own sessions.

Can you declutter a bedroom in one day?

Yes, if one day means the floor, the bed, the surfaces, the nightstands and the dresser top, which take about two to three hours for most bedrooms. The dresser drawers and the closet are about an hour to an hour and a half each, almost all of it deciding about clothes, and go better as separate sessions.

Where do I start decluttering a messy bedroom?

Start with the floor. It is the biggest visible change and the easiest decisions: worn clothes to the hamper, clean clothes onto the bed, everything else into a bag or the elsewhere basket. Then make the bed, so you have a clear surface to sort on for the rest of the day.

How do I declutter a bedroom fast?

If you only have thirty minutes, clear the floor and make the bed, then stop. Those two steps change how the room looks more than anything else. Put everything that belongs in another room in one basket and empty it once at the end instead of walking things out one at a time.

How do I declutter a very messy room when I feel overwhelmed?

Work in timed rounds of twenty minutes with a break between, starting at the door and working inward so the path to the bed is clear first. Stop when you are tired, because decisions made exhausted are the ones you undo, and come back to the same order next time.

What do I do with the chair full of clothes?

The chair is usually holding clothes that are worn but not dirty, which is a real job. Give that job a proper home instead, such as a hook on the back of the door or a single hanger rail, with a limit of one outfit, and the chair can go back to being a chair.

How do I keep my bedroom from getting messy again?

Spend two minutes each night putting clothes in the hamper or on the hook, emptying your pockets into one tray on the dresser, and returning anything else to where it lives. Then fix the reason it keeps coming back, usually a missing hamper, no place for worn clothes, or a surface everything lands on.
