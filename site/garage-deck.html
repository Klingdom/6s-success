THE GARAGE

SEVEN ZONES. START WITH THE BENCH.

●○○○○

The garage takes what every other room in the house evicts, and it carries three problems no other room has all at once: fuel and garden chemicals stored a few feet from where a car parks, heavy bins stacked overhead above where people walk and park, and edged power tools within reach of a child's hand. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. GAZ-001 Primary Workbench. Start with the workbench, not the pile inside the door. A cleared bench top gives you somewhere to lay out the contents of a bin while you judge them, and it is the one part of the garage that pays you back the same afternoon. The slab comes last, once the wall rails and the overhead racks have taken what belongs on them.

The seven zones, in order

  1. GAZ-001 Primary Workbench
  2. GAZ-002 Hand Tool Wall and Cabinets
  3. GAZ-003 Power Tool and Battery Zone
  4. GAZ-004 Automotive Care Zone
  5. GAZ-005 Sports and Recreation Zone
  6. GAZ-006 Lawn and Garden Tool Zone
  7. GAZ-007 Bulk and Overhead Storage

How to play

  1. Deal the seven ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your garage. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 4. With more than one, split the chain: one clears the bench and tool wall, another works the power tools and the overhead rack.

Safety first. Do GAA-015 The Fuel-and-Chemistry Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers what makes this room different from every other: fuel, lithium packs and garden chemicals a few feet from where the car parks.

PRIMARY WORKBENCH

Zone 1 of 7 · Garage

BARE TOP. ONE TRAY. SWEPT CONCRETE BENEATH.

●●●○○

The one surface in the house where things get fixed, glued, cut, and assembled.

How it works

Done looks like: A bare bench top holding only the vise and the task light, one tray with the current job in it, clamps racked on the rail, and swept concrete visible under the bench.

Six things to check

  1. A bare bench top holding only the vise and the task light
  2. One tray holding the current job, nothing else
  3. Clamps racked on the rail within reach
  4. Swept concrete visible under the bench
  5. No cord crossing the strip of floor you stand on
  6. No chisel or blade resting edge-up on the top

Fall, cut, or crush Chisels, utility knives, and handsaws loose in the bench drawer or lying edge-up on the top where you sweep with a bare hand.

Burn or fire Rags soaked with oil, stain, or solvent left balled in an open bin under the bench. They can heat on their own with no flame anywhere near them.

The project that has been there since spring. Name the half-finished thing sitting on your bench right now. It gets one of three verdicts today: a specific weekend written on it to finish it, a breakdown that returns the timber, hardware, and hinges to stock, or the skip. The rule that settles it is that anything which has sat untouched through one full season is not a project any more, it is furniture with your intentions stacked on top. Do not solve this by lifting it onto a shelf. That only moves the decision somewhere darker and costs you the bench in the meantime, which is the one surface the whole garage depends on.

CLEAR THE BENCH TO THE VISE AND THE LIGHT

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Clear the bench top down to the vise and the task light, put the current job in one tray, rack the clamps, and sweep until concrete shows underneath.

How it works

A bench with no boundary on what belongs there is why the next job never gets a clear surface to start on, and 'I might need it later' is not the same test as 'this is today's job.'

You need

  • a tray for the current job
  • a broom
  • nothing else beyond fifteen minutes

Steps

  1. Clear the bench top down to the vise and the task light, put the current job in one tray, rack the clamps on the rail, and sweep until you can see concrete under the bench.
  2. Wipe the vise screw and give it a drop of oil before you close up.
  3. Check the bench outlet and task light cord for nicks while you're down there.

Victory. A bare bench, one tray holding one job, and swept concrete visible underneath.

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

HAND TOOL WALL AND CABINETS

Zone 2 of 7 · Garage

EVERY TOOL ON ITS OUTLINE. NOTHING BLANK.

●●●○○

Keeps the tools you reach for weekly visible, findable, and provably all present.

How it works

Done looks like: Every hand tool hanging on its own drawn outline with no blank silhouettes, one hammer and one adjustable wrench at chest height nearest the door, saw teeth sheathed, and the cabinet latched.

Six things to check

  1. Every hand tool hanging on its own drawn outline
  2. No blank silhouette anywhere on the board
  3. One hammer at chest height nearest the door
  4. One adjustable wrench at chest height nearest the door
  5. Saw teeth sheathed
  6. The cabinet door latched

Fall, cut, or crush Unsheathed saw teeth and chisel edges hung at hand height, and heavy wrenches on shallow hooks directly above where you stand to work.

Poison, choke, or strangle An unlatched low cabinet holding loose box-cutter blades, screws, and drill bits within a toddler's reach.

The third hammer. You almost certainly own three claw hammers, four adjustable wrenches, and a fistful of Phillips drivers, and the reason you have never thinned them is that each one, held on its own, still works. Working is not the test. The test is which one your hand actually goes to. Keep that one, plus exactly one deliberate spare parked where a second person would need it, the kitchen drawer or the boot of the car. Every remaining duplicate goes today to a friend, a tool library, or the metal skip. A hammer you keep because it still works and never choose is taking the hook that would have made the hammer you love findable in one glance from the door.

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

POWER TOOL AND BATTERY ZONE

Zone 3 of 7 · Garage

ONE SHELF PER SYSTEM. NO PACK LEFT CHARGING.

●●●○○

Holds the powered tools, their packs, and their chargers so a tool is always ready to run and nothing is left charging unattended.

How it works

Done looks like: One shelf per battery system with its charger mounted at the front edge and no pack sitting on it, every drill and saw in its case or on a labelled hook with guards down, and bits, blades, and sanding discs in the shallow drawer underneath.

Six things to check

  1. One shelf per battery system
  2. A charger mounted at the front edge of each shelf
  3. No battery pack sitting on any charger
  4. Every drill and saw in its case or on a labelled hook
  5. Every blade guard down
  6. Bits, blades and sanding discs in the shallow drawer underneath

Burn or fire Lithium packs left charging overnight on a wooden shelf, or stored alongside the petrol can and the rag bin.

Fall, cut, or crush A circular saw stored with its guard wedged open, or a table saw blade left raised where a hand reaches across the shelf for a pack.

The tool whose batteries no longer exist. Somewhere on that shelf is a good tool on a dead platform: the drill from the brand you left behind, one tired pack, and a charger nobody makes any more. You keep it because the tool itself is fine, and it is. Decide this with money rather than loyalty. Price one replacement pack today. If a pack costs more than half what a current equivalent tool costs, or you cannot buy one at all, the tool is finished, so sell it with its charger while it still means something to somebody, or take it to electrical recycling. Running two battery systems is a cost you can defend. Running three means a hunt through three shelves every time you want to put a hole in a wall.

AUTOMOTIVE CARE ZONE

Zone 4 of 7 · Garage

UPRIGHT. DATED. ONLY THIS DRIVEWAY'S CARS.

●●○○○

Keeps the fluids, cables, and cleaning kit for the cars you actually own upright, labelled, and separate from everything the household touches.

How it works

Done looks like: Every fluid bottle standing upright on a clean drip tray with its opened date written on the shoulder, jumper cables and tyre gauge together in one bag by the door, rags in a closed metal can, and not a single bottle for a car nobody here drives.

Six things to check

  3. Jumper cables and tyre gauge together in one bag by the door
  4. Oily rags sealed inside a closed metal can
  5. Not a single bottle for a car nobody here drives

Poison, choke, or strangle Antifreeze tastes sweet, and a spilled puddle or a decanted unlabelled bottle will kill a dog or a curious child.

The fluids for the car you no longer own. The hardest bottles here are the two thirds full ones. They cost real money, they have not gone off, and they fit nothing on your driveway. The rule is that every bottle must match a vehicle parked at this address, and no match means no keep. Offer them this week to the neighbour who drives that model, and take whatever is unclaimed to your council's household hazardous waste collection, never down a drain. Then write the opened date on every bottle from today onward, because the only reason this shelf grew this deep is that nobody could tell which ones were still good, so nothing was ever thrown out.
