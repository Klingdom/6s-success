Built from the Manual, not around it

Six zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

A patio lives outdoors, which puts everything on it under a clock the indoor rooms never feel: sun makes plastic brittle, damp gets into fabric and end grain, and a season of putting things off arrives as a replacement cost. Start with the surface you walk on and the rail you lean on, because those are the only things out here that can put someone in hospital. Everything after that, the cushions and the grill and the pots, is about making the space usable within one trip through the back door.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-colored panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

THE PATIO OR DECK

SIX ZONES. THE SURFACE AND THE RAIL COME FIRST.

●○○○○

A patio lives outdoors, on a clock the indoor rooms never feel. This card is the map.

How it works

Start here. PDZ-006 Surface, Rail, and Safety Zone. Do the Surface, Rail, and Safety Zone first, on a dry morning, before you touch a single cushion. If the boards and the railing are not sound, everything you arrange on top of them is decoration.

The six zones

  1. PDZ-001 Outdoor Seating Zone
  2. PDZ-002 Grill and Outdoor Cooking Zone
  3. PDZ-003 Outdoor Dining Zone
  4. PDZ-004 Garden and Plant Care Zone
  5. PDZ-005 Outdoor Storage Zone
  6. PDZ-006 Surface, Rail, and Safety Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Start with the Surface, Rail, and Safety Zone on a dry morning, before you touch a single cushion.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true on your patio. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 2. Cleaning out here is how you inspect: you will never spot a popped fastener, a soft board end, or a spider web in a burner tube while sitting in a chair.

Safety first. Do PDA-013 The Full Patio Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the rail and boards, the grease tray and tank, the chemical box latch, and the deck box lid stay in one pass.

OUTDOOR SEATING ZONE

Zone 1 of 6 · Patio or Deck

FOUR CHAIRS YOU TRUST. EVERY CUSHION IN THE BOX BY NIGHTFALL.

●●○○○

Seats people can drop into without checking them first, with the cushions kept dry.

How it works

Done looks like: Four chairs you can sit in without testing, set close enough to talk across, every cushion in the deck box, and no cover crumpled on the ground behind a planter.

Six things to check

  1. Four outdoor chairs set close enough to talk across without raising your voice
  2. Every seat cushion sitting inside the closed deck box rather than on a chair
  3. A furniture cover folded flat rather than crumpled on the ground behind a planter
  4. A bare walking route from the back door to the steps with nothing parked in it
  5. A deck box lid closing flat under its own weight
  6. A chair frame standing on its side, drying after a hose-down

Fall, cut, or crush Sun-aged plastic and resin chairs fail suddenly at the leg joint, and they fail under the person least able to catch themselves.

Fall A folded lounger or a stacked chair left across the route between the door and the steps is the thing people trip on carrying a tray in the dark.

The speckled cushion. You have a cushion with gray and black speckling along the piping. It got rained on, it went into the box damp, and every spring you tell yourself you will scrub it properly. Give it exactly one honest attempt: wash it with a fabric cleaner meant for outdoor material, dry it fully in sun, and put it back. If the speckling returns within the same season, the growth is inside the foam core and no surface cleaning reaches it. That cushion is finished. Replace it or go without, and either is better than a seat nobody chooses. The pattern to break is buying replacement cushions before you have fixed the reason the last set died, which was that they had nowhere dry to go in a hurry.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Garden hose
  • Stiff-bristled scrub brush
  • Mild fabric-safe cushion cleaner
  • Broom

A CHAIR WITH A WOBBLE, A CRACKED ARM, OR A FLEXING SEAT IS STILL IN THE CIRCLE

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE. THEN WHY.

●○○○○

“A resin or plastic chair with a wobble, a cracked arm, or a seat pan that flexes when you press it hard is still standing in the evening seating circle instead of at the curb.”

How it works

Why does this happen here?

  1. A chair failing under someone is a real fall risk, and that outranks how many of the other chairs still look fine standing there → SAFETY CONSTRAINT
  2. There is no agreed point where a chair officially fails the standard, so it stays in rotation until it actually gives out under someone → MISSING STANDARD
  3. It has creaked in that same spot for months, long enough that the sound stopped registering as a warning → PERCEPTUAL BLINDNESS

PRESS EVERY CHAIR AND BOX THE CUSHIONS

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Press every chair for a wobble, a crack, or a flex, and carry every cushion sitting anywhere except a chair into the deck box.

How it works

You need

  • the deck box

Steps

GRILL AND OUTDOOR COOKING ZONE

Zone 2 of 6 · Patio or Deck

FOUR TOOLS ON THE HOOKS. THE TANK LEVEL MARKED ON THE COLLAR.

●●●○○

Cook over fire without leaving the grill to find a tool or wondering if there is gas.

How it works

Done looks like: Grates scraped bare, grease tray emptied and back in its rails, four tools on the grill's own hooks and no fifth anywhere, the propane tank upright with its level written on the collar, and a clear arm's length between the grill body and the siding, railing, and umbrella canopy.

Six things to check

  1. Grates scraped bare of built-up residue
  2. An empty grease tray sitting back in its rails
  3. Exactly four tools hanging on the grill's own hooks, nothing extra
  4. A propane tank standing upright with its fuel level marked on the collar
  5. A clear arm's length of open air between the grill body and the siding
  6. No fifth tool anywhere near the hooks

Burn or fire Accumulated grease in the tray and drip pan is what turns a normal flare-up into a fire, and a grill within reach of siding, decking rail, or a canopy gives that fire somewhere to go.

Poison, choke, or strangle Bristles shed from a worn wire brush stick to the grates and then to food, and a swallowed bristle is a genuine emergency.

The tank you are not sure about. You have two propane tanks and no idea which has gas in it, so you keep buying a third and the collection grows against the wall. Settle it with a bathroom scale. The tare weight is stamped on the collar as TW and a number; weigh the tank, subtract that number, and what remains is propane. If what is left would not carry you through one long cook with guests, that is a refill errand, not a spare. Write the answer on the collar in marker, and write it again the day a tank comes back from the exchange. While you are reading the collar, find the date stamp: tanks have a requalification life and the exchange counter will simply refuse an old one, which is worth knowing now rather than on a Saturday with people arriving. Two tanks is the right number. Anything beyond two is a storage problem pretending to be preparedness.

What it uses

  • Food-area degreaser
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Grate scraper or coiled grill brush
  • Nylon brush
  • Hot soapy water and bucket

WEIGH THE TANKS AND START THE COOK COUNT

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Weigh each propane tank on a bathroom scale, subtract the tare weight, and mark the result on the collar; then start a tracked count of cooks on a card taped inside the cabinet door for when the grease tray gets emptied.

How it works

Guessing a tank's level by lifting it is slower and less honest than the five minutes weighing takes, and a grease tray with no tracked trigger only gets scraped once somebody notices the smell, by which point the flare-up risk has already been sitting there.

You need

  • a bathroom scale
  • a marker
  • an index card

Steps

  1. Weigh each tank, subtract the tare weight stamped on the collar as TW, and mark the remaining propane weight on the collar in marker.
  2. Tape a card inside the cabinet door and start a tally of cooks toward the next grease tray emptying, and brush soapy water on the hose and regulator to check for leaking bubbles while the tanks are out.

Victory. Every tank's remaining level is marked on its collar, and a card inside the cabinet door is tracking cooks toward the next grease tray emptying.

OUTDOOR DINING ZONE

Zone 3 of 6 · Patio or Deck

BARE TABLE. ONE TRAY. THE UMBRELLA CRANKED DOWN EVERY NIGHT.

●●○○○

Feed people outdoors without carrying the kitchen out in four trips.

How it works

Done looks like: A bare tabletop except the umbrella and one weighted tray, six chairs tucked fully under, string lights clipped along the rail with no cord crossing the floor, and nothing stored beneath the table.

Six things to check

  1. A bare tabletop holding only the umbrella and one weighted tray
  2. Six chairs tucked fully under the table
  3. String lights clipped along the rail
  4. No cord crossing the deck floor
  5. A closed umbrella, cranked down
  6. Nothing stored in the open space beneath the table

Fall, cut, or crush An umbrella left open on an undersized base lifts in a gust and comes down pole first, usually across the table where people were sitting.

Water and electricity Indoor extension leads run out to string lights or a speaker sit in rain and puddles on the boards, which is the one electrical shortcut outdoors that genuinely kills.

Its own set, or nothing. Decide once whether this table gets dishware of its own, because the half-answer is what keeps it covered. Count the meals you genuinely ate out here last season. If it was more than a handful, buy one stackable set, plates, tumblers, and flatware for the number of seats, and keep it in a lidded tote in the deck box so eating outdoors costs one trip. If it was two or three meals all year, admit that, and carry the indoor plates out on those few occasions. What you must stop is the middle option: storing four faded plates, a mismatched tumbler, and a serving bowl outdoors for a set you will never complete. That orphaned half-set is precisely why the table always has something standing on it, and why it never looks ready to sit down at.
