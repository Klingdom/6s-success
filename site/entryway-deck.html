Two Entryway decks, on purpose, for now

Same room, two different zone counts, and here is why

Everything the outside world sends into your house passes through the entryway twice a day, which is why it fails faster than any other room. It fails from unmade decisions rather than dirt: mail with no verdict, keys with no home, a fourth pair of shoes nobody will admit to owning. Start here before any other room, because it is small and because you will walk through the result four times tomorrow.

The free deck at deck.html is illustrated, photographed, and has been live for months: eighty eight cards built around twelve "Micro Zone" cards, a model written before the Manual's own diagnosis layer existed. The Manual, the zone pages, the Home Quest and the print pack all teach five real Entryway zones instead, the same five this page uses. Rebuilding or retiring the older deck is a bigger decision than one generator can make alone, so both are live for now, each saying plainly what the other is. If you own the illustrated deck and the print pack, this is the version that matches the pack.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

57 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

The Entryway

⌂RoomEYR-001
⌂

THE ENTRYWAY

FIVE ZONES. START AT THE DOOR.

●○○○○

The entryway is five small places, not one big job. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. EYZ-005 Door, Mat, and Immediate Floor. Do the Door, Mat, and Immediate Floor first. It is the shortest zone in the room, it gives you somewhere to stand while you work the other four, and the change shows on the hallway floor within a week.

The five zones, in order

  1. EYZ-001 Landing Zone
  2. EYZ-002 Coat and Outerwear Zone
  3. EYZ-003 Shoe and Boot Zone
  4. EYZ-004 Entry Console or Bench
  5. EYZ-005 Door, Mat, and Immediate Floor

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your entryway. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do EYA-013 The Full Rebuild Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers every hook, shelf and door in the room.

LANDING ZONE

Zone 1 of 5 · Entryway

ONE VERDICT PER SHEET. NOTHING RETURNS UNDECIDED.

●●○○○

The spot where pockets empty on the way in and refill on the way out.

How it works

Done looks like: One tray holding keys and sunglasses, one wallet and one phone per adult, a single upright folder with fewer than ten sheets of paper standing in it, and bare surface on both sides of the tray.

Six things to check

  1. One tray holding keys and sunglasses
  2. One wallet and one phone per adult
  3. A single folder standing upright, fewer than ten sheets
  4. Bare surface on both sides of the tray
  5. Charging dock clear of the drip line under the hooks
  6. No loose paper anywhere on the surface

Water and electricity Wet umbrellas and rain-soaked sleeves drip onto whatever sits directly below the hooks. If the charging dock or a power strip is under that line, move it out of the drip.

Burn or fire A phone dock, a lamp and a plug-in heater chained together behind the console is the overload that starts here. One dock, one outlet, nothing chained.

The paper with no verdict. What you have been avoiding here is not the keys, it is the six sheets of paper you keep shifting from one end of the surface to the other. They stay because each needs a small unpleasant action: a form, a phone call, a payment. The rule is that nothing returns to the surface without a verdict, and there are only three: act, file, recycle. If it is act, it stands upright in the folder with the date written in the corner. When any item in that folder passes fourteen days, it stops being paperwork and becomes a decision. Do it tonight, or accept honestly that you never will and recycle it. Both of those are answers. Leaving it on the surface is not.

CLEAR THE TRAY AND THE FOLDER

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Empty the tray and the paper stack, give every sheet a verdict, and put back only what belongs.

How it works

Paper with no verdict is the reason this surface fills back up faster than any other reset in the house.

You need

  • a bin bag
  • the recycling bin
  • a pen

Steps

  1. Empty the tray and the paper stack onto the surface, then give every sheet a verdict: act, file, or recycle, before anything goes back.
  2. Anything that needs action stands upright in the folder with the date written in the corner.
  3. Recycle what nobody is going to act on, honestly, rather than restack it.
  4. Return only the tray, holding keys and sunglasses, and the folder, to the bare surface.

Victory. One tray holds only keys and sunglasses, and no loose paper sits anywhere on the surface.

REBUILD THE LANDING SURFACE

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Change the surface so a verdict happens in the moment, not the pile.

How it works

A pocket-emptying spot with no charging dock and no folder invites paper and cables to just sit there.

You need

  • a shallow tray
  • a slim upright folder
  • a pen
  • a cable tie or clip

Steps

  1. Stand where your hands actually stop when you walk in, and put the tray there, not wherever it currently sits.
  2. Move the charging dock clear of the drip line under any hooks, onto the outlet nearest the tray.
  3. Set the folder upright at one end of the tray, and write today's date on a card inside it as the first entry.
  4. Coil and clip any charging cable so it lies flat rather than trailing across the surface.
  5. Count what has landed here in the past week that is not keys, sunglasses, a wallet, a phone, or paper, and give each item a home somewhere else in the house.

Victory. The tray sits exactly where your hand opens on the way in, the dock is clear of the drip line, and everything on the surface is one of the five named things.

If this keeps happening

  • WRONG LOCATION
  • NO ASSIGNED HOME

COAT AND OUTERWEAR ZONE

Zone 2 of 5 · Entryway

TWO EMPTY HOOKS, ALWAYS. COUNTED BY WEATHER, NOT BY NUMBER.

●●●○○

Hold the outerwear you will actually wear this week, at the moment you reach for it.

How it works

Done looks like: One coat per person on the rail, one bag, hats and gloves together in a single labeled bin on the shelf above, umbrellas standing in the stand, and at least two hooks left empty.

Six things to check

  1. One coat per person on the rail
  2. One bag hanging, not stacked on the floor
  3. Hats and gloves together in one labeled bin
  4. Umbrellas standing upright in the stand
  5. At least two hooks left empty
  6. Nothing weighty stored above head height

Fall, cut, or crush A bin of boots or a heavy holdall stored above head height on the coat shelf lands on whoever tugs the coat underneath it. Weight goes low, light bulky things go high.

Fall A rail loaded past what its brackets were fixed for sags first and lets go later, usually while somebody is standing directly beneath it.

The coat you feel guilty about. Somewhere on this rail is a good coat that cost real money and goes outside roughly never. It survives every clear-out because getting rid of it feels like admitting to the money. The rule is that coats are counted by weather, not by number. Each person gets one coat per kind of weather your climate genuinely produces: wet, cold, and something in between. A second coat in the same category has to beat the first one on a specific, nameable day. If you cannot name that day, that is the coat that leaves. The expensive one is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm. Give it one trial: front hook, most reachable spot, two weeks. If you did not reach for it, you have your answer and you had it before you started.

REBUILD THE COAT RAIL

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Count coats by weather instead of by number, and fix what the rail can actually hold.

How it works

A rail loaded past its bracket weight sags and lets go, usually while somebody stands directly beneath it.

You need

  • a bin bag for the giveaway pile
  • a step stool

Steps

  1. Take every coat off the rail and lay them out where you can see all of them at once.
  2. Sort into the weather categories your climate actually produces: wet, cold, and something in between.
  3. Keep one per person per category. A second coat in the same category has to name a specific day it beats the first, or it goes.
  4. Hang the kept coats back with a visible gap between each, heaviest nearest the door.
  5. Move anything heavy or bulky off the shelf above head height down to a low shelf instead.

Victory. At least two hooks stand empty at all times, and nothing heavy sits above head height on the shelf.

If this keeps happening

  • INSUFFICIENT CAPACITY
  • SENTIMENTAL ATTACHMENT

SHOE AND BOOT ZONE

Zone 3 of 5 · Entryway

TWO PAIRS PER PERSON. THE SOLE DECIDES, NOT THE MEMORY.

●●○○○

Hold the footwear that comes off at the door, and keep the grit it carried in off every other floor in the house.

How it works

Done looks like: Two pairs per person on the rack, soles down, boots standing in the tray, one pair of slippers each, and a clear stretch of floor a full stride wide between the door and the rack.

Six things to check

  1. Two pairs per person on the rack, soles down
  2. Boots standing upright in the tray
  3. One pair of slippers per person
  4. A full stride of bare floor between door and rack
  5. Shoe care solvents stored up and out of reach
  6. Nothing loose on the floor beside the rack

Fall Shoes left inside the arc of the door swing are the reason people go down while carrying bags with both hands. That strip of floor stays clear at full stride width, permanently.

Poison, choke, or strangle Polish, suede cleaner and waterproofing spray are solvents, and they usually sit in an open basket at exactly toddler height. Move shoe care up onto a shelf or out of the room.

The worn out pair you keep for the garden. Everyone has a pair with the tread rubbed smooth that survives on the grounds that it is fine for mucky jobs. Count them honestly and there are usually three such pairs and about one muddy job a year. The rule is that the sole decides, not the memory. Turn the shoe over. If the tread has gone smooth or the heel has worn through on the outer edge, that shoe is no longer safe on a wet step or a slick pavement, which means it is not gardening footwear, it is rubbish with sentimental value attached. Keep exactly one dirty-job pair per person and store it where the dirty jobs happen, not at the front door. The pair you would be embarrassed to be seen in is not a spare. It is a decision you already made and have not carried out.

REBUILD THE SHOE RACK

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Move shoe care solvents out of toddler reach and fix the rack so it cannot tip.

How it works

Polish and waterproofing spray sitting in an open basket at toddler height are a poisoning risk, and a top-heavy rack a child climbs is a fall risk.

You need

  • a shelf or high hook out of small hands' reach
  • wall anchors if the rack is tall and narrow

Steps

  1. Take every polish, cleaner and waterproofing spray off the rack and move them to a shelf above reach or out of the room.
  2. Turn every shoe sole-down; nothing stands on its toe.
  3. Move the heaviest pairs to the bottom shelf and the lightest to the top.
  4. If the rack is tall and narrow, fix it to the wall so a climbing child cannot pull it forward.
  5. Clear the floor between the door and the rack down to bare boards, a full stride wide.

Victory. Shoe care solvents sit out of a toddler's reach, the rack cannot tip, and the floor between door and rack is bare.

ENTRY CONSOLE OR BENCH

Zone 4 of 5 · Entryway

THREE ITEMS ON TOP. EMPTY SEAT. EVERY SECTION NAMED.

●●○○○

Somewhere to sit while you lace a boot, and somewhere to set a bag down for thirty seconds.

How it works

Done looks like: Three items or fewer on the top, a seat with nothing on it at all, and a drawer where every divided section has one named category and you can see the base of the drawer between things.

Six things to check

  1. Three items or fewer on the top surface
  2. The seat itself bare, nothing set down on it
  3. Drawer sections each holding one named category
  4. The base of the drawer visible between things
  5. Nothing stored here that is not needed at the door
  6. Bench strapped to the wall if it stands on narrow legs

Fall, cut, or crush A narrow console with drawers is a climbing frame to a three year old, and it comes over forwards with the drawers out. Strap it to the wall and keep the heaviest contents in the bottom drawer.

Poison, choke, or strangle Loose button batteries, coins and small magnets roll into the back corners of this drawer and it opens at exactly the height a toddler can reach. Batteries go in a lidded tin or out of the room entirely.

What the drawer is for. This drawer becomes the second junk drawer within a month of being emptied. It happens every time, because it is the nearest place to set down something you do not want to carry any further. The decision being avoided is not what to throw away. It is admitting the drawer needs a job at all. The rule: this drawer serves the door and nothing else. One test question per item, would you need this while wearing your shoes, either on the way out or in the first ten seconds back in. Dog lead, yes. Sun cream, yes. Spare car charger, yes. The screwdriver, the birthday candles and the case for a phone you no longer own all fail, and they fail even though they genuinely have nowhere better to live. Find them somewhere better today, in another room, with a name on it. If you do not, they will be back in this drawer by the weekend and so will four of their friends.

How it works

DOOR, MAT, AND IMMEDIATE FLOOR

Zone 5 of 5 · Entryway

BOTH MATS FLAT. THE SWING STAYS BARE.

●○○○○

Catch grit, water and road salt at the threshold so none of it reaches the rest of the floors.

How it works

Done looks like: A coarse mat outside the door and an absorbent one inside, both lying dead flat with no curled corners, nothing whatsoever on the floor inside the door's swing, and a door that latches on the first push.

Six things to check

  1. Coarse mat outside the door, lying flat
  2. Absorbent mat inside the door, lying flat
  3. No curled corners on either mat
  4. Nothing on the floor inside the door's swing
  5. The door latches on the first push
  6. Hinge screws snug, not loose

Fall A curled mat edge at a doorway catches the toe of someone stepping in with their eyes on their phone or their arms full. Flat edges, gripped or taped, checked every time you vacuum.

Fall, cut, or crush A door with a loose hinge screw or a failing closer swings harder than expected and catches fingers against the frame. Tighten the hinge screws while you are down there with the cloth.

When the mat is finished. A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, and the decision being dodged is spending money to replace something that is not visibly broken. So test it instead of looking at it. Walk in from outside on a dry gritty day, wipe your feet the way you actually wipe them, then lift the mat and look at both the floor beneath it and the fibres themselves. A working mat holds grit down inside its pile and drops a clear line of dirt when you shake it out. A finished mat has fibres flattened all in one direction, and the grit sits on top of them and walks straight off on the next pair of shoes. If it fails that test, replace it this month rather than next winter. A worn mat is not saving you anything. It is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between this door and the kitchen.

What it uses

  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Streak-free glass and mirror cleaner (if the door has a glass panel; not in this zone's kit)
