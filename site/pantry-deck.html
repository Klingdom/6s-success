Built from the Manual, not around it

Five zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The pantry is where you find out the difference between what you buy and what you actually eat. Almost everything that goes wrong on these shelves is one of two things: food you cannot see, and food you already owned when you bought it again. Start with the dry goods shelves, because that is where the oldest opened bags are hiding, and emptying them gives every other zone somewhere to put things.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

57 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE PANTRY

FIVE SHELVES. START WITH THE ONE THAT HIDES THE OLDEST BAG.

●○○○○

The pantry is where you find out the difference between what you buy and what you actually eat. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. PNZ-001 Dry Goods Shelves. Take one shelf completely off and onto the counter before you decide anything. A shelf sorted in place keeps its own history, and the bag that got pushed behind the cereal two years ago stays behind the cereal.

The five zones, in order

  1. PNZ-001 Dry Goods Shelves
  2. PNZ-002 Canned and Jarred Goods
  3. PNZ-003 Baking Zone
  4. PNZ-004 Snack and Lunch Zone
  5. PNZ-005 Backstock and Bulk Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true on your shelf. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do PNA-011 The Full Shelf Safety And Access Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers every shelf's height, the stool, and every hard-to-reach spot.

Dry Goods Shelves

▣ZonePNZ-001

DRY GOODS SHELVES

Zone 1 of 5 · Pantry

ONE BLOCK OF STAPLES. ONE LABEL EACH. READABLE FROM THE DOORWAY.

●●●○○

Holds the grains, pasta and cereals you actually cook from in a normal week.

How it works

Done looks like: Every opened bag is either sealed in a container with its opening date written on it, or gone. Pasta, rice and oats stand as one block, breakfast cereal as another, and you can read the level in every container from the doorway without lifting a thing.

Six things to check

  1. Rice, pasta and oats standing together as one labeled block at chest height
  2. Breakfast cereal boxes standing together as their own block
  3. Every opened bag sealed inside a matching container
  4. A handwritten date visible on each container's label
  5. The fill level inside every container readable without lifting it
  6. A two-step stool standing inside the pantry beneath the top shelf

Fall Reaching the top shelf from a kitchen chair or by standing on the bottom shelf edge. Keep a two-step stool inside the pantry, and if the stool is not there, that shelf is too high for anything you need weekly.

Fall, cut, or crush Glass grain jars stored above eye level. A full one is heavy, hands in a kitchen are wet, and glass on a hard floor puts the room out of use. Glass at chest height or lower, lidded plastic up top.

The bag you opened for one recipe. Half a kilo of buckwheat, the coarse polenta, the rice flour from the week you tried a new bread. You keep them because binning food feels wrong, and because you genuinely still intend to. Settle it with a rule: if it is open, and you cannot name the specific meal you will cook with it inside the next fourteen days, it leaves now. Unopened and in date, give it away while it is still worth having. Opened and more than a year old, it goes in the bin without further debate, because stale grain is not a saving you can ever spend.

CLEAR THE SHELF AND SMELL-TEST IT

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Bring down every bag and box on this shelf and smell-test every opened one, so nothing stale or forgotten survives on a technicality.

How it works

A bag with no date on it is impossible to judge honestly, so it just keeps not getting thrown out.

You need

  • a bin bag
  • a marker

Steps

  1. Bring down every bag and box on this shelf, open anything already opened, and smell the flour and the oats: stale, webbed, or bought for a recipe you have not cooked in months leaves today.
  2. For anything unopened and still in date but unlikely to get used, set it aside to give away rather than binning it.
  3. Group what stays into two blocks on the shelf: rice, pasta and oats together, cereal together.

Victory. Every opened bag is sealed in a labeled container or gone, and pasta, rice and oats stand together as one block.

If this keeps happening

  • UNRESOLVED DECISION
  • PERCEPTUAL BLINDNESS

LABEL EVERY CONTAINER AND MOVE THE GLASS DOWN

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Give every staple one container style with a written open date, and move any glass jar down to chest height or lower.

How it works

A missing label makes every container's age a guess, and a glass jar above eye level is a real fall and cut risk beside wet hands.

You need

  • matching containers
  • a permanent marker
  • adhesive labels

Steps

  1. Decant each staple into one container style, and write the item and today's date on the label the moment it is opened.
  2. Move every glass jar down to chest height or lower, and put lidded plastic containers up top instead.
  3. Keep a two-step stool inside the pantry for anything that still sits on the top shelf.

Victory. Every staple sits in a labeled container with its opening date on it, and no glass container sits above eye level.

Canned and Jarred Goods

▣ZonePNZ-002

CANNED AND JARRED GOODS

Zone 2 of 5 · Pantry

EVERY LABEL FACING OUT. NOTHING DENTED. THE OLDEST DATE UP FRONT.

●●○○○

Holds the cans and jars that turn an empty-fridge week into dinner.

How it works

Done looks like: Cans sit in rows on risers with every label facing you: tomatoes together, beans together, soup together. No dented seams, no domed lids, no jar hidden behind another jar, and the oldest date in each row standing at the front of it.

Six things to check

  1. Cans standing in rows on a stepped riser, every label facing outward
  2. Tomatoes grouped in one row, beans in another, soup in a third
  3. No dented seam or domed lid visible on any can
  4. No jar standing hidden directly behind another jar
  5. The oldest-dated can or jar standing at the front of its row
  6. A clean, dry shelf edge with no sticky ring under any jar

Poison, choke, or strangle A bulging or leaking can is a spoilage warning and no amount of cooking makes it safe. Bag it sealed, bin it, and wash the shelf underneath where it stood.

Fall, cut, or crush A dense row of cans on a high shelf. One tin landing on a bare foot is a broken toe, and a glass sauce jar bursting on tile leaves splinters you find for weeks. Cans and glass belong below shoulder height.

The best-before you keep arguing with. The soup two years past its date, the chickpeas whose date has rubbed off, the fancy jar somebody gave you that you are saving for an occasion that has not arrived. Best-before is about quality rather than safety, so you have been keeping these on a technicality and never eating them. End the argument with one pass: anything past best-before goes into a meal in the next seven days or it goes out this week. Anything past a use-by date goes now. Anything dented on the seam, rusted, or domed goes now regardless of the date, and it does not go to the food bank either. If you would not open it tonight, you are not saving it, you are storing it.

PULL EVERY DENTED OR DOMED CAN

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Take every can and jar off the shelf, check each one for damage or a bad date, and remove anything spoiled without debate.

How it works

A dented seam or a domed lid is a spoilage warning, not a judgment call, and it does not get tasted or given away.

You need

  • a bin bag

Steps

  1. Take every can and jar off the shelf, turn each one to find the date, and put anything dented, rusted, bulging or domed straight into the bin rather than back on the shelf.
  2. Bag anything spoiled sealed before it goes in the bin, and wipe the shelf underneath where it stood.
  3. Set every surviving can and jar back with its label facing out.

Victory. No dented, rusted, domed or bulging can or jar remains on the shelf, and every label faces out.

If this keeps happening

  • MISSING STANDARD
  • POOR VISIBILITY

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

BAKING ZONE

Zone 3 of 5 · Pantry

ONE LIFT-OUT BIN. LEAVENERS DATED. THE ALLERGEN SHELF STANDS APART.

●●●○○

Holds everything you need to start a bake without a trip to the shop first.

How it works

Done looks like: Flour, sugar and brown sugar in sealed containers you can open one-handed. Baking powder, bicarbonate of soda and yeast together in one small box with their dates showing. Cutters, cases and piping tips in a single lift-out bin, and no loose sprinkles rolling around the shelf.

Six things to check

  1. Flour, sugar and brown sugar standing in sealed one-handed containers
  2. Baking powder, bicarbonate of soda and yeast together in one small dated box
  3. A single lift-out bin holding cutters, cases and piping tips
  4. A dedicated scoop and sealed container for nut flour standing apart at the end of the row
  5. No loose sprinkles rolling free on the shelf
  6. A washed scoop sitting inside the flour bin, not buried in the flour

Poison, choke, or strangle Cross-contact between nut flours and everything else on the shelf, plus small decorating pieces like silver balls and mini cutters sitting on a low shelf within a toddler's reach.

Fall Spilled flour or sugar on a hard kitchen floor is genuinely slick, and you will be walking a hot tray across it. Sweep the spill when it happens rather than when the bake is done.

The kit for the baker you meant to become. The piping set from the birthday you decorated once, the fondant, the third tart tin, the specialty flour from a month of enthusiastic bread. This is the hardest shelf in the pantry, because letting it go feels like admitting something about yourself. Here is the rule that settles it: keep the tools for what you have baked twice, and let go of the tools for what you have baked once. Twice means it is a thing you do. Once means it was a good afternoon, and the afternoon has already happened. Whatever survives that test earns its bin space. The rest goes to somebody who bakes this month, not into a box in the garage where you will meet it again in three years.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner

▴ActionPNA-005

TEST EVERY LEAVENER, TOSS WHAT'S DEAD

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Test every leavener rather than guessing, and remove anything dead along with hardened brown sugar or greyed cocoa.

How it works

A cake that will not rise on the day is the only way most households ever find out a leavener died months ago.

You need

  • hot water
  • warm sugared water
  • a spoon

Steps

  1. Test every leavener rather than guessing: a spoon of baking powder in hot water should fizz hard, and yeast in warm sugared water should foam. The dead ones go, along with any hardened brown sugar or greyed cocoa.
  2. Write today's date on the lid of anything that survives, including anything freshly opened.

Victory. Every leavener in the bin has been tested this week, and nothing dead or expired remains.

If this keeps happening

  • MISSING TRIGGER
  • UNRESOLVED DECISION

●●●○○

How it works

One shared scoop carries a trace of an allergen straight into a birthday cake, and nobody notices until somebody reacts.

Snack and Lunch Zone

▣ZonePNZ-004
▣

SNACK AND LUNCH ZONE

Zone 4 of 5 · Pantry

ONE BIN AT CHILD HEIGHT. ONE LINE TO FILL IT TO. EVERY LID MATCHED.

●●○○○

Holds what goes into a lunchbox, and what a child is allowed to take without asking.

How it works

Done looks like: One open bin at child height holding only snacks you have already said yes to, and a higher shelf holding the ones that need an adult. Every reusable container has its own lid sitting on it, and the number of containers matches the number of lunches you genuinely pack.

Six things to check

  1. One open bin standing at child height
  2. A marked fill line visible inside the open bin
  3. A higher shelf holding the snacks that need an adult's yes
  4. Every reusable container with its own lid sitting on top of it
  5. Lunch bags and ice packs standing next to the containers
  6. No loose orphaned lid or lidless tub anywhere on the shelf

Poison, choke, or strangle Hard sweets, whole nuts and small round cheeses in an open bin a two-year-old can reach. The bin's contents are set by the youngest child in the house, not the oldest.

Fall A child climbing the shelving to reach the higher snack shelf. If it is worth climbing for, either move it out of the pantry entirely or fix the shelving unit to the wall.

Whether the answer is yours or the bin's. You are negotiating snacks several times a day, and every one of those is a fresh decision made while you are busy and being asked twice. The call is to move the decision off the moment and onto the shelf. Choose once, in daylight and not at four o'clock, exactly what a child may take without asking, and put precisely that in the open bin at their height. Everything else goes above eye line and requires you. Then hold the line: when the bin is empty at four o'clock, the answer is that the bin is empty, not that you will go and find something. That single refusal is what makes the bin real. Until you hold it, the bin is decoration and you are still the one being asked.

What it uses

  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
