Built from the Manual, not around it

Six zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

This is the room where physical clutter and unmade decisions about paper live in the same drawer. Almost nothing here is heavy or dirty, which is exactly why it sits untouched for years: every item asks for a judgment rather than a lift. Start with the desk surface, because you need one finished workspace to sort everything else onto, and give the file drawer a morning of its own.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

66 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE HOME OFFICE

SIX ZONES. START WITH THE DESK.

●○○○○

The home office is six small jobs built around one shared trap: physical clutter and unmade decisions about paper. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. HOZ-001 Primary Desk. Clear the desk surface completely before you open a single drawer. Every other zone in this room gets emptied onto that surface at some point, so it has to be the first thing finished and the last thing you let refill.

The six zones, in order

  1. HOZ-001 Primary Desk
  2. HOZ-002 Desk Drawers and Pedestal
  3. HOZ-003 File Storage
  4. HOZ-004 Bookshelf and Reference Zone
  5. HOZ-005 Printer and Scanning Station
  6. HOZ-006 Supply Cabinet

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your home office. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, split the chain: one clears the desk and drawers, another works the filing and the shelf.

Safety first. Do HOA-015 The Quarterly Reach-Behind Clean and Safety Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the cables, the cabinets, the shelves and the printer.

PRIMARY DESK

Zone 1 of 6 · Home Office

ONE INBOX TRAY. UNDER A DOZEN SHEETS, ALWAYS.

●●●○○

One clear surface for the work you are actually doing right now.

How it works

Done looks like: The monitor, keyboard, and mouse in fixed positions, one notebook, one inbox tray holding fewer than a dozen sheets, no sticky notes on the monitor bezel, and the power strip hooked off the floor so the chair rolls over bare carpet.

Six things to check

  1. Monitor, keyboard and mouse sitting in fixed positions
  2. One notebook on the desk
  3. One inbox tray holding fewer than a dozen sheets
  4. No sticky notes on the monitor bezel
  5. The power strip hooked off the floor onto the desk leg
  6. Bare carpet under the full arc the chair rolls through

Water and electricity A mug of coffee sitting behind the keyboard on the same surface as a power strip and a laptop charger. Move drinks to the far side of the mouse, away from cables and the machine.

Fall Charger cables and the power strip lead running across the floor where the chair moves and where you push back to stand.

The half-finished thing. One corner of the desk is held by a project you have not touched in weeks and cannot put away, because putting it away feels like admitting it is over. Say the next physical action out loud in one sentence. If you can name it ("call the insurer", "sign page four"), it is live: write that sentence on a card, clip it to the papers, and it goes in the inbox tray, not on the desk. If you cannot name a next action, it is not stalled, it is abandoned. Box it, write today's date on the lid, put it on the shelf, and if the box is still unopened on that date next year, it leaves unopened.

CLEAR THE DESK AND HOOK THE STRIP UP

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Clear the desk down to the monitor, keyboard and mouse, and get the power strip off the floor.

How it works

Paper with no other place to land is why this desk fills back up by the end of the week, and a power strip on the floor is what the chair catches on every time.

You need

  • nothing beyond fifteen minutes and the inbox tray

Steps

  1. Take everything off the desk except the monitor, keyboard and mouse. Put the loose paper into the inbox tray, give every sheet in it a verdict until it is under a dozen, and hook the power strip up off the floor.
  2. Set the notebook on your writing-hand side and the phone face down beyond it.
  3. Check the sweep the mouse travels is left completely bare.

Victory. One notebook and one tray under a dozen sheets, and the chair rolls the width of the desk without catching.

If this keeps happening

  • NO ASSIGNED HOME
  • MISSING TRIGGER

DESK DRAWERS AND PEDESTAL

Zone 2 of 6 · Home Office

EVERY PEN WRITES. THE SCISSORS POINT DOWN.

●●○○○

Hold the small tools you reach for without looking, and the live files you touch weekly.

How it works

Done looks like: A top drawer with a divided tray holding pens that all write, one stapler, one pair of scissors point-down in its own slot, and a deep pedestal drawer of hanging files with tabs all standing in one line.

Six things to check

  1. A divided tray in the top drawer
  2. Pens in the tray that all write
  3. One stapler in the tray
  4. Scissors resting point-down in their own slot
  5. A deep pedestal drawer of hanging files
  6. File tabs all standing in one line

Fall, cut, or crush Loose scissors and a letter opener rolling free in a drawer you reach into without looking, and a pedestal that tips forward if you pull the loaded file drawer fully out while the top drawer is open.

The cable bag. Somewhere in the pedestal there is a bag or a drawer of cables, adapters, and wall bricks, and it survives because each individual cable is too cheap to think about and too specific to throw away. That decision cannot be made one cable at a time. Empty the whole lot onto the desk at once, pick up each cable, name the device it powers, and point at where that device physically is in this house. No device, no keep. Then keep exactly one spare of each connector type you still use daily, coil and band it, and take the rest to electronics recycling this week. Made in one batch at the cleared desk, it is a single sitting. Made one cable at a time, it never gets made at all.

FILE STORAGE

Zone 3 of 6 · Home Office

ONE QUESTION PER FOLDER. ONE STRAIGHT LINE OF TABS.

●●●○○

Keep the documents you are genuinely required to hold, and put your hand on any one of them without opening a second drawer.

How it works

Done looks like: Hanging folders labelled by the question you would ask when looking, tabs all in the same position so your eye runs one straight line, a closed fireproof box holding the permanent originals, and a full shred bag by the door ready to go.

Six things to check

  1. Hanging folders labeled by the question you would ask
  2. Every tab in the same position along the row
  3. A closed fireproof box holding the permanent originals
  4. A full shred bag standing by the door
  5. A retention card taped inside the top drawer
  6. The cabinet anchored to the wall

Burn or fire A four-drawer cabinet of dense paper standing against a heater, or the overflow boxes stacked on top of a power strip. Paper is the fuel load of this room, so keep it clear of heat and of the outlet.

Fall, cut, or crush A tall filing cabinet will tip forward onto you if the top drawer is loaded and pulled fully out. Anchor it to the wall and open one drawer at a time.

What you are actually required to keep. Most of this drawer is neither obviously permanent nor obviously rubbish, and that middle band is why the whole job stalls. Split it into three by rule. Permanent originals, meaning birth and marriage certificates, deeds, vehicle titles, wills, and immigration papers, go into one closed fireproof box and never into the filing cabinet. Tax returns and their supporting paper get one folder per year and are held for whatever your local retention period is, which you look up once and write on the card. Everything else, the bank statements, the utility bills, the receipts for things you no longer own, goes by this test: if the issuer will reprint it on request, or it already lives inside an online account, it is information, not a record, and information does not get a folder.

SORT INTO KEEP, SCAN, SHRED

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Work the backlog pile into three piles by rule, and get the retention card written.

How it works

The middle band, neither obviously permanent nor obviously rubbish, is why this job stalls; a rule applied once settles the whole pile instead of one sheet at a time.

You need

BOOKSHELF AND REFERENCE ZONE

Zone 4 of 6 · Home Office

A BOOKEND CLOSES EVERY GROUP. A HAND'S WIDTH LEFT OVER.

●●○○○

Hold the reference you genuinely reach for, at the height you reach for it from.

How it works

Done looks like: Working reference at seated eye level from the desk chair, heavy binders and box files on the bottom two shelves, a bookend closing each group, a hand's width of empty shelf left at the end of every row, and the unit screwed to the wall.

Six things to check

  1. Working reference books at seated eye level
  2. Heavy binders and box files on the bottom two shelves
  3. A bookend closing each group of books
  4. A hand's width of empty shelf at the end of every row
  5. The unit screwed to the wall
  6. No books stacked flat on top of the upright row

Fall, cut, or crush An unanchored shelf unit standing above the desk chair, with the heaviest binders and box files loaded on the top shelf. Anchor the unit and put the weight low.

The shelf that says who you used to be. The textbooks from a degree, the professional library from a job you left, the reference set that a website now answers better and faster. These are the hardest books in the house to release, because they stand for a version of you that you have not formally retired. The rule that settles it: ask when you last took it down since it arrived on this shelf, not whether you might one day. If you cannot name a specific occasion, and the book is one you could borrow or reorder inside a week, it is no longer reference, it is display, and it must now win a display slot in open competition with everything else on the shelf. Most of them will not. Appliance manuals are simpler: model still in the house, keep it; model gone, it goes today.
