Why cards

A book tells you the method. A deck makes you do one thing.

The hardest part of resetting a room is not knowing what to do. It is standing in the doorway looking at all of it at once and deciding it is a weekend job, then not having a weekend.

A card removes that decision. It names one micro zone, one pass, and what finished looks like. You draw it, you do it, you put it down. The entryway is the right room to start with because it is small enough to finish and public enough that everyone in the house notices.

The 46 cards

Five zones, six passes, in order

Thirty one cards walk the six S's through five micro zones. Twelve set the room up: what it is for, the kit, the launch pad, how to share it as a household. Three explain how to play.

  • Sort
  • Straighten
  • Shine
  • Safety
  • Standardize
  • Sustain

Safety is the fourth S, not an afterthought bolted onto the end, and it carries more cards than any other pass. An entryway is where the whole household moves fastest in the dark carrying things, so a blocked door swing or a curled mat edge is not a tidiness problem.

Six ways to play

However much time you actually have

  • The full reset. All five zones, all six passes, in order. A long morning.
  • One S pass. Take a single S across all five zones. Sort everywhere, then stop.
  • One zone. Take one zone through all six passes. Twenty to forty minutes.
  • Daily draw. Turn one card over with your coffee. Do that card only.
  • Family race. Deal the zone cards out and see who finishes their zone first.
  • Family co-op. Deal them out and try to clear the whole room together before a timer.

Start at the Landing Spot. It is the highest leverage square foot in the house, it takes about twenty minutes, and it is the one everybody uses within an hour of you finishing it.
