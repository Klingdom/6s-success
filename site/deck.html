Why cards

A book tells you the method. A deck makes you do one thing.

The hardest part of resetting a room is not knowing what to do. It is standing in the doorway looking at all of it at once and deciding it is a weekend job, then not having a weekend.

A card removes that decision. It names one micro zone, one pass, and what finished looks like. You draw it, you do it, you put it down. The entryway is the right room to start with because it is small enough to finish and public enough that everyone in the house notices.

72 of the deck’s 89 cards are drawn

Six ways to play

However much time you actually have

  • The full reset. The whole deck, front to back, in order. A long morning.
  • One micro zone. Draw its Micro Zone card, then follow the thread: the problem it names, the tool and skill that fix it, the habit that holds it. Twenty to forty minutes.
  • One card type. Take every card of one kind, all twelve Habit cards or all twelve Tool cards, across the whole entryway. Then stop.
  • Daily draw. Turn one card over with your coffee. Do that card only.
  • Family race. Deal the twelve Micro Zone cards out and see who finishes their zone first.
  • Family co-op. Deal them out and try to clear the whole entryway together before a timer.

Start at the Landing Spot. It is the highest leverage square foot in the house, it takes about twenty minutes, and it is the one everybody uses within an hour of you finishing it.

What comes next

The entryway is the pilot, not the whole line

The method covers twenty rooms and a hundred and fourteen micro zones, and the deck structure works for any of them. The entryway is first because it is the smallest complete room in the house and the one whose state everyone sees on the way in.
