Six zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The mudroom is where the weather stops. Everything the house wants to keep outside arrives here first: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, the dog towel that never quite dries, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. Start at the hooks, because almost everything lying on the mudroom floor is there for one reason, which is that the wall above it ran out of room.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. A mudroom-colored panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

69 cards, seven kinds

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE MUDROOM

SIX ZONES. START AT THE HOOKS.

●○○○○

The mudroom is where the weather stops: coats, boots, the pet, the seasonal gear and the tools that keep the entry floor walkable all compete for the same short run of wall. This card is the map.

How it works

Start here. MDZ-001 Family Hook Zone. Do the hook wall before anything else, and do it with the family standing there so every coat gets handed to its owner rather than judged by you. Once each person has a named column at their own reach, the boots, bags and bins underneath stop migrating onto the floor.

The six zones

  1. MDZ-001 Family Hook Zone
  2. MDZ-002 Bench and Transition Surface
  3. MDZ-003 Shoe and Boot Storage
  4. MDZ-004 Pet Station
  5. MDZ-005 Seasonal Outdoor Gear
  6. MDZ-006 Cleaning and Utility Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your mudroom. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do MDA-013 The Full Mudroom Hazard Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers hook anchoring, scarves and drawstrings at toddler height, wet-floor slip zones, poison-grade treatments and chemicals, and every tool hanging clear of a doorway.

FAMILY HOOK ZONE

Zone 1 of 6 · Mudroom

ONE COAT, ONE BAG, ONE HAT PER COLUMN. BARE FLOOR UNDER THE HOOKS.

●●●○○

Holds the coats and bags that leave in the morning and come back the same evening.

How it works

Done looks like: Two coats and one bag per person, each on their own named column, the lowest hooks set at the height of the shortest person in the house, and bare floor along the whole run of wall beneath them.

Six things to check

  1. Two coats and one bag hanging on each person's own named column
  2. A hand-lettered name tag at each column's own eye height
  3. The lowest hooks set at the height of the shortest person in the house
  4. Bare floor running the whole length of wall beneath the hooks
  5. No scarf or drawstring hanging low enough to reach a toddler's neck
  6. Every hook sitting flush against the wall with no lean or wobble

Fall, cut, or crush An overloaded hook rail tearing out of plasterboard brings the rail and every coat on it down at head height.

Poison, choke, or strangle Scarf ends and coat drawstrings dangling at toddler height are a strangling risk when a small child is alone in the room.

The fourth coat. The hard one is not the ugly coat, it is the good coat you love and have not put on since March. It stays at the door because carrying it upstairs feels like admitting something. Here is the rule that settles it: the door holds only the coats this week's weather calls for. Hang everything a person owns, then at the end of one week take down any coat still hanging exactly as you left it, untouched, and move it to their wardrobe. It is not gone, it is just not entitled to a hook. If two coats tie, keep the one that survives rain.

What it uses

  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Soft clothing brush (not in this zone's list)

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Walk the whole wall column by column, return anything on the wrong person's column, enforce one coat one bag one hat, and move any dangling scarf away from toddler height.

How it works

You need

BENCH AND TRANSITION SURFACE

Zone 2 of 6 · Mudroom

THREE THINGS MAX ON THE SEAT. THE BASKET EMPTIES ON THE NEXT TRIP.

●○○○○

Somewhere to sit while you deal with your footwear, and a brief stop for things genuinely on their way out of the house.

How it works

Done looks like: A bare seat wide enough for two people to lace boots at once, no more than three objects on the surface, and one labeled basket at the end holding only what is leaving tomorrow.

Six things to check

  1. A bare wooden seat wide enough for two people to sit and lace boots at once
  2. No more than three objects resting on the surface
  3. One labeled basket at the end of the bench
  4. The basket holding only items that are leaving the house tomorrow
  5. A completely clear floor path in front of the bench
  6. Nothing stored on the shelf directly above the seat

Fall, cut, or crush A heavy hinged bench lid dropping on small fingers, and anything stored on a shelf directly above where a head bends to tie a lace.

Fall A bench seat covered in bags forces people to pull boots off standing on one leg on a wet floor.

The lid you never lift. If your bench has a hinged storage seat, this is the decision you have been dodging: the box under the lid is full and you cannot say what is in it. Storage under a seat you sit on ten times a day is storage you will never open, because opening it means clearing the seat first. Live with it for a week and count honestly how many times you lift that lid. Fewer than three and the answer is settled. Empty it completely and refill it only with things used while sitting, spare laces, a shoe horn, a rag for wiping soles. Everything else moves somewhere you can reach without asking a person to stand up. If it is still empty in a month, leave it empty.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner
  • Portable cleaning caddy

SHOE AND BOOT STORAGE

Zone 3 of 6 · Mudroom

TWO PAIRS PER PERSON. WET STAYS ON THE TRAY.

●●○○○

Holds the footwear actually being worn this season, and gives wet pairs somewhere to drain without soaking the floor.

How it works

Done looks like: Two pairs per person on the rack, soles down and toes out, one lipped tray by the door holding whatever came in wet, and clear floor between the tray and the doorway.

Six things to check

  1. Two pairs of footwear per person standing on the rack
  2. Every pair standing soles down and toes pointing out
  3. One lipped tray sitting by the door
  4. Only wet or draining pairs standing on the tray
  5. A completely clear stretch of floor between the tray and the doorway
  6. Heavier boots on the rack's bottom row, lighter shoes on top

Fall Wet soles carried past the tray leave an invisible film on hard floor right where everyone turns towards the kitchen.

Water and electricity A boot dryer or heated mat with its flex lying across the same floor the wet boots drip onto.

The boots waiting for a winter that has not come. Everyone has a pair kept for conditions that never quite arrive: the proper wellies, the hiking boots worn twice, the smart shoes for the event you keep not going to. They are expensive and undamaged, which is why you will not decide. So separate the two questions. Keeping the pair is one decision; giving it a slot at the door is another. The rule for the door is simple: a pair earns a slot only if it has been on someone's feet in the last four weeks. Everything else goes to the wardrobe with today's date on a slip of paper tucked in the toe. When the season it was bought for has come and gone with that slip still in place, you have your answer, and it is not a hard one any more.

30 MINUTES. 1.

How it works
