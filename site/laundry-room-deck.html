Built from the Manual, not around it

Six zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

Laundry is the one job in the house that arrives dirty, sits wet, and only counts as finished when it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any of those points. It almost always stalls at the folding counter, but the fix starts two zones upstream at the machines, because a washer you have to empty before you can fill is what makes a household skip the last step. Start at the washer and dryer, get them clean, clear and safe, and the rest of the room unblocks behind them.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

67 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE LAUNDRY ROOM

SIX ZONES. START AT THE MACHINES.

●○○○○

Laundry is the one job that arrives dirty, sits wet, and only counts as finished once it is back in a drawer. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. LRZ-001 Washer and Dryer. Both machines first. Empty the drums, clear the lids, then pull the dryer out and look at what is behind it. Every other zone in this room is waiting on those two machines being ready to take a load.

The six zones, in order

  1. LRZ-001 Washer and Dryer
  2. LRZ-002 Detergent and Treatment Zone
  3. LRZ-003 Sorting and Hamper Zone
  4. LRZ-004 Folding Surface
  5. LRZ-005 Hanging and Air-Dry Zone
  6. LRZ-006 Utility and Cleaning Zone

How to play

  1. Deal the six ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your laundry room. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do LRA-013 The Full Laundry Safety Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers the duct, the hoses, the chemicals, the rack and every hung tool in the room.

WASHER AND DRYER

Zone 1 of 6 · Laundry Room

BOTH LIDS BARE. THE DUCT DATE ON THE TAPE.

●●●○○

Run loads reliably, and stay maintained well enough that the machines are never the reason laundry stops.

How it works

Done looks like: Both drums empty with the washer door propped ajar, nothing at all sitting on either lid, a clean lint screen back in its slot, and a strip of masking tape on the side of the dryer with the month you last cleared the duct written on it.

Six things to check

  1. Washer door propped ajar, drum empty
  2. Dryer drum empty behind its door
  3. Bare surface across both lids
  4. Lint screen back in its slot
  5. A strip of masking tape on the dryer's side
  6. Nothing stacked on either machine

Burn or fire Lint packed in the duct behind the dryer, and lint on the heating element side of the screen housing. This is the highest heat and the most fuel in any indoor room outside the kitchen.

Water and electricity Rubber fill hoses that have gone hard or bulged at the collar, sitting a hand's width from a wall outlet. A split hose sprays under pressure and does not stop until you reach the tap.

The duct you have not looked at. You have been telling yourself the dryer just runs a bit slow these days, and that towels have always needed two cycles. The dryer is not slow, it is blocked. The rule: if you cannot name the month you last pulled the machine out and cleared the duct, that is today's job, no debate. And while the dryer is already out and the duct is disconnected, look at what it is made of. If it is the flexible foil accordion kind, replace it with rigid metal now rather than promising yourself you will do it next time, because there is rarely a next time. Once a year is a cheap habit, and the failure it prevents burns the room it happens in.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Small detail brush
  • Washer drum cleaning tablet

EMPTY THE DRUMS AND DATE THE DUCT

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Empty both drums, clear both lids, clear the lint screen, and write this month's date on the duct tape strip.

How it works

A drum you have to empty before you can fill is the single reason a household skips the last step of a wash day.

You need

  • masking tape and a pen
  • the vent brush if the lint screen is due a clean

Steps

  1. Empty both drums, take everything off both lids, clear the lint screen and put it back, prop the washer door ajar, and write this month on a strip of tape on the side of the dryer.
  2. Check the tape already on the dryer: if the month reads more than a year ago, treat the duct itself as this cycle's real job, not next month's.
  3. Wipe down anything sticky left on either lid before you leave the room.

Victory. Both drums empty, both lids bare, the door propped open, and a date on the dryer you can read from the doorway.

If this keeps happening

  • MISSING TRIGGER
  • MISSING STANDARD

PULL THE DRYER AND CLEAR THE DUCT

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Disconnect the duct, clear what has built up behind the machine, and check the fill hoses while everything is already pulled out.

How it works

Lint packed behind the dryer is the highest fuel and the highest heat of any indoor room outside the kitchen, and a hose gone hard at the collar sprays without warning.

You need

  • a step stool or helper to ease the machine forward
  • a vacuum with a hose attachment
  • rigid metal duct if the current run is a foil accordion

Steps

  1. Unplug the dryer and ease it forward on its feet far enough to reach the duct connection.
  2. Disconnect the duct and vacuum out any soft grey packing built up at the bend behind the machine.
  3. If the duct is a flexible foil accordion, replace the run with rigid metal now rather than promising to do it later.
  4. Check both fill hoses for a hard or bulged collar while the machines are already pulled out, and replace either one that has gone stiff.
  5. Push both machines back, level on their feet, and reconnect the duct fully before running the next load.

Victory. The duct is clear and rigid where it can be, both fill hoses are supple at the collar, and both machines sit level on their feet.

If this keeps happening

  • SAFETY CONSTRAINT
  • NO ASSIGNED HOME

DETERGENT AND TREATMENT ZONE

Zone 2 of 6 · Laundry Room

ONE OF EACH KIND. THE DOSE LINE INKED.

●●○○○

Hold the chemistry that actually gets used, at a dose you can measure and out of a child's reach.

How it works

Done looks like: One working detergent, one bleach, one tub of oxygen powder and one stain treatment standing on a wipeable tray, the pod tub latched and above shoulder height, the true dose line inked on the cap, and not one unlabeled bottle on the shelf.

Six things to check

  1. One detergent bottle standing on the tray
  2. One bleach bottle beside it
  3. One tub of oxygen powder
  4. One stain treatment bottle
  5. The pod tub latched above shoulder height
  6. A dose line marked on the cap

Poison, choke, or strangle Detergent pods within a child's reach, and bleach stored next to an ammonia based cleaner where a spill can mix the two into a gas in a small room with the door shut.

Fall, cut, or crush A full jug of detergent stored above head height. Wet hands, a smooth handle, and a shelf you reach into blind is how a heavy bottle lands on a face.

The five half used stain removers. Every laundry shelf has them: a box of dye grabbing sheets, two spray bottles, a stick that dried solid in the cap, and something a relative swore by. You keep them because binning a mostly full bottle feels wasteful, so instead they sit there being wasteful slowly. The rule: keep the one treatment you have personally watched lift a stain, plus a tub of oxygen powder, which handles most of what the others promise. Everything else leaves today. A treatment you do not trust enough to reach for first is not a supply, it is a decision you have been postponing, and it is holding the one arm's reach of shelf next to the open washer lid.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Small detail brush
  • Floor-material-compatible cleaner

ONE TRAY, ONE BOTTLE OF EACH

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Bring every laundry product onto one tray, keep one working bottle of each kind, ink the true dose line, and latch the pod tub high.

How it works

A second open bottle of the same product almost always means the first one had gone invisible, and a low, unlatched pod tub is a poisoning risk in a house with children.

You need

  • a permanent marker
  • a wipeable tray
  • a high shelf or latch for the pod tub

Steps

  1. Bring every laundry product onto one wipeable tray. Keep one working bottle of each kind, ink the true dose line on each cap, and latch the pod tub above shoulder height.
  2. Recycle the empty or near-empty duplicate the moment the working bottle is confirmed.
  3. Wipe the shelf under the tray before anything goes back onto it.

Victory. One of each kind standing on a wipeable tray, a dose line you can see on every cap, and nothing a child can reach.

If this keeps happening

  • EXCESS
  • SAFETY CONSTRAINT

SEPARATE THE SHELF FROM THE FLOOR

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Move bleach away from any ammonia-based cleaner, fix the shelf bracket if it is corroding, and give the tray a rinse.

How it works

Bleach and an ammonia-based cleaner sharing a shelf, or a bucket, can mix into a gas in a small room with the door shut.

You need

  • a spare shelf or bracket if the bleach shelf is corroding
  • the drip tray
  • a permanent marker

Steps

  1. Move bleach and any ammonia-based cleaner onto separate shelves, never sharing a tray or a bucket.
  2. Check the bracket under the bleach shelf for rust or corrosion from fumes, and replace it if it has started to give.
  3. Carry the drip tray to the sink, scrub the tacky corners, dry it, and set it back under the containers.
  4. Write the job on the tray edge under each bottle, wash, whites, stains, so a returned bottle lands in its own footprint.

Victory. Bleach and any ammonia-based cleaner sit on separate shelves, the bracket underneath is sound, and the tray is clean and marked by job.

If this keeps happening

  • CONFLICTING USERS
  • DIFFICULT TO CLEAN

SORTING AND HAMPER ZONE

Zone 3 of 6 · Laundry Room

THREE BAGS. NEVER PAST HALF FULL.

●●○○○

Turn dirty clothes into loads that can go straight into the machine without being handled twice.

How it works

Done looks like: Three labeled bags standing clear of the walking line, each below half full, a small dish on the shelf holding nothing older than today, and bare floor in front of the washer door.

Six things to check

  1. Three sorting bags standing upright
  2. Each bag below half full
  3. Bags standing clear of the walking line
  4. A small dish on the shelf
  5. The dish holding only today's pocket contents
  6. Bare floor in front of the washer door

Fall A hamper standing in the walking line between the door and the machines, or resting on a stair tread while you go back for the rest.

Fall, cut, or crush Reaching blind into a bag of work clothes where a utility knife, a razor blade or a pocketful of screws came home in a trouser pocket.

The delicates bag that never fills. You sort into five streams because a care label once told you to, and one of those bags has been sitting a third full for three weeks while the thing you actually wanted washed waits inside it. The rule: a stream earns its own bag only if it has run as a full load, by itself, in the last month. Everything that fails that test merges into cold and gentle with a mesh bag inside the drum. Sorting is only worth doing when it produces a load you can run today, and a bag that waits three weeks is not sorting, it is a small pile of clothes you have taken out of circulation.

CUT TO THREE BAGS

Cut the sorting streams to three, label those bags, stand them clear of the walking line, and add a dish for pockets.

A sock that has waited a whole season is not going to be reunited. It is just going to keep charging you the counter, and the counter is where the whole room jams.
