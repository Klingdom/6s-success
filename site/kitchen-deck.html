The Kitchen Deck

72 cards: seven zones, the frictions each one causes, the twelve root causes underneath, the actions that fix them, and the standard each zone keeps. Typeset and free. No illustrations yet, so every card reads as text, not a photograph.

Read the deck

The Entryway deck, illustrated: deck.html.

Why unillustrated

The words are finished. The pictures are not.

The kitchen is the busiest room in the house, and the one 6S pays back fastest, because you cross it a dozen times a day and feel every extra step. Start at the sink: it is the room's recovery point, and every other zone eventually drains into it, so nothing else can be judged fairly while dirty dishes have nowhere to be. Then work outward to the counter and the hob, and save the refrigerator for a morning when you have real time.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

72 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE KITCHEN

SEVEN ZONES. START AT THE SINK.

●○○○○

The kitchen is seven small places, not one big job. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. KZ-003 Sink and Dishwashing Zone. It is the shortest zone, it is the point the whole kitchen resets from, and you will have finished something in fifteen minutes.

The seven zones, in order

  1. KZ-001 Primary Prep Counter
  2. KZ-002 Cooking Zone
  3. KZ-003 Sink and Dishwashing Zone
  4. KZ-004 Upper Cabinet Zone
  5. KZ-005 Lower Cabinet and Cookware Zone
  6. KZ-006 Utensil and Utility Drawers
  7. KZ-007 Refrigerator and Freezer

How to play

  1. Deal the seven ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your kitchen. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: five, fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do KA-016 The Hot Zone Walk before any rebuild. It takes fifteen minutes and the rebuilds move heavy and sharp things around.

PRIMARY PREP COUNTER

ONE CLEAR RUN. THE REST GOES SOMEWHERE ELSE.

●●●○○

The stretch of counter where food is actually cut, mixed, and plated.

How it works

Done looks like: One clear run of counter at least as wide as your largest cutting board, holding only the board, the knife block or strip, and the salt. The kettle and the toaster stand back against the wall with their cords coiled, and there is no mail, no fruit bowl, and no charging phone anywhere on the surface.

Six things to check

  1. Clear run, at least as wide as your largest cutting board
  2. The cutting board, down on bare counter
  3. Knife block or wall strip, on your dominant side
  4. Salt within one reach
  5. Kettle and toaster back against the wall, cords coiled
  6. Nothing parked: no mail, no fruit bowl, no charging phone

Fall, cut, or crush A chef's knife left blade-up in the drying rack, or slid under suds in a full sink, is the most common serious cut in a home kitchen.

Burn or fire A toaster pushed under a low cabinet or against a paper towel roll puts heat and flame right where nothing can escape it.

The stand mixer question. The hard one is the appliance you genuinely love and rarely use: the mixer, the bread machine, the espresso setup. Count real uses over the past month, not the ones you intended. Anything you reach for less than a couple of times a week comes off the counter and goes to a low cabinet, ideally on a pull-out. Anything you have not switched on in a year leaves the kitchen entirely. And if your honest reason for keeping it out is that lifting it from a cabinet would stop you using it, you have just proved the machine is not part of your cooking, and it is worth more sold than stored.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Food-area degreaser
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Portable cleaning caddy

CLEAR THE RUN

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Get one unbroken run of counter back, and prove it can hold.

How it works

You cannot cook in twenty centimetres, and every other habit in this kitchen starts on this surface.

You need

  • the dining table, as a staging area
  • a bin bag
  • a damp cloth

Steps

  1. Take everything off the prep counter and set it on the dining table. Everything, including the things you are sure belong there.
  2. Let only three things back: what you cut on, what you cut with, and what you season with.
  3. Lift the kettle and the toaster rather than wiping around them, clean under their feet, set them back against the wall and coil the cords.
  4. Everything still on the table goes to a home elsewhere, or leaves. Do not put one item back to decide about later, because that is the item the pile grows from.
  5. Wipe the run and lay the board down on bare counter.

Victory. Your largest cutting board lies flat with room on both sides, and nothing else is on the run.

REBUILD THE PREP COUNTER

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Change the counter so clear is the easy state rather than the daily effort.

How it works

A surface you have to defend every evening was laid out wrong. Fix the layout and the defending stops.

You need

  • a tape measure
  • low tack tape or a marker
  • one empty low cabinet shelf

Steps

  1. Stand where you actually cut, and do not move. That spot is the anchor and everything else is measured from it.
  2. Measure your largest board and mark that width under the counter lip with tape, so where the work surface starts is a fact and not an opinion.
  3. Knives on your dominant side, on a wall strip or in a block. Oil and salt behind the board rather than beside it.
  4. Mixing bowls into the cabinet directly underneath, so you can reach one without turning around.
  5. Count real uses of every appliance still on the counter over the past month, not intended ones. Under a couple of times a week, it goes to a low cabinet. Not switched on in a year, it leaves the kitchen.

Victory. You can cut, season, and reach a mixing bowl without taking a step or turning around.

COOKING ZONE

HANDS BUSY, HEAT ON. EVERYTHING WITHIN ONE REACH.

●●●○○

The step of space either side of the burners where cooking happens with your hands busy and heat already on.

How it works

Done looks like: Oil, salt, and pepper within one reach of the burners. A utensil crock holding no more than six tools, every one of which you used this month. Pot holders on a hook you can find without looking, the spices you reach for weekly at the front of the rack, and a clear landing space beside the hob wide enough to set down a hot pan.

Six things to check

  1. Oil, salt and pepper within one reach of the burners
  2. Utensil crock holding six tools, no more
  3. Pot holders on a hook you find without looking
  4. Weekly spices at the front of the rack
  5. Clear landing space beside the hob, wide enough for a hot pan
  6. Pan handles turned in, never over the front edge

Burn or fire Oil left heating unattended, and a tea towel or a paper roll stored within reach of a live burner, are the two ways a kitchen fire actually starts.

Fall, cut, or crush A pan handle turned out over the front edge of the hob is at exactly the height a toddler grabs, and it comes down with the contents.

The spice rack you are not honest about. Every cook keeps jars bought for one recipe from one holiday. Open them and smell them, one by one, over the bin. Ground spices fade first, and if a jar smells of dust rather than of itself, it is no longer seasoning anything you cook, and keeping it does not recover what you paid. Then make the harder call underneath: keep spices for the food you cooked in the last year, not the food you imagine cooking. If you want that cuisine back, buy the two spices fresh on the day you cook it. A rack you can read is worth more than a rack that is complete.

What it uses

  • Food-area degreaser
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Small detail brush set

COOKING ZONE RESET

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Put oil, salt and one turner back within a single reach, and clear the landing space.

How it works

Everything here is done with your hands full and the heat already on. A step taken now is a step you cannot take then.

You need

  • a tray or a large bowl to hold the tools you pull
  • a damp cloth

Steps

  1. Empty the utensil crock out onto the counter.
  2. Put back six tools, each one you actually used this month. The rest go to a drawer or leave the kitchen.
  3. Oil, salt and pepper within one reach of the burner you use most.
  4. Clear the landing space beside the hob down to bare counter, wide enough to set a hot pan on.
  5. Turn every pan handle in, so nothing sticks out over the front edge.

Victory. With your feet still, you can reach oil, salt and a turner, and you have somewhere to set a hot pan down.

REBUILD THE COOKING ZONE

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Lay the burner surround out for one reach, and take the fire risk out of it.

How it works

Oil left heating and a tea towel stored within reach of a live burner are the two ways a kitchen fire actually starts.

You need

  • a small hook and its fixing
  • a bin bag
  • a food area degreaser and a microfibre cloth

Steps

  1. Mount a hook for the pot holders within a hand's reach of the burners and clear of the heat, so you never hunt for them with a hot pan.
  2. Pull every spice jar. Anything not used in a year leaves. The ones you reach for weekly go to the front row.
  3. Move the paper towel roll, the tea towels and any packaging off the burner side entirely.
  4. Degrease the surround and the hob, working top down so what you loosen falls onto surfaces you have not cleaned yet.
  5. After cleaning, light every burner once and watch for an even blue ring. A lazy yellow flame, or a burner that clicks and will not catch, is a call to a registered engineer and not a thing to adjust yourself.

Victory. Pot holders found without looking, weekly spices in the front row, nothing that burns within reach of a burner, and every burner lighting evenly.

SINK AND DISHWASHING ZONE

THE WHOLE KITCHEN RESETS FROM HERE.

●●○○○

The washing point for dishes, hands, and produce, and the place the whole kitchen resets from.

How it works

Done looks like: An empty sink with a dry basin and a wiped drain flange. One brush and one sponge standing upright where they drain rather than lying in a puddle. The drying rack either empty or holding only what is still wet. Under the sink, a single tray holding dish soap, dishwasher tablets, and one all-purpose cleaner, with the pipes visible around them.

Six things to check

  1. Empty sink, dry basin
  2. Drain flange wiped
  3. One brush and one sponge standing where they drain
  4. Drying rack empty, or holding only what is still wet
  5. Under the sink: one tray, dish soap, tablets, one cleaner
  6. Pipes visible around the tray

Water and electricity A kettle, a socket, or a charging phone within splash distance of the basin, handled with wet hands, is the electrical risk this room actually has.

Poison, choke, or strangle Bleach mixed with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the basin releases a gas that will drive you out of the room, and both bottles usually live under this sink.

Whether soaking is allowed. The real decision in this zone is not what to keep, it is whether you let the basin be a staging area. Soaking is the word a kitchen uses for postponing, and one soaking pan gives every other dish permission to join it. Settle it with a deadline rather than a ban: a pan may soak, but only until the current dishwasher cycle ends or until you next leave the room, whichever comes first. If a dish cannot meet that deadline, it gets washed now or goes in the machine dirty. The rule holds because it names a moment, and moments arrive on their own.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Small detail brush set
  • Portable cleaning caddy

SINK RESET

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Empty basin, dry basin, two tools standing.

How it works

This is the point the whole kitchen resets from. When it is clear the rest is recoverable, and when it is not, nothing else starts.

You need

  • a microfibre cloth
  • a neutral pH cleaner

Steps

  1. Empty the sink completely, including the pan you were going to soak.
  2. Wipe the basin dry, and wipe the drain flange, which is the part nobody cleans and everybody smells.
  3. One brush and one sponge, standing where they drain. Everything else comes off the surround.
  4. Take out of the drying rack anything that is already dry.
  5. Wipe the tap and the surround, and dry them so no water sits.

Victory. Dry basin, two tools standing, nothing lying in water.

▴ActionKA-006

REBUILD UNDER THE SINK

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

One tray, the pipes visible, and nothing stored that can react with anything beside it.

How it works

This is the one cabinet in the house where two ordinary bottles make a gas that will drive you out of the room.

You need

  • one shallow tray
  • a bin bag
  • your local household chemical disposal rules

Steps

  1. Take everything out. Everything, including the bottles at the back.
  2. Separate bleach from any acidic or ammonia based cleaner, and never store them in the same tray. If you cannot tell what a bottle is, read the label. If it has no label, dispose of it by your local rules rather than guessing.
  3. Anything hazardous that sits within a child's reach moves up and out of this cabinet, or the cabinet gets a latch.
  4. One shallow tray goes back, holding dish soap, dishwasher tablets and one all purpose cleaner. That is the whole inventory.
  5. Leave the pipework visible, so a leak is something you see early instead of something you smell late.

Victory. One tray, three bottles, and bare pipes you can see all the way around.

DAILY THINGS AT DAILY HEIGHT.

Pick one everyday set, sized to the people who eat here plus two, and give it the shelf between shoulder and eye height. Then decide the second set honestly. What you cannot do is keep both sets at eye level and then blame the cabinet for being full.

REBUILD THE UPPER CABINETS

30 MINUTES. 1 TO 2.

●●●○○

Band the cabinet by height so nobody climbs for anything they use every day.

How it works

Reaching a top shelf from a chair, a counter edge or an open drawer is how people fall in kitchens, and it happens while they are holding something breakable.

You need

  • somewhere outside the kitchen for the second set
