The Family Hook Zone

Holds the coats and bags that leave in the morning and come back the same evening.

One session: 45-75 min. most of it in Sort, because every coat has to be handed to a person

What done looks like

Two coats and one bag per person, each on their own named column, the lowest hooks set at the height of the shortest person in the house, and bare floor along the whole run of wall beneath them.

The six passes, in order

Work them in this order. Sorting after you have arranged things means arranging things you were about to remove.

1. Sort

Decide what stays. Everything else leaves the zone now.

Take every coat, bag, hat and scarf off the wall into one pile and hand each item to its owner to keep or release. Anything nobody has worn since the last change of season leaves the door and goes up to a wardrobe.

2. Straighten

Give what stays a home, placed where the hand actually reaches.

Give each person a vertical strip of wall rather than a shared rail, and set their hooks at their own reach so a child never has to ask for their coat. Bag on the lower hook, coat on the upper, so a backpack is never lifted over a wet jacket.

3. Shine

Clean it, and use the cleaning to inspect what you cannot see when it is full.

Wipe the rail and the wall behind it where shoulders and sleeves leave a grey smear, then vacuum the narrow strip of floor directly under the hooks where hood fluff and door grit gather.

4. Safety

Make the zone safe for everybody who uses it, including the shortest person.

A hook carrying four wet coats will pull a screw straight out of plasterboard, so check that every hook sits in a stud or a proper anchor before you reload the wall. Keep scarves, cords and drawstrings off the lowest hooks where a small child plays alone.

5. Standardize

Write down the best way you know today, so it survives you forgetting.

Write each name on its column at that person's eye height, and say the limit out loud so everyone hears it: one coat, one bag, one hat. A standard is the best way you know today, written down.

6. Sustain

Attach the reset to something that already happens, so it keeps itself.

When you hang your own coat up at the end of the day, look along the wall and move anything sitting on the wrong column back to its owner's.

The fourth coat

The hard one is not the ugly coat, it is the good coat you love and have not put on since March. It stays at the door because carrying it upstairs feels like admitting something. Here is the rule that settles it: the door holds only the coats this week's weather calls for. Hang everything a person owns, then at the end of one week take down any coat still hanging exactly as you left it, untouched, and move it to their wardrobe. It is not gone, it is just not entitled to a hook. If two coats tie, keep the one that survives rain.

Check these before you start

  • Fall, cut, or crush An overloaded hook rail tearing out of plasterboard brings the rail and every coat on it down at head height.
  • Poison, choke, or strangle Scarf ends and coat drawstrings dangling at toddler height are a strangling risk when a small child is alone in the room.
  • Fall Bags dropped under a full wall sit right in the walking line from the door, which is where people go over in the dark.

Cleaning it properly

Work this wall from the top down. Wipe the rail and the wall behind it where shoulders and sleeves leave a grey smear, then finish on the narrow strip of floor under the hooks where hood fluff and door grit gather.

Inspect as you clean. Cleaning is the only time you see the zone empty, so use it.

  • As you take each coat off its hook, flag any hook that has started to lean, wobble, or leave a dark rim of pulled plaster around its screw.
  • Look for a fine crack in the plaster running from a screw head, and flag it, since it tells you the fixing has begun to work loose under the wet-coat load.
  • Note any coat or bag that comes down smelling musty or still feeling damp, which means it never dried through and wants flagging before it goes back up.
  • Watch for a spreading stain or a soft grey bloom on the wall behind the rail, and flag it as a possible leak or damp track coming through from behind.

The standard that keeps it fixed

One coat, one bag, one hat per named column, and the floor under the hooks stays empty.

Reset trigger. When you hang up your own coat at the end of the day.

Before you start. This is guidance for organising and cleaning a home. It is not instruction for electrical, gas, structural, or any other licensed work. Do not lift or move anything unsafe, do not mix cleaning products, and follow the label on anything you use. If a job needs a professional, that is the right answer. See the full safety notice.
