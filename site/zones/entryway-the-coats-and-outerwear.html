Entryway micro zone

The Coats and Outerwear

Hold the outerwear you will actually wear this week, at the moment you reach for it.

One session: 45-75 min. most of it in Sort, since every coat has to come down and be held

What done looks like

One coat per person on the rail, one bag, hats and gloves together in a single labeled bin on the shelf above, umbrellas standing in the stand, and at least two hooks left empty.

Check these before you start

  • Fall, cut, or crushA bin of boots or a heavy holdall stored above head height on the coat shelf lands on whoever tugs the coat underneath it. Weight goes low, light bulky things go high.
  • FallA rail loaded past what its brackets were fixed for sags first and lets go later, usually while somebody is standing directly beneath it.
  • Poison, choke, or strangleLong scarves and bag straps left on the low hooks hang as a loop at a small child's neck height. Coats on the low hooks, straps and scarves up high.

The six passes, in order

Work them in this order. Sorting after you have arranged things means arranging things you were about to remove.

Sort

Decide what stays. Everything else leaves the zone now.

Take every coat down. Anything that did not go outdoors once through the last cold season leaves, and so do the single gloves you kept in case the other one turned up. Off-season coats go to a bedroom closet, not to the far end of the same rail.

Straighten

Give what stays a home, placed where the hand actually reaches.

Hooks for what goes on and off daily, hangers only for the wool coat that creases. Set the lowest hook at the height of the shortest person who uses it, and keep the two hooks nearest the door empty for visitors.

Shine

Clean it, and use the cleaning to inspect what you cannot see when it is full.

Brush the shoulders of the hanging coats, wipe the shelf where dust settles on top of the hat bin, and tip out the umbrella stand. There is always water and grit in the bottom of it.

Safety

Make the zone safe for everybody who uses it, including the shortest person.

Hang your full weight on each hook before you trust it with a soaked winter coat. Hooks driven into plasterboard without an anchor pull out later, with the coat still attached and a lump of wall behind it.

Standardize

Write down the best way you know today, so it survives you forgetting.

Write the contents and the season on the shelf bin, hats and gloves, winter, so whoever reaches up for it in October knows without opening the lid whether they have the right bin.

Sustain

Attach the reset to something that already happens, so it keeps itself.

When you put a wash on, walk the rail on your way past and return anything that has been left over a chair back or the bannister.

The coat you feel guilty about

Somewhere on this rail is a good coat that cost real money and goes outside roughly never. It survives every clear-out because getting rid of it feels like admitting to the money. The rule is that coats are counted by weather, not by number. Each person gets one coat per kind of weather your climate genuinely produces: wet, cold, and something in between. A second coat in the same category has to beat the first one on a specific, nameable day. If you cannot name that day, that is the coat that leaves. The expensive one is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm. Give it one trial: front hook, most reachable spot, two weeks. If you did not reach for it, you have your answer and you had it before you started.

Cleaning it properly

Brush the coats, clear the shelf dust, and tip out the umbrella stand, there is always water and grit in the bottom of it. Work from the shelf above down to the floor.

Inspect as you clean. Cleaning is the only time you see the zone empty, so use it.

  • A hook whose base plate has started to pull away from the wall, a gap opening behind it or the screw head standing proud. Flag it before it lets go with a coat on it.
  • A rail bracket with a screw backing out or a rail that has begun to bow in the middle. Note it.
  • A brown damp bloom or a soft patch on the wall behind the soaked coats, where the plaster is taking on water. Flag it.
  • A musty mildew smell coming off a coat or out of the bin, the sign something went away wet. Note which piece.
  • Rust eating through the base of the umbrella stand, or a crack in it that lets water seep onto the floor. Flag it.

The standard that keeps it fixed

Two empty hooks at all times. If there is no empty hook, one coat is in the wrong room.

Reset trigger. On the first morning cold enough that you go looking for gloves, that is the seasonal swap. Do it that morning, standing there, rather than noting it for later.

Before you start. This is guidance for organising and cleaning a home. It is not instruction for electrical, gas, structural, or any other licensed work. Do not lift or move anything unsafe, do not mix cleaning products, and follow the label on anything you use. If a job needs a professional, that is the right answer. See the full safety notice.
