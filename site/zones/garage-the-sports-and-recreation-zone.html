The Sports and Recreation Zone

The sports gear storage, in the Garage. Holds the gear for the sports this household currently plays, with the in-season kit easiest to grab. Bikes on wall hooks with bare floor beneath them, one labelled bin per activity, this season's kit at the front of the shelf, and every helmet in the garage belonging to a head that still wears it.

One session: 60-90 min, most of it in Sort, and the slow part is matching each item to a person who still plays.

The short version

  1. Sort. Carry every helmet, ball, racket, and pair of cleats out onto the driveway
  2. Straighten. Bikes hang on wall hooks nearest the door
  3. Shine. Air out and wipe down helmet liners and shin pads
  4. Safety. A helmet that has taken one real impact is finished even
  5. Standardize. One labelled bin per activity, and the label carries the sport plus the name
  6. Sustain. The front and back of the shelf trade places the first weekend you turn

Each step in full, with what to have on hand and what done looks like, below.

What done looks like

Bikes on wall hooks with bare floor beneath them, one labelled bin per activity, this season's kit at the front of the shelf, and every helmet in the garage belonging to a head that still wears it.

Check these before you start

  • Fall, cut, or crushA bike coming off an overhead hook onto a parked car roof or a person underneath, and skis stored edge-out at head height.
  • Burn or fireCamp stove canisters and lantern fuel stored in among the sports bins instead of outside with the other flammables.
  • FallStanding on the cooler rather than a ladder to reach over the ball rack for the tent bag.

Which of these is true here?

Match what you are actually seeing in the sports gear storage to why it keeps happening, then start at the pass that fixes that, not the top of the list.

Getting the bikes out means moving three other things first

  • Bikes stand on the floor rather than hanging on hooks. Confirm in 30 seconds: Fetch it once and count the movements. More than two and the storage is charging you rent every time. If that checks out, start at Straighten.
  • Bins are stored in the path to the door. Confirm in 30 seconds: Stand where you use it and count the steps to where it is kept. More than two is the wrong cabinet. If that checks out, start at Straighten.
  • The floor under the bikes has become storage. Confirm in 30 seconds: Ask where one specific item lives. If two people answer differently, or nobody answers, it has no home. If that checks out, start at Straighten.

Kit for the sport we are playing this month is the hardest to reach

  • Nothing moves the in-season kit to the front when the season turns. Confirm in 30 seconds: Name the moment it happens. If the answer is "when I get round to it", there is no trigger and it will not survive a bad week. If that checks out, start at Sustain.
  • Bins are not labelled, so the nearest one gets used for anything. Confirm in 30 seconds: Open the door and, without moving anything, name what is at the back. If you cannot, that stock is invisible. If that checks out, start at Straighten.
  • The bins a child needs are above their reach. Confirm in 30 seconds: Have the shortest person who uses this zone fetch what they need daily. If they climb, reach overhead, or ask for help, the height is wrong. If that checks out, start at Safety.

There is gear here for sports nobody plays any more

  • It cost a lot and giving it up feels like admitting something. Confirm in 30 seconds: Ask if you would buy this again today for what it does. If the answer is no but you still cannot decide, the hold is sentimental. If that checks out, start at Sort.
  • Nobody has decided whether that sport is coming back. Confirm in 30 seconds: Pick up one piece. If your first thought is a verb (pay, sign, call, decide) rather than a place, it needs a decision, not a home. If that checks out, start at Sort.
  • It has been in the corner so long the eye stops seeing it. Confirm in 30 seconds: Ask someone who does not live here to name what looks out of place. If they see it instantly and you had stopped noticing, this is the cause. If that checks out, start at Sort.

I am not sure the helmets here are safe to wear

  • Nobody knows how old any of them are or whether one was dropped. Confirm in 30 seconds: Ask two people what this surface should look like at bedtime. Two answers means there is no standard to keep. If that checks out, start at Standardize.
  • They are kept for a head that has outgrown them. Confirm in 30 seconds: Count what you used in the past month. If the honest number is a third of what is stored here, the space is not too small. If that checks out, start at Sort.
  • Nothing ever prompts a check. Confirm in 30 seconds: Name the moment it happens. If the answer is "when I get round to it", there is no trigger and it will not survive a bad week. If that checks out, start at Sustain.

Only have 15 minutes? Hang the bikes on wall hooks and clear the floor beneath them, label one bin per activity this household currently plays, move this season's kit to the front of the shelf, and try every helmet on the head it belongs to. Victory: Bikes hanging over bare floor, one labelled bin per sport still played, this season's kit at the front, and every helmet fitting a head that still wears it.

The six passes, in order

Work them in this order. Sorting after you have arranged things means arranging things you were about to remove.

This zone's full kit list is below, after the method. Skim it first if you would rather gather everything before you start.

Sort

Decide what stays. Everything else leaves the zone now.

Carry every helmet, ball, racket, and pair of cleats out onto the driveway and match each one to a person who plays that sport now. Cleats a size too small and the paddle from that one river trip do not come back through the door.

Straighten

Give what stays a home, placed where the hand actually reaches.

Bikes hang on wall hooks nearest the door so nobody unhooks three to get one out on a Saturday morning. Balls go in a tall bin or a bungee-front rack rather than loose on the floor where they roll under the car, and this season's kit sits at the front of the shelf with the off-season behind it.

Shine

Clean it, and use the cleaning to inspect what you cannot see when it is full.

Air out and wipe down helmet liners and shin pads, hose the mud off cleats and dry them right through before they go back, and leave the cooler propped open with a rag of baking soda inside so it does not come out smelling of last summer.

Safety

Make the zone safe for everybody who uses it, including the shortest person.

A helmet that has taken one real impact is finished even if the shell looks perfect, and so is one past the date moulded inside it. Overhead bike hooks must be rated and driven into studs, never drywall anchors, and never hung where a car door swings open into a hanging wheel.

Standardize

Write down the best way you know today, so it survives you forgetting.

One labelled bin per activity, and the label carries the sport plus the name of the person it belongs to. Gear that fits no named bin has no owner in this house and does not stay in it.

Sustain

Attach the reset to something that already happens, so it keeps itself.

The front and back of the shelf trade places the first weekend you turn the heating on, and again the first weekend you leave the windows open all day. Weather calls it, one adult announces it, half an hour. Slip in between is out of season kit at the front, or a bin whose label carries no person's name, which means that gear has no owner here. Recovery is the swap plus one bin emptied. What this shelf holds that the rest of the garage does not is growth, and growth is silent.

The gear from the sport you stopped playing

Skis, golf clubs, a good tent, the road bike. Each was expensive, each still works, and each stands for a version of your week that has not actually happened in years. That is what makes this the slowest shelf in the garage, and no amount of restacking will settle it. The rule is two full seasons of the right weather with no use, which means you are not on a break, you have stopped. Sell it while the bindings and the groupset are still current, or hand it to someone just starting out, which is a better ending than a decade under a dust sheet. The one exception worth honouring is a named date: if you can finish the sentence "I will use this on ___", keep it and write that date on the bin. If you cannot finish the sentence, it goes this month.

Cleaning it properly, surface by surface

Air out the liners, hose the mud off the cleats, and never pack a single thing away damp. Work down from the bikes on the wall to the bare floor beneath them.

Work down, not across. Whatever comes off a high surface lands on the one below it, so cleaning the floor first means cleaning it twice.

Carry these in with you. Portable cleaning caddy; Compact vacuum with attachments; Color-coded microfiber cloth set; Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner; Garden hose; Baking soda; Mild gear disinfectant.

  1. The bikes on the wall hooks
    Wipe the frames down and lift the chain grit off with a rag, working top to bottom, then wipe the hooks themselves before the wheels go back up.
  2. The shelf of this season's kit
    Clear the shelf, vacuum it, wipe it with the neutral pH cleaner, and set the in-season gear back at the front where it is grabbed.
  3. The helmet liners and shin pads
    Take the liners out where they lift, air them, and wipe the pads and shells with a cloth and a mild gear disinfectant, then let them dry all the way through before they go in the bin.
  4. The muddy cleats and boots
    Hose the caked mud off the cleats outdoors, knock the studs clean, and dry them right through before they return to the shelf.
  5. The cooler interior
    Wash the cooler inside, dry it, then leave it propped open with a rag of baking soda inside so it does not come back smelling of last summer.
  6. The labelled activity bins
    Tip each bin out, wipe the grit and sand from the corners, and refit the lid before it goes back on the shelf.
  7. The floor under the bikes
    Vacuum the bare floor under the hanging bikes where dried mud and grass drop off the tyres.

How to clean anything else in the house, surface by surface.

Inspect as you clean. Cleaning is the only time you see the zone empty, so use it.

  • A hairline crack in a helmet shell or a crushed, compressed liner, the sign that helmet is finished even if the shell looks fine.
  • A bike wall hook working loose in its anchor, or the wood around it starting to split.
  • Rust or seized links on a bike chain, and frayed or torn straps on helmets and pads.
  • Mildew growing in the cooler seal or in the corners of a bin that went away damp.

How much this sports gear storage can actually hold

Bikes on wall hooks with bare floor beneath them, one labelled bin per activity, this season's kit at the front of the shelf, and every helmet in the garage belonging to a head that still wears it.

One bin per activity is the capacity, and it is the honest count of what this household currently plays, not what it owns equipment for. If an activity needs two bins it is really two activities, or one of them ended some years ago. Helmets are the exception to every keep-it-just-in-case instinct: a helmet that has been dropped hard, outgrown, or is more than about five years old protects nobody, and passing it on passes on the risk. Run the honest count.

If your sports gear storage is not like this

The passes above assume a fairly ordinary sports gear storage. A few real households are not, and the fix is not to skip the method, it is to run it against a different starting shape.

Children who outgrow kit every year
Add one clearly labelled outgrown bin and empty it on a fixed date each season, rather than letting each item make its own case. Everything except helmets can go on to the next child or the next family; helmets do not, and that is worth saying out loud to whoever is doing the passing on.
Sports played by one person only
Then ownership is already clear and the bins can be smaller and more specific, but the in-season rule matters more: with nobody else moving the kit, last season's gear will sit at the front of the shelf until it is next season's problem.
