Family Room micro zone

The Blanket and Comfort Zone

Hold the throws and floor cushions people actually reach for while watching or reading, in a container that tells the truth about how many there are.

One session: 15-30 min. most of it in Straighten, folding everything to one size

What done looks like

One basket holding four throws folded to the same rectangle so the fold edges line up, two floor cushions stacked beside it, and nothing draped over the sofa back, the radiator, or the arm of a chair.

The six passes, in order

Work them in this order. Sorting after you have arranged things means arranging things you were about to remove.

1. Sort

Decide what stays. Everything else leaves the zone now.

Hold every throw up to the window light. Pilled through, thinned along the fold lines, or holding a smell that survives washing: those become car blankets, pet bedding, or rags, and they leave this room today.

2. Straighten

Give what stays a home, placed where the hand actually reaches.

One basket, and the rim is the limit. Fold every throw to the same rectangle so the stack squares up and anyone can see at a glance whether one has gone missing or one too many has crept in.

3. Shine

Clean it, and use the cleaning to inspect what you cannot see when it is full.

Wash everything in the basket, including the throws that look clean, because this is where pet hair and popcorn oil concentrate. Vacuum the basket's inside corners before the clean throws go back.

4. Safety

Make the zone safe for everybody who uses it, including the shortest person.

Look at where blankets end up when nobody is folding them. A throw laid over a radiator, across a floor heater, or hung on a lamp shade is the quiet fire risk in this room, and the basket rule exists partly to keep them off those surfaces.

5. Standardize

Write down the best way you know today, so it survives you forgetting.

The count is one throw per regular seat plus one spare. Write that number on a small label inside the basket rim, so whoever is folding can see the rule without being told it.

6. Sustain

Attach the reset to something that already happens, so it keeps itself.

When the last lamp goes off for the night, whatever is on the sofa gets folded and dropped in the basket on the way past.

The ugly favourite

You own a pilled grey fleece that everyone in the house fights over, and a handsome wool throw that has been folded on the arm of the chair since the day it was bought. The instinct is to keep the wool one because it looks like the room you want, and quietly retire the fleece because it looks tired. Do the opposite, and be clear about why: this basket stores comfort, not decoration. The test is reaches, not looks. Watch a week and count which throws come off the pile. The tired fleece stays, because it is doing the job the basket exists for. The wool one goes to the guest bed, where being beautiful and unused is actually the job, or it goes out. The same rule handles the blanket a relative knitted. If someone genuinely wraps up in it, it stays in the basket. If it is an heirloom nobody unfolds, it does not belong in a family room basket at all. Fold it into the linen closet, or give it to the person in the family who will actually use it.

Check these before you start

  • Burn or fire A fleece throw laid over a radiator, a portable heater, or a lamp shade to dry or to be out of the way. Synthetic fabric against a hot surface is a slow fire that starts once the room is empty.
  • Fall A throw that slides off the sofa arm onto the floor beside it lands exactly where the next person puts their foot when they stand up in the dark.

Cleaning it properly

This basket is where pet hair and popcorn oil concentrate, so everything in it gets washed, even the throw that looks clean. Then check the warm surfaces a stray blanket drifts onto.

Inspect as you clean. Cleaning is the only time you see the zone empty, so use it.

  • A scorched, melted, or stiffened patch on a throw that has been sitting on a radiator or heater.
  • A warm or discolored patch on a lamp shade where fabric has been resting against the bulb.
  • A musty smell in a throw that went into the basket damp, the start of mildew in the fibres.
  • Pet hair and an oily film building in the basket base that never quite comes out.
  • A cracked, splintered, or loosening rim on a woven basket that could snag a blanket or a hand.

The standard that keeps it fixed

One basket, one throw per regular seat plus one spare, every throw folded to the same rectangle, and no blanket left over a radiator or a heater.

Reset trigger. When the last lamp goes off for the night.

Before you start. This is guidance for organising and cleaning a home. It is not instruction for electrical, gas, structural, or any other licensed work. Do not lift or move anything unsafe, do not mix cleaning products, and follow the label on anything you use. If a job needs a professional, that is the right answer. See the full safety notice.
