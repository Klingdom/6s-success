Entryway micro zone

The Door, Mat, and Immediate Floor

Catch grit, water and road salt at the threshold so none of it reaches the rest of the floors.

One session: 15-30 min. most of it in Shine, and honestly the mat is the whole job

What done looks like

A coarse mat outside the door and an absorbent one inside, both lying dead flat with no curled corners, nothing whatsoever on the floor inside the door's swing, and a door that latches on the first push.

The six passes, in order

Work them in this order. Sorting after you have arranged things means arranging things you were about to remove.

1. Sort

Decide what stays. Everything else leaves the zone now.

Nothing is stored on this patch of floor, so the recycling bag, the parcel waiting to go back and the sports kit all move tonight. Give outgoing returns one named spot in another room and carry the rest to where they actually belong.

2. Straighten

Give what stays a home, placed where the hand actually reaches.

Two mats and only two. Coarse outside to scrape, absorbent inside to dry. The inside mat needs to be long enough that both feet land on it while walking, and most are not, which is exactly how the grit gets past.

3. Shine

Clean it, and use the cleaning to inspect what you cannot see when it is full.

Vacuum the inside mat on both faces, then take it outdoors, beat it and wash it. Run a cloth along the bottom edge of the door and along the threshold strip, because that dark line is all the grit the mat never caught.

4. Safety

Make the zone safe for everybody who uses it, including the shortest person.

A curled mat corner and a mat that slides on hard floor are the two ways this threshold puts somebody down. Grip or tape the edges flat, and dry the floor immediately when someone comes in soaked rather than leaving it to evaporate.

5. Standardize

Write down the best way you know today, so it survives you forgetting.

Run a thin line of tape around where the mat should sit, so a mat kicked out of position is obvious to anyone walking in rather than only to you.

6. Sustain

Attach the reset to something that already happens, so it keeps itself.

The mat is on your route back to the cupboard with the vacuum, so it gets done last, every time, as part of putting the vacuum away.

When the mat is finished

A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, and the decision being dodged is spending money to replace something that is not visibly broken. So test it instead of looking at it. Walk in from outside on a dry gritty day, wipe your feet the way you actually wipe them, then lift the mat and look at both the floor beneath it and the fibres themselves. A working mat holds grit down inside its pile and drops a clear line of dirt when you shake it out. A finished mat has fibres flattened all in one direction, and the grit sits on top of them and walks straight off on the next pair of shoes. If it fails that test, replace it this month rather than next winter. A worn mat is not saving you anything. It is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between this door and the kitchen.

Check these before you start

  • Fall A curled mat edge at a doorway catches the toe of someone stepping in with their eyes on their phone or their arms full. Flat edges, gripped or taped, checked every time you vacuum.
  • Fall, cut, or crush A door with a loose hinge screw or a failing closer swings harder than expected and catches fingers against the frame. Tighten the hinge screws while you are down there with the cloth.

Cleaning it properly

Vacuum the inside mat on both faces, then take it out, beat it and wash it. Run a cloth along the bottom edge of the door and the threshold strip, because that dark line is all the grit the mat never caught. Work the door down to the floor.

Inspect as you clean. Cleaning is the only time you see the zone empty, so use it.

  • A mat with a corner that has curled and stopped lying flat, or backing that has gone brittle and is shedding crumbs, both signs the grip is failing. Flag it.
  • A hinge that has dropped so the door scrapes the frame, or a hinge screw standing loose in its hole. Note it.
  • A swelling, a dark line, or a soft patch along the bottom of the door or the threshold where rain keeps sitting, the start of rot. Flag it.
  • A door that no longer latches on the first push, or a threshold seal squashed flat so daylight and draught come under it. Note it.
  • A water stain spreading on the floor just inside the threshold, where wet feet land before the mat. Flag it.

The standard that keeps it fixed

Nothing is stored on the floor inside the door's swing, ever, and both mats lie flat inside their taped outline.

Reset trigger. Whoever comes home last and locks the door straightens the mat with a foot before turning the key.
