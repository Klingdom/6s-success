Built from the Manual, not around it

Five zones, real diagnosis, nothing invented

The dining room has one job, and it is the only room in the house that can quietly lose that job without anyone noticing. The table becomes a desk, the sideboard becomes a filing cabinet, and dinner moves to the couch. Start with the table, because a room that can seat everyone tonight is a room worth defending tomorrow.

Every card here is real: written from the same source as the Manual and the zone pages, gated so a root cause with no friction pointing at it, or an action nobody's problem needs, cannot ship. None of it is generated placeholder copy. What is missing is photography, which this deck does not pretend to have. A family-coloured panel and the card's own symbol stand where a photograph will eventually go.

61 cards, seven kinds

One zone, one friction, one cause, one fix, one standard

A Zone card names the place. Friction cards say what a household actually complains about there. Each friction points at a Root Cause, and each cause names the Action that fixes it. A Standard card is what you write down and keep. Event cards are the days that test whether it held.

THE DINING ROOM

ONE JOB. THE ONLY ROOM THAT CAN LOSE IT QUIETLY.

●○○○○

The dining room has one job, and it is the only room in the house that can quietly lose that job without anyone noticing. This card is the map and the order.

How it works

Start here. DRZ-001 Dining Table. Start with the Dining Table. It is the quickest zone in the room and the only one whose result shows up at dinner the same evening, which buys you the goodwill to open the sideboard drawers tomorrow.

The five zones, in order

  1. DRZ-001 Dining Table
  2. DRZ-002 Buffet or Sideboard Surface
  3. DRZ-003 Buffet or Sideboard Storage
  4. DRZ-004 China or Display Cabinet
  5. DRZ-005 Beverage or Coffee Station

How to play

  1. Deal the five ZONE cards face up. Pick the one that is annoying you today, or take the one this card says to start at.
  2. Lay out that zone's three FRICTION cards. Keep the ones that are true in your dining room. Put the rest back.
  3. Turn a friction card over and pick the answer that is true. It names a ROOT CAUSE.
  4. The root cause card names the ACTION cards that fix that cause. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have: fifteen or thirty minutes.
  5. Do it standing up, with the card in your hand.
  6. Read the victory condition out loud. If it is not true yet, you are not finished, and that is the whole scoring system.
  7. Fill in the zone's STANDARD card with a pen and put it where the zone is.
  8. Draw an EVENT card when an ordinary hard day happens, and see whether the standard held.

Players. 1 to 6. With more than one, deal the friction cards out and let each person keep the ones they believe. Disagreement is the useful part, not a problem to resolve before starting.

Safety first. Do DRA-011 The Full Dining Room Safety Walk before any rebuild. It takes thirty minutes and covers every chair, the cabinet strap, and whether alcohol and candles are kept out of a child's reach.

DINING TABLE

Zone 1 of 5 · Dining Room

ONE CENTERPIECE. BARE WOOD. NOTHING ELSE ON IT.

●●○○○

Seat everyone for a shared meal without having to move anything first.

How it works

Done looks like: One centerpiece on bare wood and nothing else, every chair pushed all the way in with nothing hanging off its back, placemats and trivets in the sideboard drawer one step away, and no paper anywhere on the surface.

Six things to check

  1. One centerpiece sitting alone on bare wood
  2. Every chair pushed fully in, nothing hanging off any chair back
  3. No paper anywhere on the tabletop
  4. A trivet visible just inside the nearest sideboard drawer
  5. Placemats stacked in that same drawer
  6. The chair rungs and legs clear of anything leaning against them

Burn or fire Candles set at the ends of a table runner catch the runner before anyone sitting at the table notices, and a hot serving dish on bare wood scorches it and can crack a glass top.

Fall, cut, or crush A drop leaf or an extension table with a worn latch can fold on a hand when someone leans on it, and dining chairs work loose at the rungs long before they actually break.

The person who works at the table. Someone in the house has made the table their desk, and every reset you have run so far has been a polite request that they stop. Requests do not hold. Here is the rule that settles it: if an activity lands on the table more than three evenings in a week, it is not clutter, it is an activity with nowhere else to be, and no table standard will survive it. Give that activity a caddy it lives in and a named shelf it goes back to, or accept that this is a desk and stop calling the room a dining room. Then decide which meal you are protecting, Sunday lunch or weeknight dinner, say it out loud to the household, and let everything outside that meal be negotiable.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Portable cleaning caddy

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE. THEN WHY.

●○○○○

CLEAR THE TABLE BY OWNER, NOT BY TYPE

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Clear the whole table onto the floor in one armful and sort what came off by the person it belongs to, so nothing stays homeless on a technicality.

How it works

A pile sorted by type just becomes three piles; sorting by owner means somebody actually carries it away.

You need

  • a basket for each person's things

Steps

  1. Clear the whole table onto the floor in one armful, then sort what came off by owner rather than by type. The homework, the unopened mail, and the half-finished craft project each go back to a person, not back to the table.
  2. Hand each pile to the person it belongs to, right now, rather than setting it back down anywhere else in the room.
  3. Wipe the bare table once everything is off it, then set the centerpiece back down alone.

Victory. The table holds nothing but its centerpiece, and every activity that was on it has gone home with the person who owns it.

If this keeps happening

  • NO ASSIGNED HOME
  • CONFLICTING USERS

BUFFET OR SIDEBOARD SURFACE

Zone 2 of 5 · Dining Room

ONE BARE RUN FOR SERVING. THREE PIECES FOR DISPLAY.

●●○○○

Hold serving dishes during a meal and a small, deliberate display between them.

How it works

Done looks like: A taped run of bare wood at the end nearest the table long enough for the roasting pan, three display objects grouped at the lamp end, no paper on the wood, and the lamp cord the only cord in sight.

Six things to check

  1. A strip of tape marking a bare serving run at one end of the sideboard
  2. Three display objects grouped together at the lamp end
  3. A lamp standing among the display objects
  4. The lamp's cord the only cord visible
  5. No paper anywhere on the wood
  6. The serving run empty and clear of any object

Burn or fire A sideboard often ends up carrying a lamp, a phone charger, and a warming tray on one outlet behind the furniture where you never look. Count what is on that plug and feel the strip for warmth or discoloring.

Fall, cut, or crush Tall vases and heavy candlesticks on a narrow sideboard sit at head height for a small child and topple when the drawer below is yanked open.

The clear run you actually need. You have been asking one surface to do three jobs: serving, display, and mail. It cannot, and the mail always wins, because paper arrives daily and decor does not. Settle it with a measurement rather than an intention. Take the largest dish you genuinely serve from this surface, the roasting pan or the big salad bowl, set it down where you would really set it, and mark that length of wood with a strip of tape. That run is protected and nothing lives on it, ever. Now look at your decor in what is left. Whatever does not fit in the remaining length leaves the room, however much you like it, and the mail never had a claim here to begin with.

What it uses

  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Streak-free glass and mirror cleaner
  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Small detail brush set
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Plastic card for lifting wax

CLEAR THE WOOD AND GIVE EVERY PAPER A VERDICT

15 MINUTES. 1.

●●○○○

Take everything off the sideboard's wood and give every piece of paper a real verdict tonight, so nothing sits there by default.

How it works

Paper left 'for now' is exactly how a serving surface turns into a filing cabinet.

You need

  • a recycling bin
  • a folder for anything to file

Steps

  1. Take everything off the wood, including the picture frames and the candlesticks, and give every piece of paper a verdict tonight: act on it, file it, or recycle it. Burnt-down candle stubs, dried flowers, and chargers belonging to other rooms leave now.
  2. Return chargers and stray items to the room they actually belong to.
  3. Set only the lamp and the display group you're keeping back down at the far end.

TAPE THE SERVING RUN AND CAP THE DISPLAY AT THREE

30 MINUTES. 1.

●●●○○

Mark the serving run with tape sized to your largest dish, and settle the display group at exactly three objects at the far end.

How it works

A run you can see is a run you'll actually keep clear; an unlimited display always grows until it swallows the space mail wants to claim.

You need

  • a large serving dish to measure with
  • tape

Steps

  1. Set your largest serving dish where you'd really put it and mark that length with tape.
  2. Choose exactly three display objects for the far end and put everything else away or elsewhere.
  3. Pull the sideboard out and trace the lamp cord to the outlet so you can actually see what else shares that plug.

Victory. The taped run stays bare, exactly three display objects sit at the far end, and you know what else shares the lamp's outlet.

If this keeps happening

  • MISSING STANDARD
  • EXCESS
  • POOR VISIBILITY

BUFFET OR SIDEBOARD STORAGE

Zone 3 of 5 · Dining Room

TWO CLOTHS ROLLED WITH THEIR NAPKINS. A COUNT CARD INSIDE THE DOOR.

●●●○○

Hold the things that only come out when the table is being set.

How it works

Done looks like: Two tablecloths rolled with their matching napkins inside them in one drawer, serving bowls nested by size in the next, the large platters standing on edge behind a divider, taper candles of one length in a latched drawer, and a count card taped inside the door.

Six things to check

  1. Two rolled tablecloths sitting in one open drawer
  2. Napkins visible tucked inside each rolled cloth
  3. Serving bowls nested together by size in a separate drawer
  4. Large platters standing upright on edge behind a divider
  5. A latched drawer holding taper candles of matching length
  6. A count card taped to the inside of the door

Fall, cut, or crush Platters and the punch bowl stored above shoulder height come down while your arms are already loaded, and a drawer packed tight with linens can pull clean out of its runners.

Burn or fire Boxes of taper candles and long matches sitting loose in a low sideboard drawer are exactly at a child's reach and open on all sides.

The tablecloth for a table you don't own. This drawer is full of linen from other houses, other tables, and other decades, and you keep it because letting go of good cloth feels wasteful. Make it a measuring problem instead of a feeling. Measure your table, then measure every cloth: one that does not hang at least a hand's depth over your actual table edge on all sides will never get used, because it looks wrong the moment it goes on and you will fold it away again. Keep the cloths that fit the table you own, plus one sized for the table with the leaf in. Anything carrying a stain you have already treated once and failed to shift becomes rags today, not next season. Partial napkin sets travel with the cloth they match, or they go with it.

What it uses

  • Compact vacuum with attachments
  • Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
  • Color-coded microfiber cloth set
  • Portable cleaning caddy
  • Silver polish

CHINA OR DISPLAY CABINET

Zone 4 of 5 · Dining Room

EIGHT PLATES HIGH, WITH FELT BETWEEN THEM. STRAPPED TO THE WALL.

●●●○○

Keep the good tableware safe, visible, and genuinely usable.

How it works

Done looks like: Plates stacked no more than eight high with felt between them, cups standing single file rather than nested, glasses rim up on a clean liner, tureens on the bottom shelf, the cabinet strapped to a wall stud, and a photo of the finished shelves taped inside the door.

Six things to check

  1. A stack of plates no taller than eight, felt visible between each plate
  2. Cups standing single file in a row rather than nested together
  3. Glasses standing rim up on a clean shelf liner
  4. A tureen sitting on the cabinet's bottom shelf
  5. A visible strap securing the cabinet to the wall
  6. A small photo taped to the inside of the cabinet door

Fall, cut, or crush A tall glass-fronted cabinet loaded with heavy plates is the largest tip-over risk in this room, and its shelves make an inviting ladder for a small child.

Poison, choke, or strangle Decorative antique and hand-painted pieces of unknown origin can carry lead in the glaze, particularly on gilt rims and bright reds. Display them, but keep acidic food and drink off them.

The twelve settings from your grandmother. This is the hardest decision in the room and everyone knows why: the china is not china, it is a person. Separate the two on purpose. Keep the number of settings that fits the largest meal you have actually hosted in the past three years, plus two for breakage. For the rest, write down whose they were and what you remember about them, photograph them alongside that note, and offer them to the relative most likely to set a table with them, then sell or donate whatever nobody claims. Then the part people skip: what you keep, you owe use. Name an occasion, once a year at minimum, when it goes on the table. China that never leaves the cabinet is not being honored.
