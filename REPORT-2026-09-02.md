### What shipped today

**Captions as text, 114 files.** The videos always had words, but they were
baked into pixels. That is invisible to everything that matters for reach:
YouTube cannot index it, a screen reader cannot speak it, a translation cannot
touch it, and a deaf viewer got nothing a hearing viewer did not, which is the
one case burned-in text exists to cover.

The timings are not estimated. The builder returns the exact (seconds, slide)
pairs the video is assembled from, so each caption starts and ends with its beat
by construction. Two quality rules came from reading the first output rather
than from theory: the brand watermark is stripped, because it is painted on
every slide and repeating it in every caption is noise to somebody who can
already see it; and a slide holding a whole checklist is split into one cue per
item instead of a wall of text sitting over the picture it describes.

All 114 parse, none is empty, none has fewer than three cues.

**A 16:9 cut.** The same beats now render at 1920x1080 into a separate
directory, so the vertical and horizontal versions never overwrite each other.
Vertical stays the default, because that is where a two-minute instructional
clip actually gets watched. Rendering is running now at roughly 150 seconds per
zone.

**A correction worth your attention.** My media plan told you the whole
114-video stream was blocked on a narration decision from you. That was wrong.
ffmpeg with the right libraries was already installed and the tooling already
drove it. You pushed back, correctly, and the answer was to try it rather than
ask. Captions-only was always viable, costs nothing, and is the accessible
option anyway. The question is withdrawn.
