## 1. Decision

**REJECTED for the Home Quest. APPROVED WITH NON-BLOCKING FINDINGS for the
rest of the site.**

The commerce layer is in materially better shape than the repository's own
records suggest, and the quest's core loop is correct underneath. The reason
for the rejection is a single presentation defect that makes the free product
appear not to respond at all on a small phone.

**Risk: MEDIUM overall, HIGH on the quest.**

## 2. The headline: the Home Quest looks broken on a phone

`GOALS.md` records 53 views of `/quest.html` against 61 of the home page, and
that only 2 sessions have ever finished a card and 1 has ever held a zone. I
was asked to drive it rather than read it. Here is why those numbers are what
they are.

### CONFIRMED, P1: tapping the primary call to action produces no visible change

`quest.html` renders as one long document. The marketing hero occupies the
whole first screen. `#view-start` and `#view-card` both live below it. When you
tap **"Start at the door, 15 minutes"**, `show()` in `assets/js/quest.js`
(around line 407) does this:

```js
window.scrollTo(0, 0);
if (shown && everShown) { shown.focus({ preventScroll: true }); }
```

It scrolls the user to the **top of the document**, which is the hero they have
already read, and then explicitly suppresses the scroll that moving focus would
otherwise have caused. Measured on production at 11:13 MDT, build
`5047354c0c302755`:

| device | viewport pixels changed by the tap | top of the card | card visible | "Done" button |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone SE 320x568 | **none, screenshots byte-identical** | 685px | **0px** | 4.1 screens down |
| iPhone 14 390x844 | yes | 632px | 212px (25% of screen, bottom edge) | 2.4 screens down |
| iPhone 15 Pro Max 430x932 | yes | 558px | 373px | 1.9 screens down |

The 320px row is not a figure of speech. I screenshotted the visible viewport
before and after the tap and hashed both:

```
before 3895fc47380799ee441732ee2d62ae1b
after  3895fc47380799ee441732ee2d62ae1b
IDENTICAL SCREEN
```

**How a customer experiences it.** On a small phone they tap the one orange
button the page is built around and nothing happens. Not "something happens
slowly", nothing. They tap again, or they leave. On a 390px phone they get a
quarter-screen sliver of a card at the bottom edge, with the instruction itself
cut off, and no cue that the page changed state. The action button is then two
and a half screens further down.

It is not only the first tap. `show()` is the single view switch for the whole
app, so **"Draw a card"** does the same (card top 632px, Done 2.3 screens down),
as does **"Skip this one"**. The one path that scrolls correctly is `#go-other`
("or pick a different room"), which lands at `scrollY` 847, so the app is
inconsistent with itself about whether an action moves you.

**Acceptance criterion.** After any `show()` transition, at 320x568, 390x844 and
430x932: the newly shown section's `getBoundingClientRect().top` is between 0
and 80, and the primary action for that view (`#c-done` where present) is
reachable within one screen of scrolling or is pinned. A before/after viewport
screenshot pair must differ.

### CONFIRMED, P2: focus is dropped on the user's first action

`show()` guards focus behind `everShown`, with a comment saying this stops the
first render on page load stealing focus from the hero `h1`. It does not do
that. Page load does not consume the guard, so the **user's first click** does.
Driven with the keyboard only:

```
on load                                    active: BODY
after 1st view change (Start at the door)  active: BODY
after 2nd view change (Done)               active: view-card
after 3rd view change (Skip)               active: view-card
```

The sections are correctly `tabindex="-1"`, so the mechanism works, it is
simply skipped exactly once, on the interaction that matters most. A screen
reader user tapping "Start at the door" gets no announcement, no focus position,
and the document scrolled back to the top. Every later action is announced.

**Acceptance criterion.** `document.activeElement.id` equals the id of the newly
shown section after *every* view change including the first.

### CONFIRMED, P2: the funnel event fires when the customer saw nothing

The analytics are wired and correct in isolation. Captured live from a full
six-pass run, the beacons to `/stats/api/send` were, in order:
`quest-first-start {zone:"Door, Mat, and Immediate Floor"}`,
`quest-start {mode:"zone",first:1,cards:6}`, six
`quest-card-done {s:...,nth:1..6}`, and `quest-zone-held`. `localStorage`
accumulated `6s.quest.v1` correctly and the state survived a reload
("1 of 114 zones holding, 6 of 684 cards done").

But `quest-start` fires on the click, and the defect above means the click can
happen without the customer ever seeing a card. **So `quest-start` currently
counts intent, not exposure.** Any read of the funnel that treats it as "a card
was shown" overstates the top of the funnel and understates how bad the drop
is. `analytics-intelligence` should know this before interpreting the number.

### What does work in the quest: verified, not assumed

- Six passes complete, one card at a time, landing on a finish view ("That is a
  session. 6 cards done. 1 sort, 1 straighten, ... 6 of 684 across the house,
  1 percent.").
- Progress persists across a full page reload and the app resumes into a "Your
  house" summary with the 19 remaining rooms and their card counts.
- `Skip this one` and `Stop here` both work and both keep state.
- Zero JavaScript errors, zero console errors, zero failed requests.
- Keyboard: Tab reaches `#go-first` in 4 tabs, Enter starts it, Tab then reaches
  `#c-done` in 1, Enter completes the card and writes `localStorage`.
- Works with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

The loop is sound. It is the presentation of the transition that is failing.

## 3. What the site claims against what is true

### CONFIRMED, P1: the bundle checkout shows an invented saving

The site and the Stripe checkout disagree about the bundle's comparison price,
and the checkout is the one the customer reads with their card out.

| surface | text |
|---|---|
| `data.js` / `shop.html` / `book.html` | "Bought separately they are **$57.99**." Badge: "Save $8.99" |
| `buy.stripe.com/fZu6oAcHYckycs21BC0kF27` | "Bought separately they are **$66**." |

$9.99 (eBook) + $29 (Manual) + $19 (Print Pack) = **$57.99**, and I verified all
three prices on their own live Stripe pages. `$66` is a stale figure. At $49 the
checkout implies a $17 saving where the real one is $8.99.

This is not a typo, it is the thing this site explicitly promises not to do.
`how-we-make-money.html`, live, under "What we will not do": *"Use countdowns,
fake scarcity or **invented discounts**."*

**How a customer experiences it.** They compare the bundle against the three
items, the arithmetic does not work, and the number that is wrong is the one
that makes the bundle look better. That is the exact shape of a defect that
costs trust permanently for $8 of apparent margin.

**Acceptance criterion.** The Stripe product description equals the catalogue
blurb for every SKU, asserted by tooling rather than by hand, extend whatever
`ops/stripe_catalog.py --apply` asserts on `price` to cover `description`, and
fail preflight on drift.

### CONFIRMED, P2: 8 of 155 Stripe pages carry copy the site does not

## 4. Accessibility and mobile, checked independently

The brief said the last audit reported contrast 0, focus 0, touch targets 0

### CONFIRMED, P2: contrast is not zero, and the repository's audit cannot see why

I ran `axe-core` 4.10.2 against all 187 sitemap pages at 390x844.

**Result: 7 serious `color-contrast` violations on 2 pages, and nothing else.**

```
shop.html         3 nodes   <span class="n">19</span>
deck-gallery.html 4 nodes   <span class="n">10</span>
contrast 2.28:1 (fg #aba59d on bg #fbf7ef, 14px normal), WCAG AA needs 4.5:1
```

These are the item counts inside the category filter chips: "Books & Guides
**6**", "Room Packs **19**", "Micro Zone Packs **109**". A low-vision user
cannot read how many products are in a category. They fail at desktop 1280 as
well as at 390, so this is not a mobile-only case.

**Why the repository's audit reports 0.** `ops/audit_visual.py --all`, run just
now, prints `viewport: 1280x2600 (desktop)` and `text below contrast: 0`. Its
measurement loop (around line 183) reads:

```js
if(st.display==='none'||st.visibility==='hidden'||parseFloat(st.opacity)<0.1)continue;
...
var fg=parse(st.color);
```

It composites the **alpha channel of the colour value** but never applies the
element's CSS **`opacity`**. It only uses opacity to skip. The failing spans are
`color:#6A625A` with `.n{opacity:.55}`. The tool computes 5.37:1 from the
declared colour; the browser renders 2.28:1. It is not that the tool looked and
saw nothing: it cannot see this class of defect at all, and the site uses
`opacity` on de-emphasised text in several places (the `&times;12` quantity
markers on every zone page use `opacity:.7`).

Two separate acceptance criteria:

1. The seven spans reach 4.5:1 as rendered.
2. `audit_visual.py` multiplies through `opacity` (including inherited opacity
   from ancestors) before computing the ratio, **and** is proved by planting a
   known-failing `opacity` case and watching it fail. A check that has never
   failed on a real defect is not evidence.

### The rest of the accessibility sweep: genuinely clean

Verified, not assumed, across 187 pages at 390x844:

- **Horizontal overflow: 0 pages.** `document.documentElement.scrollWidth`
  equals `innerWidth` on every one.
- **No other WCAG 2.0 / 2.1 / 2.2 A or AA violation of any rule**, from the full

## 6. Smaller confirmed findings

### P2: the cart cannot be reached from anywhere

`site.js` ships a complete cart: `Cart.add`, quantity controls, a drawer, a
toast, `subtotal()`, `lineTotal()`, `hasQuoteItem()`, and `cart.html` renders
line items and an order summary. The click delegate binds on
`[data-add-sku],[data-rm],[data-inc],[data-dec]`.

**`data-add-sku` appears on zero of the 187 live pages.** `shop.html` has no
"add to cart" control; every tile is a direct "Buy it now" to Stripe.
`cart.html` is in no page's link graph and is not in the sitemap. Nothing can
put an item in the cart, so `cart.html` can only ever say "Your cart is empty".

Consequences: dead JavaScript on every page load; `privacy.html` documents a
cart feature that does not exist ("If you add something to the cart, that list is
saved in your own browser using localStorage, under the key `sixs_cart_v2`"); and
`STATUS.md`'s 2026-08-19 entry *"Cart fixed to hand off what a customer actually
selected"* describes a path no customer can enter.

I should be clear about a wrong turn: I first seeded `sixs_cart_v2` with
`[{sku,qty}]` and reported that the cart rendered unnamed "Quote" rows and a 404
on `assets/img/`. The real row shape is `{sku,name,variant,price,img,qty}`.
**That finding is retracted**: it was my malformed fixture, not a defect.

**Acceptance criterion.** Either the cart is removed from `site.js`,
`cart.html` and `privacy.html`, or an add-to-cart control exists.

### P2: the repository's working copy of a shipped PDF is byte-corrupt

`site/downloads/6S-Entryway-Deck-PrintAndPlay.pdf` in the working tree is
26,194,263 bytes. The file production serves is 26,192,171. Same `CreationDate`.
The difference is 2,092 injected `\r` bytes: the tree copy has CRLF line endings
throughout a **binary** file.

The consequence is not cosmetic. Both files declare `startxref 26187468`. In the
live file, byte 26187468 is `xref\n0 224\n0000000000 65535 f`. In the tree copy
it is `Rn8:m*mV-tTg'V:6NSCsAbQI(<p$'3:)`, the middle of a compressed stream.
`pypdf` recovers by rebuilding the table and warns `incorrect startxref
pointer(1)`; stricter readers may not.

Root cause: **there is no `.gitattributes` in this repository.** Git's binary
detection looks for NUL bytes early in the file; this ReportLab PDF has none, so
it is treated as text and line-ending conversion is applied on checkout. The
MuPDF-written sample PDF has NULs near the header and survives.

Production is fine today because deploys run from a Linux checkout. It stops
being fine the moment anything publishes from a Windows working tree.

**Acceptance criterion.** `.gitattributes` declares `*.pdf binary` (and the other
binary types), and the tree copy's bytes match what production serves.

### P2: no security headers at all

```
$ curl -sI https://6s-success.com/
HTTP/1.1 200 OK
Server: openresty
Content-Type: text/html
Cache-Control: no-cache
X-Served-By: 6s-success.com
```

Absent: `Strict-Transport-Security`, `Content-Security-Policy`,
`X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options`,
`Permissions-Policy`.

No card data touches this origin: checkout is a redirect to Stripe, so this is
not a payment vulnerability, and I am not escalating it as one. But without HSTS
the first `http://` request of every visit is interceptable before the 301, and
without `Referrer-Policy` the full URL of the zone page a customer was reading is
sent to `target.com` and `homedepot.com` on every supply click, which sits
awkwardly beside a privacy policy built around telling nobody anything. Referred
to `security-auditor` and `devops-sre` as a hardening item, not an incident.

### P3: trailing-slash zone URLs 404

`https://6s-success.com/zones/entryway-the-door-mat-and-immediate-floor/` returns
**404** rather than redirecting to the extensionless form. A human appending a
slash, or a crawler normalising one, gets a dead end.

## 10. One process observation

Every confirmed finding above came from opening the artifact a customer opens.
Not one came from a repository record, and three of them contradict a record that
says the work is done: the sample PDF cover, the deck count, and the forms
paragraph in the privacy policy.

The pattern in all three is identical. **The source was corrected and the shipped
artifact was not re-derived from it**, and the check that should have caught it
either measured the source instead of the artifact (`gate_deck_count` reading
`data.js`), could not model the thing that breaks it (`audit_visual.py` and
`opacity`), or returned early when it could not see (`gate_deck_count` again, on
any machine without the rendered deck on disk).

The most useful gate this repository could add is not another one. It is a rule
that a check must be able to demonstrate it has failed on a real instance of the
defect it claims to prevent.
