that number, and what evidence would change it. A price with no recorded reason
gets re-argued every quarter by whoever feels strongest that day.

Last set: 2026-08-20. Covers the card decks. The rest of the catalogue is
recorded here as observed, not as decided, and is flagged at the end.

### 0.3 The problem with that ladder: everything under $19 is dominated

The $19 Whole House Print Pack is a **strict superset** of all 149 packs above
it. Same cards, same renderer, same file format. So:

| Tier | Price | Share of the $19 price | Share of the 684 cards |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Area bundle | $16 | **84%** | 12 to 20% |
| Situation kit | $14 | **74%** | 7 to 20% |
| Room pack | $9 | 47% | 3 to 6% |
| Micro zone pack | $4 | 21% | 0.9% |

`ops/build_catalog.py` asserts only that no tier is priced **at or above** $19.
That assert catches the absurd case, and it was written for a good reason (six
bundles once shipped at $24 above a $19 superset), but every row in the table
passes it. A floor of absurdity is not a value test.

The $4 and $9 tiers survive this on a real argument: they are smaller than the
job on purpose, and somebody who wants only the room they are standing in is
entitled to buy only that. The $14 and $16 tiers survive it less well. At 74
and 84 percent of the price of everything, the only thing being sold is the
curation, and the curation has to be worth $14 on its own for that to hold.

**Done 2026-09-03, without touching a price:**

1. Every pack tile in the shop now states its superset in plain words, read
   from the catalogue and never typed: *"Part of The Whole House Print Pack,
   $19 for all 684 cards."* The 114 zone pages already made this comparison at
   the point of sale; the shop rendered the identical product with that context
   stripped off. One surface honest and another not is worse than neither,
   because whichever the buyer saw second is the one that reads as the trick.
2. Area bundles moved to last of the paid groups in the shop order (0.5).

**Recommended, NOT executed, because each one is a Stripe write:**

- **Retire the six Area Bundles, or reprice them to $12.** They are the one
  tier that maps to no job a customer names out loud. "Every wet room" is how
  we group rooms by function; nobody with a messy bathroom thinks that
  sentence. Evidence that would overturn this: any measured demand for a
  cross-room functional grouping. There is none today in either direction, so
  this is a judgement about coherence, not a measurement.
- **Separate the $9 room pack from the $9.99 eBook.** Ninety-nine cents apart,
  wildly different scope: one room on 42 cards, or fifty chapters. Whichever a
  buyer compares first makes the other look mispriced. Moving the room pack to
  $7 restores a legible ladder ($4 zone, $7 room, $19 house) and costs $1.55 of
  contribution per order. Do not do this before at least one real room-pack
  sale exists to reason from.
- **Re-derive the $29 Micro Zone Manual against the $9.99 book.** The companion
  volume costs three times the main volume and both cover the same twenty
  rooms. That may well be right, because the manual is the deeper reference and
  the book is read once, but no record says it was argued, and nothing on
  either tile explains the difference to somebody looking at both.

### 0.4 Unit economics, as far as the data actually goes

The Stripe fee is **measured, not assumed**: the single real transaction was
$19.00 gross and $18.15 net, which is exactly 2.9% plus $0.30.

| Product | Price | Stripe fee | Contribution | Fee load |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Micro zone pack | $4.00 | $0.42 | $3.58 | **10.5%** |
| Room pack | $9.00 | $0.56 | $8.44 | 6.2% |
| eBook | $9.99 | $0.59 | $9.40 | 5.9% |
| Situation kit | $14.00 | $0.71 | $13.29 | 5.1% |
| Area bundle | $16.00 | $0.76 | $15.24 | 4.8% |
| Whole House Print Pack | $19.00 | $0.85 | $18.15 | 4.5% |
| Micro Zone Manual | $29.00 | $1.14 | $27.86 | 3.9% |
| Complete Digital Bundle | $49.00 | $1.72 | $47.28 | 3.5% |
| Virtual Home Consult | $250.00 | $7.55 | $242.45 | 3.0% |
| In-Home Reset Day | $1,200.00 | $35.10 | $1,164.90 | 2.9% |

Marginal COGS on every digital SKU is **zero**. The deliverable is a file that
already exists on disk, attached by a scheduled GitHub Actions job that runs on
the free tier whether or not an order arrives. So for the digital catalogue,
contribution equals price less the Stripe fee, and the table above is the whole
of it.

The 10.5% fee load on the $4 zone pack is the reason `ops/build_catalog.py`
refuses to go below $4, and that reasoning is sound and should stay.

**What cannot be computed, and is therefore not estimated here:**

- **Operating profit.** `COST-GOVERNANCE.md` records infrastructure, storage
  and backup monthly cost as UNKNOWN, and `SPENDING.md` says the VPS, domain
  and mailbox are "already paid" without naming an amount. Until somebody
  writes down what this costs per month, **contribution is the deepest honest
  line, and revenue less fees must never be called profit.**
- **Service margin.** The Virtual Home Consult and the In-Home Reset Day
  consume Phil's own hours and, for the on-site day, his travel. Neither a cost
  of time nor a travel cost is recorded anywhere, so the $242.45 and $1,164.90
  rows are contribution **before the cost of delivering them**, which for a
  service is most of the cost. Those two rows do not mean what the digital rows
  mean and must not be added to them.
- **Refund rate, repeat rate, average order value.** One transaction, ever.
  There is no denominator.

## 2. Card decks

### The ladder

| Tier | Product | Price | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Entryway Deck, print at home, 46 cards, line art | **Free** | Live |
| 2 | Entryway Deck, illustrated print and play PDF | **$12** | Needs 46 images |
| 3 | Entryway Deck, printed and boxed | **$29** plus shipping | Needs a printer |
| 4 | Both editions, PDF and printed | **$34** | Needs both above |

Each further room deck, when one exists, takes the same prices. A room deck is
a room deck; charging differently for the Kitchen than the Entryway would need
a reason, and there is not one.

### Why tier 1 is free

Not a discount and not a funnel trick. The binding constraint on this business
is that **nobody arrives**, not that nothing is for sale. A complete, genuinely
useful thing given away is a real answer to that. A preorder for something that
does not exist is not.

It also buys the one thing no amount of reasoning can produce: evidence about
whether the card format works in a real house. That evidence is worth more
right now than the revenue a $9 paywall would earn on a site with no traffic.

### Why tier 2 is $12

Comparable **physical** prompt and method decks, retail, checked 2026-08-20:

| Deck | Cards | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Side Quest Deck (chores) | not stated | $19.45 |
| School of Life, Know Yourself | 60 | $24.50 |
| TableTopics Family | 135 | $24.99 |
| School of Life, across the line | varies | $20 to $36 |
| School of Life, Therapy Cards | varies | $36 |
| IDEO Method Cards | 51 | $49.00 |

So the premium physical market for this kind of deck clusters **$20 to $36**,
with a professional tier around $49. Our 46 card instructional deck sits
naturally inside that cluster, not above it: IDEO prices at $49 on thirty years
of name, which we do not have.

I could not verify digital printable comparables. Etsy blocks automated
fetching and the searches returned category pages without prices, so **there is
no digital comparable behind this number and I am not going to invent one.**

Instead tier 2 is anchored on the two things that are real:

1. **This business's own digital to print ratio.** The ebook is $18 against a
   $34 hardcover, which is 0.529. Applied to a $29 physical deck that gives
   $15.
2. **The free tier directly below it.** Unlike the book, tier 2 competes with a
   free version of the same 46 cards. The only thing the buyer gains is the
   illustration. That is a real difference for a deck aimed at children, but it
   is one difference rather than a fuller product, so it should not carry the
   full 0.529 ratio.

$12 is 41% of the physical, below every physical comparable found, and low
enough that the first hundred buyers are a signal rather than a negotiation.

**The honest uncertainty:** $12 could be wrong by a factor of two in either
direction and nothing currently in place would detect it. See the revision
triggers.

### Why tier 3 is $29 plus shipping

Positioned in the middle of the verified $20 to $36 cluster. Below TableTopics
and School of Life, which are established brands, and well below IDEO.

Unit economics, per deck, at low volume:

| Line | Amount | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Print, 54 card deck with custom tuck box | about $11.35 | **Unverified.** Reported for MakePlayingCards in a January 2026 comparison. No quote has been requested. |
| Stripe, 2.9% plus $0.30 on $29 | $1.14 | Verified, standard published rate |
| Contribution before shipping | **about $16.51**, 57% | Only as good as the print line above |

**Shipping is charged at checkout, not absorbed.** Free shipping on a $29 item
costs roughly $6 domestic, which takes contribution from 57% to about 36%. That
is a fine trade at volume, funded by the volume. At zero volume it is just a
smaller margin. Revisit when there is a second physical product to build a free
shipping threshold around.

**Before anything is printed, get a real quote.** The $11.35 is a search
result, not a number anybody has been given. If the true landed cost is $15 the
whole tier needs repricing, and finding that out after a print run is the
expensive way to learn it.

### Why tier 4 is $34

$12 plus $29 is $41, so the bundle saves $7, which is 17%. The book bundle
saves $8 on $52, which is 15%. Same shape, so the catalogue reads as one
business rather than as a set of separate decisions.

### What is deliberately not priced

**Rooms 2 through 20.** Nineteen more decks could be priced today and none of
them exist. Pricing a product line before its pilot has sold a single unit is
forecasting, not pricing.

# The Standards Pack: priced at zero, and why that was a reversal

Scoped 2026-08-22 as a $12 product on the recommendation that leave_behind
carried content sold nowhere else. Built, measured, and repriced to free before
it ever reached the shop.

## What the measurement said

The claim was that `leave_behind.standard` and `leave_behind.trigger` were
distinct from the `standardize` and `sustain` passes already sold inside the $19
Whole House Print Pack. Against a shared-vocabulary overlap test:

| Comparison | Median overlap | Above 0.6 |
|---|---|---|
| `standard` vs `passes.standardize` | 0.27 | 6 of 114 |
| `trigger` vs `passes.sustain` | 0.58 | **49 of 114** |

Forty nine of the triggers are near verbatim restatements. Two are identical in
every content word. The pack is a condensation of something a Print Pack buyer
has already paid for.

## Why that means free rather than cheaper

A smaller price on the same content is still a second charge for one purchase.
The compression is genuinely useful, and useful does not make it a separate
sale. Discounting would have been the version of this that looks like a
concession while keeping the problem.

## Why free is also the better commercial call

The binding constraint remains that nobody arrives. This is the strongest free
artifact the business has: 114 standards across all twenty rooms against the
Entryway deck's 46 cards in one. It shows whole house scope, which is exactly
what the $19 pack is the deep version of, and it costs nothing to reproduce.

## Revision trigger

If the free pack is downloaded 200 times and the Print Pack conversion from
`standards.html` stays under 1%, the page is failing as a route to the paid
product and the offer block at the bottom is what to change, not the price.

**Do not reprice this to anything above zero without new content.** The overlap
numbers above are the reason, and they are re-measured on every build by
`ops/build_standards.py`, which prints the median at build time.

## Corporate Lean 6S (CN-CORP): why there is still no price, decided 2026-09-03

**Price: none. Scoped and quoted per engagement. This is a decision, not an
omission, and this section exists so it stops being re-argued.**

### What changed today, and what deliberately did not

CN-CORP now has a page of its own, `site/corporate.html`, generated by
`ops/build_corporate.py`. Before today its entire presence on the live site was
one sentence on consulting.html, a `price: null` catalogue record, and a
"Request a quote" badge that led to the generic contact form. The page is new.
The pricing is unchanged: no figure, no range, no "from".

### Why no number, when the review named one

`REVENUE-REVIEW-2026-09-04.md` says Lean training contracts commonly run
$5,000 to $15,000, and that two to four a month is the whole $20,000 target.
That is the strongest revenue argument in the catalogue and it is still not a
price, because the same review says so in its own limits section: it is **a
market range read off the industry, not a quote we have given or received**.
Printing it would turn somebody else's benchmark into our price, which is
CLAUDE.md section 8 on the page most likely to be checked by a reader with a
procurement department.

There is also a commercial reason, not only an honesty one. Two engagements
with identical headcount differ by a factor of several depending on sites,
travel, shift patterns and whether we train their trainers. A published rate
would be wrong in both directions at once: it anchors the large engagement
down and prices the small one out.

### What is published instead

Nine scope drivers, on the page, in the buyer's own terms: headcount in the
room, number of sites, zones in the baseline, share delivered on site, travel,
train-the-trainer, who writes the standards, how long we stay for the audit,
and shifts, unions and language. A buyer can answer all nine in about a minute
and self-qualify without talking to anybody. That is more useful to them than a
number, and it is the part that makes the quote fast when they do write.

The page also commits to a written scope and a **fixed** fee before any work
starts, and to re-agreeing in writing before the work changes rather than
invoicing a surprise afterwards.

### What would change this

A price goes in this file when there is a price: the first quote actually
issued, and then the first engagement actually delivered. At that point record
the figure, what was in scope, and how long it took, because two or three real
data points beat any market range. Until then, `price: null` in
`site/assets/js/data.js` is the accurate value and
`ops/tests/test_corporate_page.py` fails the build if a figure appears on the
page or a price appears in its structured data.

### Related, and unchanged

`CN-VIRTUAL` at $250 and `CN-INHOME` at $1,200 keep their published prices.
They are fixed-shape products, one hour and one day, so a number is meaningful
for them in a way it is not here. The corporate page links to both rather than
competing with them.
