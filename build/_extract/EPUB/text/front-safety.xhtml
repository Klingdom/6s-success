Important notice: please read before you begin

This book is a guide to organizing and cleaning a home. It is not a safety manual, a medical reference, a veterinary reference, or a substitute for a qualified professional.

The author and publisher have made a genuine effort to describe safe, ordinary household practice. Even so, homes differ, materials differ, and people differ. You are the only person who can see your home, and you remain responsible for judging what is safe to do in it.

By using this book you accept the following.

Do the work at your own risk. Every task described here is undertaken at your own risk. The author and publisher accept no liability for any loss, injury, damage, or expense arising from following, or failing to follow, anything in this book, to the fullest extent permitted by law.

Know your limits, and stop at them. This book does not teach structural, electrical, plumbing, gas, roofing, or carpentry work, and it never asks you to attempt any of them. Where a task approaches one of those trades, the instruction is always to stop and call a qualified professional. Follow that instruction. If a job requires a licensed trade in your area, use one.

Read the label, every time. Cleaning products are chemicals. Always read and follow the manufacturer's instructions, warnings, and dilution directions for every product you use, and the manufacturer's instructions for every tool and appliance. Where this book names a type of product, the manufacturer's own label always takes precedence over anything written here.

Never mix cleaning products. Mixing products, in particular anything containing bleach with anything containing ammonia or acid, can release dangerous gases. Use one product at a time, and ventilate the room.

Test first. Test any cleaning method on a small, hidden area before you use it on a whole surface. Finishes, stone, fabrics, and older materials vary widely.

Protect children and pets. Keep all cleaning products, tools, batteries, and medicines out of reach and, where possible, locked. Never leave a task, a chemical, or a tool unattended where a child or an animal can reach it.

Power down before you clean. Unplug corded tools and appliances, or remove the battery from cordless ones, before cleaning them.

Ladders and height. Use a stable step ladder on a level surface. Do not stand on furniture, boxes, or the top step. Where possible, have another adult present.

In an emergency, stop and call for help. If someone is injured, swallows a household product, or is exposed to fumes, contact your local emergency number or your regional poison control service immediately. If you smell gas, leave the area, do not operate switches or flames, and call your gas supplier or emergency services from outside.

Medicines and hazardous waste. Dispose of medicines and hazardous household waste according to the guidance of your local authority, pharmacy take-back scheme, or national regulator. Local rules differ, and they take precedence over any general suggestion in this book.

Nothing here is medical, veterinary, or professional advice. Where this book touches on infant sleep, allergies, medicines, pets, or anything affecting health, it is describing general household organization only. Consult a qualified professional for advice about your own circumstances.
