VIDEO: CAPABILITY IN, FIRST TWO CUT, AND ONE CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF

The first page video and the first card video are made. Both are built
entirely from existing content, nothing was written to fill a beat, and both
were fixed several times after looking at actual frames rather than at exit
codes.


THE CORRECTION FIRST, BECAUSE IT CHANGES WHAT I DID

There were no video skills, agents or media capabilities to replicate. The
mediafier .claude folder holds one settings file and nothing else. What is on
the Desktop and in CascadeProjects is architecture documentation for the
Mediafier platform's own internal skill system, plus code review agents, plus
MCP capability discovery tools. None of it generates video.

So rather than replicate nothing, I checked what this machine can actually do
and built on that: ffmpeg 8.1.1 with libass and zoompan, PIL, numpy and
OpenCV, headless Chromium, and Windows speech. That turns out to be better
news than it sounds, because the capability is local and owes nothing to the
other repository.


WHAT THE CONSTRAINT DECIDED

109 of the 114 micro zones have no photograph, and there is no stock library
here. A photo led format would therefore reuse about thirteen pictures across
a hundred and fourteen videos, which is exactly the generated filler worth
avoiding.

So the zone format is fully typographic. It needs no imagery at all, which
means all 114 are buildable rather than five. The constraint chose the format
rather than limiting it.

Cards are different: 90 finished illustrations exist, so that format is image
led and the captions carry the copy the printed boxes hold.


NO VOICEOVER, AND THAT IS A DECISION

The two available voices are Microsoft David and Zira, which are the older
concatenative voices rather than the current neural ones. They are robotic
enough to make a careful brand sound like a scam advert. Short form is watched
muted anyway, so the captions are the content rather than an accessibility
afterthought, and silence is the normal format rather than a compromise.

The captions are the highlighted kind you asked for: a phrase on screen with
each word lighting up in turn, in the brand terracotta rather than the usual
yellow, because yellow is the fastest way to look like every other generated
video.


THE TWO VIDEOS

Entryway, Landing Zone. 30 seconds, typographic, every word from the manual's
own content file: the zone's purpose, four things that make done checkable,
the passes in method order with the six S spine filling in, and the call.

Key Station, card EM-003. 19 seconds over the card's own illustration, with
the copy transcribed off the printed card.


FOUR THINGS I GOT WRONG AND FIXED BY LOOKING

The card front made a poor background. It is designed for print at two and a
half by three and a half inches, so its boxes are unreadable on a phone and my
caption landed on top of the callout legend.

A landscape photograph placed whole filled about forty percent of a vertical
frame and the rest read as dead blur.

Every typographic beat hugged the top of the frame with six hundred pixels of
nothing beneath it, which reads as a slide that failed to load.

Captions wider than the frame were cut off at both edges and unreadable,
because the subtitle format I chose disables wrapping by default.

None of those show up in an exit code. All four came from extracting a frame
and looking at it, which is now part of the build.


WHAT IS NEXT, WITHOUT WAITING

Batch rendering the remaining zones and cards, embedding them on their pages,
and then measuring rather than assuming. I would rather cut ten, watch which
opening line holds attention, and let that decide the other two hundred than
render everything to a format nobody has tested.


ONE THING WORTH YOUR ATTENTION

Card EE-001 is titled AMAZON DELIVERY. A brand name in a card title is ruled
out by the rules we work to, and it is the second instance after the Amazon
logo found in EP-005's artwork. Both need a decision: rename and regenerate,
or leave and accept the risk. I would rename.
