# 6S Success Home Quest
## Idea Analysis and Monetization Plan

Version 1.0 · August 31, 2026

## Executive conclusion

Home Quest is a commercially promising idea with a credible path to a differentiated consumer product. Its strongest asset is not the quantity of content, the timer, or gamification. It is a complete transformation system that reduces a stressful home problem to one bounded action and then protects the result through Standardize and Sustain.

The idea should proceed, but not as a subscription-only cleaning app. That market already contains inexpensive, mature chore schedulers. Home Quest should become the free engagement engine for a broader 6S Success product system:

1. A genuinely useful free app that creates trust and repeat use.
2. A paid household membership for sync, collaboration, history, guidance, and sustain automation.
3. Digital room packs, printable cards, standards, and guided programs sold on the website.
4. Contextual affiliate commerce linked to verified needs in each micro zone.
5. Physical card products and room kits after demand is proven.
6. Premium virtual and in-home services for the small percentage wanting hands-on help.

The recommended goal is $20,000 in monthly gross revenue by the end of year one, generated across several streams. Requiring the app subscription alone to reach that goal would create excessive pressure to overprice, over-gate, and over-notify users.

## 1. The commercial idea

### Customer problem

Most home-organization products solve only part of the problem:

- Cleaning apps schedule recurring chores.
- Decluttering books teach principles.
- Organizers provide expensive hands-on help.
- Storage retailers sell containers before the household has decided what should remain.
- Habit apps track behavior but do not define a safe, functional home standard.

Home Quest combines the missing pieces:

- 20 rooms divided into 114 manageable micro zones.
- Six ordered actions for every zone.
- 684 bounded cards.
- Clear definitions of function and done.
- Safety as a protected fourth step.
- Standardize and Sustain after the visible cleanup.
- A low-friction card draw that avoids planning paralysis.
- A path from free guidance to products, print materials, memberships, and services.

### Core value proposition

> Turn the part of your home that is fighting you into one small job you can finish, then build a standard that keeps it from sliding back.

### Best initial audiences

Prioritize these audiences in order:

1. Busy adults and couples, roughly 25 to 50, who are dissatisfied with recurring clutter but do not identify as naturally organized.
2. Families who need visible shared standards rather than one person remembering everything.
3. Neurodivergent adults and users experiencing task-initiation difficulty, approached respectfully and without medical claims.
4. People preparing for a move, new baby, return to work, holiday guests, or another life transition.
5. Homeowners tackling garages, workshops, pantries, and storage zones poorly served by ordinary cleaning schedules.
6. Professional organizers and Lean practitioners as a later professional tier.

### Jobs to be done

- When the whole room feels overwhelming, give me one useful action I can start now.
- When I have a free hour, guide me through the right sequence so I do not create rework.
- When my household keeps putting things in different places, help us agree on one visible standard.
- When I buy organizing products, tell me what the completed system actually requires.
- When a room was fixed once but keeps drifting, help us identify why and redesign the standard.

## 2. Competitive position

Home Quest should not compete primarily on recurring cleaning schedules. Tody currently advertises a free solo core, a $9.99 annual Premium plan, and household plans from $25 to $80 per year depending on size. Sweepy emphasizes automated daily schedules, shared chores, and game-like household competition. These are strong products with years of category learning.

Home Quest can occupy a different category position:

| Category dimension | Typical chore app | Home Quest position |
|---|---|---|
| Primary unit | Recurring task | Micro-zone transformation |
| Main question | What needs cleaning today? | What is one finished state I can create now? |
| Method | User-created or generic task list | Complete six-step 6S method |
| Decluttering | Usually weak | Sort is the required first step |
| Safety | Often another task | Protected fourth-S interlock |
| Definition of done | Task checked | Visible functional standard |
| Long-term mechanism | Recurrence schedule | Standard, trigger, audit, redesign |
| Commerce moment | General recommendations | Need identified inside a specific zone |
| Emotional promise | Keep up with chores | Reduce friction and build lasting calm |

### Defensibility

The moat is the integrated system, not the software framework:

- Canonical 20-room, 114-zone, 684-card content architecture.
- Detailed functions, hazards, target states, standards, and sustain triggers.
- Product-to-zone mappings.
- Consistent digital, printable, physical, educational, and service products.
- Before/after and holding-state data that improves recommendations over time.
- Phil Kling's authentic Lean Six Sigma expertise.

Competitors can copy a random-task button. Reproducing a safe, coherent, cross-format home operating system is substantially harder.

## 3. Monetization principles

1. Free must produce a real outcome. Do not offer a useless teaser.
2. Charge for durable leverage, not essential instructions.
3. Do not sell a container before Sort establishes the need.
4. Never place safety guidance, accessibility, export, deletion, or earned progress behind payment.
5. Paywalls should follow a moment of demonstrated value, ideally after a completed card or first completed zone.
6. Monetize household coordination and longitudinal value because those create ongoing costs and recurring value.
7. Keep AI optional. It can justify a higher tier, but it should not become the entire product identity.
8. Avoid ads. Home-interior photos and family routines make surveillance advertising strategically incompatible with the brand.
9. Use multiple revenue streams so no single feature must carry the business.
10. Measure zones holding, not screen time.

## 4. Recommended product and pricing ladder

### Level 1: Home Quest Free

Price: $0, no advertising.

Include:

- All 684 core cards across 114 zones.
- Random draw, room run, and one-S room pass.
- Timer and interruption recovery.
- Local progress and zone-holding status.
- Basic standard and sustain trigger.
- Limited local before/after photos.
- Backup, export, and web-to-mobile import.
- Basic local reminders.
- Full safety and accessibility features.

Why this much should remain free:

- The current web app already establishes this expectation.
- A broad free product supports SEO, referrals, word of mouth, affiliate commerce, and digital-product sales.
- The user needs to experience a visible win before trusting a new home system.
- Tody has established a meaningful free benchmark.

### Level 2: Home Quest Plus

Recommended launch price:

- $5.99 monthly.
- $39.99 annually.
- Founding annual price: $29.99 for the first qualified cohort, renewable at that price while continuously subscribed.
- A 14-day trial is acceptable only after the user has completed at least one meaningful action. Test against no-card-required annual discount rather than assuming a trial wins.

Include:

- Secure cross-device backup and sync.
- One household with up to five adult members or adult-managed player profiles.
- Zone ownership and cooperative household progress.
- Unlimited reset generations and before/after history within reasonable storage limits.
- Advanced sustain reminders and audit scheduling.
- Calm Track, House Passport, cooperative Reset Runs, and selected game modes.
- Share-card and printable-standard generation.
- Advanced insights about holding, drift, and unfinished zones.
- A modest monthly allowance of optional AI Sort Plans and Coach Reviews after the AI system is ready.
- Member discounts on selected 6S digital products.

Pricing rationale:

- It is above low-cost solo chore plans because it includes a complete transformation system and household features.
- It remains close to the current $40 family benchmark from Tody.
- Annual pricing reduces churn and matches the long-term nature of home maintenance.
- At the current U.S. small-developer fee structure, modeling approximately 15 percent store commission is reasonable, subject to enrollment and current store rules.

### Level 4: Physical card products

Do not manufacture the entire system immediately. Validate demand with print-on-demand samples and preorders.

Recommended ladder:

| Physical product | Target retail | Target landed cost | Target contribution before marketing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Entryway Starter Deck | $24 | $7 to $10 | $14 to $17 |
| Room Deck | $29 | $9 to $12 | $17 to $20 |

Use the leaner Card System v3 architecture. A roughly 298-card complete system is more manufacturable and understandable than repeating six near-identical method cards for every zone.

Preorder gate before a meaningful print run:

- At least 500 qualified waitlist subscribers for the product.
- At least 100 refundable deposits or preorders.
- Delivered sample passes usability, legibility, durability, and safety review.
- Landed unit economics support at least 65 percent gross margin on direct sales or a viable wholesale margin if retail distribution is planned.

Realm Reset should remain a separate product exploration. It can expand the brand, but it should not delay the mobile core or be counted in the first-year base forecast.

### Level 5: Contextual affiliate commerce

The product library already provides a strong foundation: each micro zone can identify the type of tool or container required, why it is useful, the proper quantity, storage location, and safety considerations.

Recommended experience:

1. User completes Sort.
2. App identifies a genuine unmet need during Straighten, Shine, Safety, or Standardize.
3. The product sheet first offers "use what you own."
4. It then provides good, better, and best options from more than one retailer.
5. Links carry clear affiliate disclosure.
6. Price and availability are verified or presented without false precision.

Revenue assumptions should stay conservative. Amazon's current standard U.S. rate is approximately 3 percent for many Home, Home Improvement, Furniture, Lawn and Garden, Tools, Baby, and related categories, while Kitchen is listed at 4.5 percent. Target advertises commissions up to 8 percent. Actual mix, exclusions, attribution windows, returns, and program approval will reduce realized revenue.

Never build the financial plan around the maximum advertised rate. Use a blended realized commission assumption of 3.0 to 4.0 percent until actual reporting proves otherwise.

### Level 6: Services

Services create near-term cash while the app audience grows and teach the product team what households struggle with.

Recommended offers:

Do not let low-priced calls consume Phil's calendar. The $49 offer should be highly structured, limited, and useful as a qualification path to the $249 or $599 service.

### Month-12 target mix

| Premium services | One in-home day or equivalent workshop revenue | $1,200 |
| **Total** |  | **$20,282** |

This mix requires meaningful traffic and activation, but it avoids betting the company on one channel.

### Revenue ramp and decision gates

| Period | Exit monthly revenue target | Primary revenue engines | Required proof before increasing spend |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Months 1 to 3 | $1,500 to $3,000 | Digital packs and services | Real purchases, completed zones, repeatable service offer |
| Months 4 to 6 | $4,000 to $7,000 | Digital packs, services, early affiliates, founding Plus | Stable activation, reliable mobile core, qualified paid conversion |
| Months 7 to 9 | $9,000 to $13,000 | Plus growth, catalog expansion, affiliate scale, deck preorder | Acceptable renewal signals, sync quality, physical preorder gate |
| Months 10 to 12 | $16,000 to $20,000+ | Balanced six-stream model | Healthy cohort economics and no trust or quality regression |

Illustrative first-year gross revenue range:

- Conservative: $55,000 to $75,000 if mobile or subscription conversion is delayed.
- Base: $95,000 to $125,000 with Plus launched around midyear and steady digital/service sales.
- Stretch: $145,000 to $175,000 if organic traffic, paid conversion, and physical preorders all outperform.

Do not treat the stretch case as the operating budget. Commit recurring costs against the conservative case and release marketing or inventory funds only as the gates above are achieved.

### Subscription math

At $39.99 annually:

- Gross recognized monthly per annual subscriber: approximately $3.33.
- After a modeled 15 percent store fee: approximately $2.83 before backend, support, AI, taxes, and refunds.
- 1,800 active annual-equivalent subscribers produce about $6,000 gross or $5,100 after store commission.

If 2.5 percent of activated free users convert, 1,800 active subscribers requires roughly 72,000 activated users accumulated and retained across cohorts. That is demanding but possible with a strong web funnel, app-store acquisition, print products, and creator partnerships. A 1 percent conversion rate would require 180,000 activated users, so improving product activation matters far more than small price changes.

### Unit economics guardrails

| Metric | Initial target | Intervention threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| Free install to first card started | 60%+ | Below 40% |
| First card start to completion | 55%+ | Below 35% |
| Activated user to first zone reset within 7 days | 25%+ | Below 15% |
| First zone reset to Plus trial/start | 4% to 8% | Below 2% |
| Activated user to paid by day 35 | 2.5%+ target | Below 1.5% |
| Annual-plan share of new subscribers | 65%+ | Below 45% |
| Annual renewal | 55%+ initial target | Below 40% |
| Refund rate | Under 5% | Above 8% |
| AI cost as share of Plus net revenue | Under 12% | Above 20% |
| Paid acquisition payback | Under 9 months | Above 12 months |
| Digital-product gross margin | 90%+ | Below 80% |
| Physical direct-sale gross margin | 65%+ | Below 55% |

RevenueCat's broad subscription reporting suggests higher-value apps can outperform very low-priced products on download-to-paid conversion, but subscription markets are unforgiving and AI-heavy subscriptions can suffer weaker long-term retention. Home Quest should therefore use AI as a supporting capability and make durable household value the reason to renew.

## 6. The conversion funnel

### Stage 1: Discover

Acquisition sources:

- Search pages for all 114 micro zones.
- Before/after case studies.
- YouTube and short-form demonstrations.
- LinkedIn content anchored in Phil's Lean expertise.
- Pinterest content for visual home problems.
- App Store and Google Play search.
- Partnerships with professional organizers, cleaners, occupational therapists where appropriate, parenting creators, and Lean practitioners.
- Free Entryway deck and useful calculators/checklists.

Primary promise: "Pick one small job and make the space easier to live in."

Do not lead with "684 cards." The number proves depth after the user understands the benefit, but it can feel like 684 chores at first contact.

### Stage 2: Activate

Activation should take less than 60 seconds:

1. Select the room you are in, choose available time, or draw anything.
2. Receive one card.
3. Start without an account.
4. Complete or stop without punishment.

Primary activation event: first card completed.

Stronger activation event: first standard or sustain trigger established.

### Stage 3: Demonstrate value

The best paywall moment is after one of these events:

- First full zone reset.
- First before/after comparison.
- User attempts to sync to another device.
- User invites another adult.
- User wants reset history or an advanced sustain schedule.

Show exactly what Plus preserves or enables. Do not interrupt active work with a paywall.

### Stage 4: Convert

Recommended paywall order:

1. Annual founding offer or standard annual plan.
2. Monthly alternative.
3. Restore purchase and "continue free" in equally understandable positions.

Test messaging based on durable outcomes:

- Keep your household in sync.
- Keep every before-and-after generation.
- Give each zone an owner and a rhythm.
- See what is holding and what needs redesign.

Avoid generic claims such as "unlock your best life."

### Stage 5: Expand revenue

After demonstrated usage:

- Offer a relevant digital guided program.
- Show product suggestions only when a need exists.
- Offer a virtual reset after repeated drift or a stalled high-friction room.
- Invite successful users to physical-deck preorders.

### Stage 6: Retain

Renewal must come from continuing value:

- Household coordination.
- Reset history.
- Audit and drift insights.
- New guided programs.
- Seasonal room plans.
- A home that measurably holds better.

Do not manufacture daily activity. A well-designed home may require less app use over time. Use meaningful monthly and seasonal value instead of optimizing daily opens.

## 7. Go-to-market plan

### Phase 0: Validate willingness to pay, weeks 1 to 6

- Keep the current web Quest free.
- Add privacy-safe funnel measurement.
- Interview 15 to 25 users after real card usage.
- Offer the Entryway, Pantry, and Family Launch digital packs.
- Test $19, $24, and $29 pricing for the Whole-Home Print Pack using distinct cohorts, not deceptive personalized pricing.
- Create a Plus waitlist with a clear feature concept and price range.
- Offer five paid virtual room resets and document the objections and outcomes.

Exit evidence:

- At least 100 users complete a card.
- At least 25 complete a full zone.
- At least 10 digital purchases or a 2 percent purchase rate from qualified product-page visitors.
- At least five paid service sessions.
- Clear evidence of which Plus capability creates willingness to pay.

### Phase 1: Mobile core and revenue foundation, months 2 to 4

- Release storeworthy free iOS and Android apps.
- Implement anonymous local use, export/import, and full content.
- Add website digital-product ladder.
- Add properly disclosed affiliate links to the highest-intent 20 zones first.
- Establish subscription analytics and store enrollment for eligible reduced commission programs.
- Keep Plus behind a waitlist or beta until sync and household value are reliable.

Exit evidence:

- Crash-free and migration gates met.
- First-card completion above 40 percent.
- At least 20 percent of activated users complete a zone within seven days.
- Affiliate clicks show relevance without reducing completion.

### Phase 2: Plus launch, months 4 to 7

- Launch sync, household ownership, history, sustain scheduling, and share/print outputs.
- Offer founding annual price to qualified early users.
- Run a staged rollout and monitor refunds, support, sync conflicts, and notification opt-outs.
- Add optional AI only after the non-AI membership has retention value.

Exit evidence:

- At least 2.5 percent activated-to-paid conversion or strong evidence explaining the gap.
- Majority annual-plan mix.
- AI costs within guardrail.
- Plus users retain materially better because of real value, not excessive notifications.

### Phase 3: Product-system expansion, months 7 to 12

- Expand the digital-program catalog based on actual room demand.
- Launch Entryway physical deck preorder.
- Add contextual affiliate coverage across the 114 zones after product-data quality checks.
- Establish creator and professional-organizer partnerships.
- Productize the $599 three-session service.
- Test a professional organizer tier without building it into the household interface prematurely.

## 8. Marketing economics

### Organic-first strategy

The economics are strongest when the free content and web app produce acquisition:

- Each micro-zone page targets a specific problem and can lead directly to one relevant free card.
- Before/after examples provide high-intent visual proof.
- Every digital pack can introduce the app.
- The app can refer users to relevant articles, packs, products, and services without becoming a catalog.

### Paid acquisition rule

Do not scale paid app-install campaigns until:

- Activation is stable.
- Paid conversion is proven.
- Cohort retention and refunds are known.
- Contribution LTV exceeds customer acquisition cost by at least three times.
- Payback is under nine months.

At a $39.99 annual plan with approximately $34 after a modeled 15 percent store fee, an initial subscription-only CAC ceiling might be around $10 to $12. Blended downstream digital, affiliate, and service revenue can raise that ceiling, but only after cohort data proves it.

### Referral system

Use non-manipulative referral rewards:

- Referrer and invited household receive a premium guided room pack or one additional month after the invitee completes a first zone.
- Do not reward raw invites.
- Never publish private progress or contact lists.

## 9. Analytics and operating dashboard

### North-star measure

Active households with at least one zone verified as holding its standard during the period.

### Funnel measures

- Visitor to app start.
- Install to first card drawn.
- Card drawn to started.
- Started to completed.
- First zone reset.
- Standard created.
- Sustain trigger set.
- Audit due and completed.
- Holding versus drift.
- Qualified paywall view.
- Trial or purchase.
- Renewal, downgrade, cancellation, and refund.
- Digital-product attach.
- Affiliate product-sheet view, outbound click, and reported conversion.
- Service lead and purchase.

### Required segmentation

Keep segmentation minimal and privacy-safe:

- Platform.
- Acquisition channel.
- New versus returning.
- Solo versus household, when known.
- Free versus paid.
- Broad room category.
- Accessibility settings only when the platform provides behavior needed to render correctly, not as a marketing profile.

Never capture photo contents, household names, user-written standards, addresses, precise inventories, or sensitive room details in analytics.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Looks like another chore app | Crowded, low-priced category | Own micro-zone transformation and holding standards |
| Free product is too complete to convert | Users may not need Plus | Charge for household leverage, history, sync, automation, and guided depth |
| Paywall damages trust | Current web app promises free access | Preserve core free value and paywall after demonstrated outcome |
| 684 cards feel overwhelming | Depth can look like obligation | Lead with one card and available time, reveal system depth later |
| Affiliate experience becomes spammy | Undermines method and trust | Show products only after a verified need, with retailer choice and disclosure |
| AI costs or novelty churn | Weakens margins and retention | Meter AI, include allowance, preserve manual core, optimize for household value |
| Physical inventory consumes cash | Manufacturing risk | Samples, deposits, preorder thresholds, lean card architecture |
| Services do not scale | Phil becomes the bottleneck | Standardize offers, cap availability, train partners later |
| Safety advice creates liability | Home tasks include genuine hazards | Expert content review, clear limits, escalation and do-not-attempt guidance |
| Children and photos increase privacy risk | Sensitive home context | Adult accounts, private default, metadata minimization, no ad tracking |
| Successful sustain reduces app opens | Conventional engagement can fall | Measure holding zones and seasonal value, not daily screen time |

## 11. What not to monetize

- Basic six-S instructions.
- Safety cards and warnings.
- Accessibility functions.
- Data export and account deletion.
- Restore purchases.
- User-earned progress.
- Essential offline use.
- The right to decline AI.
- Notifications required to avoid an artificial penalty.
- Household shame, competition, or fear.
- User photos or inferred household data.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with the iOS and Android apps, but build Home Quest as the engagement center of the entire 6S Success ecosystem. Preserve the complete free method. Monetize the things that make the result more durable, collaborative, guided, tangible, and convenient.

The highest-priority business experiment is not an AI feature or a large card print run. It is proving that users who complete one zone will pay roughly $40 per year for household sync, history, ownership, and sustain support. In parallel, sell digital guided programs and virtual resets immediately. Those channels can generate early revenue and reveal what the subscription must do well.

If Home Quest can reliably help users create and maintain a visible standard, the product has a stronger commercial position than a chore scheduler. It becomes a home operating system, supported by software, cards, products, education, and expert help.
