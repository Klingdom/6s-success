# The deck system

**Owner:** product-manager. **Written 2026-09-04.** Every number in section 1
was measured today against Stripe, the analytics database, the live site, or
the repository. Where a number is a hypothesis it says so and carries its
evidence tier from `CLAUDE.md` section 15.

This document decides what a 6S Success deck **is**, specifies the next one,
and says how it is sold. It does not create a SKU, a Stripe object, or a price.

---

## 1. The tension, and the answer

Phil asked for new and improved micro zone decks with detailed images and
instructions, to market and sell. `REVENUE-REVIEW-2026-09-04.md` says: do not
add products, 159 SKUs for a business with one lifetime sale is a warehouse.

Both are right, and they are not actually in conflict, because the review is
about **SKUs** and the request is about **an artefact**. The answer is to build
one deck, make it a different kind of object from anything in the catalogue,
and have it retire more SKUs than it adds.

### 1.1 What the arithmetic actually says

Measured: $19.00 gross all time, one charge, 2026-08-21. 52 visitors in the
last 30 days. 0 email subscribers. 1 visit from a search engine in 30 days.

At a 2% conversion rate (an industry benchmark, **not our measurement**, we
have one sale and it supports no conversion estimate at all):

| Price | Sales/month for $20k | Visitors/month | vs 52 today |
|---|---|---|---|
| $4 zone pack | 5,000 | 250,000 | 4,808x |
| $12 printable deck | 1,667 | 83,333 | 1,603x |
| $19 whole house pack | 1,053 | 52,632 | 1,012x |
| $39 physical deck | 513 | 25,641 | **493x** |
| $250 virtual consult | 80 | 4,000 | 77x |
| $1,200 in-home reset | 17 | 833 | 16x |

A $39 physical deck looks nine times better than a $4 pack. On **contribution**
it is worse than that table suggests. A 72 card deck with a tuck box, printed
on demand at low volume, costs roughly $9 to $18 a unit (a market range from
published print-on-demand pricing, **not a quote we hold**). Add card fees of
about $1.43 and unrecovered shipping of about $5 and contribution is near
$18.50. Twenty thousand dollars of contribution is then **1,077 decks a month,
about 53,800 visitors, 1,036x today**, worse than the gross figure, and it
comes with pick, pack, returns and stock.

**So: a deck cannot be the revenue engine at any price we could honestly
charge.** Saying otherwise would be the kind of fabricated demand `CLAUDE.md`
section 8 forbids.

### 1.2 What a deck can do that nothing else in the catalogue does

The one measured chain in `GOALS.md` is
`STRANGER -> VISITOR -> ENGAGED -> SUBSCRIBER -> CUSTOMER`. The constraint is
the first link, and the **second** worst number in the business is the third
one: 52 visitors produced **zero** subscribers. Every arrival is currently
spent and thrown away.

A deck is the best instrument we own for that link, because it is the only
thing in the catalogue a person will trade an email address for and then
actually use in front of other people.

The chain, with the hypotheses labelled:

- 42 consults at $250 plus 8 in-home resets at $1,200 is $20,100 a month from
  50 transactions. *(arithmetic, from the review)*
- If a list converts at 1.5% a month to a $250 consult, 42 consults needs
  about **2,800 subscribers**. *(tier 8, informed hypothesis, no measurement)*
- If 30% of visitors download the deck and 40% of those leave an email, 100
  visitors becomes 12 subscribers. *(tier 8, both numbers)*
- 2,800 subscribers is then about **23,300 cumulative visitors**. At 52 a
  month that is 37 years. At O1's own target of 500 a week it is **about 11
  months.**

**The deck is a bet on O1 succeeding, not an alternative to it.** That is the
honest sentence. If traffic never moves, the deck earns nothing, and so does
everything else.

One consequence worth stating plainly: on those hypotheses a single email
address is worth more than a single $4 pack sale, and it costs the customer
nothing. That is why the deck should be free and the pack should not exist.

### 1.3 The recommendation

1. **Build one deck, the Kitchen, at 72 cards.** Not 113. Not another
   Entryway.
2. **Make the deck a diagnostic instrument, not a content pack.** Section 2.
3. **Price the print-at-home PDF at $0**, with the eight card sampler free and
   ungated and the full 72 behind an email address. That converts the deck from
   a giveaway into the only working link in the chain.
4. **Do not create a physical SKU yet.** Specify it, cost it, and hold it until
   the free deck has produced measurable downloads. A physical product with
   stock and returns, sold to 52 visitors, is the warehouse mistake again with
   a nicer box.
5. **Retire more than you add.** See section 9. The Kitchen deck adds zero or
   one SKU and supersedes eight.

## 2. What a deck is, and what it is not

Today a buyer cannot tell these apart, and that is our fault, not theirs:

| Thing | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| The Home Quest | 684 cards in the browser, 114 zones by six passes | free |
| The Whole House Print Pack | the same 684 cards on paper | $19 |
| A Micro Zone Pack | 6 of those 684 cards, one zone | $4 |
| The Entryway Deck | 88 illustrated cards, a different taxonomy, no rules | free |

The first three are the same content at three sizes. The $4 pack is a subset of
the $19 pack, which is a print of the free app. The Entryway deck is the only
genuinely different artefact and it is the one we charge nothing for.

**The distinction, and it goes on both products in one sentence each:**

> **The pack tells you the steps for a zone. The deck works out which zone,
> what is wrong with it, why, and what to do in the next fifteen minutes.**

A pack is a reference. You read it. A deck is an instrument. You deal it,
you argue with it, and it ends with something written down and signed.

Three tests a thing must pass to be called a deck here:

1. **It diagnoses.** There is a path from a symptom to a cause you did not
   already know.
2. **It has a loop.** Cards route to other cards. A pile you read front to back
   is a book cut into pieces.
3. **It ends in a standard.** Something is written, signed, and left in the
   room. If nothing survives the session, nothing was built.

The Entryway deck passes none of the three. That is the honest verdict on the
only deck we have, and it is fixable.

## 3. The card system

Seven types. Each earns its place by doing one job in the loop, and anything
that does not do a job in the loop is cut.

| Type | Job in the loop | Count in a 7-zone deck |
|---|---|---|
| **Room** | the map, the order, and the rules | 1 |
| **Zone** | what this small place is for, and what done looks like | 1 per zone |
| **Friction** | the symptom in the household's own words, and the branch to why | 3 per zone |
| **Root Cause** | the reusable diagnosis, and how to confirm it in 30 seconds | 12 |
| **Action** | the timed thing you do standing up, ending in a victory condition | 2 per zone + 4 |
| **Standard** | the sentence you write, sign, and leave in the room | 1 per zone |
| **Event** | the ordinary hard day that tests whether the standard held | 6 |

### 3.1 What was cut from the Entryway set, and why

- **Skill cards (12).** Sort, Straighten, Visual Control, Point Of Use. These
  are the book's concepts. They belong on the room card's back and in the
  rules, not as a twelfth of the deck.
- **Win / Reward cards (8).** A win you can draw from a pile is a
  participation trophy. The win is now the victory condition printed on the
  action card you actually did.
- **Upgrade cards (12).** Smart Lighting, Mudroom Conversion, Expanded Shoe
  Storage. This is where buying pressure lives, and `CLAUDE.md` section 3 is
  explicit that we do not sell people more stuff because they are trying to
  organise their stuff. Upgrades belong in the affiliate catalogue, after a
  root cause has justified them, never in the base deck.
- **Tool cards (12).** A tool without a diagnosis is shopping. Tools are now
  the `inputs` line on the action card that needs them.

That is 44 cards of the 89 removed, and the deck gets more useful, not less.

### 3.2 Why 72 cards

Two independent economics land on the same number, so it is fixed first and
the content is cut to fit, which is the opposite of how 89 happened.

- Print on demand card decks price in 18 card steps. 72 is a tier. 75 is 90
  with fifteen blanks paid for.
- Print at home, nine to a US Letter sheet: 72 is exactly **eight sheets of
  fronts and eight of backs**. The Entryway deck is twenty sheets and a 25 MB
  PDF, which is a real barrier at a home printer.

## 4. How a player uses it

Printed on the back of the room card, in eight lines:

1. Deal the seven **zone** cards face up. Take the one annoying you today.
2. Lay out that zone's three **friction** cards. Keep the ones true in your
   kitchen, put the rest back.
3. Turn a friction card over. Pick the answer that is true. It names a **root
   cause**.
4. The root cause card names the **action** cards that fix that cause. Take
   the one matching the time you actually have: 5, 15 or 30 minutes.
5. Do it standing up with the card in your hand.
6. Read the **victory condition** out loud. If it is not true yet you have not
   finished. That is the entire scoring system.
7. Fill in the zone's **standard** card with a pen. Put it where the zone is.
8. Draw an **event** card when an ordinary hard day happens, and find out
   whether the standard held.

**Multiplayer, 1 to 6.** Deal the friction cards out and let each person keep
the ones they believe. The disagreement is the useful part and the deck is
designed to surface it rather than resolve it early: `KA-018 The Two Cook
Treaty` exists precisely because two people running one kitchen by two designs
is a design problem, not a character problem.

**Safety is not optional and not a card family.** `KA-016 The Hot Zone Walk` is
done before any rebuild, because rebuilds move heavy and sharp things around.
Its six checks are blades, weight at height, heat next to what burns, water
near electricity, chemicals that react, and raw food above ready-to-eat food.
Every one is quoted from the Manual's own `watch_for` entries. Where a check
fails on gas, a scorch mark, a frayed cord or a burner that will not light, the
card says call a qualified person and does not offer a fix.

## 5. The next deck: the Kitchen

### 5.1 Why the Kitchen and not another room

- **Distribution already exists there and nowhere else.** Of 12 published
  YouTube videos, 7 are Kitchen zones and that room is complete: cooking zone,
  sink, prep counter, upper cabinets, lower cabinets, utensils, fridge. The
  other five are Entryway, which already has a deck. Every other room in the
  house has zero published videos. *(measured from `ops/youtube-published.json`,
  2026-09-04)*
- It is the room with the most micro zones after the garage, and the one where
  the safety content is real rather than decorative.
- Entryway would be a rebuild of an existing free product, which the review
  correctly calls adding nothing.

### 5.2 What is in it

72 cards, built and validated: `ops/cardtext/kitchen-deck.json`.

```
 1  KR-001  Room card, the map and the rules
 7  KZ-001..007  the seven Manual zones, in method order
21  KF-001..021  three frictions per zone, each branching to a root cause
12  KC-001..012  the twelve root causes that occur in a kitchen
18  KA-001..018  14 zone actions plus 4 whole-kitchen actions
 7  KS-001..007  one standard per zone, write-on
 6  KE-001..006  six days that test a kitchen
```

The seven zones are the Manual's seven zones, **verbatim**, and a gate in the
generator asserts it. This matters: the Entryway deck has twelve micro zones
and the Manual's Entryway has five, so a household owning both the free deck
and the $19 pack is taught two different zone lists for the same room. That
cannot happen again by construction.

The twelve root causes, each reachable from at least one friction and treated
by at least one action, both gated: Excess, No Assigned Home, Wrong Location,
Excess Motion, Poor Visibility, Poor Accessibility, Insufficient Capacity,
Missing Standard, Missing Trigger, Safety Constraint, Poor Replenishment,
Conflicting Users.

### 5.3 Where the words come from

Zone purpose, what done looks like, the standard, the trigger, the session
length, the safety checks and the supplies are **quoted from**
`content/manual/source/content.json`, the same file the book, the zone pages,
the Home Quest and the print pack are built from. A gate asserts they are still
character-for-character identical, so the deck cannot drift from the book.

The friction, root cause, action and event layers are hand authored, marked
`"source": "hand authored"` on every card, and grounded in the Manual's own
pass text. Nothing in the deck claims a customer said anything.

### 5.4 What the instructions have to do

A person is standing in their kitchen holding a phone or a card. The contract,
enforced per type:

| Type | Front carries | Back carries |
|---|---|---|
| Zone | purpose, six numbered callouts, tagline | what done looks like, two safety checks, the hard call, session time |
| Friction | the symptom as a household says it out loud | "Why does this happen here?" and three answers, each naming a root cause |
| Root Cause | what it means, in one sentence | how to confirm it in 30 seconds, which S, which actions |
| Action | goal, time, players | inputs, 3 to 5 numbered physical steps, root causes addressed, victory condition, next card |
| Standard | the standard sentence and its trigger | signature lines, date, where this card lives |
| Event | the setup and what it tests | held if, and what to draw if it did not |

Rules that make an instruction acceptable:

- **Every step is a physical movement**, not an intention. "Tip the drawer out
  onto a tray" passes. "Declutter the drawer" does not.
- **Every action card ends in something observable.** "Your largest cutting
  board lies flat with room on both sides, and nothing else is on the run."
- **Numbers are real or absent.** Six tools in the crock, stacks capped at
  three, plate stacks about six high, no glass above head height. Where a
  figure is external guidance it is named as such: the fridge card says *the
  usual food safety guidance* is 5C / 41F and -18C / 0F, and says a fridge that
  will not hold it is a repair call.
- **No moralising.** `CLAUDE.md` section 10: an ordinary household problem is
  not a character defect. The junk drawer card says the drawer has never been
  given a job.
- **Where the fix is a professional's, say so and stop.** Gas, scorch marks,
  frayed cords.

## 6. The art brief

**Blocked, verified today.** The Gemini image endpoint returns HTTP 429,
`generate_content_free_tier_requests, limit: 0`, pending billing. So the
deliverable this cycle is the queue, not the pictures.

**It is built and it is on disk:** `build/prompts/kitchen/`, 72 prompt files
plus `ALL.md` and `index.json`, produced by `ops/build_card_prompts.py --deck
kitchen`, every one carrying the frozen Style Bible prefix and its hash
`3766b13583`. Each names its own output filename, so a generated image drops
into the pipeline with no renaming.

### 6.1 The defect this brief is designed to prevent

The existing deck's art does not depict its own cards. Two examples, both
verified today, both marked `"verdict": "ok"` in `ops/card-hero-verdicts.json`:

- **EM-003 Key Station** prints six callouts, key bowl, key hooks by user,
  outgoing mail slot, daily catch-all tray, visual cue, over a photograph of a
  generic entryway containing **none of them**. There is not a key in the
  picture.
- **EP-001 Lost Keys**, a problem card whose own callouts say *visual clutter,
  piles of items hide what matters* and *floor drop zone*, is illustrated with
  an immaculate, empty, magazine-styled console table.

The review pass that passed these was checking style, not subject. Style was
fine. The picture was of something else.

### 6.2 The fix, per card type

Every card record carries an `art` object with four fields, and the generator
refuses to build the deck if any is missing:

- `subject`, the scene as a list of countable nouns, not a mood. "A key
  station" is a phrase. "A small dish holding keys, a row of four hooks, a
  shallow tray" is something a model can draw and a reviewer can count.
- `must_show`, the things that have to be separately visible.
- `must_show_kind`, `objects` or `condition`. Zone and room cards list objects
  to count. Every other type states a condition to satisfy, because handing a
  sentence to a model under the heading "objects in the frame" asks it to draw
  the sentence, which is how lettering gets into an image that forbade
  lettering.
- `accept_test`, the sentence a reviewer applies. **This is the new part.**

The accept tests, by type:

| Type | Accept test |
|---|---|
| Room | you can point at where each of the seven zones is; if two are out of frame, reshoot |
| Zone | count the six callouts; if any one is not a visible object, reject |
| Friction | the problem is visible and looks like a real Tuesday; a tidy picture on a friction card is a rejection |
| Root Cause | one idea, one object group; if it could illustrate three different causes, reject |
| Action | the finished state the victory condition describes, not the work and not a person |
| Standard | the lower half is quiet enough to print three blank write-on lines over and still read |
| Event | the pressure is visible in the objects, since there are no people to show it |

Two constraints are baked into every prompt and gated:

- **No lettering, ever.** All card wording is set as real type by the template.
  Where a callout is inherently textual, "a name and a date on every lid" , 
  the subject asks for *a small blank white label with no writing on it*, and
  the generator asserts no subject contains "written", "text", "label
  reading", "sign saying", "logo" or "brand".
- **No room may name another room.** The frozen prefix says "entryway" and it
  is substituted, never inherited. This gate fired for real during this work:
  my own friction accept-test mentioned the Entryway deck by name and 21
  prompts were correctly refused until it was removed.

### 6.3 Cost and running it

72 images at the current provider's $0.045 to $0.10 is **$3.24 to $7.20** for
the whole deck. The moment billing is enabled:

```
python ops/cardtext/build_kitchen_deck.py      # 72 cards, all gates
python ops/build_card_prompts.py --deck kitchen # 72 prompts, style 3766b13583
python ops/generate_card_art.py --room Kitchen --apply
```

Then a human looks at all 72 against the accept test in the table above before
any of them reaches a card. Not a spot check. The last review passed 76 of 88
and two of the ones it passed had nothing to do with their card.

## 7. How it is marketed and sold

Grounded in the review's own proposals, in its own priority order.

### 7.1 Channel

**Primary: YouTube, because it is the only channel where the Kitchen already
has an audience surface.** Seven Kitchen zone videos are live. Each one gets
the deck in the description and in the last ten seconds: *the whole kitchen on
72 cards, free, no account.* This costs nothing, needs no new asset, and it is
the review's proposal 4.3 pointed at a product instead of at nothing.

**Second: Etsy, printable instant download.** The review's 4.1. Etsy's
organisation-printable category has buyers searching now, and a 72 card
diagnostic deck is a better listing than a 6 card zone pack because it is a
whole product rather than a fragment. Needs a seller account, which is Phil's.
**Etsy comparables could not be verified, Etsy blocks automated access, so
any volume estimate here would be invented, and there is none in this
document.**

**Not: paid acquisition, and not a launch.** At 52 visitors a month there is
nothing to launch to, and the review's own registry computes 1,427 days to
A/B significance. Make changes that are correct on their merits.

### 7.2 Price

| Form | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen sampler, 8 cards (room + 7 zones) | **free, no email** | proves the format in the room the videos already cover |
| Full deck, 72 cards, print at home | **free, for an email address** | the only working link in the chain; see 1.2 |
| Physical printed deck | **specified, not listed** | hold until downloads are measurable; section 1.1 |
| The consult it qualifies for | **$250, already live** | this is where the goal is reachable |

The email gate is a fair trade stated plainly, not a dark pattern: the sampler
is genuinely useful on its own and there is no countdown, no fake scarcity and
no hidden billing anywhere near it.

### 7.3 To whom

The household that already cooks and already knows their kitchen is not
working. Not "people who want to be organised". The deck's opening move is a
friction card in their own words, *I wipe it on Sunday and by Tuesday it is
gone again*, because recognition is the only thing that earns the next thirty
seconds.

### 7.4 What would have to be true

Listed so it can be checked rather than believed:

1. **O1 has to work.** 500 visitors a week, not 12. Without it the arithmetic
   in 1.2 is 37 years. *This is the load-bearing assumption and it is not ours
   to assume away.*
2. **The email capture has to exist.** Listmonk currently returns HTTP 500 and
   there are 0 subscribers. A gated deck with no list is a deck with a broken
   button. **This is a hard dependency: do not ship the gate before the list
   works.**
3. **Downloads have to convert to emails at some non-trivial rate.** 40% is a
   hypothesis with no evidence. The first 100 downloads measure it, and if it
   comes in under about 10% the gate is wrong and the deck should go back to
   ungated.
4. **A deck holder has to be more likely to book a consult than a stranger.**
   Unmeasured. The deck ends in a written standard and a photograph, which is
   exactly the artefact a consult starts from, so the hypothesis is reasonable
   and it is still a hypothesis.
5. **The art has to actually get generated.** Blocked on billing. A deck whose
   card fronts are stock photographs of somebody else's kitchen is the product
   we already have.

### 7.5 How we will know

Events to instrument, and none of them need personal data:

`deck_sampler_download`, `deck_full_download`, `deck_email_captured`,
`card_type_viewed` (gallery), `action_card_started`, `victory_confirmed`,
`consult_page_from_deck`, `consult_booked_from_deck`.

The one number that decides whether this was worth doing:
**subscribers per 100 visitors**, baseline **0.0**.

## 9. The SKU rule this deck introduces

> **No new deck ships unless it retires at least as many SKUs as it adds.**

For the Kitchen deck, proposed to `commerce-manager`, who owns the catalogue
and the payment links:

- **Adds:** 0 SKUs as a free download. 1 if a physical deck is later listed.
- **Supersedes:** the 7 Kitchen micro zone packs at $4
  (`ZP-KITCHE-*`, $28 of catalogue) and `RP-KITCHEN` at $9. The deck contains
  all seven zones with more per zone than any of them.
- **Net: 159 SKUs to 151.**

**Caution, and it is not theoretical.** `CLAUDE.md` section 0.3 records that
retiring a superseded payment link was correct for the repository and took a
live buy button down, because the deployed site was older than the repository.
Any retirement here must be verified against what the live site is serving,
not against `data.js`, and staged: remove from navigation first, watch, then
retire the link.
