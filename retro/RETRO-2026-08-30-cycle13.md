# Retrospective, thirteenth cycle ending 2026-08-30

Two commits. One change, and the most useful thing about it was the mistake it
nearly made.

---

## What went well

**73 KB of product catalogue stopped shipping to 172 pages that never open
it.** Every zone page, every room page and every article fetched and parsed the
whole shop for a variable nothing on them reads. Those are the three page types
organic traffic actually lands on, so it was the worst possible place for it.

**The removal was safe because something else was already careful.** site.js
opens with `window.CATALOG || []`, so it builds an empty index and carries on.
That guard is the entire reason this is a deletion rather than a rewrite, and
the script now checks the guard is still present rather than remembering that
it was.

**The near miss is the useful part.** The first version pruned cart.html. That
page renders its lines from rows snapshotted when they were added, so it would
have looked completely fine, and its markup happens never to mention CATALOG by
name. But Cart.add resolves a SKU through the catalogue, and a commerce page is
the wrong place to save 73 KB against a maybe. I caught it by listing which
pages kept the script and noticing cart.html was not among them, which took one
extra command.

**Conservative by construction.** A page keeps the catalogue on any sign it
might render a product. A false positive costs 73 KB. A false negative is an
empty shop.

---

## What did not go well

**My "broad on purpose" detector was not broad enough, and I still called it
conservative in the code comment before testing it.** The comment said a false
negative costs an empty shop, and the very first run produced one. Writing the
right principle into a comment is not the same as implementing it.

**One change in a cycle.** Verifying this properly took most of the time:
tracing what cart.html renders from, what Cart.add depends on, what site.js
does without a catalogue. That was the right way to spend it, and it is also
why nothing else got done.

---

## What to change next cycle

**Check the keep list, not just the remove list.** Both are outputs of the same
decision and only one of them was printed by default. What a filter keeps is
the half that can hurt somebody.

**The outage is five days old tomorrow.** Every cycle since it was found has
shipped work that cannot reach anybody, which is the correct thing to do while
it is blocked, but it is worth naming plainly rather than quietly continuing.
