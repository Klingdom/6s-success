# Retrospective, fourteenth cycle ending 2026-08-30

Two commits. The site now links to its own addresses, which it had not been
doing 2,704 times.

---

## What went well

**The canonical split was worse than the audit described and simpler to fix
than it looked.** 166 pages declared an extensionless canonical and then linked
the .html form 2,704 times. The site told every crawler "the real address has
no extension" and then, on every page, linked the other one. Nothing broke and
nothing complained, because nginx serves either form, which is exactly why it
survived this long.

**The analytics consequence is the one that mattered most.** Umami stores the
path verbatim, so an organic visitor landing on /zones/foo and an internal
visitor arriving at /zones/foo.html were two rows for the same page. Every
per-page number would have been split by an arbitrary ratio, on the analytics
this business is about to depend on to decide whether a traffic push worked.

**One pass rather than 26 edits.** The .html links come from hard coded strings
across five generators. Rewriting them there would have been 26 separate
changes with 26 chances to typo a path. A single post-processing pass is one
thing to verify, and it verifies itself: every rewritten link was resolved
against the filesystem the way nginx resolves it, all 3,203, zero
unresolvable.

**It refuses to touch a link whose target does not exist.** A typo in a
generator stays a visible broken link rather than becoming a broken link in a
new shape. That distinction matters because the pass would otherwise quietly
launder mistakes into a form that looks deliberate.

---

## What did not go well

**I nearly reached for nginx again.** A 301 from .html to extensionless was the
first idea, and it would have worked. But the .html form is now unreachable
except by direct guess, external inbound links are close to zero, and I cannot
test an nginx config on this machine. Doing both would have been belt and
braces bought with the one risk I have no way to measure. Deciding not to was
right, and I only got there by asking what the redirect would actually catch
that the rewrite does not.

**Two commits again, and both structural.** Nothing shipped this cycle that a
visitor would notice, other than the pages being correct. That is the fifth
cycle in a row where the work is real and invisible, because the deploy has not
happened.

---

## What to change next cycle

**Ask what a second fix would catch that the first does not.** That question is
what stopped the redirect, and it would have stopped at least two of the
belt-and-braces changes I have made in earlier cycles.

**The remaining audit items are getting thin.** Breadcrumb markup on 29
articles, an Organization logo, a custom 404, and stable anchors for answer
engines. After those, the honest position is that everything I can do without
the deploy or without Phil's decisions is done, and saying so is better than
finding smaller and smaller things.
