## Identity strings (frozen; copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)
- **Title:** The Entryway
- **Subtitle:** The entryway is the smallest room in the house and the one that sets the tone for every other, because it is the first thing you touch coming in and the last thing you fight on the way out. This playbook points the whole 6S loop at it, one small zone at a time, until the door simply works: coats land, shoes stop tracking grit through the house, keys are always where your hand reaches, and the paper that used to breed on the console leaves again with a verdict instead of moving in.
- **Masthead:** "Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks" / "Chapter Thirty-One"
- **The rule (frozen):** You do not 6S a whole room. You 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done. A room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, and a zone is a job you can finish in an afternoon.
- **The five zones (frozen):** The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. Do them one at a time and the room takes a few short sessions, not a lost weekend.
- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished entryway is one where everyone can land and launch in seconds: keys and phone drop into one tray, today's coat and shoes have an easy home, wet things stay on the mat, and the paper that comes through the door leaves again with a verdict. Nothing lives on the floor inside the door's swing, and two hooks always stand empty for whoever arrives next.
- **The first-and-last reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The entryway is the room you spend the least time in and notice the most, because you cross it every single time you come or go. A calm doorway is the first thing that greets you home and the last thing that speeds you out, which is why the smallest room in the house pays back a 6S loop faster than any other room you own.
- **The count-by-weather reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Coats are counted by weather, not by number. Each person gets one coat per kind of weather your climate genuinely produces, and a second in the same category has to beat the first on a specific, nameable day. The expensive coat you never wear is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm.
- **The worn-mat reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so you test it instead of looking at it. A finished mat sits with its fibres flattened one way and the grit rides straight over it onto the next pair of shoes. A worn mat is not saving you anything, it is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between the front door and the kitchen.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / count by weather, not by number / land and launch in seconds. Part 9 applies the loop you built in Parts 1 to 8; it does not teach it again.

## Hero device: the room as micro-zones (point the finished loop at one room, one zone at a time)
A room is not one big job but a handful of small, finishable zones. Run the six S's through each zone with its specific things. This is the Micro Zone Manual's core idea, brought into the book as the engine of every Part 9 playbook. Source: the Manual's Entryway zones; grounded in the method chapters it cross-references.

## Motifs
- **Zones, not rooms.** A room is a short list of finishable jobs; face the zone, not the room.
- **The loop, pointed.** The method is done; Part 9 aims it at real rooms.
- **The calls that matter.** Each room has a few genuinely hard decisions; name them and make them.
- **The kit for the job.** Product types the room rewards, and permission to skip the rest.
- **Before and after.** The room's own transformation, and the two photos that hold it.

## Emotional purpose
Warm, practical, encouraging, and a little celebratory: this is where the reader gets to spend the method they worked to build. The feeling is capability, not instruction: "you already know how, watch a real room change." No shame about the before state; the before photo is honoured, not mocked. No hype, no force.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to decompose the entryway into five zones and run them one at a time; apply the six S's with the room's specific things; make the paper, coat, and mat calls; choose the room's product-type kit; run the five safety questions at the door; and take and use the before-and-after pair. Do NOT re-teach the six S's. Do NOT drift into other rooms except the light hand-off to the kitchen. Types, never brand names.
