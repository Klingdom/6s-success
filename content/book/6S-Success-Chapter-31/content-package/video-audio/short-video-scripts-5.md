## SHORT 1: Cleaning to a method, not wiping
**Topic: the universal nine-rule Shine method, the secondary hero. Runtime ~55 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A tidy room and a genuinely clean room are not the same thing, and the difference is not effort. It is method.

**Script:**
Most of us wipe at a surface until it looks a bit better. A professional cleans to a method, and it is not complicated. Here it is. Shine is cleaning to a method, not wiping until it looks better: work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes over. So, in order: work top to bottom, so the dust you release falls onto what you have not cleaned yet. Dry before wet, because a damp cloth over dry grit just makes mud and scratches the finish. Mist the cloth, never the surface, so nothing runs behind the frame. Two cloths, one to clean and one to dry, because a wet floor at a doorway is a fall waiting to happen. Colour-code your cloths, and never wash them with fabric softener, which clogs the fibres and kills their grab. Match the cleaner to the surface, give it a moment to work, and clean to inspect: flag the frayed cord, do not stop to fix it. Shine finds it, the safety pass fixes it. That is the whole method, and it works in every room.

- 12s: "The difference is method, not effort."
- 28s: "Top to bottom. Dry before wet. Mist the cloth. Two cloths."

## SHORT 2: Five zones, not one room
**Topic: the hero device, the room as micro-zones. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The reason your entryway feels like too much to face on a Saturday is that you are looking at it as one big job. It is not.

**Script:**
You have all six S's now and the loop that keeps them turning, so the rest of the book is not more teaching, it is somewhere to point what you already know, one room at a time. And here is the most freeing idea in the whole applied half of the book. You do not 6S a whole room. You 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done. A room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, and a zone is a job you can finish in an afternoon. You never face "the entryway", a thing so vague you could work at it for hours and not say you were done. You face the shoe zone, which has an obvious edge and a clear finish. The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. Do them one at a time and the room takes a few short sessions, not a lost weekend.

- 12s: "You are looking at one big job. It is five small ones."
- 28s: "You do not 6S a whole room. One zone, then the next."
- 40s: "Five zones. Do them one at a time."

## SHORT 3: How to clean the landing spot, step by step
**Topic: the worked centerpiece, Zone 1 inputs and numbered steps. Runtime ~60 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The little tray by the door is the entryway's highest-leverage square foot. Here is exactly how a professional cleans it, in order.

**Script:**
First, gather the inputs into a caddy so you never walk off mid-clean: a glass cleaner, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a floor cleaner matched to your floor, a colour-coded microfiber set, a soft detail brush, and a vacuum with the crevice tool. Now the steps, in the order you actually do them. One, clear and unplug: everything off, lift the tray out, and unplug the charging dock before any damp cloth comes near it. Two, dust high and dry first, the top of the mirror and the console's back edge, because a wet cloth over dry dust just makes grime. Three, the mirror: mist the glass cloth, never the glass, wipe one way, then buff dry in the crossing direction so any streak shows against the light. Four, the wall and switch plate. Five, the console top, working from the back edge toward you, giving a sticky ring a few seconds before you dry it. Six, wash the tray, detail brush into the corners where key grit packs in. Seven, the dock and cable, dry only, still unplugged. Eight, the floor last: vacuum the grit, then mop, and let it dry before anything goes back. That is one zone, cleaned to a professional standard, in about twenty minutes.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "How to clean the landing spot, in order."
- 12s: "Inputs in the caddy first. Clear and unplug."
- 30s: "Dust dry. Mirror. Wall. Top. Tray. Dock."
- 50s: "Floor last. About twenty minutes."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The landing spot cleaned in sequence, numbered 1 to 8, the caddy set down first, the tray lifted out, the dock unplugged, the glass cloth misted not the mirror, the console top wiped back to front, the tray brushed in the corners, the floor vacuumed and mopped last. A small side strip shows the inputs as labelled icons, types not brands. Warm paper, slate labels, green Shine accents, warm and hands-on.

## SHORT 4: The salt-caked boot tray
**Topic: Zone 3, the shoes, and why the entryway is the dirtiest room for its size. Runtime ~55 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The dirtiest object in your house is probably the boot tray by your front door, and the salt in it is quietly scratching every floor you own.

**Script:**
The entryway is the dirtiest room in the house for its size, because it is where the outside comes in: grit, road salt, rain, everything that rides in on shoes and sleeves. So the shoe zone takes longer than you expect, and the boot tray is the whole point. Gather your inputs: a floor cleaner, a multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a stiff dry brush, a vacuum with the crevice tool, and access to an outside tap. Then, in order: outside, knock the soles together and brush the caked mud off with the stiff dry brush. Wipe the shoes you keep. Wipe the rack shelves front to back and dry them, so damp mud does not cake back on. Now the tray: take it outside, hose it down, then scrub the salt film and grit out of the corners with the stiff brush and cleaner, and rinse it clear. Dry it fully before it comes back in, because a wet tray just re-wets the floor. Wipe the skirting, vacuum the grit trap under the rack, and mop the clear strip between the door and the rack last. That salt is exactly what sands the finish off every floor between the front door and the kitchen.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "The dirtiest room in the house for its size."
- 14s: "It is where the outside comes in."
- 30s: "The boot tray: outside, hose, scrub the salt, dry fully."
- 46s: "Dry before it comes back. A wet tray re-wets the floor."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The shoe zone worked in order: soles knocked and brushed outside, rack shelves wiped front to back, then the boot tray carried out, hosed, scrubbed in the corners with a stiff brush, rinsed, and stood to dry. Then under the rack vacuumed and the clear floor strip mopped last. The salt and grit drawn as the villain, the thing that scratches floors. Warm paper, slate labels, green Shine accents.

## SHORT 5: Take the before before you touch a thing
**Topic: the before-and-after pair, the Part 9 signature. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The most valuable photo of your entryway is the one you will be most tempted not to take, because the room is embarrassing. Take it anyway.

**Script:**
Here is the ritual that closes every room from here on, and it is what makes the clean stick. Before you touch a thing, stand just inside the door and photograph the room exactly as it is. Then do the work, five zones cleaned to the method, one at a time. And when it is done, stand in exactly the same spot and take the second photo. The two do different jobs. The before is the valuable one, the proof, on the days the work feels invisible, of how far the room has come. The after is the standard: tape it inside the coat cupboard, and "keep the entryway nice" turns from a vague hope into a specific target anyone can check in a glance. This is the entryway the whole loop was for: the same doorway you started with, cleared, calmed, and holding only what the door actually needs. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, because within a month you will not believe the room was ever the other one, and take the after photo as the standard, because it is the picture the door has to match from now on.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Take the before before you touch a thing."
- 12s: "Do the work. Five zones, cleaned to the method."
- 28s: "Same spot. Same angle. Take the after."
- 40s: "The after is the standard. Tape it inside the cupboard."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The matched before-and-after pair shot from just inside the door at standing height: on the left "before", the console and coats as they really were, mail drifting, keys loose, chargers tangled; on the right "after", the same frame cleared and visibly cleaned, one tray and one folder, the mirror bright, coats down to one per person with two empty hooks, the floor mopped. A small camera icon and two notes, "the before, kept" and "the after, taped inside the cupboard as the standard". Warm, illustrated, honouring the before, never mocking it.
