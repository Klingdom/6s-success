# Teleprompter Script: The Entryway

**Chapter 31, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-entryway
**Length:** about 890 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading. This OPENS Part Nine, the room-by-room playbooks, and its heart is HOW TO CLEAN, so read it warm, capable, and a little celebratory; you already know the loop, now clean a real room to a professional standard. No hype, no shame about the before.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is where the teaching stops and the doing begins.

Behind you are all six S's, and the loop that keeps them turning.

You do not need another lesson.

You need somewhere to point what you have learned.

And instructions precise enough to follow with your sleeves rolled up.

So we begin at the door, and not by accident.

The entryway is the room you cross most and notice most.

It is also the dirtiest room in the house for its size.

Because it is where the outside comes in.

Grit, road salt, rain, everything that rides in on shoes and sleeves.

Which is exactly why the clean matters most here.

Now, a whole entryway feels like too much.

Because you are looking at it as one big job.

It is not.

The entryway is five small zones, not one big job.

The landing spot where pockets empty.

The coats. The shoes. The bench or console.

And the door, with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing.

Do them one at a time, and the room takes a few short sessions.

Before the deep clean, four passes are quick.

Sort each zone first, because you cannot clean a surface that is covered.

Straighten what survives into a home chosen by use.

Safety, Standardize, and Sustain come after the shine.

Now we slow right down, for Shine.

Because Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean.

And it is the pass most people do worst.

Because they were never taught a method.

There is one, and it is the same in every zone and every room.

Here are the nine rules it rests on.

Work top to bottom, so dust falls onto what you have not cleaned yet.

Work dry before wet. A damp cloth over dry grit just makes mud.

Work back to front, clean to dirty.

Mist the cloth, not the surface, so nothing runs behind a frame.

Use two cloths. One to clean, one to dry.

A wet floor at a doorway is a fall waiting to happen.

Colour-code your cloths, and never use fabric softener.

Softener clogs the fibres and kills their grab.

Match the cleaner to the surface.

Give the cleaner a moment to work.

And clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix.

Your hand passes over every inch, so your eye should too.

Find the frayed cord, the loose bracket, the soft patch of rot.

Note it, and keep cleaning.

Shine finds it. The safety pass fixes it.

Now watch that method clean one zone, the landing spot.

Gather the inputs into your caddy first.

Glass cleaner, multi-surface cleaner, floor cleaner.

Your colour-coded cloths, a soft detail brush, a vacuum with the crevice tool.

Step one. Clear and unplug.

Everything off, the tray lifted out.

And unplug the charging dock before any damp cloth comes near it.

Step two. Dust high and dry, first.

The top of the mirror, the back edge of the console.

Step three. Do the mirror.

Mist the glass cloth, never the glass.

Wipe one way, then buff dry in the crossing direction.

Step four. Wipe the wall and the switch plate.

Step five. Clean the console top.

Work back edge toward you, give a sticky ring a few seconds, then dry it.

Step six. Wash the tray.

Detail brush into the corners where key grit packs in.

Step seven. The dock and cable, dry only. Still unplugged.

Step eight. The floor, last.

Vacuum the grit, then mop, and let it dry before you set anything back.

That is one zone, cleaned to a method.

The other four run the very same way.

The coats, from the shelf down. Dry the rail so nothing rusts.

The shoes, where the salt-caked boot tray goes outside and gets hosed.

That grit is what scratches every floor in the house.

The bench, with the drawer pulled fully out, corners tipped and brushed.

And the door, where the mat is most of the job.

Vacuum both faces of it, beat it, wash it, and return it only bone dry.

Then three hard calls hold the room.

The paper gets a verdict. Act, file, or recycle.

The coat is counted by weather, not by number.

And the mat you test, you do not just look at it.

A worn mat is quietly sanding the finish off every floor in the house.

When the five zones are done, take the second photograph.

Stand where you stood at the start, just inside the door.

The before is the valuable one, the proof.

The after is the standard, taped inside the cupboard.

The picture the door has to match from now on.

A zone is not clean because it looks clean.

It is clean because you gathered the right inputs.

Took it apart, and cleaned every surface to a method.

The door is done, and genuinely clean.
