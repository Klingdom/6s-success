Ten options. Mix of curiosity, plain clarity, and benefit angles. Honest, no clickbait, no hype, no smugness. 1. Welcome to the part of the book where the teaching stops and the doing begins. This is where 6S gets truly practical: the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine one room, one small zone at a time. (new section, hero)
2. Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean, and it is the pass most people do worst, because they were never taught a method. Here is the method. (the Shine method)
3. You do not 6S a whole room. You 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done. A room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, and a zone is a job you can finish in an afternoon. (the rule)
4. The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. (the five zones)
5. The entryway is the room you spend the least time in and notice the most, which is why the smallest room in the house pays back a 6S loop faster than any other room you own. (first and last)
6. A zone is not clean because it looks clean. It is clean because you cleaned every surface to a method, top to bottom, dry before wet, one cloth to clean and one to dry, inspecting as you went. (One Idea to Keep)
7. Work top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second. The nine rules that clean any room genuinely clean. (the nine rules)
8. Coats are counted by weather, not by number. The expensive coat you never wear is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm. (count by weather)
9. A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so you test it instead of looking at it. (test the mat)
10. Give one zone twenty minutes this weekend, cleaned to the method, and feel the difference the next time you walk in. (to try this week)
