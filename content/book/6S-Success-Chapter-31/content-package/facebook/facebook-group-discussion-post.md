Okay, the one idea that changed how I actually clean, and then I want to hear from you all.

For years I thought my entryway was clean because it looked clean. I wiped the console, straightened the coats, called it done. And the floor kept going dull and gritty anyway, and I could not work out why.

Here is what I had never been taught. There is a difference between tidy and clean, and the difference is method.

"Shine is cleaning to a method, not wiping until it looks better: work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes over. You leave the zone genuinely clean, and you find what needs fixing on the way."

That one paragraph fixed years of frustration. A few things I now do every single time:

- Top to bottom, so the dust and drips fall onto what I have not cleaned yet, never onto what I just did.
- Dry before wet, so I dust the grit off first instead of dragging a wet cloth over it and making mud.
- Mist the cloth, not the surface, so nothing runs down behind the mirror frame.
- Two cloths, one to clean and one to dry, because a wet floor at a doorway is a fall waiting to happen.
- Colour-code the cloths and never wash them with fabric softener, which clogs the fibres and kills the grab that makes them work.

And it matters most right here, because the entryway is the dirtiest room in the house for its size. It is where the outside comes in on every shoe and sleeve: grit, road salt, rain, all of it.

The other thing that made it feel doable was refusing to see it as one big job.

"The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. Do them one at a time and the room takes a few short sessions, not a lost weekend."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

If you did just one entryway zone this weekend, to the method, which would you point at first, the landing spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench, or the door and mat? And what is the one surface in yours that never actually gets cleaned properly?

I will go first in the comments. 😄
