3/
"The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing." Run them one at a time, not as a lost weekend.

4/
Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean, and it is the pass most people do worst, because they were never taught a method. There is one, it is the same in every zone, and once you have it here you use it in every room that follows. Here it is.

(263 chars)

---

5/
"Shine is cleaning to a method, not wiping until it looks better: work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes over."

(250 chars)

---

6/
Top to bottom, so dust and drips fall onto what you have not cleaned yet. Dry before wet, because a damp cloth over dry grit makes mud and scratches the finish. Back to front, clean to dirty, so you never drag grime across ground you have already cleaned.

(258 chars)

---

7/
Mist the cloth, not the surface, so nothing runs down behind a frame or onto electronics. Then two cloths, one to clean and one to dry, because a surface left wet dries to streaks, and at a doorway a wet floor is a fall waiting to happen.

(241 chars)

---

8/
Colour-code your cloths: one for glass, one for surfaces, one for the floor and the boot tray, so you never carry grit from a dirty surface onto a clean one. Never wash them with fabric softener, which coats the fibres and destroys the grab that makes microfiber work.

(271 chars)

---

9/
Match the cleaner to the surface, and give it a moment to work rather than scrubbing at the finish. And clean to inspect: your hand passes over every inch, so your eye should too. Flag the frayed cord and keep going. Shine finds it; the safety pass fixes it.

(261 chars)

---

10/
Zone by zone now. The landing spot, about twenty minutes and the highest-leverage square foot. Clear the surface and unplug the dock before any damp cloth comes near it. Wipe the dock and cable dry only, never wet, and do the floor last. Feel it next time you walk in.

(272 chars)

---

11/
The shoes take longer, and the boot tray is the point: its salt and grit is what scratches every floor in the house. Take it outside, hose it down, scrub the film out of the corners, and dry it fully before it comes back, because a wet tray just re-wets the floor.

(268 chars)

---

12/
The mat is most of the door zone. Vacuum the top face, then flip it and vacuum the underside where grit sifts through, beat it outside, and return it only once it is fully dry. It is the one thing standing between the outside and every other floor in the house.

(265 chars)

13/
"A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so you test it instead of looking at it. A worn mat is not saving you anything, it is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between the front door and the kitchen." Lift it and look.
