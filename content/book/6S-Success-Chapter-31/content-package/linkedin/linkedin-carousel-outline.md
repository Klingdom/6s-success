# Chapter 31 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: The Entryway

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, practical, expert voice, the friendly pro who shows you how; foreground the how-to value, the nine-rule Shine method, the per-zone inputs and steps, and clean to inspect while the safety pass fixes; no hype, no brand names, product types only.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** A zone is not clean because it looks clean. Tidy and clean are not the same thing, and the difference is method.

**Body:** The teaching is done. Now the doing begins. Swipe for exactly how to clean and shine the entryway to a professional standard, one small zone at a time.

---

## Slide 2: The whole game

**Headline:** Most of us wipe at a surface until it looks a bit better, then stop. A professional works in an order.

**Body:** The order you work in, the cloth you use, the cleaner you match to the surface, and the fact that while your hand is on every surface, your eye is inspecting it. That distinction is the whole game.

---

## Slide 3: Five small zones, not one big job

**Headline:** The entryway is five small zones, not one big job.

**Body:** The landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. You do not 6S a whole room. You 6S one zone, then the next.

---

## Slide 4: The Shine method

**Headline:** Shine is cleaning to a method, not wiping until it looks better.

**Body:** Work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes over. Learn it once, use it in every room.

---

## Slide 5: The nine rules

**Headline:** The nine rules every zone procedure is built on.

**Body:** Top to bottom. Dry before wet. Back to front, clean to dirty. Mist the cloth, not the surface. Two cloths, clean then dry. Colour-code, no fabric softener. Match the cleaner to the surface. Give it a moment. Clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix.

---

## Slide 6: Clean to inspect, the safety pass fixes

**Headline:** Your hand passes over every inch of a surface, so your eye should too.

**Body:** A frayed cord, a loose bracket, a soft patch of rot: note it and keep cleaning. Shine finds it; the safety pass fixes it. Do not stop your clean to chase a repair.

---

## Slide 7: The landing spot, step by step

**Headline:** The entryway's highest-leverage square foot, in about twenty minutes.

**Body:** Gather the caddy first. Clear and unplug. Dust high and dry. Do the mirror, misting the cloth. Wipe the wall, then the console top, back to front. Wash the tray. Then the floor, last.

---

## Slide 8: The boot tray is the whole point

**Headline:** The salt and grit in the boot tray is exactly what scratches every floor in the house.

**Body:** Knock the mud off the soles outside. Wipe the shoes and shelves front to back. Take the tray out, hose it, scrub the corners, dry it fully. A wet tray just re-wets the floor.

---

## Slide 9: The 6S Tip, colour-code your cloths

**Headline:** One colour for glass, one for surfaces, one for the floor and the boot tray.

**Body:** So you never carry grit from a dirty surface onto a clean one. And never wash them with fabric softener, which coats the fibres and destroys the grab, so microfiber wipes like a wet rag instead.

---

## Slide 10: Coats are counted by weather

**Headline:** Coats are counted by weather, not by number.

**Body:** Each person gets one coat per kind of weather your climate genuinely produces. The expensive coat you never wear is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm.

---

## Slide 11: Test the mat, do not look at it

**Headline:** A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so you test it instead of looking at it.

**Body:** A worn mat is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between the front door and the kitchen. Wipe your feet, lift it, look. If the grit walks straight off, replace it this month.
