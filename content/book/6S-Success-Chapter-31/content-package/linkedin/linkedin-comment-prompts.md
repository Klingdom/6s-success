# Chapter 31 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: The Entryway

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, practical, expert voice, the friendly pro who shows you how. Foreground the how-to method without lecturing. No corporate framing, no hype, no force or shame language, no brand names, product types only.

---

1. Be honest about how you actually clean a surface: do you wipe at it until it looks a bit better and then stop, or do you work to an order? The claim in this chapter is that tidy and genuinely clean are not the same thing, and the whole difference is method, the order you work in, the cloth you use, the cleaner you match to the surface, and inspecting every inch as your hand passes over it. Were you ever actually taught a way to clean, or did you pick it up by watching and guessing? Which of the nine rules, top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths clean then dry, would change your results the most if you started doing it tomorrow?

2. Here is the rule the whole chapter turns on: you do not 6S a whole room, you 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done. The entryway is really five small zones, the landing spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench, and the door with its mat. Most of us look at "the entryway" as one overwhelming Saturday job and put it off for a year. Does having the exact inputs to gather and the numbered steps for a single zone, say the landing spot in about twenty minutes, change whether you would actually start this weekend? Which zone would you clean first?

3. The idea I keep coming back to is "clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix": your hand passes over every inch of a surface while you clean, so your eye should too, and Shine finds the frayed cord or the loose bracket while the safety pass fixes it. Have you ever found a real hazard, a hardened charging cable, a wobbling console, a soft patch at the base of the door, only because you finally cleaned properly behind or under something? What did cleaning turn up in your home that you would otherwise have walked past for another year?

4. Two small habits from this chapter tend to divide a room: colour-coding your cloths, one for glass, one for surfaces, one for the floor and the boot tray, and never washing them with fabric softener, which coats the fibres and kills the grab that makes microfiber work. Be honest, do you use one grey cloth for everything and quietly move salt from the boot tray onto the mirror? And did anyone ever tell you softener wrecks a microfiber cloth, or is that news? What is the one cleaning-kit habit that made the biggest difference for you?

5. The quiet one nobody checks: the mat. A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so the chapter says you test it instead of looking at it, wipe your feet the way you actually do, then lift it and see whether the grit is walking straight over onto the next pair of shoes and off down the hall. Have you ever actually tested yours, or has it just sat there for years looking acceptable? For those who care about their floors, does the idea that a worn mat is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between the door and the kitchen change how soon you would replace it?
