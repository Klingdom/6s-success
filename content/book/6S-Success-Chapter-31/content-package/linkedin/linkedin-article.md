## Five zones, not one room

The reason a whole entryway feels like too much to face is that you are looking at it as one big job. It is not.

> You do not 6S a whole room. You 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done. A room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, and a zone is a job you can finish in an afternoon.

> The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. Do them one at a time and the room takes a few short sessions, not a lost weekend.

You can run the zones in any order, but within each one the loop runs in the same fixed sequence, and the deep, detailed part of it is Shine.

## How to Shine: the method

Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean, and it is the pass most people do worst, because they were never taught a method. There is one, it is not complicated, and it is the same in every zone and every room in this book. Learn it once and you use it in all nineteen chapters that follow.

> Shine is cleaning to a method, not wiping until it looks better: work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes over. You leave the zone genuinely clean, and you find what needs fixing on the way.

Here are the nine rules that method rests on. Every step-by-step procedure below is just these rules applied to a particular zone.

- work top to bottom
- work dry before wet
- work back to front, clean to dirty
- mist the cloth, not the surface
- use two cloths, one to clean and one to dry
- colour-code your cloths and never use fabric softener
- match the cleaner to the surface
- give the cleaner a moment to work
- clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix

That last rule is the oldest idea in the book. Cleaning is how you inspect: your hand passes over every inch of a surface, so your eye should too. When you find the thing you never normally see, a frayed cord, a loose bracket, a soft patch of rot, you note it and keep cleaning. Shine finds it; the safety pass fixes it. Do not stop your clean to chase a repair.

## Zone by zone: exactly how to clean it

Each zone gets the same three things: the inputs to gather, the numbered steps in the order you actually do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there. Gather the inputs into your caddy before you start, so you are not walking off mid-clean to fetch a cloth.

**Zone 1, the landing spot.** Inputs: streak-free glass and mirror cleaner, neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a floor cleaner matched to your floor, a colour-coded microfiber set, a small soft detail brush, a compact vacuum with the crevice tool, your caddy. Steps: clear the console and unplug the dock before any damp cloth comes near it; dust high and dry, first; do the mirror, misting the cleaner onto the glass cloth and buffing dry in the crossing direction; wipe the wall and switch plate; clean the console top from the back edge forward; wash the tray and work the detail brush into the corners; wipe the dock and cable dry only; then vacuum and mop the floor, last.

**Zone 3, the shoes and boots.** The boot tray takes longer than you expect, and it is the whole point: the salt and grit in it is exactly what scratches every floor in the house. Inputs add a stiff dry brush and access to an outside tap. Steps: clear the rack and, outside, knock the mud off the soles; wipe the shoes you keep and the rack shelves, front to back; carry the boot tray outside, hose it, scrub the salt film out of the corners, and dry it fully before it comes back; vacuum under the rack; then mop the clear stride-wide strip between the door and the rack, last.

The other three zones, the coats, the bench or console, and the door with its mat, run the same way: gather the inputs, work top to bottom and back to front, clean cloth then dry cloth, floor last, and inspect every surface your hand passes over. And honestly, at the door, the mat is most of the job, because it is the one thing standing between the outside and every other floor in the house: vacuum both faces, beat it outdoors, wash it if it is washable, and return it only once it is fully dry.

> **6S Tip.** Colour-code your cloths and never wash them with fabric softener. One colour for glass, one for surfaces, one for the floor and the boot tray, so you never carry grit or grime from a dirty surface onto a clean one. Softener coats the fibres and destroys the grab that makes microfiber work, so it wipes like a wet rag instead.

## The calls that matter here

Cleaning to a method will make the room shine, but three genuinely hard decisions decide whether it stays clear enough to keep clean.

The first is the paper on the landing spot. Nothing returns to the surface without a verdict, and there are only three: act, file, or recycle. A sheet that has sat more than fourteen days has stopped being paperwork and become a decision, so do it tonight or recycle it.

The second is the coat you feel guilty about.

> Coats are counted by weather, not by number. Each person gets one coat per kind of weather your climate genuinely produces, and a second in the same category has to beat the first on a specific, nameable day. The expensive coat you never wear is not exempt, because the money is already spent whether it hangs here or keeps someone else warm.

The third is the mat, which wears out while looking perfectly fine.

> A mat looks perfectly fine long after it has stopped working, so you test it instead of looking at it. A finished mat sits with its fibres flattened one way and the grit rides straight over it onto the next pair of shoes. A worn mat is not saving you anything, it is quietly sanding the finish off every floor between the front door and the kitchen.

Walk in on a dry, gritty day, wipe your feet the way you actually do, then lift the mat and look at both the floor beneath it and the fibres. If the grit is sitting on top and walking straight off, replace it this month.
