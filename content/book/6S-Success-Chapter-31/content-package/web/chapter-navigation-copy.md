## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 31, the first chapter of Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks, and the opening chapter of the applied half of the book. Behind you is the whole six-S method: Chapter 30 completed Part Eight and closed the loop, so you now own all six S's and the loop that keeps them turning. From here the teaching stops and the doing begins. A playbook does not re-teach you Sort or Shine, and it does not stop at telling you what to do: for every small zone of the room it gives you the exact inputs to gather and the exact, ordered, step-by-step way to clean and shine it, done the way a professional would do it rather than the way most of us wipe at a surface until it looks a bit better. That distinction is the whole game, because a tidy room and a genuinely clean room are not the same thing, and the difference is method. This chapter begins at the door, and not by accident: the entryway is the room you cross most and notice most, and it is also the dirtiest room in the house for its size, because it is where the outside comes in, grit, road salt, rain, and everything that rides in on shoes and sleeves, which is exactly why the clean-and-shine step matters most here and why the chapter spends most of its length on precisely how to do it. The reason a whole entryway feels like too much to face is that you are looking at it as one big job, and it is not: it is a small collection of separate zones that share a name and a bit of floor, and the trick to all of Part 9 is to stop seeing the room and start seeing the zones. The hero move, frozen: you do not 6S a whole room, you 6S one zone of it, then the next, until the room is done, a room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, and a zone is a job you can finish in an afternoon. The entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing spot where pockets empty, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. It moves briskly through the quick passes and then slows right down for Shine, the pass most people do worst because they were never taught a method, and teaches the universal nine-rule Shine method once: work top to bottom, work dry before wet, work back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, use two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths and never use fabric softener, match the cleaner to the surface, give the cleaner a moment to work, and clean to inspect and flag, do not fix. Then, as the heart of the chapter, Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It gives every zone the same three things, the exact inputs to gather into the caddy, the numbered steps in the order you actually do them (clear and unplug, dust high and dry, glass, surfaces, details, floor last), and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. It lands the definition of a finished entryway, one where everyone can land and launch in seconds. It names the three calls that matter here, the paper with no verdict (act, file, or recycle; a sheet past fourteen days is a decision, not paperwork), the guilty coat (count by weather, not by number), and the tired mat (test it, do not look at it; a worn mat is quietly sanding the finish off every floor to the kitchen). It runs the door's real hazards through the five safety questions. And it closes with the Part 9 signature that retires the friction meter and takes over the recurring close for every room from here: the before-and-after pair, the before you keep as private proof of how far the room has come, and the after, the ideal-state photo, taped inside the coat cupboard as the picture the door has to match from now on. The rule to carry: a zone is not clean because it looks clean, it is clean because you gathered the right inputs, took it apart, and cleaned every surface to a method, so point the loop at one zone this weekend, the landing spot or the shoes, and let the door start working for you every time you cross it. You have pointed the method at your first real room and it worked. The door is done, and genuinely clean, and Part Nine hands off to Chapter 32, The Kitchen, worked the same way.
