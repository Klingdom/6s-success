# Infographic Spec: The Shine Method

**Chapter 31, "The Entryway"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE FIRST PLAYBOOK**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter, and the centerpiece of the whole applied half of the book:
the SHINE METHOD, the nine rules plus the order of work plus the exact inputs. It carries the chapter's spine in
one glance: a tidy room and a genuinely clean room are not the same thing, and the difference is method, the
order you work in, the cloth you use, the cleaner you match to the surface, and the fact that while your hand is
on every surface your eye is inspecting it. The concept pairs the hero method line, **Shine is cleaning to a
method, not wiping until it looks better: work top to bottom and dry before wet, mist the cloth and not the
surface, clean with one cloth and dry with a second, and let your eye inspect every surface your hand passes
over. You leave the zone genuinely clean, and you find what needs fixing on the way.**, with the picture at its
heart: the order of work drawn top to bottom (dust dry and high, then glass, then surfaces, then the floor
last), the nine rules the method rests on, and the entryway kit, the exact inputs, laid out by type. So the
viewer sees the whole move at once: this is how you clean to a professional standard, and every zone procedure
in the chapter is just this method applied to a particular surface.

It stays consistent with the four inline figures in `chapter_31_final.html`, and in particular with Figure 3,
the two-panel `THE SHINE METHOD AND THE ENTRYWAY KIT`, drawn in the accent greens of the Shine step. The Shine
step owns the green accent: the method arrow, the "surfaces" colour-coded cloth, and the numbered nodes are
green; slate carries the "glass" cloth and the labels; terracotta carries the "floor" cloth. The single small
warm red is held back entirely on this calm image. This is not a lecture and not a scolding: it is a
professional's method laid out plainly so anyone can follow it with their sleeves rolled up. Types, never
brands: every cleaner, cloth, tool, and mat is a plain generic type, never a brand name or a real logo. The
register is warm, practical, and expert, the confidence of a method that is faster because it actually works.
Clean to a method, not until it looks better.

## Layout notes

- The method line is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole Shine pass compressed: cleaning to a
  method, not wiping until it looks better. The eye should read the method line, then see the order of work
  (top to bottom, dust high and dry first, the floor last) and the nine rules make it concrete.
- The order of work and the nine rules are the hero, so make the top-to-bottom direction unmistakable, a single
  green arrow from dust-dry-and-high at the head to floor-last at the foot, with the two-cloth technique (one
  damp to clean, one dry to buff) and the three colour-coded cloths (glass, surfaces, floor) drawn as clear
  small icons. Set the method line and the nine rule strings byte-for-byte as they read in the canonical quotes;
  they are the frozen strings.
- The kit sits in support, not competing with the method: the exact inputs laid out by TYPE, a neutral pH
  multi-surface cleaner, a streak-free glass and mirror cleaner, a floor-material-compatible cleaner, a
  colour-coded microfiber set, a compact vacuum with crevice and soft-brush attachments, a small soft detail
  brush, a stiff dry brush for the boot tray, and a portable caddy. Label every one by type, never by brand,
  draw no logo and no recognisable packaging.
- Hold the color discipline: the green Shine accent carries the method arrow, the numbered nodes, and the
  "surfaces" cloth; slate carries the "glass" cloth and the labels; terracotta carries the "floor" cloth and the
  single inspect-and-flag flag; the deeper label green carries the small labels; the single small warm red is
  held back entirely, because this is a calm method card and not a hazard.
- This image makes the concept, the heart of the whole applied half of the book: Shine is a method, and here is
  the method. Do NOT re-teach the first five S's or the mindset, audit, problem-solving, daily rhythm, or loop
  of Chapters 26 to 30 (finished, not re-explained), and do NOT teach the kitchen of Chapter 32, which is named
  only as where the reader points the loop next. Draw no friction meter, retired for Part 9. And name no brand
  and draw no specific product; the bottles, cloths, tools, and cloth colours are plain and generic, teaching
  the method, not a shopping list.
- Register: warm, practical, and expert, sleeves-rolled-up instruction rather than a lecture; the method exists
  because it is faster and it actually works. No force or discipline-as-punishment language, no scolding, no
  hype, no corporate or boardroom look, no smugness. Give the inspect rule its due, cleaning is how you inspect,
  the oldest idea in the book: your hand passes over every inch of a surface, so your eye should too, and you
  flag what you find and keep cleaning.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the order of work and the nine
  rules drawn by hand as a calm method card rather than a clinical diagram, the green arrow a hand-drawn stroke,
  the type icons plain and friendly, the whole image the warm, capable register of a professional method taught
  plainly: not a tidy room, a genuinely clean one.
