# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 31

**"The Entryway"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE FIRST PLAYBOOK*

Six prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across all
six: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the warm
accent, the eyebrows, the "floor" colour-coded cloth, the "before, kept" note), honey `#DDA63A` (the single
accent), slate blue `#3C5A6B` (data, labels, the plan, the "glass" colour-coded cloth), soft green `#6E8B5B`
(the SHINE STEP accent, the method arrow, the "surfaces" colour-coded cloth, the "after" standard), a deeper
label green for small labels on the Shine figure, a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks
only, used barely if at all on these calm images. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real
textures over polish.

The named space throughout is one real entryway, the smallest and dirtiest room in the house for its size,
because it is where the outside comes in. This chapter OPENS Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks, and is the FIRST
of twenty room chapters: the six-S method is finished, and from here the book points the completed loop at one
room at a time. Chapters 26 to 30 gave the mindset, the audit, problem-solving, the daily rhythm, and the
self-renewing loop; none of them is re-taught here. The spine of this chapter is a single distinction: a tidy
room and a genuinely clean room are not the same thing, and the difference is method, the order you work in, the
cloth you use, the cleaner you match to the surface, and the fact that while your hand is on every surface your
eye is inspecting it. It delivers the frame (the entryway is five small zones, not one big job: the landing
spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its
swing); the heart (the Shine method, nine rules that govern every zone procedure); the five zone-by-zone
procedures with their exact inputs and ordered steps; the three hard calls (the paper, the guilty coat, the worn
mat); and the Part 9 close (a matched before-and-after photo pair of the doorway). The friction meter, which
reached its goal at Chapter 30, is RETIRED for Part 9 and replaced by the before-and-after room signature: do
not draw a gauge. Keep the tone warm, practical, and expert, sleeves-rolled-up instruction rather than a
lecture. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated house style: no corporate or boardroom look, no
hype (no unlock, elevate, supercharge, optimize), no scolding, no photoreal polish, and no brand names or real
logos anywhere, every cleaner, cloth, tool, and mat is a plain generic TYPE, never a recognisable product.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in the
scene, a hand-lettered card, a hand-drawn label on a plan or a bottle, a mark on a cloth, or a note under a
photo, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered label: use the middot,
for example `CHAPTER 31 · THE ENTRYWAY`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms
keep their internal hyphen: top-to-bottom, dry-before-wet, back-to-front, clean-to-dirty, mist-the-cloth,
two-cloth, colour-coded, clean-then-dry, clean-to-inspect, flag-not-fix, before-and-after, matched-pair,
boot-tray, landing-spot, zone-by-zone, six-S, step-by-step.

- Mood: nothing here was bought new to fix; the same room was sorted, homed, cleaned to a standard, and given a

## 4. The nine rules of Shine (the method as a visual)

A warm, capable editorial illustration of a hand-made card titled the nine rules of Shine, on a soft cream
panel, in soft daylight, drawn in the soft green accent of the Shine step, rendering the heart of the chapter as
one memorable image. The nine rules are laid out as a calm grouped column, each a short hand-lettered line with a
tiny hand-drawn type icon. At the top, bound by a faint green top-to-bottom arrow so the sequence reads, the
three order rules: work top to bottom, a high shelf and a low floor with a downward arrow; work dry before wet, a
dry duster then a damp cloth; work back to front and clean to dirty, a hand pulling from the wall toward you. In
the middle, the cloth and cleaner rules: mist the cloth not the surface, a spray hitting a cloth and not a wall;
use two cloths, one to clean and one to dry, a damp cloth then a dry cloth; colour-code your cloths and never use

a sticky spot. At the foot, given the most weight, the inspect rule: clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix, an
eye following a hand across a surface with a small flag planted on a frayed cord, hand-tagged Shine finds it, the

- Mood: Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean, and it is the pass most people do worst

## 5. How to clean a zone (the step ribbon)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-made step ribbon titled how to clean a zone, on a soft cream panel, in
soft daylight, drawn in the soft green Shine accent, showing the five moves every zone shares as a gentle
left-to-right ribbon of small step cards, each with a tiny hand-drawn type icon and a hand-numbered green node.
First, gather the inputs, a portable caddy filled before you start so you never walk off mid-clean. Second, clear
the surface, a hand lifting everything off and unplugging anything electrical, because a clear surface is the
only one you can truly clean. Third, clean top to bottom, drawn a notch larger and greener because it is the deep
part, dust dry and high, then glass, then surfaces, misting the cloth and cleaning with a damp cloth then drying
with a second. Fourth, inspect and flag, an eye following the hand with a small flag on a loose bracket, hand-
lettered flag it, do not fix it. Fifth, the floor last, a vacuum then a mop and a note to let it dry before
anyone crosses it. Across the foot, a single hand-lettered line reads gather, clear, clean top to bottom,
inspect, then the floor. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, soft green for the step nodes and the
enlarged clean step, a deeper label green for labels, a small terracotta flag on the inspect step, no warm-red.
A picture of one plain idea: every zone, and every room in the book, runs the same five moves in the same fixed
order.

- Mood: each zone gets the same three things, the inputs to gather, the numbered steps in the order you actually
  do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there; learn the shape once at the door and
  run it in every room; the register is calm, plain, and capable.
- Avoid: a cluttered checklist, gridlines, neon color, floating typeset text, a corporate look, brand names on
  the caddy, cloths, vacuum, or mop, a friction gauge; keep it a calm five-step ribbon, the clean step largest
  and greenest, a small terracotta flag on the inspect step, plainly one repeatable method rather than a to-do
  list.
