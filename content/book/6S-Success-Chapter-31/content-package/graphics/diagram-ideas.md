room and a genuinely clean room are not the same thing, and the difference is method, the order you work in, the

eye is inspecting it. It delivers the frame, the entryway is five small zones and not one big job (the landing

do NOT draw the gauge. This is the ENTRYWAY only, five zones at a door, NOT the kitchen of Chapter 32; name the
kitchen only as where the reader points the loop next. Types, never brands: every cleaner, cloth, tool, and mat
is a plain generic type icon, never a brand name or a real logo. The register is warm, practical, and expert:
sleeves-rolled-up instruction, no hype, no scolding, the quiet confidence of a professional method taught
plainly. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 31 · THE ENTRYWAY`, never a floating
hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: top-to-bottom, dry-before-wet,
back-to-front, clean-to-dirty, mist-the-cloth, two-cloth, colour-coded, clean-then-dry, clean-to-inspect,
flag-not-fix, before-and-after, matched-pair, boot-tray, landing-spot, zone-by-zone, six-S, step-by-step,
whole-area, highest-leverage. No hype (no unlock, elevate, supercharge, optimize), no scolding, no corporate or
boardroom register, no brand names, no photoreal polish. The register is warm, homely, capable, and calm: not a
lecture but a method you can follow with your sleeves rolled up. Clean to a method, not until it looks better.

## 1. Before and After, One Doorway (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole promise in one image, before a word is read: this is a playbook about
transforming one real room, and the before-and-after pair is the signature of Part 9. The same doorway, same
furniture, drawn in two completely different mornings, so the only variable the eye reads is whether the loop
has been pointed at it. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** One entryway in two states, side by side. On the LEFT, `BEFORE`, the everyday reality: coats
piled three deep and sliding off overloaded hooks, a scarf trailing to the floor, shoes strewn across the swing

dock and a plug-in heater all chained to one power strip, and a rubber mat curled up at one corner. On the

## 2. The Five Zones (the overhead plan, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Turn the abstract "do the entryway" into a literal map of five bounded, finishable jobs, so the
reader can see the room as its zones and pick one to start. The value is that the whole room stops looking like
one big job and starts looking like five small ones sharing a bit of floor. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A simple, warm overhead plan of a typical entryway with its five zones marked and gently
shaded in the palette accents, the door itself drawn open to show its swing. Zone 1, `THE LANDING SPOT`, a small

a low rack and a boot tray by the wall. Zone 4, `THE BENCH OR CONSOLE`, the seat and the drawer. Zone 5, `THE
DOOR, MAT, AND FLOOR`, the threshold and a marked strip of clear floor inside the door's swing. Each zone
numbered and labelled in plain words. Across the foot, a single line reads `one room, five small jobs`.

## 5. How to Clean a Zone (the step ribbon, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the shape every zone procedure shares, so the reader sees that all five zones (and all twenty
rooms) run the same five moves, and that the fixed order is what makes the method faster and cleaner. This
diagram has no direct HTML figure twin; it renders the "same three things for every zone" frame, gather the
inputs, take the surface apart, clean top to bottom, inspect and flag, and finish with the floor, as one calm
left-to-right ribbon, so the five detailed procedures in the chapter read as this one shape applied five times.

**What it shows:** A gentle left-to-right ribbon of the moves every zone shares, each a small step card with a
tiny type icon. `GATHER` the exact inputs into the caddy before you start, so you never walk off mid-clean.
`CLEAR` the surface, empty it and unplug anything electrical, because a clear surface is the only one you can
truly clean. `TOP TO BOTTOM`, dust high and dry first, then glass, then surfaces, misting the cloth and cleaning
with the damp cloth then drying with the second. `INSPECT AND FLAG`, your hand is on every surface so your eye
inspects it, and you flag the frayed cord or the loose bracket and keep cleaning rather than stopping to fix.
`FLOOR, LAST`, vacuum then mop the sealed floor and let it dry before anyone crosses it. A faint note under the
ribbon reminds that the same five moves run in every zone and every room.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel `#FBF7EF`. Top, sans caps letter-spaced soft: `HOW TO CLEAN A ZONE`, a Fraunces line beneath in
  the label green: "the same five moves, every zone, every room".
- Five step cards left to right, each a green-numbered node with a Fraunces label and a Newsreader italic gloss:
  - `1 · gather the inputs` · into the caddy, before you start
  - `2 · clear the surface` · empty it, unplug the electrical
  - `3 · clean, top to bottom` · dust dry, glass, surfaces, mist the cloth, clean then dry
  - `4 · inspect and flag` · the eye follows the hand; flag it, do not fix it
  - `5 · the floor, last` · vacuum then mop, let it dry
- The clean step (`3`) drawn a notch larger and greener, because it is the deep part; the inspect step (`4`)
  carries a small terracotta flag.
- A foot line in Fraunces slate: `gather, clear, clean top to bottom, inspect, then the floor`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "Each zone gets the same three things: the inputs to gather, the numbered steps in
  the order you actually do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there. The landing
  spot takes about twenty minutes and is the entryway's single highest-leverage square foot."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the shape under all five procedures, so keep it a calm ribbon, five moves
left to right, plainly one repeatable method rather than a checklist. Make the clean step (top to bottom) the
largest and greenest because it is the deep part, and hang a small terracotta flag on the inspect step because
flagging without stopping to fix is the move readers most often skip. Hold the color discipline: the green Shine
accent for the step nodes and the clean step, the deeper label green for labels, a small terracotta flag on the
inspect step, spark-red does not appear. Warm line-and-flat-color style, friendly and capable, the register of a
method that is the same in every zone, so a reader who learns it once at the door can run it in every room that
follows.
