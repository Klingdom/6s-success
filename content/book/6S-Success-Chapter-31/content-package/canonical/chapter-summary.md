# Chapter 31 Summary: The Entryway

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks*

## One-line summary

Chapter 31 OPENS Part Nine and is the first of twenty room playbooks, and its centerpiece is the detailed, step-by-step clean-and-shine instruction for each of the entryway's five micro-zones, with the exact inputs to gather: the teaching is done, so the book stops explaining the six S's and shows you precisely how to clean and shine a real room, one small zone at a time. It establishes the room-as-zones principle with the rule (you do not 6S a whole room, you 6S one zone of it, then the next) and the five-zones line (the landing spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing); it moves briskly through Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain and then slows right down for Shine, the pass most people do worst because they were never taught a method; it teaches the universal nine-rule Shine method (work top to bottom, dry before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths and never use fabric softener, match the cleaner to the surface, give the cleaner a moment to work, and clean to inspect and flag, do not fix) with the Shine method line; then, as the heart of the chapter, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It" gives every zone the same three things, the exact INPUTS to gather into the caddy, the NUMBERED steps in the true order of work (clear and unplug, dust high and dry, glass, surfaces, details, floor last), and an inspect-and-flag list; it lands the definition box (a finished entryway is one where everyone can land and launch in seconds); it names the room's three genuinely hard calls, the paper with no verdict, the guilty coat counted by weather, and the tired mat you test rather than look at, with the count-by-weather and worn-mat reframes; it runs the room's real hazards through the five safety questions, where the safety pass fixes everything the clean flagged; and it closes with the Part 9 signature that replaces the friction meter, the before-and-after room pair, the before kept and the after taped inside the cupboard as the standard, before a light hand-off to Chapter 32, the kitchen. Shine finds the hazards; the safety pass fixes them.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

The teaching is done; now the doing begins, and the doing is the point. Part Nine points the finished six-S loop at one real room, and this chapter does not stop at telling you what to do: for every zone it gives the exact inputs to gather and the precise, step-by-step way to clean and shine it to a professional standard. The entryway is five small zones, not one big job. ## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 31 opens Part Nine, the applied half of the book, and is the first of twenty room playbooks. The reader now owns the whole six-S method, so a playbook does not re-teach it; instead it makes the detailed, step-by-step clean-and-shine instruction for each micro-zone, with the exact inputs, the heart of the chapter. It begins at the door because the entryway is the room you cross most and notice most, and also the dirtiest for its size, which is exactly why the clean-and-shine step matters most here.

The freeing idea is that a room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name. The entryway is five: the landing spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat and the strip of floor inside its swing. Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain get a brisk pass; then the chapter slows right down for Shine and teaches the universal nine-rule method that governs every zone. The centerpiece, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It," gives each zone the same three parts: the exact inputs (cleaners, colour-coded cloths, tools) to gather into the caddy; the numbered steps in the true order of work, clear and unplug, dust high and dry, glass, surfaces, details, floor last; and an inspect-and-flag list, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. It names the three hard calls, the paper, the guilty coat, and the tired mat, runs the door's hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after taped up as the standard. Then a light hand-off to the kitchen.
