## Search intent
Getting one real room, the entryway, genuinely and inspectably clean, after the reader has learned the whole 6S method and wants to apply it. This chapter opens Part Nine, the applied half of the book, and its centerpiece is the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine each zone, with the exact inputs to gather. Readers arriving here are overwhelmed by an entryway that collects the whole house's grit, paper, coats, and shoes, and they do not just want to tidy it, they want to clean it to a professional standard and know precisely how. The chapter answers with one freeing idea and one method. The idea: you do not 6S a whole room, you 6S one zone of it, then the next, and the entryway is only five small zones (the landing spot, the coats, the shoes, the bench or console, and the door with its mat). The method: the universal nine-rule Shine method, taught once and applied in every zone, work top to bottom, work dry before wet, work back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, use two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths and never use fabric softener, match the cleaner to the surface, give the cleaner a moment to work, and clean to inspect and flag, do not fix (Shine finds the hazards, the safety pass fixes them). Then, zone by zone, it gives the exact INPUTS (cleaners, colour-coded cloths, tools) and the NUMBERED steps in the true order of work (clear and unplug, dust high and dry, glass, surfaces, details, floor last), plus an inspect-and-flag list. It names the room's three hard calls (the paper's verdict, the coat counted by weather, the mat you test), runs the door's hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after taped inside the cupboard as the standard. This is the first of twenty room playbooks; the friction meter reached its goal at Chapter 30 and is retired, replaced by each room's own transformation.
