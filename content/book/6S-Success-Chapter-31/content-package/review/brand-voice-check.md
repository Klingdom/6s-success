# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 31 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 31 is the FIRST chapter of Part Nine (Room-by-Room Playbooks) and the OPENING of the applied half of the book. The six-S method is complete (Parts 1 to 8), and from here the book stops teaching in the abstract and hands the reader detailed, do-it-now instructions for one real room at a time. This chapter was REWRITTEN to be instruction-centric: per Phil's directive, the step-by-step clean-and-shine procedures for each entryway micro-zone, with their exact inputs, are now the CORE of the chapter, which is why it runs longer than a method chapter. The register is warm, capable, and lightly celebratory: this is the fun part, the first place the reader spends the method they worked to build, so the feeling must be capability rather than instruction-from-above, the before state must be honoured rather than mocked, and the whole thing must stay free of shame, force, and hype. The chapter governs every zone procedure with a single universal nine-rule Shine method (clean to a method, not wiping until it looks better) and keeps a clean-to-inspect-and-flag discipline: Shine finds the hazards, the safety pass fixes them. The friction meter, which reached its goal at Chapter 30, is RETIRED for Part 9; each room now closes on its own concrete before-and-after transformation.

## Voice fingerprint (from the rewritten manuscript and final HTML)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," treats the entryway as the room you cross most and notice most, and walks the reader through the actual work: gather these inputs into a caddy, clear the surface, then top to bottom, dry before wet, one cloth to clean and one to dry, inspecting as you go. "Do that to one zone this weekend, and let the door start working for you every time you cross it."
- **Concrete and procedural over abstract.** The core is not a concept but a set of instructions with exact inputs, zone by zone (landing spot, coats, shoes, bench or console, the door with its mat). Each procedure is built on the nine-rule Shine method, so the reader is given a repeatable order, not a pep talk.
- **Capable and lightly celebratory, never smug or bossy.** This is framed as the fun part, the first real payoff of the finished loop: the method was the hard part and you already have it. The instructions read as a competent friend working alongside you, not a manual barking steps.
- **Honest about the state of the room.** The before is honoured, not mocked; one finished zone beats five half-done ones; a mat is tested rather than looked at; a coat is counted by weather rather than by number. Nothing implies the room must be conquered in one exhausting go.
- **Method, not brand.** The kit and cleaners are given as product TYPES only. "Colour-code your cloths and never use fabric softener" is a technique, not an endorsement; the word "method" throughout means the Shine method, never a brand.

## Voice risks to watch
- **Hero vocabulary is dense.** "zone," "method," "cloth," "top to bottom," "dry before wet," "inspect," "flag," "door," "coats," "shoes," "mat" recur because they are the subject of a procedure; expected in an instruction-heavy chapter, not a tic, but vary the surrounding language when excerpting so a single post does not stack "zone" and "method."
- **Procedure can read as a manual if stripped of warmth.** Because the core is genuinely step-by-step, short excerpts must keep the capable-friend register (the fun part, the first payoff) and not tip into a cold instruction list.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the LinkedIn and X sign-offs; several close on "Read the free chapter" / "Read it free online."
- **Keep the before honoured, never mocked.** The chapter honours the starting-state room; watch that excerpts never shame the reader's current entryway or imply it must be conquered in one exhausting go.
