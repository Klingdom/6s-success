# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 21 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line or image hitting the same follower until it feels canned. This check flags where that can happen.

## High-frequency elements
| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find it, and fix it now | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics, slides, HTML | A follower on several platforms sees the hero rule repeatedly | Calendar staggers it; vary the phrasing around it per platform |
| A first pass finds the dangers that are obvious ... a hunt finds the ones that never will be (thoroughness reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics | Medium to High | The premise the chapter opens on; the most shareable turn. Use across formats, not stacked on one day. |
| Go room by room. Ask the same few questions in each. Fix what you find before you leave (the method) | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, quote card | Medium | The frozen method; pair with a different worked room (kitchen / bathroom / living room) each time. |
| The five safety questions (frozen set) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, quote card, graphics, slides | Medium | The portable tool; long as a set, so lead with a fresh hook or a single question before the full five. |
| A hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled. It is scheduled ... The only safe place for a danger is gone (found-not-fixed reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card | Medium | The rule's logic; vary the surrounding worked example (button battery / heater / cord). |
| The camouflage reframe (the lamp cord that is also a tripwire ...) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, quote card | Medium | Long; lead with a fresh hook before it, and rotate which of the three objects leads. |
| The friction-meter caption ("The needle takes a second Safety step ...") | Canonical, graphics, HTML | Low to Medium | Long; it is the honest second-step beat, specific to this chapter. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording ("Read the free chapter", "Read it free online", "the online book") | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings; most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision
The calendar avoids two assets sharing a hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push one back a day so "Find it, and fix it now." or the thoroughness reframe does not land twice in one day.

## Cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same Safety content. Post only one to LinkedIn (use the copy-ready slides file; keep the outline as planning).
- The LinkedIn newsletter and the email newsletter share structure; do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- Web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 21)
- Chapter 21 is the SECOND chapter of Part Six and the MAKE-SAFETY-THOROUGH chapter. Keep it distinct from Chapter 20 behind it: Chapter 20 opened Safety with the lens, the triage, Heinrich's pyramid, and one first pass on the obvious dangers; Chapter 21 runs the thorough, room-by-room hunt for the hidden many. Assets must USE the safety lens and the finished-plus-first-pass home as the starting point but must NOT re-teach the lens, re-derive the pyramid, or re-tell the 5S+1 history. Grep confirms the assets run the hunt and the five questions and do not rebuild Chapter 20.
- Do not let this chapter slide FORWARD into making safety last. Ch 21 assets must not build ongoing safety systems, lasting safety consciousness, or emergency preparation (that is Chapter 22). This fence is held: the making-it-last work is named in the package only as "the next chapter" and "not yet done," never built. The hunt makes the home safe as of the day you run it; keeping it safe is deliberately left ahead.
- Do NOT drift into documented standards (Standardize) or long-term adherence (Sustain); these are noted lightly at most, not built.
- Do NOT re-open Sort, Straighten, or Shine, or re-teach cleaning or inspection (Chapters 16 to 19).
- The friction-meter second-Safety-step is specific to this chapter (a real step, a touch larger than Chapter 20's, to (68.0,154.8), a little further off Chapter 19's Shine-complete mark, with Chapter 20's first-Safety-step position at (69.5,154.5) now the dashed ghost behind it, a SAFETY · STEP 2 marker for the new live position, and the GOAL crosshair still ahead at (67,155)). As in Chapter 20 the needle moved because the chance of harm dropped, not because daily friction did. Do not imply a new milestone was reached or that Safety is finished; it is a STEP, not arriving. Keep the goal honestly ahead (the fourth S completes in Chapter 22, with Standardize and Sustain still to come).

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter is a draft authored in the book voice, grounded in the 6S source ("Principles of 6S: Safety Step," "Steps to Complete Safety," "identify and eliminate hazards," "address all possible safety issues immediately"), not supplied verbatim source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft; re-sync if the chapter is revised.
- Quote cards are verbatim: every card in `graphics/quote-card-copy.md` verified byte-identical to a quoted line in `canonical/chapter-quotes.md` (0 mismatches). Nothing borrowed from outside the book; no brand-name products are named, and the Safety Step / Steps to Complete Safety origin is referenced lightly, home-scaled, not overclaimed, with no alarmist statistics or graphic injury. "Hunt" and "remove / eliminate" are topic words used calmly and non-violently.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording (highest-value, lowest-effort fix; several posts close on "Read the free chapter").
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical.
4. Keep Chapter 21 from blurring into its neighbors: distinct from Chapter 20 behind it (the lens, triage, pyramid, and first pass) and distinct from making-safety-last ahead of it (Ch 22) and the later S's (Standardize, Sustain). Chapter 21 is the MAKE-SAFETY-THOROUGH chapter: the room-by-room hunt, the five questions, and find-it-and-fix-it-now.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
