## Key extracted assets
- **Title:** Find and Remove Hazards
- **Part:** Part Six, Safety (the second chapter of Part 6; it CONTINUES Safety, the fourth S, and makes it thorough)
- **Hero device:** the hazard hunt (a calm, systematic, room-by-room search that removes each hazard on the spot)
- **The rule (frozen):** Find it, and fix it now.
- **The method (frozen):** Go room by room. Ask the same few questions in each. Fix what you find before you leave.
- **The five safety questions (frozen set):** Could someone fall here? Could something here burn, or catch fire? Could something here poison, choke, or strangle? Could something here fall, cut, or crush? Could water and electricity meet?
- **The hazard-hunt definition (frozen):** A hazard hunt is a calm, systematic walk through the whole home, one room at a time, asking the same short set of safety questions in every space and removing each hazard you find before you move on. Where a first pass catches what is obvious, the hunt is built to catch what is hidden.
- **The thoroughness reframe (frozen):** A first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look. A hunt finds the ones that never will be. Most of what can quietly hurt a person at home lives in the second group.
- **The found-not-fixed reframe (frozen):** A hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled. It is scheduled. And a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard, still able to hurt someone on any ordinary day before its turn comes up. The only safe place for a danger is gone.
- **The camouflage reframe (frozen):** The dangerous thing is rarely the ugly one. It is the ordinary object doing a second, quiet job you never gave it: the lamp cord that is also a tripwire, the old remote that is also a battery a small child can reach, the space heater that is also a flame a foot from the curtain.
- **One Idea to Keep (frozen):** Go room by room, and in each one ask the same few questions: could someone fall, could something burn, could something poison or choke or strangle, could something fall or cut or crush, could water and electricity meet. Then fix what you find before you leave the room, because a danger is only handled once it is gone.
