## Search intent
Finding and removing the hazards a first look misses. Readers arriving here already have a home that is cleared, arranged, kept clean and read, and given one first safety pass on the obvious dangers (Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Chapter 20). They sense, correctly, that a glance handled only the obvious few and that more is hiding. They want a repeatable, thorough method they can run themselves, room by room, without turning the home into a place of dread. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: the hazard hunt, a calm, systematic, room-by-room walk carrying the same five safety questions into every space; and find it, and fix it now, removing each hazard on the spot because a hazard found but not fixed is scheduled, not handled. It home-scales the source's Steps to Complete Safety into the five questions, walks a worked whole-home sweep, and keeps the register calm and anti-shame. The friction meter takes a second Safety step, a little larger than the first, with the goal still ahead because Safety completes only when it is made to last.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Separates the obvious few from the hidden many, naming that a first pass catches only what stands out and that most of what can hurt someone at home is camouflaged, rather than treating one walk-through as enough
- Gives a repeatable method, the hazard hunt, a systematic room-by-room walk carrying a fixed short list of questions, instead of a one-off tour that depends on what you happen to notice
- Home-scales the source's Steps to Complete Safety into five plain questions any reader can carry into any room, rather than an inspector's checklist
- Names camouflage as the real enemy, the ordinary object doing a quiet second job, so readers learn to catch the lamp cord that is also a tripwire and the remote that is also a reachable battery
- Insists on fix-on-the-spot with the found-not-fixed reframe, a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard, correcting the common notepad trap of listing dangers to deal with later
- Gives the honest exception, make it safe now even if you cannot make it finished now, so a bigger fix does not become an excuse to leave a live hazard in place
- Keeps the register calm and anti-shame, with no fear-mongering, no graphic injury, no alarmist numbers, and no force or violence framing of the hunt, and no brand names
- Keeps scope tight: this finds and removes the hazards already in the home; it does not re-teach the safety lens or triage (Chapter 20), build ongoing safety systems or emergency preparedness (Chapter 22), write documented standards (Standardize), or solve long-term adherence (Sustain)
- Takes the second Safety step honestly, a little larger than the first because the whole home was hunted, with the goal still ahead because Safety completes only when it is made to last
