## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 21 is the second chapter of Part Six and continues Safety, the fourth of the six S's. Chapter 20 opened Safety, taught the safety lens (could this hurt someone), and did one first high-value pass on the worst and most obvious dangers. This chapter delivers the thorough part it handed off.

It begins with the reframe the chapter rests on: a first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look, a hunt finds the ones that never will be, and most of what can quietly hurt a person at home lives in the second group. Familiarity makes the dangerous objects invisible right along with the harmless ones, so the only reliable way to find the hidden hazards is to stop relying on what catches your eye and run a method instead.

The hero is the hazard hunt: a calm, systematic walk through the whole home, one room at a time, asking the same short set of safety questions in every space and removing each hazard before moving on. The five frozen questions, home-scaled from the source's Steps to Complete Safety, each surface a different family of hidden hazard. The rule is find it, and fix it now: a hazard found but not fixed is not handled, it is scheduled, and the only safe place for a danger is gone. A worked whole-home sweep pays off Chapter 20's teasers. The needle takes a second Safety step, a little larger than the first; the goal is still ahead, because Safety completes only when it is made to last (Chapter 22).
