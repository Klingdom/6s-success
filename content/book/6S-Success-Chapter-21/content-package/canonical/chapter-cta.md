# Chapter 21 Calls to Action: Find and Remove Hazards

This is the second chapter of Part Six, and it makes Safety thorough. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea: pick one room, ask the five safety questions, and fix everything you can in ten minutes before you walk out. The CTAs match the chapter's tone of calm, loving attention, not alarm: a home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space, and the fix is a short set of questions carried room by room, not guilt or fear. The move costs a few minutes, once per room, because the value is trading a low hum of worry for the quiet of a real danger handled and gone.

## Primary CTA
Give one room a full hunt today. "Pick one room and give it a full hunt right now. Ask the five questions, and fix everything you can in ten minutes before you walk out: the cord tucked away, the pills moved up high, the rug taped down, the heavy thing brought off the top shelf. One room, thoroughly safe, today. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "A first pass catches the dangers that are obvious the moment you finally look, and walks straight past the rest, and the rest are the majority. Chapter 21 makes Safety thorough with the hazard hunt: a calm, room-by-room walk that carries the same five safety questions into every space and removes each hazard the moment it is found. Read it free online, then Chapter 22 makes Safety last, completing the fourth S."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one room and give it a full hunt right now. Ask the five questions, and fix everything you can in ten minutes before you walk out: the cord tucked away, the pills moved up high, the rug taped down, the heavy thing brought off the top shelf. One room, thoroughly safe, today.
- **Family Challenge:** Turn the hunt into a game. Give each person a room and the five questions and send everyone off to find hazards at once, and the younger the sharper for the low ones. Then come back together and fix the worst finds as a team. A home searched by five pairs of eyes misses far less than one.
- **6S Tip:** Hunt every room twice, once standing and once low. Crouch to a small child's height and look again: the outlet at ankle level, the tablecloth a toddler can pull, the cleaning spray under the sink, the wobbling table leg. A whole layer of hazards lives in the band of space adults never occupy.

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the brief)
- **The Hazard Hunt checklist:** a printable carrying the five safety questions with room to tick found-and-fixed, a block per room, so a whole-home sweep can be run and nothing skipped.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: pick one room, ask the five questions, and fix everything you can in ten minutes before you leave. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in a few minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of calm, loving attention and relief. A home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space; the fix is a short set of questions carried room by room, not guilt.
- The rule (find it, and fix it now), the method (go room by room, ask the same few questions in each, fix what you find before you leave), the found-not-fixed reframe, and the thoroughness reframe are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype, alarmist statistics, graphic injury, and any force or violence framing. Nothing is attacked, blitzed, or waged war on; the hunt is calm and non-violent, you hunt for hazards the way you hunt for your keys, and you remove them the way you remove a typo. Keep the calm register throughout, and avoid brand names (say "a cabinet latch" or "grip tape", not a brand).
- Scope note: this makes Safety thorough with the room-by-room hazard hunt and removes the hazards already in the home; it does not re-teach the safety lens or triage (Chapter 20), build ongoing safety systems or emergency preparedness (Chapter 22), write documented standards (Standardize), or solve long-term adherence (Sustain). Safety completes only when it is made to last, so the goal is still ahead.
