# Chapter 21 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Find and Remove Hazards

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, calm, anti-shame voice; safety is love, not fear; a careful walk with a roll of tape, not a fearful inventory; no force metaphors, no graphic injury, no scary statistics.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You handled the obvious dangers. The hidden ones are still here.

**Body:** A first pass finds what is obvious. Most of what can quietly hurt someone at home hides in plain sight. Swipe for the hunt that finds it.

---

## Slide 2: The obvious are the few

**Headline:** A first pass finds the obvious. A hunt finds the rest.

**Body:** A first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look. A hunt finds the ones that never will be. Most of what can hurt a person at home lives in the second group.

---

## Slide 3: Why the hidden ones hide

**Headline:** Familiarity is a kind of camouflage.

**Body:** The things we live with become invisible to us, and the dangerous ones become invisible right along with the harmless ones. The bookcase that has never once fallen reads, to a glance, as furniture.

---

## Slide 4: The hero device

**Headline:** The hazard hunt.

**Body:** A calm, systematic walk through the whole home, one room at a time, asking the same short set of safety questions in every space and removing each hazard you find before you move on.

---

## Slide 5: What makes it a hunt

**Headline:** Systematic, fixed, and it ends in action.

**Body:** Room by room, so nothing is skipped. The same questions every time, so nothing depends on memory. Fixed on the spot, so finding and removing are one motion. Boring is what thorough looks like.

---

## Slide 6: The method

**Headline:** Go room by room. Ask the same few questions in each. Fix what you find before you leave.

**Body:** That is the whole method. Almost aggressively boring, and that is exactly the point.

---

## Slide 7: What the hunt defeats

**Headline:** The dangerous thing is rarely the ugly one.

**Body:** It is the ordinary object doing a second, quiet job you never gave it: the lamp cord that is also a tripwire, the old remote that is also a battery a small child can reach, the space heater that is also a flame a foot from the curtain.

---

## Slide 8: The five questions

**Headline:** Five questions, carried into every room.

**Body:** Could someone fall here? Could something here burn, or catch fire? Could something here poison, choke, or strangle? Could something here fall, cut, or crush? Could water and electricity meet?

---

## Slide 9: The rule

**Headline:** Find it, and fix it now.

**Body:** A hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled. It is scheduled. And a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard. The only safe place for a danger is gone.

---

## Slide 10: Carry tape, not a notepad

**Headline:** The list is not the fix.

**Body:** A written-down hazard is exactly as dangerous as an un-written-down one. Carry a roll of grip tape, a few latches, and an empty box, not a notepad, and let finding and fixing be one motion.

---

## Slide 11: A hunt, worked

**Headline:** The fix is usually smaller than the worry was.

**Body:** Pot handle turned in. Blind cord cleated up. Bookcase strapped to the wall. Lamp cord out from under the rug. Space heater moved from the curtain. Ordinary objects, quiet second jobs, gone.
