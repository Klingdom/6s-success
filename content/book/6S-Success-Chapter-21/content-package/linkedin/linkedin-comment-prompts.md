# Chapter 21 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Find and Remove Hazards

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, calm, anti-shame voice, reassurance and permission not alarm: a home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space, and the fix is a short set of questions carried room by room, not guilt or fear. No graphic injury, no scary numbers.

---

1. A first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look. A hunt finds the ones that never will be, and most of what can quietly hurt a person at home lives in that second group: the cord under the rug for three years, the bookcase that reads as furniture, the remote with a loose battery cover. When you slow down and look at one room on purpose, what is the ordinary object you have walked past for years that is quietly doing a dangerous second job?

2. Here is the whole method in one line: go room by room, ask the same few questions in each, fix what you find before you leave. The five questions are, could someone fall, could something burn, could something poison or choke or strangle, could something fall or cut or crush, could water and electricity meet. Pick the room you are sitting near right now and ask all five. Which question lit something up first?

3. The rule is find it, and fix it now, because a hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled, it is scheduled, and a hazard on a schedule is still able to hurt someone before its turn comes up. Be honest with me: what is the small safety fix that has been living on your to-do list for months, and what would it actually take to just do it today?

4. The common trap is walking the home with a notepad, listing hazards to deal with later, and calling that safety. The list is not the fix. The trick is to carry a roll of tape and an empty box instead, and let finding and fixing be one motion. When you have tried to make your home safer, did you make a list, or did you fix as you went, and which one actually got things handled?

5. Try this: hunt one room twice, once standing and once crouched down to a small child's height. A whole layer of hazards lives in the band of space adults never occupy, the outlet at ankle level, the tablecloth a toddler can pull, the cleaning spray under the sink. Have you ever looked at your own home from down there? What did you find that you had never once noticed from standing height?
