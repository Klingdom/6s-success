# Teleprompter Script: Find and Remove Hazards

**Chapter 21, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** find-and-remove-hazards
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Last time you opened Safety.

You took the safety lens for a first walk.

It showed you a few real dangers, and you handled them.

The smoke alarm. The cleaning bottles. The rug at the top of the stairs.

You felt the relief of a worry set down.

Here is the honest next thing.

The dangers you found were the obvious ones.

And the obvious hazards are the minority.

A first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look.

A hunt finds the ones that never will be.

Most of what can quietly hurt a person at home is hidden.

Because a glance is not built to catch it.

The cord that has run under the rug for three years will not announce itself now.

The bookcase that has never once fallen reads, to a glance, as furniture.

That is not a failure of care. It is how familiarity works.

So you stop trusting the glance.

And you run a method instead.

Here is the method.

A hazard hunt is a calm, room-by-room walk through the whole home.

You ask the same short set of questions in every space.

And you remove each hazard before you move on.

Three things make it a hunt, not another glance.

It is systematic. Room by room, in an order, so no space is skipped.

It is fixed. The same short list carried into every room.

And it ends in action. You fix what you find before you leave.

Keep it this simple.

Go room by room. Ask the same few questions in each.

Fix what you find before you leave.

The method is almost aggressively boring.

And that is the point. Boring is what thorough looks like.

What the hunt defeats is camouflage.

The dangerous thing is rarely the ugly one.

It is the ordinary object doing a second, quiet job.

The lamp cord that is also a tripwire.

The old remote that is also a battery a small child can reach.

The space heater that is also a flame a foot from the curtain.

Here are the five questions you carry into every room.

Could someone fall here.

Could something here burn, or catch fire.

Could something here poison, choke, or strangle.

Could something here fall, cut, or crush.

Could water and electricity meet.

Five questions. Ask all five, in every room.

Each one is a different lens.

Fall looks down and at the edges.

Burn looks for heat near things that burn.

Poison, choke, or strangle looks low, for the small and the looped.

Fall, cut, or crush looks up, at what is heavy and free.

Water and electricity looks in the wet rooms.

Now the rule. Find it, and fix it now.

There is a temptation to find now and fix later.

To walk the home with a notepad and come back.

Resist it.

That turns a hazard hunt back into a hazard museum.

A hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled.

It is scheduled.

And a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard.

The only safe place for a danger is gone.

So carry a roll of tape and a box, not a notepad.

Let finding and fixing be one motion.

The fix is usually smaller than the worry was.

A cord tucked. A bottle moved up. A latch fitted. A rug taped.

When a full fix is bigger than now, the rule still holds.

Make it safe now, even if you cannot make it finished now.

Let me walk it with you, room by room.

Kitchen. Pot handle turned in. Lighter up. Knives back from the edge.

Bathroom. Hairdryer away from the basin. A grip mat. A nightlight.

Living room. Bookcase strapped to the wall.

The remote's loose battery cover taped shut.

The lamp cord out from under the rug.

Stairs. Runner tacked flat. A nightlight at the top.

Child's room. Blind cord cleated up out of reach. Small parts moved up.

Launch pad. Heater moved from the curtain. Cords tucked.

Nothing needed a clean or a rearrange. It was already all three.

It just, in small ways, could have hurt someone. Now it cannot.

Now go look at the friction meter.

It has moved again, for the same reason, only more so.

The first step removed a few dangers. This one removed a home's worth.

And again, nothing got cleaner or tidier.

What dropped is the home's exposure to harm.

The deepest kind of friction there is. The friction of the worst day.

The step is a touch larger, and still measured. Both, on purpose.

The goal is still ahead. Safety completes only when it is made to last.

So here is your job.

Pick one room. Give it a full hunt right now.

Ask the five questions. Fix everything you can in ten minutes.

One room, thoroughly safe, today.
