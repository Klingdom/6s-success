# Chapter 21 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- A first pass catches the dangers that are obvious the moment you finally look, and walks straight past the rest, and the rest are the majority; a first pass finds the dangers that are obvious the moment you look, a hunt finds the ones that never will be, and most of what can quietly hurt a person at home lives in the second group
- Familiarity is why the hidden hazards hide: the things we live with become invisible to us, and the dangerous ones become invisible right along with the harmless ones; the cord under the rug will not announce itself, and the unanchored bookcase reads, to a glance, as furniture
- A glance is not built to catch a hidden hazard, so the only reliable way to find the rest is to stop relying on what catches your eye and run a method instead
- The hero is the hazard hunt: a calm, systematic walk through the whole home, one room at a time, asking the same short set of safety questions in every space and removing each hazard you find before you move on; three things make it a hunt, not another glance, it is systematic, it is fixed, and it ends in action
- The method, frozen: go room by room; ask the same few questions in each; fix what you find before you leave. It is almost aggressively boring, and that is the point, because boring is what thorough looks like
- The five safety questions, frozen, home-scaled from the source's Steps to Complete Safety: could someone fall here; could something here burn, or catch fire; could something here poison, choke, or strangle; could something here fall, cut, or crush; could water and electricity meet. Each is a different lens; you do not memorize categories, only ask all five in every room
- What the hunt defeats is camouflage: the dangerous thing is rarely the ugly one, it is the ordinary object doing a quiet second job you never gave it, the lamp cord that is also a tripwire, the old remote that is also a reachable battery, the space heater that is also a flame a foot from the curtain
- The rule is find it, and fix it now: the found-not-fixed reframe, a hazard found but not fixed is not handled, it is scheduled, and a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard; the only safe place for a danger is gone; carry a roll of tape and a box, not a notepad
- The honest exception: now and then a full fix is genuinely bigger than now, so make it safe now even if you cannot make it finished now, reduce the danger to near nothing immediately and schedule only the part that truly cannot be done on the spot
- A worked room-by-room sweep pays off Chapter 20's teasers: the button battery in the old remote, the space heater a foot from the curtain, the cord run under the rug, found and removed here
- The friction meter takes a SECOND Safety step, a little larger than the first because the whole home was hunted rather than glanced at; nothing got cleaner or tidier, but the home's exposure to harm dropped again, the deepest kind of friction there is, with the goal still ahead

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the second chapter of Part Six and it CONTINUES Safety, the fourth S, made thorough. It USES the finished home the first three S's built and the safety lens Chapter 20 opened, but it does not re-open any of them. Do not re-teach cleaning or inspection (Chapters 16 to 18) or the reset (Chapter 19); the hunt is a different pass over the same home.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 20, Protect People First): the safety lens ("could this hurt someone?"), the protect-people-first triage, Heinrich's pyramid, the 5S+1 history, and safety-is-love-not-fear are already done. Chapter 21 USES the lens and the triage; a brief one-line callback is fine, a re-teach or re-derivation is not.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 22, Improve Overall Safety): the ongoing, lasting, forward-looking layer belongs there, the safeguards that PREVENT new hazards, emergency PREPAREDNESS (an exit plan, extinguishers, detectors run as a maintained system), and the home versions of safety habit, training, and review. Chapter 21 finds and removes the hazards ALREADY in the home; it may note once and lightly that keeping safe is the next chapter's job, but it does NOT build any lasting system or preparedness here.
- CRITICAL fence (Standardize and Sustain, later S's): do NOT write documented standards (Standardize) or long-term adherence systems (Sustain). Chapter 21 only makes the fourth S thorough.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space; the hunt is a method, not a verdict on the reader. Keep it calm, loving attention and relief, not lecture.
- Safety is love, not fear: no alarmist statistics, no graphic injury, no fear-mongering. No hype words and no force or violence framing of the hunt; "eliminate" and "hunt" stay calm and non-violent (you hunt for hazards the way you hunt for your keys; you remove a hazard the way you remove a typo). Nothing is attacked, blitzed, blasted, waged war on, or purged. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "a cabinet latch" or "grip tape", not a brand); teach the hunt and the five questions, not a shopping list.
- The source's Safety principles (identify and eliminate hazards; address all possible safety issues immediately) and the Steps to Complete Safety are drawn from the source, home-scaled into the hunt and the five questions, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed.
- The friction-meter movement is specific to Ch 21: it takes a SECOND Safety step, a little past Chapter 20's first-Safety-step position and a little larger than it, because the whole home was hunted rather than glanced at, so more hazards are gone; nothing got cleaner or tidier, but the home's exposure to harm dropped again. Be honest that this is a real but measured STEP (not a new milestone, not arriving); Chapter 20's first-Safety-step position is now the dashed ghost just behind, Chapter 19's Shine-complete milestone sits further back, and the goal is still ahead on the green calm side, because Safety completes only when it is made to last (Chapter 22) and Standardize and Sustain still follow.
- Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical; point syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium) back to it.
- Keep the framing plain: "the hazard hunt", "the five questions", "find it, and fix it now", "go room by room", "fix what you find before you leave", "make it safe now". A first pass finds what is obvious; a hunt finds what is hidden.
