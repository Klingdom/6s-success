# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 21

**"Find and Remove Hazards"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Six · Safety*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_21_final.html`: the
whole-home cutaway seen at once, a few green handled dots from Chapter 20's first pass and many red
hazard-marks on ordinary objects the hunt still has to catch; the five-questions card, each frozen
question in a slate heading beside the hidden hazards it surfaces; the room-by-room hunt, a home
walked in order with each hazard drawn as a red mark turning into a green fixed-dot and a fixed-count
growing along the route; and the friction meter, which takes its SECOND small Safety step this
chapter, a little further off Chapter 19's marked SHINE COMPLETE milestone toward calm, with the goal
still ahead.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`
(handled and fixed dots), friction spark / hazard-mark red `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`.
Incidental fills used in the figures: green wash `#EDF1E4`, gauge track `#EDE3CE`, cream `#FCF8F0`,
quiet fill `#F3EEE2`, wood-warm `#EFE7D6`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the SECOND chapter of Part Six, and it CONTINUES Safety, the fourth S. It
does one turn: it takes the safety lens Chapter 20 opened and makes it THOROUGH, moving the book from a
first glance at the obvious dangers to a calm, room-by-room hunt that finds the hidden many and removes
each on the spot. It teaches the thoroughness reframe (a first pass finds the obvious, a hunt finds the
rest, and the rest are the majority), the hazard hunt itself (systematic, fixed set of questions,
ending in action), the five frozen safety questions and the family of hidden hazard each one surfaces,
the rule find-it-and-fix-it-now (the found-not-fixed reframe: a hazard found but not fixed is scheduled,
not handled, with the honest amendment that when a full fix is bigger than now you make it safe now and
schedule only the rest), the camouflage reframe (the dangerous thing is the ordinary object doing a
quiet second job), a worked whole-home sweep that pays off Chapter 20's teasers, and the second Safety
step on the meter. It USES the finished, cared-for home from Shine and the first pass from Chapter 20,
and does NOT re-teach Sort, Straighten, or Shine, and does NOT re-teach the safety lens or the triage,
which Chapter 20 already opened. CRITICAL fence: this is the THOROUGH hunt for hidden hazards, NOT the
opening first pass on the obvious (Chapter 20, done, shown here only as the few green handled dots), NOT
the work of making safety LAST or preparing calmly for emergencies (Chapter 22 · Improve Overall
Safety, teaser and handoff only, which completes the fourth S), and NOT the documented standard-work of
Standardize or the long-term adherence of Sustain (later S's, named only as still to come). It opens no
safety tool beyond the hunt and its five questions. Because a whole home's worth of hidden dangers were
removed, not because anything was cleaned or tidied, the friction needle takes its SECOND small Safety
step off the marked Shine-complete milestone, honoring the book's rule that the meter moves only when
the home really changes; the step is a touch larger than the first because the hunt cleared more than a
glance did, and it is still measured, and the goal is still ahead. Separator discipline: labels use the
middot, for example `CHAPTER 21 · SAFETY`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words.
Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: room-by-room, first-pass, hazard-hunt, hazard-mark,
fixed-dot, found-not-fixed, fix-it-now, button-battery, blind-cord, anti-tip, home-scaled, whole-home,
line-and-flat-color, second-job, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, Sort-complete, launch-pad,
child-height, worst-day. Anti-shame throughout: a home holds hidden hazards because ordinary, useful,
tidy things quietly do a dangerous second job and have become furniture to the eye, not because anyone
was negligent; the fix is a method run on purpose, not vigilance or blame. Safety is love, not fear, so
no diagram carries an alarm tone, an injury statistic, or graphic harm; the register is calm, warm, and
reassuring. No force or violence framing anywhere; no war on hazards, no blitz, no purge; the whole move
is one calm walk, the same five questions asked in every room, each danger fixed the moment it is found.
No hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements; teach the habit, not a shopping
list.

## 1. The Whole Home, Hunted (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole premise in one image. Chapter 20 handled the obvious few, and
they are the small handful of green handled-dots. Scattered through every room, quiet and easy to miss,
are far more red hazard-marks, and they sit on perfectly ordinary objects a single glance never reached.
A finished home, already cleared and arranged and clean, still holds one or two hidden dangers in every
room, and finding them takes a hunt. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A warm cutaway of the whole home, several rooms visible at once, the kitchen and the
living room and the stairs and a bathroom and a child's room and the entry, all drawn lovely and
cared-for. A few things carry small green handled-dots, the marks of Chapter 20's first pass: a smoke
alarm with a lit indicator, a rug taped down at the top of the stairs, a latched cupboard under the
sink. But scattered through every room, quiet and easy to miss, are many more small red hazard-marks,
and they sit on ordinary objects: a remote control on the sofa arm holding a button battery, a looped
blind cord by the window, a tall bookcase standing free of the wall, a small heater near a curtain, an
outlet with too many plugs crowded into it, a lamp cord running under the edge of a rug, a pot handle
turned out over the front of the stove. The green dots are few and the red marks are many.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm daylight wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint
  Newsreader italic line across the top, muted: `a finished home · glanced at once, now hunted`.
- A single ink floor line across the base, faint `#E2D8C4` room dividers, the whole home read as a
  warm cutaway of several rooms at once, lived-in rather than staged.
- A few small green handled-dots on Chapter 20's first-pass finds, each with a muted Newsreader italic
  tag: the smoke alarm in cream `#FCF8F0` with a lit green indicator, `alarm checked · last chapter`;
  a rug taped at the stair top, `rug taped · last chapter`; a latched low cupboard in wood-warm
  `#EFE7D6`, `cupboard latched · last chapter`. Keep these few and quiet, the obvious already handled.
- Many small spark-red hazard-marks on ordinary objects, one or two per room, each with a muted italic
  line: living room, a remote on the sofa arm, `a remote · a battery in reach`; a tall bookcase free of
  the wall, `a bookcase · tall, heavy, unanchored`; a lamp cord under the rug edge, `a cord · under the
  rug`; window, a looped blind cord, `a blind cord · looped at child-height`; launch pad, a small
  heater by a curtain, `a heater · a foot from the curtain`; an outlet crowded with plugs, `an outlet ·
  too many plugs`; kitchen, a pot handle turned out over the stove front, `a pot handle · turned out`.
- A small sans caps line low across the scene, terracotta: `THE OBVIOUS FEW ARE HANDLED · THE HIDDEN
  MANY ARE NOT`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "The green dots are last chapter's first pass: the obvious dangers,
  handled. The red marks are everything else, the hazards wearing the disguise of ordinary objects, one
  or two in every room. There are far more of them than there were of the obvious kind, and finding them
  takes more than a glance. It takes a hunt."

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is the ratio, so draw only a small handful of green
handled-dots and clearly many more red hazard-marks, so the eye reads at once that the obvious were the
few and the hidden are the many. Hold the spark-red discipline: the red hazard-marks are the only
warm-red on the image, used to mark the quiet dangers on ordinary objects, never to dramatize gore or a
mess; nothing here is dirty or misplaced, and the home is genuinely finished and clean. Draw no injury
and no accident and no person at fault; the hazards are patient, not happening, and any child in frame
is calm and safe. The register is a thorough second look at a good home, not a warning poster. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, the register of a calm, complete noticing.

## 2. The Five Questions (the portable tool, one card)

**Purpose:** Give the reader the whole portable tool on one page: the five frozen questions and,
concretely, the family of hidden hazard each one is built to surface, so the list is usable in any room.
Five questions, and between them they catch the great majority of what hurts people at home. Mirrors the
five-questions infographic in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A clean card titled "The Five Questions · ask in every room". Five rows, each with
the frozen question in a bold slate heading and, beside it, two or three small icons or short labels of
the hidden hazards it surfaces. At the foot, in terracotta, the rule: Find it, and fix it now.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a Fraunces slate title `The Five Questions`, with a small
  sans caps subtitle, letter-spaced, soft gray: `ASK IN EVERY ROOM`.
- Five rows, each a slate-bordered strip, the frozen question set in a bold slate Fraunces heading and,
  beside it, small sketched icons or short italic labels of the hidden hazards it surfaces:
  - Row 1, `Could someone fall here?` : a sliding rug, a cluttered stair tread, a wet floor by the tub.
  - Row 2, `Could something here burn, or catch fire?` : a heater by a curtain, an overloaded outlet, a
    candle under a shelf, dryer lint.
  - Row 3, `Could something here poison, choke, or strangle?` : chemicals and medicine in reach, button
    batteries and small parts, a looped blind cord.
  - Row 4, `Could something here fall, cut, or crush?` : an unanchored tall bookcase, a heavy box up
    high, knives at the counter edge.
  - Row 5, `Could water and electricity meet?` : a hairdryer by the basin, a frayed cord near the sink.
- At the foot, a terracotta strip carrying the frozen rule in Fraunces: `Find it, and fix it now.`
- Caption (from the chapter): "The same five questions, carried into every room. Each one is a
  different lens on the same space, and each finds a family of hazard the others miss. Ask all five
  everywhere, and the hidden dangers stop being able to hide."

**Note for the illustrator:** each question is a different lens, so give each row its own small family
of hidden hazards and keep them ordinary and calm, sketched rather than clinical. Set the five frozen
questions byte-for-byte as they read in the chapter, each ending in its question mark; they are the
frozen set and must not be trimmed or reworded. Hold the color discipline: slate for the five headings,
one warm terracotta for the rule strip at the foot, the small hazard icons drawn in ink and soft gray
with spark-red reserved only for the smallest hazard-marks, green nowhere here except an optional fixed
tick. No injury imagery, no scare; the register is a clean, usable tool card, the whole hunt on one
page. Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate headings, the register of a calm checklist.

## 3. A Room-by-Room Hunt (the walk, found turning to fixed)

**Purpose:** Show a complete hunt as it actually runs: a calm route through the whole home, a small
number of very ordinary hazards found in each room, each one fixed on the spot before moving to the
next, so the reader sees how ordinary the finds are and how quickly each one closes. Mirrors the
room-by-room infographic in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A simple warm floor-plan of a home, or an ordered vertical list of rooms, walked in
sequence: Kitchen, Bathroom, Living Room, Stairs and Hall, Child's Room, Launch Pad. In each room, one
or two representative hazards are shown found and removed, drawn with a small red hazard-mark turning
into a green fixed-dot. A green count of fixed grows as the route goes on.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `ONE HOME
  · WALKED ROOM BY ROOM · FIXED BEFORE MOVING ON`.
- A warm floor-plan or a numbered vertical route, six rooms in order, each a small slate-labeled panel,
  each carrying its one or two finds drawn as a red hazard-mark with an arrow to a green fixed-dot and a
  short Newsreader italic line:
  - `1 · Kitchen` : a pot handle turned in, a lighter moved up high. `turned in · moved up`.
  - `2 · Bathroom` : the hairdryer moved away from the basin, a grip mat down. `away from water · grip
    down`.
  - `3 · Living Room` : the bookcase strapped to the wall, the remote battery taped shut, the lamp cord
    out from under the rug. `strapped · taped · rerouted`.
  - `4 · Stairs and Hall` : the runner tacked flat, a nightlight in. `tacked flat · lit`.
  - `5 · Child's Room` : the blind cord cleated up, small parts moved high. `cleated up · moved high`.
  - `6 · Launch Pad` : the space heater moved from the curtain, the cords tucked. `moved clear ·
    tucked`.
- A running green count along the route, a small `fixed` tally that grows room by room, so the eye
  reads the hunt accumulating; each fixed-dot in calm green, each pre-fix hazard-mark in spark-red with
  the arrow between them.
- Caption (from the chapter): "One home, walked room by room, each hazard fixed before moving on. None
  of these were messes; they were ordinary objects doing a quiet second job. The hunt is just the
  discipline of visiting every room and asking, and the fix, nearly always, is smaller than the worry
  was."

**Note for the illustrator:** the motion is the message, so draw each hazard as a red mark with a clear
arrow to a green fixed-dot, found becoming fixed in one step, never a red mark left standing at the end.
Keep the route calm and ordered and the finds ordinary and small; this is a doable afternoon, not a
crisis. Hold the color discipline: spark-red for the pre-fix hazard-marks only, calm green for the
fixed-dots and the growing count, slate for the room labels and the route line, terracotta reserved for
an optional `find it, and fix it now` footer. No injury imagery, no scare, no mess; every find is an
ordinary object doing a quiet second job, and every one closes on the spot. Warm line-and-flat-color
style, slate labels, the register of a calm, complete walk.

## 4. Found Is Not Fixed (the notepad and the box, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's hardest discipline as a picture: a hazard you have found but not
fixed is not handled, it is scheduled, and a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard. This diagram has
no direct HTML figure twin; it renders the "Find It, and Fix It Now" section as a calm contrast between
the notepad that feels like progress and the box that actually is, and it carries the honest amendment
(make it safe now, finish it later) without turning into a scare.

**What it shows:** Two calm facing panels of the same found hazard, read left to right as the two ways
you can respond. Panel one, THE NOTEPAD: a hand writes the hazard onto a list, and the hazard itself,
an ordinary object with its small red hazard-mark, sits exactly where it was, unchanged, still live,
above it a muted line reading that a written-down danger is only scheduled. Panel two, THE BOX: the same
hand fixes the hazard on the spot, the red hazard-mark turning to a green fixed-dot, a roll of tape and
an empty box beside it instead of a notepad, the danger simply gone. A small third strip carries the
honest amendment: for the rare hazard whose full fix is bigger than now, make it safe now and schedule
only the rest.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `FIND IT, AND FIX IT NOW`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath carrying
  the section's spine: "Between finding a danger and removing it there is a gap, and everything bad a
  hazard can do, it can do in that gap."
- Two warm cream `#FCF8F0` panels with ink outlines, left to right, each headed with a small slate tab.
  Panel `THE NOTEPAD`: a hand writing a hazard onto a list, the ordinary object still carrying its
  spark-red hazard-mark, unchanged, a muted Newsreader italic note, `found, and still live · only
  scheduled`. Panel `THE BOX`: the same object, the hazard-mark now a green fixed-dot, a roll of grip
  tape, a few latches, and an empty box beside it, a muted italic note, `found, and gone · handled`.
- Beneath, a slate-bordered strip carrying the honest amendment in ink: "make it safe now, even if you
  cannot make it finished now", with a small worked example in muted italic, `move the toys off the
  unanchored bookcase today · buy the bracket this week`.
- To the right, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `CHAPTER 22 · NEXT` in muted honey sans
  caps, a muted italic note beneath, `Safety made to last: the safeguards that keep new hazards from
  taking hold`, with two fainter panel outlines behind it, unlabeled, for Standardize and Sustain. Hold
  the whole right group quiet and low-contrast, clearly ahead, not yet reached.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "The list is not the fix. The only safe place for a danger is gone."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the discipline at the heart of the chapter, so keep it calm and
plain, never a scare; the point is not guilt about the notepad but the simple fact that a written-down
hazard is exactly as dangerous as an un-written-down one, so finding and fixing are one motion. Draw the
notepad panel honest and even a little tempting, it feels like progress, and make the box panel visibly
lighter, the hazard gone. Hold the color discipline: spark-red for the still-live hazard-mark on the
notepad side, calm green for the fixed-dot on the box side, slate for the tabs and the amendment strip,
honey for the dim Chapter 22 panel; no spark-red on the resolved box side. Keep the Chapter 22 panel
dim, unlit, and clearly ahead; you may name it as next, but teach nothing of it, no lasting-safety
method, only that Safety gets made to last. The register is calm resolve, not alarm: a danger is only
handled once it is gone.
