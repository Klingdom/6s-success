## Beat 3 · From a glance to a search

Why the hidden hazards hide: familiarity makes the dangerous objects invisible right along with the harmless ones, so you stop relying on what catches your eye and run a method instead.

- Composition: the two lookings on panel #FBF7EF: the identical living room drawn twice. On the LEFT, headed "A glance," the room reads calm and ordinary, the tall bookcase reading as plain furniture, the lamp cord invisible under the rug, no marks. On the RIGHT, headed "A hunt," the same objects sit unmoved but now carry red hazard marks #CB4B36: the unanchored bookcase, the lamp cord under the rug, the old remote on the arm.
- Palette: paper ground, slate #3C5A6B on the panel frames and the two headings, spark red #CB4B36 on the three hazards lit in the right panel. Calm and legible at a glance.
- Caption, from the book: "The bookcase that has never once fallen reads, to a glance, as furniture, not as a tall heavy thing that is not attached to anything."
- Point: a first pass works by noticing what stands out, and the eye doing the scan is the same eye that has looked at this room ten thousand times and long since stopped seeing most of what is in it. The cord that has run under the rug for three years will not announce itself now. This is not a failure of care; it is just how familiarity works, and the dangerous things become invisible right along with the harmless ones. So the only reliable way to find the hidden hazards is to stop relying on what catches your eye and start running a method.
- The line runs as a Fraunces pull-quote: "You do not wait to notice a hazard. You go looking for it, deliberately, in every room, asking the same questions you would ask in a room you had never seen before." ## Beat 7 · Find it, and fix it now

The rule, the pivot of the whole method: finding and fixing are one motion, and the gap between them is where everything bad a hazard can do gets done.

- Composition: the rule set large in Fraunces terracotta #BC4B2A on panel #FBF7EF, "Find it, and fix it now," slate #3C5A6B rules. Beside it, a hand turning a pot handle in, a strip of grip tape going under a rug, a bottle moving to a high shelf, each a red hazard mark #CB4B36 becoming a green fixed-dot #6E8B5B in one motion.
- Palette: paper ground, terracotta #BC4B2A on the rule, spark red #CB4B36 on the hazards being handled, green fixed-dot #6E8B5B on the ones just closed off. Warm; steady hands, no drama.
- Slate #3C5A6B label: "finding and fixing are one motion."
- Point: the temptation is to find now and fix later, walking the whole home with a notepad, and you resist it, because that is the one move that quietly turns a hazard hunt back into a hazard museum. A written-down hazard is exactly as dangerous as an un-written-down one; the child does not know the button battery is on your list, the fire does not check whether you meant to move the heater this weekend. Between finding a danger and removing it there is a gap, and everything bad a hazard can do, it can do in that gap, so you close the gap to nothing. Most of what you find will yield, because most home hazards are cheap and quick to remove and the fix is usually smaller than the worry was.
- The frozen found-not-fixed reframe sits as a Fraunces pull-quote on panel #FBF7EF with slate #3C5A6B rules: "A hazard you have found but not fixed is not handled. It is scheduled. And a hazard on a schedule is still a hazard, still able to hurt someone on any ordinary day before its turn comes up. The only safe place for a danger is gone." The 6S Tip sits in a slate #3C5A6B bordered callout, verbatim: "Hunt every room twice, once standing and once low. Crouch to a small child's height and look again: the outlet at ankle level, the tablecloth a toddler can pull, the cleaning spray under the sink, the wobbling table leg. A whole layer of hazards lives in the band of space adults never occupy." The Quick Win sits in a green #6E8B5B bordered callout, verbatim: "Pick one room and give it a full hunt right now. Ask the five questions, and fix everything you can in ten minutes before you walk out: the cord tucked away, the pills moved up high, the rug taped down, the heavy thing brought off the top shelf. One room, thoroughly safe, today."
