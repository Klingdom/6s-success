## The room in one line
The kids bedroom is the first space a child runs themselves, so you build it and clean it at their height and their reading level, with them beside you, and short enough that a child can finish, so the room mostly holds itself.

## The kids-bedroom Shine layer (nine rules from Ch 31 PLUS)
- **Their height, their reading level**: every hook, rod, and bin works at the child's reach, and every label is a picture a child who cannot read can follow.
- **With them, not for them**: do the dull cleaning alone, but make every keep-or-go call with the child beside you, because a child keeps only what a child chose.
- **The two-song clean**: keep the child's share short enough to finish while their attention lasts; floor first.
- **A room a small child uses alone**: choke-sized things up high, blind cords cleated, tall furniture strapped, the dark path clear.

## Six zones (start on the floor, out of the toy storage zone)
1. **Bed and Sleep Zone** - wind down and sleep only; strip and wash, vacuum and turn the mattress, clear the strip underneath, keep the pillow-to-door lane clear.
2. **Toy Storage Zone** - picture-labelled open bins at shoulder height; wash the hard toys, hunt the small parts in the skirting corners, a dated rotation box up high.
3. **Study Desk** - one clear surface homework starts on; take the top to bare wood, lift glue and marker, clean the cable nest and casters.
4. **Clothing Closet** - a lowered rod a child dresses from; clean top-down while emptied, the grey rod line, the back wall, one next-size box up high.
5. **Dresser Drawers** - picture labels, one category each, closes with one push; pull the drawers out, clean the runners, strap it to the wall.
6. **School and Activity Launch Zone** - tomorrow assembled the night before; wash the lunchbox seals and the bottle lid where mould hides, clear the 7am lane.

## FROZEN STRINGS (byte-verbatim in manuscript AND final.html)

### Six zones
The kids bedroom is six small zones, not one big job: the bed and sleep zone, the toy storage zone, the study desk, the clothing closet, the dresser drawers, and the school and activity launch zone. Do them one at a time, and start on the floor, because the floor is what everything else in this room is failing onto.

### The rule (build and clean it at the child's height and reading level, with them)
The kids bedroom is the first space a child gets to run themselves, so here you build it and clean it at their height and their reading level rather than yours, and you do it with them beside you. Every hook, rod, and bin has to work for a child's reach and be labelled with a picture a child who cannot yet read can follow, because a room built for your reach is a room you will still be tidying when they are fifteen. And a child keeps only what a child chose, so you do the dull cleaning alone but make every keep-or-go call with them standing next to you, and you keep the clean short enough that they can finish it before their attention runs out.

### The definition (defbox)
A finished kids bedroom is one a child can run and reset themselves: the bed made flat with the sleepers on it and the rest in an open basket, toys in picture-labelled bins no higher than the child's shoulder, a desk clear enough to start homework on, a rod lowered so they reach a hanger without a stool, drawers that close with one push and a picture label on each front, a packed backpack on a low hook, the dresser and the tall furniture strapped to the wall, and a clear path from the pillow to the door to cross in the dark.

### Their-height reframe (build it at their height and reading level)
Every hook, rod, and bin in this room has to work at the child's height and their reading level, not yours. The rod comes down so a five year old reaches a hanger without a stool, the bins sit no higher than their shoulder, the hooks go low, and every label is a photo of what goes there rather than a written word, so a child who cannot yet read can still put the blocks back in the right bin. Build it for your reach and the room never becomes theirs; build it for theirs and a small child can put the room away without calling down the stairs.

### With-them reframe (owned is kept)
A child will not maintain a home they did not choose, and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. So you split the work: you do the dull parts alone, the runners and the skirting corners and the cable nest under the desk, but you make every keep-or-go call with the child standing next to you. Nothing leaves the house the same day it leaves the bed, the drift goes into a dated box for six weeks, and what nobody asks for by name goes quietly. The room they helped decide is the room they will put back.

### Two-song reframe (finish before attention runs out)
A child sustains only what they can finish while their attention lasts, so a clean that outruns them teaches them that cleaning never ends. Make the child's share of Shine a two song job: one song for the surfaces with a damp cloth, one song for picking the floor up, and stop when the music does. The floor comes first, because the floor is what everything else in this room is failing onto, and a job with an end a child can see is a job a child will do again next week.

### Safety reframe (a room a small child uses alone, and a younger sibling reaches into)
This is a room a small child is in without you, and often a younger sibling too, so the hazards here are the ones a curious hand finds. Anything that fits through a toilet roll tube is choke sized and goes up high, out of a crawler's reach, and that includes the loose button battery and the smallest toy parts. A blind or curtain cord within reach of the pillow gets cleated above child height or swapped for a cordless blind, the dresser and any tall unit get strapped to the wall so an opened drawer cannot become a climbing frame that tips, and the path from the pillow to the door stays clear for the half asleep walk to the bathroom.

### Before-and-after caption
This is the kids bedroom the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the floor clear enough to lie down on, the toys in picture-labelled bins a child can reach and return, the rod and hooks lowered to their height, and a clear path from the pillow to the door. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and let the child take the after, because a room a child can run and reset themselves is the one that stays good, and the photo they took is the standard they will keep it to.

### One Idea to Keep
A kids bedroom is not clean because you cleaned it while they were at school. It is clean, and it stays clean, because you built it at their height with picture labels a child can follow, made every keep-or-go call with them beside you, and kept their share of the work short enough to finish. Do one zone this weekend with your child, the toy zone or the floor, and give them a room they can actually run themselves.

## Four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win (win)**: Clear the floor first, working out of the toy storage zone. The strip under the bed, the closet floor, and the space under the desk all get easier to judge once you can see carpet, and the room stops looking like one enormous job. It is also the lane a child crosses in the dark, so it is the one patch that has to end clear.
- **6S Tip (tip)**: Make the child's share a two song job. One song for surfaces with a damp cloth, one song for picking the floor up, and stop when the music does. A child sustains what they can finish while their attention lasts, so a clean with an end they can see is a clean they will do again, while a clean that outruns the music teaches them that cleaning never ends.
- **The Child's-Room Challenge (family)**: Do the dull cleaning alone, but make every keep-or-go call with your child standing next to you, because a child will not maintain a room they did not choose and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. Put the drift in a dated box for six weeks; what nobody asks for by name goes quietly, and what they ask for by name comes back.
- **Common Mistake (mistake)**: Doing the whole room while they are at school because it is quicker, building every bin and rod at your height instead of theirs, and labelling with words a child cannot yet read, so you end up with a tidy room the child cannot run and will not maintain, and you are still tidying it when they are fifteen.

## The calls that matter here
The animals that never get picked (the child picks the sleepers, the drift goes in a dated box for six weeks); the set that gets tipped out and abandoned (rotation-box test: watch what they do with it the afternoon it comes back); the finished schoolwork (one memory box per child per school year, full is full, make room by taking something out); the outgrown thing you are keeping for someone (name the child AND the season, or it is being deferred not stored); how many shirts is enough (days between washes plus two); who actually packs the bag (hand it over against a five line list, check the list not the bag).

## Watch for in the kids bedroom (five safety questions)
- **Poison, choke, or strangle**: anything that fits through a toilet roll tube, and loose button batteries, reachable by a younger sibling; a blind or curtain cord within reach of the pillow; loose medication in a backpack pocket (keep an inhaler or epipen in one named pocket).
- **Fall, cut, or crush**: an unanchored dresser or overloaded closet rod that tips when a drawer is opened or climbed; heavy kit on a top shelf a child pulls down; craft knives and open scissors loose in a desk drawer or point-up in the pen cup.
- **Fall**: a bag, bin, or pile of books between the bed and the door; a jammed drawer that pulls a child off balance; a stool used as a step on a floor that is not clear.
- **Water and electricity**: chained extension strips under the desk feeding a charger, a lamp, and a console; a water bottle on the same end of the desk as the plugs.
- **Burn or fire**: a frayed or warm nightlight flex or laptop charger; a scorched power strip under the desk.

## Kit (product TYPES only)
Key input: a compact vacuum with upholstery, crevice, and brush tools (the mattress, the skirting corners where small parts gather, the drawer runners, the cable nest, the floor a child lies on). Plus a colour-coded microfiber cloth set, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a floor-material-compatible cleaner, a food-area degreaser (desk glue and marker, the lunchbox seals and bottle lid), a small detail brush set, a lint or clothes brush, a portable cleaning caddy. Storage at the child's scale: open toy bins with picture labels, front-facing book bins, a matching child-height hanger set, a low shoe rack, low hooks, a dated rotation box, a memory box, a moisture absorber or humidity monitor, and an anti-tip anchor and hardware for the dresser and any tall unit. Labels are pictures, not words. No harsh chemistry.
