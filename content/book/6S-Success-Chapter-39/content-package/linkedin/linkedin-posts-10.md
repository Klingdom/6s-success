# Chapter 39 LinkedIn Posts: The Kids Bedroom

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. Build it at their height, or tidy it forever
The kids bedroom is the first space a child gets to run themselves, and whether they can is not about willpower. It is about whether the room was built for their reach or for yours.

"A room built for your reach is a room you will still be tidying when they are fifteen, because a child cannot maintain what a child cannot reach, and a child will not maintain a home they did not choose."

## 2. Labels are pictures, not words
A child who cannot yet read cannot use a bin labelled in your tidy handwriting. So every label in this room is a photo of what goes there. Put a picture of blocks on the block bin and a small child can put the blocks back in the right place without asking anyone. Words are for you. Pictures are for them.

## 3. Lower the rod
Your child cannot hang a coat on a rod they cannot reach, so they will not, and you will. Lower the rod so a five year old reaches a hanger without a stool, drop the hooks, and keep the bins no higher than their shoulder. Build it for their reach and a small child can put the room away without calling down the stairs.

## 4. Clean it with them, not for them
It is quicker to clean the whole room while they are at school, and it costs you the point. A child will not maintain a room they did not choose, and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. Do the dull parts alone, but make every keep-or-go call with the child standing next to you.

## 5. Nothing leaves the same day it leaves the bed
When you thin the stuffed animals or the toys, the child picks the keepers and you handle the drift, but nothing leaves the house that day. The rest goes into a dated box in the wardrobe for six weeks. What nobody asks for by name goes quietly; what they ask for by name comes back. The box does the deciding, not the argument.

## 6. Make the clean a two-song job
A child sustains only what they can finish while their attention lasts, so a clean that outruns them teaches them that cleaning never ends. Make their share a two song job: one song for surfaces, one song for the floor, and stop when the music does. A job with an end a child can see is a job they will do again next week.

## 7. Start on the floor
The kids room looks like one enormous job until you clear the floor, because the floor is what everything else has been failing onto. Clear it first, working out of the toy zone, and the strip under the bed, the closet floor, and the space under the desk all get easier to judge. It is also the lane a child crosses in the dark.

## 8. The mould is in the water bottle lid
The dirtiest thing in a kids room is not the floor, it is the water bottle lid and the lunchbox seals, where mould grows unseen. Scrub the lid threads and the drinking valve with a detail brush, run the brush into the lunchbox seal groove, rinse well, and stand both open to dry, never capped or closed wet.

## 9. Strap the dresser
An unanchored chest of drawers tips forward the moment a child opens the drawers and climbs them, and a kids room is exactly where that happens. Fix an anti-tip strap to a wall stud, keep the heavy clothes in the low drawers, and keep the dresser top bare of anything worth climbing for.

## 10. A room a child can actually run
"A kids bedroom is not clean because you cleaned it while they were at school. It is clean, and it stays clean, because you built it at their height with picture labels a child can follow, made every keep-or-go call with them beside you, and kept their share of the work short enough to finish."
