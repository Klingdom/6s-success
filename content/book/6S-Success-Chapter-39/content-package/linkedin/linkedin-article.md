# The Kids Bedroom: A Room a Child Can Run Themselves

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 39 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

The kids bedroom is the first space in the house a child gets to run themselves, and that changes the whole job. Whether a five year old can put their own blocks away, hang their own coat, and find their own socks is not about willpower. It is about whether the room was built for their reach or for yours.

> The kids bedroom is the first space a child gets to run themselves, so here you build it and clean it at their height and their reading level rather than yours, and you do it with them beside you. Every hook, rod, and bin has to work for a child's reach and be labelled with a picture a child who cannot yet read can follow, because a room built for your reach is a room you will still be tidying when they are fifteen. And a child keeps only what a child chose, so you do the dull cleaning alone but make every keep-or-go call with them standing next to you, and you keep the clean short enough that they can finish it before their attention runs out.

## Six zones, not one room

> The kids bedroom is six small zones, not one big job: the bed and sleep zone, the toy storage zone, the study desk, the clothing closet, the dresser drawers, and the school and activity launch zone. Do them one at a time, and start on the floor, because the floor is what everything else in this room is failing onto.

## Build it at their height and reading level

> Every hook, rod, and bin in this room has to work at the child's height and their reading level, not yours. The rod comes down so a five year old reaches a hanger without a stool, the bins sit no higher than their shoulder, the hooks go low, and every label is a photo of what goes there rather than a written word, so a child who cannot yet read can still put the blocks back in the right bin.

## Do it with them, not for them

> A child will not maintain a home they did not choose, and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. So you split the work: you do the dull parts alone, the runners and the skirting corners and the cable nest under the desk, but you make every keep-or-go call with the child standing next to you.

## Keep the clean short enough to finish

> A child sustains only what they can finish while their attention lasts, so a clean that outruns them teaches them that cleaning never ends. Make the child's share of Shine a two song job: one song for the surfaces with a damp cloth, one song for picking the floor up, and stop when the music does.
