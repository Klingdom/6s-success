# Chapter 39 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Kids Bedroom

Single host, warm and practical.

## Cold open
Here is why you are still tidying your child's room every single day: you built it for your reach, not theirs. Today we fix that, and set up a room a child can actually run themselves.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. Today we open the door a child closes, and the whole job changes, because a child, not you, is meant to run this room. Whether a five year old can put their own blocks away is not about willpower, it is about whether the room was built for their reach or yours. So we do three things the other rooms never needed: we build it at the child's height and their reading level, we clean it with them rather than for them, and we keep their share of the work short enough to finish.

## Part 2: The six zones
The room is six zones: the bed and sleep zone, the toy storage zone, the study desk, the clothing closet, the dresser drawers, and the school and activity launch zone. Start on the floor, out of the toy zone, because the floor is what everything else has been failing onto.

## Part 3: Their height and reading level
Everything comes down to the child's scale. The rod lowers so they reach a hanger without a stool. The bins sit no higher than their shoulder. The hooks go low. And every label is a photo of what goes there, not a written word, so a child who cannot yet read can still put the blocks back in the right bin.

## Part 4: With them, not for them
It is quicker to clean the whole room while they are at school, and it costs you the point, because a child will not maintain a room they did not choose, and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. So you do the dull parts alone, the runners and the skirting corners, but you make every keep-or-go call with the child beside you. Nothing leaves the house the day it leaves the bed. The drift goes into a dated box for six weeks, and what nobody asks for by name goes quietly.

## Part 5: The two-song clean
A child sustains only what they can finish while their attention lasts, so a clean that outruns them teaches them that cleaning never ends. Make their share a two song job: one song for surfaces, one song for the floor, and stop when the music does. A job with an end they can see is a job they will do again.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
This is the most important safety pass in the house, because a small child is in here without you. Anything that fits through a toilet roll tube goes up high, button batteries included. Cleat the blind cords, strap the dresser, and keep the path from the bed to the door clear. And the calls are all about ownership: the unslept animals, the tipped-out set, the schoolwork box, who packs the bag.

## Close
A kids bedroom is not clean because you cleaned it while they were at school. It is clean because you built it at their height, sorted it with them, and kept their share short enough to finish. Do one zone this weekend with your child. Next time: the nursery.
