# Facebook Longform Post: A Room a Child Can Run Themselves

A story-driven post from Chapter 39 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the kids bedroom.

---

Here is why you are still tidying your child's room every day: you built it for your reach, not theirs.

The kids bedroom is the first space a child gets to run themselves, and whether they can is not about willpower. It is about height and reading level. A child cannot hang a coat on a rod they cannot reach, and cannot use a bin labelled in your tidy handwriting. So they will not, and you will, right up until they are fifteen.

So this room is built and cleaned differently. Everything comes down to the child's scale. The rod lowers so a five year old reaches a hanger without a stool. The bins sit no higher than their shoulder. The hooks go low. And every label is a photo of what goes there rather than a word, so a child who cannot yet read can still put the blocks back in the right bin.

Then you clean it with them, not for them. It is quicker to do the whole room while they are at school, and it costs you the point, because a child will not maintain a room they did not choose, and they will notice exactly which animal went missing. So you do the dull parts alone, the runners and the skirting corners, but you make every keep-or-go call with the child standing next to you. Nothing leaves the house the day it leaves the bed. The drift goes into a dated box for six weeks, and what nobody asks for by name goes quietly.

And you keep their share short. A child sustains only what they can finish while their attention lasts, so make the reset a two song job: one song for surfaces, one song for the floor, and stop when the music does. A job with an end a child can see is a job they will do again next week.

Do one zone this weekend with your child, the toy zone or the floor, and give them a room they can actually run themselves.

Is your child's rod at their height, or yours?
