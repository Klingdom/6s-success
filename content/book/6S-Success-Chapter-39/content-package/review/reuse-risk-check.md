# Chapter 39 Reuse and Risk Check: The Kids Bedroom

## Safety-claim review
- **Choke and button-battery hazards**: correctly framed and central to the chapter; the toilet-roll-tube test ("anything that fits through a toilet roll tube is choke sized and goes up high") is standard, well-established child-safety guidance, and singling out loose button batteries is accurate and important. PASS.
- **Blind and curtain cords**: correctly flagged as a strangulation risk within reach of the pillow; the fix (cleat above child height or fit a cordless blind) is standard guidance. PASS.
- **Anti-tip dresser**: strapping a chest a child climbs and keeping the top bare of anything worth climbing for are correct, mainstream safety advice. PASS.
- **The dark path, blades in the drawer, chained extension strips, frayed flexes**: all framed as real hazards with safe fixes (clear the path, store blades safely, one strip on one socket, replace a frayed flex). No electrical-repair or medical instruction beyond flag-and-fix. PASS.
- **Backpack medication**: keeping an inhaler or epipen in one named pocket, checked when the bag is packed, is sensible and non-prescriptive. PASS.
