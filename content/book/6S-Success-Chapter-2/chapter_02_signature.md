# Chapter 2 Signature Plan
## "What Is 6S?"
### 6S Success: Home Edition

---

## Signature Overview

**Length:** 16 pages (8 spreads)
**Part:** Part 1, Discovering 6S
**Reading time:** 9 to 12 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 60% text, 40% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Answer the question the title asks, plainly and warmly. Pay off Chapter 1's "factory floor in Japan" promise. Define 5S, 5S+1, and 6S in plain English, give just enough history to be interesting, and reassure the reader that a method born in a factory belongs in a home. Do not deep-teach the six steps. That is Chapter 3.

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapter 1. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data. The green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns. The friction meter appears once more at the close as the book's recurring progress ritual.

**New visual motif introduced:** the "findability" close-up. A space answering your question before you finish asking it. Establish it here with the bottle-opener drawer.

---

## Page-by-Page Layout

### PAGE 1 (recto) | Chapter Opener
- **Spread objective:** Open on a tiny, familiar moment of findability so the abstract question "what is 6S" lands as something the reader has already felt.
- **Layout:** Full-bleed image. Title lower third. Kicker "Part 1 - Discovering 6S."
- **Visual:** [VISUAL: Full-page photo] A top-down shot of a beautifully simple kitchen drawer. A small tray holds a bottle opener, corkscrew, and can opener together. Utensils sorted. Nothing fancy, just instantly readable.
- **Caption:** "Some drawers answer your question. Others just ask more. The difference is a method, and it has a name."

### PAGE 2-3 (Spread A) | A Small Moment of Findability
- **Spread objective:** Drop into the opening scene (the bottle-opener drawer at a friend's house). Name the feeling. Tease the surprising origin.
- **Layout:** Left page narrative. Right page the findability close-up illustration with green calm-dots.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Opening" plus "The Calm You Can Feel," roughly 450 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Hand-drawn top-down drawer] The same drawer as a warm line drawing, green dots marking each item that has a clear home.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > A good space answers questions. A cluttered one asks them. You can feel the difference before you can name it.

### PAGE 4-5 (Spread B) | Where This Actually Came From
- **Spread objective:** Deliver the short, interesting history. Postwar Japan, Toyota, the visual workplace. Keep it light and quickly relevant.
- **Layout:** Text left, a clean horizontal timeline infographic right (or spanning the gutter).
- **Body copy:** "Where This Actually Came From," roughly 400 to 500 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "From Factory Floor to Front Hall." A four-node timeline: Postwar Japan (1950s) - Toyota Production System - Spreads worldwide (hospitals, offices, airlines) - Into the home.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Thinking 6S is "a Japanese cleaning system." Cleaning is one part. The real point is findability, flow, and safety.

### PAGE 6-7 (Spread C) | The Five S's in Plain English
- **Spread objective:** Define the original five, map the Japanese words to the English ones, and explain why the English names wobble between books.
- **Layout:** Short intro text, then a full bilingual mapping diagram dominating the spread.
- **Body copy:** "The Five S's, in Plain English," roughly 350 to 450 words plus the five one-liners.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "Five Japanese Words, Five English Ones." Seiri to Sort, Seiton to Straighten, Seiso to Shine, Seiketsu to Standardize, Shitsuke to Sustain, each with a one-line home meaning.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > If you ever blank on a name, hold on to the order instead. The sequence is the method. The labels are just handles.

### PAGE 8-9 (Spread D) | How Five Became Six
- **Spread objective:** Explain 5S+1 and 6S. Why Safety was added at work, and why it matters even more at home.
- **Layout:** Text left, "growth" diagram right.
- **Body copy:** "How Five Became Six," roughly 350 to 450 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "How 5S Became 6S." Stage one, five tiles. Stage two, a Safety tile slides in. Stage three, the full set of six. Safety highlighted in slate.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Walk one path you take in the dark, bedroom to bathroom. Anything you would trip on? You just did your first thirty seconds of the Safety S.

### PAGE 10-11 (Spread E) | Your Home Is Not a Factory
- **Spread objective:** The reassurance spread. Borrow the logic, not the aesthetic. A home should feel like a home.
- **Layout:** Text with a centered split illustration.
- **Body copy:** "Your Home Is Not a Factory (and That Is the Point)," roughly 400 to 500 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Hand-drawn split] Left, a factory bench with floor tape and a shadow board. Right, a warm kitchen counter with a plant, a fruit bowl, a mug. The same green calm-dots glow under both. Caption: "Same logic underneath. Completely different feeling on top."
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Believing organized means bare or sterile. 6S serves the life in your home. It does not erase it.

### PAGE 12-13 (Spread F) | Same Method, Any Size
- **Spread objective:** Show that the six steps scale. One method, pointed anywhere from a drawer to a garage.
- **Layout:** A scale diagram across the spread, light text framing it.
- **Body copy:** "Same Method, Any Size," roughly 300 to 400 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "One Method, Any Scale." Four nested targets: a drawer, a shelf, a room, a garage, each stamped with the same small six-step loop badge.
- **Callout:**
  > **Family Challenge**
  > Each person names the most findable spot in the house and the least findable. Loser's pick becomes a future project. Winner gets bragging rights and possibly the last cookie.

### PAGE 14-15 (Spread G) | Putting It Together
- **Spread objective:** Consolidate. A reader should be able to say, in one breath, what 6S is and why it works at home.
- **Layout:** A tight summary section, a "say it in one sentence" boxed definition, and a small recap of the six words.
- **Body copy:** Short synthesis, roughly 250 to 350 words.
- **Boxed definition:**
  > **6S in one sentence**
  > A simple, repeatable method for making the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe, in any space, and keeping it that way.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Open any drawer right now and ask one question: does this drawer answer me, or interrogate me? That instinct is all six S's talking at once.

### PAGE 16 (verso) | Chapter Close and What's Next
- **Spread objective:** Land warmly and tee up Chapter 3.
- **Layout:** Short close, "One Idea to Keep" box, friction-meter spot, "Next" strip.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Close," roughly 200 to 300 words.
- **Boxed recap:**
  > **One Idea to Keep**
  > 6S was built so ordinary people could do good work under pressure. That is also a pretty good description of running a home.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Friction-meter spot] The meter, holding steady, with a note that understanding loads the spring and the next chapters release it.
- **Next teaser:** "Next: Chapter 3 - The Six Steps That Transform Any Space. We walk the whole loop once, start to finish, so you can see how each step hands off to the next."
