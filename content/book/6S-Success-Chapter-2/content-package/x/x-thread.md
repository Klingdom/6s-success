1/
You ask a friend for the bottle opener. You open the drawer and it's right there, lined up with the corkscrew and can opener. No digging. That easy two seconds isn't luck. Someone set the drawer up to behave that way. There's a name for how.
(~241 chars)

2/
What you felt in that drawer wasn't tidiness. A drawer can be neat and still useless. What you felt was findability: a space answering your question before you finish asking it. You only really notice it when it's missing.
(~222 chars)

3/
Findability is the quiet thing under every space that feels good to use. The towel where your wet hand expects it. The scissors you reach for without looking. The charger in one known spot. None of it announces itself. You just stop fighting your own house.
(~257 chars)

4/
Here's the strange part. The method behind that drawer wasn't built for kitchens. It came from a car factory. Postwar Japan, Toyota, up against bigger and richer rivals. They couldn't win by spending more. They had to win by wasting less.
(~238 chars)

5/
Out of that came the visual workplace. Set a space up so anyone can glance and tell whether things are where they belong. A missing tool leaves an obvious gap. A box in the aisle looks wrong. Normal becomes visible, so problems get caught early.
(~245 chars)

6/
Those practices were five steps, each starting with S in Japanese. People named the set 5S. It walked out of the auto plant into hospitals, airlines, offices, warehouses, and eventually homes. Because "make the right thing easy" was never really a car idea.
(~257 chars)

7/
The six words, in order: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Three make space, one keeps people safe, two make it last. If you blank on a name, hold the order instead. The sequence is the method. The labels are just handles.
(~243 chars)

8/
The sixth S is Safety. None of it matters if someone gets hurt. At home it may be the most important step. Workplaces have inspectors and rules. Your home has you. The stairs in the dark, the knives, the medicine in reach. Nobody audits those for you.
(~251 chars)

9/
Worried about yellow floor tape and every spatula traced on a pegboard? Relax. We borrow the logic of 6S, not the look of a factory. Make the right action easy, make problems show early, protect the people. Your home dresses that logic in its own warm clothes.
(~260 chars)

10/
A well-run home is not an empty one. Not a showroom, not a museum. The fruit bowl, the kids' art on the fridge, the worn spot on the couch where everyone sits, those are the life 6S exists to protect, not failures to erase.
(~223 chars)

11/
And you don't learn a new system for every room. You learn one and point it anywhere. The same six steps fix a junk drawer in fifteen minutes or a garage over a few weekends. Scale changes, the steps don't. Every small project is practice for the big ones.
(~256 chars)

12/
So what is 6S? A simple, repeatable method for making the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe in any space, and keeping it that way. You already get more than most people do, and you haven't moved a single object yet. That's Chapter 2 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
(~275 chars)
