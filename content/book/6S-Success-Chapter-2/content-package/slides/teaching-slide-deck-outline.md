## Slide 1 | What Is 6S?

- Six odd little words, one factory floor in Japan, a method that belongs in your kitchen drawer.
- A simple, repeatable way to make the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe.
- By the end, you will know the six words, where they came from, and why they work at home.

- Those two easy seconds are not luck. Somebody set that drawer up to behave that way.

## Slide 3 | Findable Is Not the Same as Tidy

- What you felt was not tidiness. A drawer can be neat and still useless.
- It was findability: the space answering your question before you finish asking it.
- You only notice findability when it is missing, like a chair that is not there when you sit.

[VISUAL] Split frame on paper. Left, a neat but useless drawer (lined-up clutter, no calm-dots). Right, the same drawer made findable (green calm-dots glowing). Slate label "tidy" vs terracotta label "findable."

## Slide 4 | Where It Actually Came From

- Postwar Japan. Toyota had to compete by wasting less, because they did not have more to spend.
- Out of that pressure came the Toyota Production System and the idea of the visual workplace.
- The foundation was five practices, each starting with S in Japanese. Later named 5S.

[VISUAL] The four-node timeline infographic "From Factory Floor to Front Hall." Nodes in terracotta-ringed circles with honey arrows between them: Postwar Japan, Toyota system, Spreads worldwide, Into your home (the home node in slate).

## Slide 5 | The Big Idea: Make Normal Visible

- Set a space up so a missing tool leaves an obvious gap and a box in the aisle looks wrong.
- When normal is visible, abnormal stands out, so problems get caught early instead of festering.
- A labeled basket by the door does the same job at home that a shadow board does at work.

[VISUAL] Simple slate-on-paper diagram. A pegboard with one empty tool outline (the obvious gap), beside a home entry hook with one bag missing. Honey arrow connecting "factory" to "home" to show the shared idea.

---

## Slide 6 | The Five S's, in Plain English

- Seiri / Sort: keep what belongs, remove what does not.
- Seiton / Straighten: a home for everything, by how often you use it.
- Seiso / Shine: clean, and notice problems while you do.
- Seiketsu / Standardize and Shitsuke / Sustain: make the good way normal, then keep it up.

[VISUAL] The bilingual mapping infographic "Five Japanese Words, Five English Ones." Japanese term in italic slate-grey, English name in terracotta, one-line home meaning in Newsreader body, honey arrows between the columns.

## Slide 7 | The Labels Wobble, the Order Holds

- Three different books may give you three slightly different sets of English S words.
- That is fine. The originals are Japanese, and translators kept both the meaning and the run of S's.
- The words are just handles. What matters is the idea behind each one and the order they come in.

[VISUAL] Three faint book spines on paper, each showing a slightly different second-S label (Straighten, Set in Order, Systematize). Below them, one steady terracotta arrow labeled "the order" running left to right, unchanged.

## Slide 8 | Six Words Carry the Whole Thing

- Sort. Straighten. Shine. Safety. Standardize. Sustain.
- Three make space, one keeps people safe, two make it last.
- If you blank on a name, hold on to the order. The sequence is the method.

[VISUAL] Six clean tiles in a row on warm paper, Fraunces caps. Five in honey, the Safety tile in slate to mark the added sixth. A small "6S" badge in green calm-dot color sits above the row.

## Slide 9 | How Five Became Six: Safety

- People running the method kept hitting the same truth. None of it matters if someone gets hurt.
- So they made Safety explicit rather than assumed. 5S plus 1 becomes 6S.
- In this book Safety is the fourth S, right after Shine, the moment your hands are on everything.

[VISUAL] The growth infographic "How 5S Became 6S." Five honey tiles, plus a slate Safety tile (small shield with a check), equals the full set of six. Caption strip: "protect people first."

## Slide 10 | At Home, Safety May Be the Most Important S

- Workplaces have inspectors and rules. Your home has you.
- The stairs in the dark, the knives in the drawer, the medicine within a toddler's reach.
- The same walk-through that organizes a space can secure it, if you remember to look.

[VISUAL] Warm home scene line art: a dim hallway, a stair edge, a high cabinet. Three small slate Safety markers highlight the hazards. Soft note: "Nobody is going to audit these for you."

---

## Slide 11 | Your Home Is Not a Factory

- We borrow the logic of 6S, not the look of a factory. No yellow floor tape required.
- The logic: make the right action easy, make normal visible, protect the people who use the space.
- The fruit bowl, the kids' art, the worn spot on the couch are the life 6S exists to protect.

[VISUAL] The factory-bench versus warm-counter split illustration. Left, a pegboard and floor tape. Right, a counter with a plant, a fruit bowl, a mug. The same green calm-dots glow under both. Caption: "Same logic underneath. Completely different feeling on top."

---

## Slide 12 | One Method, Any Size

- You do not learn a different system for every room. You learn one and point it anywhere.
- A drawer in fifteen minutes, a shelf in thirty, a room in an afternoon, a garage over a few weekends.
- The scale changes. The steps do not. Every small project is practice for the big ones.

[VISUAL] The nested-scale infographic "One Method, Any Scale." Drawer, shelf, room, garage growing larger left to right, each stamped with the same small green "6S" loop badge.

---

## Slide 13 | 6S in One Sentence

- A simple, repeatable method for making the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe, in any space, and keeping it that way.
- Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain.
- You already understand more about this than most people, and you have not moved a single object.
