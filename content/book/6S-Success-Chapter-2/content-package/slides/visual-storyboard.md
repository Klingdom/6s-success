# Visual Storyboard: What Is 6S?

A beat-by-beat visual guide tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the design system of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Use this so every slide looks like it came out of the book, not out of a stock template.

---

## The design system (use everywhere)

**Background:** warm paper. Hex `#F7F2E9`. Panels and cards a shade lighter, panel cream `#FBF7EF`.

**Type:**
- Display and headlines: Fraunces (the book's display face).
- Body and captions: Newsreader (the book's serif body).
- Small labels, eyebrows, and data tags: a clean sans (Inter) in all caps with wide letter-spacing.

**Accent palette:**
- Terracotta `#BC4B2A`: primary accent, English step names, eyebrows, the active rule.
- Honey `#DDA63A`: arrows, connectors, the original five tiles, hazard triangle.
- Slate `#3C5A6B`: data and structure, the Safety element, the "home" and "factory" labels.
- Ink `#2B2622`: text and outlines.
- Soft grey `#6A625A`: secondary text, Japanese terms, captions.

**The green calm-dot motif:** green `#6E8B5B`. This is the book's quiet signature. A calm-dot marks a thing that has found its home, a space that answers rather than asks. It should recur on nearly every slide, small and unobtrusive, never decorative for its own sake. When something is sorted, safe, or settled, a calm-dot appears. That is the whole grammar.

**The friction-spark counterpart:** spark red `#CB4B36`. Used sparingly to mark friction, hazards, or the "before." It is the opposite pole of the calm-dot.

**Texture rule:** flat color and warm line art, the style established in Chapter 1. No gradients, no gloss, no drop shadows beyond the book's single hairline card edge.

## Beat 1: The findability hook (drawer)

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slides 2 and 3. Carousel slides 1 and 3.

**Recommended image:** A top-down view of a simply organized kitchen drawer. In the hero version, a real photograph in warm light. In diagram versions, the book's warm line drawing: a bar-tools tray (bottle opener, corkscrew, can opener grouped), then separate lanes for forks, spoons, knives. Honest and readable, not styled like a catalog.

**Palette cues:** paper and panel cream base, ink outlines, a touch of terracotta on the corkscrew and slate on the bottle opener and knives to echo the book's drawer illustration.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** one green dot floating above each grouped zone, four in total, marking the items that have a clear home. This is the motif's origin point for the chapter. Establish it cleanly here so it reads everywhere after.

**Contrast beat (slide 3):** show the "tidy but useless" drawer with no calm-dots, then the "findable" drawer with the dots lit. The presence or absence of green is the entire message.

## Beat 2: Factory-to-home timeline

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 4. Carousel slide 4.

**Recommended diagram:** The four-node horizontal timeline, "From Factory Floor to Front Hall." Four circles connected by a horizontal rule: Postwar Japan, Toyota system (5S is born), Spreads worldwide (hospitals, offices, airlines), Into your home.

**Palette cues:** the three origin nodes are white circles with terracotta rings. The final "home" node is filled solid slate, so the eye lands on home as the destination. Connectors are honey arrowheads. A small sans caps title sits above in soft grey.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** optional single calm-dot resting on the "Into your home" node, signaling that the method has arrived somewhere it belongs.

## Beat 3: Make normal visible

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 5. Carousel slide 5.

**Recommended diagram:** A pegboard with one empty tool outline (the obvious gap) beside a row of home entry hooks with one bag missing. The parallel makes the point without words: same logic, two settings.

**Palette cues:** slate dashed outlines for the "expected" positions, ink for the real objects, one honey arrow linking factory to home.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** calm-dots under the filled positions, with a conspicuous gap where the missing item belongs. The missing dot is the abnormal made visible.

---

## Beat 4: The five S's, bilingual mapping

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 6. Carousel uses the six-word tile set on slide 6 instead.

**Recommended diagram:** The bilingual mapping table, "Five Japanese Words, Five English Ones." Five rows, alternating white and faint honey-tinted bands. Each row: Japanese term, arrow, English name, one-line home meaning.

**Palette cues:** Japanese term in italic soft grey, English name in terracotta Fraunces, home meaning in Newsreader ink. Honey arrowheads between the Japanese and English columns. A closing italic line in soft grey: "Different books use slightly different English words. The ideas, and the order, stay the same."

**Calm-dot recurrence:** a single calm-dot can sit at the end of the last row, marking that the set is complete and settled.

---

## Beat 5: 5S plus Safety equals 6S

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slides 8 and 9. Carousel slides 6 and 7.

**Recommended diagram:** The growth equation. A cluster of five honey tiles (the original five), a large plus sign, a slate Safety tile carrying a small white shield-with-check, an equals sign, then the full set of six tiles with the sixth in slate.

**Palette cues:** the five always honey, Safety always slate. This color rule is consistent with the book and makes Safety instantly legible as the added, deliberate sixth. Small caption strip under the Safety tile: "protect people first."

**Calm-dot recurrence:** a green "6S" badge (calm-dot colored) can crown the final six-tile set, tying the completed method back to the calm the dots represent.

**Home-safety variant (deck slide 10):** a dim hallway and stair edge with slate Safety markers on the hazards, paired with a small spark-red accent on the single worst hazard to show friction before it is cleared.

---

## Beat 6: Home is not a factory (the split)

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 11. Carousel slide 8.

**Recommended illustration:** The centered split. Left half labeled FACTORY: a pegboard with dashed tool outlines, honey floor tape, a honey hazard triangle. Right half labeled HOME: a warm counter with a terracotta plant pot and green foliage, a fruit bowl (one spark-red apple, one honey one), a white mug. A dashed vertical divider down the middle.

**Palette cues:** the factory side leans slate and honey and reads cooler. The home side carries terracotta, honey, and green and reads warmer. Same structural logic, visibly different temperature.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** this is the key recurrence. The same row of green calm-dots runs along the baseline under both halves, three on each side. That shared baseline is the visual thesis: same logic underneath, different feeling on top. Caption in italic soft grey: "Same logic underneath. Completely different feeling on top."

---

## Beat 7: One method, any scale

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 12. Carousel slide 9.

**Recommended diagram:** Four objects growing larger left to right: a drawer, a shelf, a room, a garage, each in warm line art on paper. Time labels beneath in Newsreader: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, an afternoon, a few weekends.

**Palette cues:** ink outlines, small honey-filled accents (a drawer pull, an open door) to keep them warm rather than technical.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** each object is stamped with the same small "6S" loop badge in green calm-dot color, sitting at its upper corner. The repeating green badge is the point: one method, repeated, at every size.

---

## Beat 8: The one-sentence definition

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 13. Carousel slide 10.

**Recommended treatment:** The slate-bordered definition box on panel cream, the way the book frames its boxed definition. The one sentence set in Fraunces, large enough to breathe. Beneath it, the six words in small caps: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain.

**Palette cues:** slate border, ink text, soft grey label "6S in one sentence" in sans caps above.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** a single green calm-dot placed where the sentence ends, used like a period that means "calm." A small, satisfying full stop for the whole chapter.

---

## Beat 9: The friction meter (close)

**Where it appears:** Teaching deck slide 14 (closing). Carousel slide 10 corner accent.

**Recommended illustration:** The book's recurring friction meter, a half-dial gauge. From left to right the arc moves green (calm), through honey, to spark red (friction). The needle rests near the middle, holding steady, because this chapter built understanding rather than moving objects.

**Palette cues:** green-honey-spark arc on paper, ink needle and hub, sans caps labels CALM (green) on the left and FRICTION (spark) on the right.

**Calm-dot recurrence:** the green end of the meter is the calm-dot color at full scale. The whole motif resolves here: every small green dot through the deck was a preview of where the needle is heading. Caption: "You have loaded the spring. Chapter 3 is where it starts to move."

## Recurrence summary (the green calm-dot through the deck)

1. Born on the drawer (Beat 1): a dot marks each item that found its home.
2. Withheld on the "tidy but useless" drawer (Beat 1 contrast): absence carries meaning.
3. Lands on "home" in the timeline (Beat 2): the method arrives somewhere it belongs.
4. Goes missing in the visible gap (Beat 3): the missing dot is the abnormal made visible.
5. Crowns the completed six (Beat 5): the badge ties the method to calm.
6. Shared baseline under factory and home (Beat 6): the visual thesis of the chapter.
7. Repeats as the "6S" badge at every scale (Beat 7): one method, any size.
8. A period that means calm on the definition (Beat 8).
9. Resolves into the green end of the friction meter (Beat 9): a preview paid off.

Keep it small, keep it consistent, and let it be the one element a reader starts to look for from slide to slide.
