# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 2, "What Is 6S?"

You are at a friend's place for dinner. "Could you grab the bottle opener?" she says, nodding at a drawer. You open it, and there it is, right on top, in a little tray with the corkscrew and the can opener, the three of them lined up like friends waiting for the same bus. No digging. No tangle of takeout menus and dead batteries. The drawer practically handed you the thing you came for.

That two-second feeling, a space answering your question almost before you finish asking it, has a name and a method behind it. It is called 6S, and the strange part is that it was not invented for kitchens at all. It was worked out on a factory floor in postwar Japan by people building cars with very little money and no room to waste.

This chapter explains what those six S words mean, where they came from, and why a method built for factories belongs in a busy home. No yellow floor tape required. If the bottle-opener drawer rang a bell, this is where you find out why. Read the chapter free in the online book.
