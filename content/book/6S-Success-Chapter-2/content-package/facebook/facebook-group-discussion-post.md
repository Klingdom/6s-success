# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget, then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad.

---

Okay, small idea that quietly fixed half the clutter in my house, and I want to hear yours too.

I used to think the goal of organizing was "tidy." Make it neat, line everything up, done. But I kept re-tidying the same spots over and over, and they never actually felt better to use.

Then it clicked. The thing I was actually chasing wasn't tidy. It was findable. There's a real difference. A drawer can be perfectly neat and still useless, because neat just means lined up. Findable means the thing answers you the second you open it. The towel is right where your wet hand reaches. The scissors are where you grab without looking. The phone charger lives in one known spot instead of vanishing every single night.

The little test I use now: I open a drawer or a cabinet and ask, "Does this answer me, or does it interrogate me?" If I have to dig and think, it's not done yet, no matter how neat it looks.

Funny part is you usually only notice findability when it's missing, like a chair you never think about until it's gone and you sit down hard on the floor.

So here's what I'm curious about, and I genuinely want to steal your ideas:

What's the MOST findable spot in your home, the one place where everything just works and you never lose anything?

And what's the LEAST findable, the spot that swallows things whole? (Mine is a kitchen drawer that currently contains menus, a dead battery, and one chopstick. Just one.)

Drop both in the comments. I'm taking notes and tackling my worst spot this weekend. 🙂

(Side note in case anyone asks where this "answer me vs interrogate me" idea came from: it's a line from Chapter 2 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, which is free to read online. Not trying to sell anyone anything, the findability test is just genuinely the most useful organizing idea I've picked up in years and I had to share it.)
