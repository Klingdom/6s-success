# Facebook Longform Post: The Bottle-Opener Drawer

A single longer post (about 300 to 450 words) telling the bottle-opener-drawer story, gently explaining what 6S is and why it works at home, ending with a soft CTA. Written like talking to a friend.

---

Let me tell you about a drawer that changed how I think about my whole house.

I was over at a friend's place for dinner and offered to help. "Could you grab the bottle opener?" she said, tipping her head toward a drawer. I opened it, and there it was, right on top, sitting in a little tray with the corkscrew and the can opener. The three of them lined up like friends waiting for the same bus.

I didn't have to ask which drawer. I didn't have to dig past a tangle of takeout menus and rubber bands. The drawer just handed me the thing I came for.

It was a tiny moment. Two seconds, maybe three. But if you have ever spent a frustrated minute pawing through a drawer that held eleven menus, a dead battery, a warranty for a blender you no longer own, and exactly one chopstick, then you know those two easy seconds are not luck. Somebody set that drawer up to behave that way.

That feeling, a space answering your question almost before you finish asking it, has a name. It's called findability. And here is the part that surprised me. The method behind it was not invented for kitchens. It came from a car factory in postwar Japan, where Toyota was trying to build cars with very little money and no room to waste. They figured out how to set up a workspace so anyone could glance at it and instantly tell whether everything was where it belonged. Make the right thing easy, make problems easy to spot.

Turns out that is not a car idea. It is a human idea. So it spread from factories to hospitals to offices, and eventually into regular homes.

The whole thing is called 6S, and you can say what it is in one breath. It is a simple, repeatable method for making the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe in any space, and then keeping it that way. The six words are Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. That's it. No yellow tape, no label maker gone rogue, no turning your cozy home into a warehouse.

It works because it's built around how people actually behave when they're busy and tired, not how we wish we behaved on our best day.
