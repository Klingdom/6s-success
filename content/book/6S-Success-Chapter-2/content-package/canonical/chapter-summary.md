# Chapter 2 Summary: What Is 6S?

*6S Success: Home Edition - Part One, Discovering 6S*

## One-line summary

6S is a simple, repeatable method for making the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe in any space, and then keeping it that way. It came out of a car factory and it works just as well in a home.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Chapter 2 answers the question its title asks. It opens with a small, familiar moment: reaching into a friend's kitchen drawer and finding the bottle opener right on top, sitting with the corkscrew and the can opener. That easy two seconds is not luck. It is findability, built on purpose. The chapter traces where that idea came from, postwar Japan and the Toyota Production System, then names the six steps in plain English: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. It reassures the reader that a method born in a factory belongs in a home, and that organized never has to mean bare or cold.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 2 of 6S Success: Home Edition defines the method the whole book is built on, and it does so without asking the reader to move a single object. The chapter is teaching, not yet doing.

It opens on a tiny scene most people have lived: you are helping at a friend's house, you open a drawer for the bottle opener, and the drawer practically hands it to you. The author names the feeling underneath that moment, findability, and separates it from mere tidiness. A drawer can be neat and still useless. Findability is when a space answers your question before you finish asking it.

From there the chapter delivers a short, interesting origin story. After the Second World War, Toyota had to compete by wasting less rather than spending more. Out of that pressure came the Toyota Production System and the idea of the visual workplace, where normal is visible so abnormal stands out. The foundation was a set of five Japanese practices, each starting with S, later named 5S, which spread to hospitals, offices, airlines, and eventually homes.

The chapter then maps the five Japanese words to plain English (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Standardize, Sustain), explains why a sixth S, Safety, was added to make 5S into 6S, and places Safety deliberately as the fourth step. It reassures readers that borrowing the logic of 6S does not mean importing the look of a factory, shows that the same six steps scale from a drawer to a garage, and closes by promising Chapter 3 will walk all six steps in one connected flow.
