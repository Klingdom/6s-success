## 1. Chapter Opening
The friend's-house scene. You reach for the bottle opener and the drawer hands it to you. Two easy seconds are not luck. The method behind that drawer was worked out on a factory floor, not in a kitchen. Pays off Chapter 1's promise of "a factory in Japan that has everything to do with your kitchen drawer."

## 2. In This Chapter (learning objectives)
- What the five original S's mean, in plain English
- The short story of where 6S came from
- The difference between 5S, 5S plus 1, and 6S
- Why a factory method works at home
- How to remember six words, even the odd ones

## 3. The Calm You Can Feel
Names findability and separates it from tidiness. A drawer can be tidy and still useless. Findability is the quiet superpower under every space that feels good to use. You only notice it when it is missing.
- Callout (6S Tip): A good space answers questions. A cluttered one asks them.

## 4. Where This Actually Came From
Postwar Japan. Toyota competing by wasting less. The Toyota Production System and the visual workplace. The original five S practices, later named 5S, spreading to hospitals, airlines, offices, and homes. The truth underneath was always about helping ordinary people do good work under pressure.
- Infographic: "From Factory Floor to Front Hall" (four-node timeline)
- Callout (Common Mistake): 6S is not "a Japanese cleaning system." Cleaning is one part of one step.

## 5. The Five S's, in Plain English
Maps each Japanese word to its English idea and home meaning: Seiri/Sort, Seiton/Straighten, Seiso/Shine, Seiketsu/Standardize, Shitsuke/Sustain. Notes that English labels wobble between books. The words are handles. The order is the method.
- Infographic: "Five Japanese Words, Five English Ones" (bilingual mapping)
- Callout (6S Tip): If you blank on a name, hold on to the order instead.

## 6. How Five Became Six
Why Safety was added: none of it matters if someone gets hurt. 5S plus 1 becomes 6S. In this book Safety is the fourth S, placed right after Shine, because that is the moment your hands are on everything. At home, Safety may be the most important step, because no inspector is coming.
- Infographic: "How 5S Became 6S" (three-stage growth)
- Callout (Quick Win): Walk the path you take in the dark. Move anything you would trip on.

## 7. Your Home Is Not a Factory (and That Is the Point)
Reassurance. Borrow the logic, not the look. The logic: make the right action easy, make normal visible, protect people. A well-run home is not empty. The fruit bowl, the kids' art, the worn spot on the couch are the life 6S exists to protect.
- Illustration: factory bench vs warm counter, shared green calm-dots
- Callout (Common Mistake): Organized does not mean bare. Tidy does not mean cold.

## 8. Same Method, Any Size
One system, pointed anywhere from a drawer to a garage. The scale changes, the steps do not. Every small project is practice for the big ones. This is why the second half of the book is room-by-room playbooks, not new methods.
- Infographic: "One Method, Any Scale" (drawer, shelf, room, garage)
- Callout (Family Challenge): Name the most and least findable spot in the house.

## 9. Putting It Together
The one-sentence definition. The six words: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Three make space, one keeps people safe, two make it last.
- Definition box (6S in one sentence)
- Callout (Quick Win): Open any drawer and ask, do you answer me or interrogate me?

You arrived with six odd words and leave knowing what they mean and where they came from. 6S was built so ordinary people could do good work under pressure, which is also a good description of running a home. Teases Chapter 3.
