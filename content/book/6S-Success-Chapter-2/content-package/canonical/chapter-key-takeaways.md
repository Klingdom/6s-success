# Chapter 2 Key Takeaways: What Is 6S?

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **6S is one method, stated in one sentence.** It makes the right thing easy to find, easy to use, and safe in any space, and then keeps it that way.

2. **The point is findability, not tidiness.** A drawer can be neat and still useless. Findability is when a space answers your question before you finish asking it. You only notice it when it is missing.

3. **It came from a factory, not a home.** Postwar Toyota had to compete by wasting less. The Toyota Production System and the idea of the visual workplace produced a set of five practices later named 5S.

4. **The visual workplace makes normal visible, so abnormal stands out.** A missing tool leaves an obvious gap. A box in the aisle looks wrong immediately. Problems get caught early instead of festering.

5. **The five original S's are Japanese words with plain English ideas.** Seiri/Sort, Seiton/Straighten, Seiso/Shine, Seiketsu/Standardize, Shitsuke/Sustain.

6. **The English labels wobble between books, and that is fine.** The words are just handles. The order is the method. If you blank on a name, hold on to the sequence.

7. **The sixth S is Safety, and at home it may be the most important step.** Workplaces have inspectors. Your home has you. This book places Safety fourth, right after Shine, because that is the moment your hands are on everything.

8. **Borrow the logic, not the look.** You are taking the thinking of 6S, not the yellow floor tape. Your home gets to dress that logic in its own warm clothes.

9. **Organized does not mean bare or cold.** The fruit bowl, the kids' art, the worn spot on the couch are the life 6S exists to protect, not failures to erase.

10. **One method scales to any size.** The same six steps fix a drawer in fifteen minutes or a garage over a few weekends. Every small project is practice for the big ones.

11. **You already understand more than most people, and you have not moved a single object.** This chapter builds understanding. Chapter 3 starts the doing.
