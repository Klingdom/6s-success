# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 2 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar already manages it.

## High-frequency elements (used in many assets)

| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The bottle-opener drawer scene | LinkedIn post 1, X thread 1, FB longform, YouTube open, podcast open, landing intro, infographic, image prompt 1 | A follower on several platforms sees the same opening repeatedly | Calendar staggers these across Days 1 to 8. Vary the first sentence per platform (already done: each retells it slightly differently). |
| "Findability, not tidiness" | LinkedIn post 2, X short post, newsletter teaser, short video, quote card | Medium | Spread across the two weeks. It is a core idea, so some repetition reinforces rather than bores. |
| One-sentence definition | Meta description, schema, quote card, infographic footer, deck close, X thread 12 | Low | It is a definition, meant to be repeated verbatim. Consistency here is a feature. |
| "Borrow the logic, not the look" | LinkedIn post 7, X thread 9, FB longform, factory-vs-counter diagram, quote card | Medium | Use as a recurring refrain, but not twice in the same week on the same channel. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings from the CTA bank. Identical sign-offs are the most likely thing to feel templated. |
| "Tuesday morning, three kids, one missing shoe" | LinkedIn post 10, manuscript, podcast | Low | Strong line, used sparingly. Fine. |

## Same-day collision check
The publishing calendar was built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. Confirmed clear:
- Day 1: drawer hook appears once on LinkedIn and on the web page (different formats, acceptable).
- Day 3: X thread (drawer) and quote card 1 (different line). No collision.
- Day 4: LinkedIn article and FB post 1 both touch the drawer, but on different platforms with different audiences. Low risk. If your LinkedIn and FB audiences overlap heavily, move FB post 1 to Day 5.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline and the slides carousel copy cover the same six-words content. Do not post both to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) for the actual post and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version and the email newsletter version share structure. That is fine because they reach different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber on the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set the LinkedIn article and any syndicated copies to point back (canonical link or a "first published at" note) so they do not compete with the chapter page in search.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- Low. Nothing is plagiarized from outside the book. All quotes are the author's own words. The history is general and uncontroversial (Toyota, 5S, the visual workplace), stated at the chapter's level of detail without borrowed phrasing from third-party sources.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies accordingly.
4. If LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap, add one more day of spacing between the two drawer-led posts.
5. Cap the drawer hook at roughly one use per platform per week.
