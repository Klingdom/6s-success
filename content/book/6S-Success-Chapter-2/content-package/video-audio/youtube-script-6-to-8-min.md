## SECTION 1: THE CALM YOU CAN FEEL (1:10 to 2:20)

[VISUAL: Split screen. Left, the tidy bottle-opener drawer. Right, a chaotic junk drawer with eleven menus, a dead battery, a lone chopstick.]

[HOST, voice over]
Let us go back to that drawer for a second, because it is teaching us something.

[B-ROLL: A frustrated hand digging through the junk drawer, pushing things around.]

[HOST]
What you felt when the good drawer handed you the bottle opener was not tidiness. A drawer can be tidy and still useless. You can line up junk very neatly and still not find the one thing that matters.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Findability"]

[VISUAL: Back to the clean drawer. Small green dots gently appear over each item that has a clear home. The calm-dot motif.]

[HOST]
What you felt was findability. The drawer was arranged so the answer came to you. Fast, with no effort, no thinking. The towel where your wet hand expects it. The scissors where you reach without looking. The charger in one known place.

[HOST]
You only notice findability when it is missing. Kind of like a chair. You only notice it is gone when you sit down hard on the floor.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "A good space answers questions. A cluttered one asks them."]

[HOST]
6S is just the method for building that on purpose, and then keeping it.

## SECTION 2: WHERE IT CAME FROM (2:20 to 3:50)

[VISUAL: Transition to archival-style imagery, postwar Japan, a car factory floor. Keep it warm, not cold.]

[HOST, voice over]
So where did this come from? Here is the short version, and it is a good story.

[B-ROLL: A simple animated timeline. Node one: "Postwar Japan, 1950s."]

[HOST]
After the Second World War, Japan was rebuilding, and a car company called Toyota was trying to compete with much bigger, much richer rivals. They could not win by spending more, because they did not have more. So they had to win by wasting less. Less material, less motion, less searching, less time.

[B-ROLL: Timeline node two: "Toyota Production System."]

[HOST]
Out of that came the Toyota Production System, and one of its most human ideas, the visual workplace. The thinking was simple. Set a space up well, and anyone can glance at it and instantly tell if things are where they should be.

[VISUAL: A shadow board with tool outlines. One tool missing, leaving an obvious gap. Highlight the gap.]

[HOST]
The tool is outlined on the board, so a missing tool leaves an obvious gap. The walkway is marked, so a box in the aisle looks wrong right away. Normal becomes visible, which means problems get caught early instead of festering.

[B-ROLL: Timeline nodes three and four slide in: "Hospitals, airlines, offices" then "Into the home."]

[HOST]
The foundation of all that was five practices, each starting, in Japanese, with the letter S. Later someone gave them a tidy name, 5S. And it spread. Into hospitals, where a missing instrument is an emergency. Into airline maintenance, where a tool left in an engine can cost lives. Into offices, kitchens, and eventually, homes.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Make the right thing easy. Make the wrong thing obvious."]

[HOST]
Because that idea, make the right thing easy and the wrong thing obvious, is not a car idea. It is a human idea. It is really about helping ordinary people do good work under pressure, without having to be heroes about it.

[HOST, slight smile]
Which is a pretty accurate description of a Tuesday morning with three kids and one missing shoe.

## SECTION 3: THE SIX WORDS (3:50 to 5:30)

[VISUAL: Clean bilingual card animation. Each Japanese word fades in beside its English word and a one-line home meaning.]

[HOST, on camera, then voice over]
Okay, let us meet the words. The original five, then the one that got added.

[ON SCREEN TEXT, building one line at a time:]
- Seiri = Sort
- Seiton = Straighten
- Seiso = Shine
- Seiketsu = Standardize
- Shitsuke = Sustain

[HOST]
Sort. Separate what you need from what you do not, and remove what you do not. Most spaces are not short on room. They are full of things that belong somewhere else.

[B-ROLL: Hands pulling unused items out of a drawer.]

[HOST]
Straighten. Give everything that stays a clear home, arranged by how often you use it. Daily things get the easy spots. Rare things move out of the way.

[HOST]
Shine. Clean it, and clean it in a way that lets you notice problems. In 6S, cleaning is partly inspection. You find the leak, the crack, the loose hinge while your hands are already there.

[HOST]
Standardize. Turn the good arrangement into the normal one, out in the open, so it survives a busy week.

[HOST]
And Sustain. Keep it up. The small habits that stop your good work from sliding back into a junk drawer.

[VISUAL: A "5 plus 1" graphic. Five tiles, then a sixth tile labeled "Safety" slides in.]

[HOST]
So that is five. Where does the sixth come from? People running this in real workplaces kept hitting the same wall. None of it matters if someone gets hurt. A perfectly labeled workshop is a failure if a frayed cord or a blocked exit puts a person in danger. So they added Safety. Five S's plus one. That is 6S.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Sort. Straighten. Shine. Safety. Standardize. Sustain."]

[HOST]
In this book, Safety sits fourth, right after Shine. Because that is the moment your hands are on everything, so it is the perfect time to spot the wobbly shelf, the overloaded outlet, the cleaning chemicals stored above the cereal.

[HOST, on camera]
And quick note. If you ever blank on one of these words, do not stress. The words are just handles. The order is the method. Hold on to the sequence.

## SECTION 4: YOUR HOME IS NOT A FACTORY (5:30 to 6:50)

[VISUAL: A worried homeowner imagines yellow floor tape, every spatula traced on a pegboard, a sterile warehouse kitchen. Slightly comic.]

[HOST, voice over]
Now, right about here, some people start to panic. They picture yellow tape on the kitchen floor and a label maker gone rogue. A home that looks like a tidy little warehouse where nobody would ever curl up with a book.

[HOST, on camera, reassuring]
Please relax. That is not where we are going.

[VISUAL: Split illustration. Left, a factory bench with floor tape. Right, a warm kitchen counter with a plant, a fruit bowl, a mug. The same green calm-dots glow under both.]

[HOST]
Here is the line that makes the whole thing work. We are borrowing the logic of 6S, not the look of a factory. The logic is gentle. Make the right action the easy action. Make normal visible so problems show up early. Protect the people who use the space.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Same logic underneath. Completely different feeling on top."]

[HOST]
In a hospital, good 6S might mean every crash cart is identical across the whole building. In your home, good 6S might just mean a basket by the door and a hook for each kid's bag. Same idea. Totally different feeling.

[B-ROLL: Warm home details. Kids' art on the fridge. The worn spot on the couch. The fruit bowl.]

[HOST]
A well-run home is not an empty one. The fruit bowl, the kids' art, the worn spot on the couch where everyone sits. Those are not failures. That is the life 6S exists to protect. We are not trying to make your house quieter than a hospital. We are trying to make your Tuesday mornings quieter. Different goal entirely.

## SECTION 5: ONE METHOD, ANY SIZE (6:50 to 7:30)

[VISUAL: Nested targets animation. A drawer, a shelf, a room, a garage, each stamped with the same small six-step loop badge.]

[HOST, voice over]
Last thing, and this is the part that makes it forgiving. You do not learn a different system for every room. You learn one system and point it wherever you like.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Drawer: 15 min. Shelf: 30 min. Room: an afternoon. Garage: a few weekends."]

[HOST]
The same six steps that fix a junk drawer will fix a shelf, a closet, a whole bedroom, the garage, even the photos on your phone. The scale changes. The steps do not. A drawer might take fifteen minutes. A garage might take a few weekends. But it is always sort, straighten, shine, make it safe, standardize, sustain.

[HOST]
Which means every small project you do is practice for the big ones. The drawer you organize tonight is teaching you the exact moves you will use on the garage in spring.
