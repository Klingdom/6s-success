# B-Roll and Visual Notes: What Is 6S?

**Source:** Chapter 2, "What Is 6S?" from *6S Success: Home Edition*
**Slug:** what-is-6s
**Purpose:** A shot list and visual reference for the YouTube, short-form, and any visual cuts. Built around the chapter's core motifs so the videos stay on-brand with the book.

---

## The recurring motifs (keep these consistent across every video)

- **Findability close-up:** The bottle-opener drawer. A top-down hero shot. This is the signature image of the chapter and can open or close any piece.
- **Green calm-dots:** Small soft green dots that appear over an item that has a clear, deliberate home. Use them to mark "this is findable." Gentle fade-in, never flashy.
- **Friction meter:** A simple needle gauge. It sits steady or low when a space is calm and findable, and climbs into the red when a space is cluttered or unsafe. Use it as a quiet progress motif, not a gimmick. The chapter notes that understanding "loads the spring," so a low, ready needle works well on the close.
- **Borrow the logic, not the look:** Any time you show a factory image, pair or contrast it with a warm home image so the viewer feels the difference.

---

## Hero / signature shots

1. **The good drawer (top-down).** A simple kitchen drawer, warm light, a small tray holding the bottle opener, corkscrew, and can opener together. Utensils sorted into clear lanes. Real but tidy. This is the anchor image.
2. **A hand retrieving the bottle opener** in one easy motion, no searching. Sells the "two easy seconds."
3. **The junk drawer (contrast).** A chaotic drawer for comparison: eleven takeout menus, a dead battery, a blender warranty, exactly one chopstick, tangled rubber bands. Frustrated hand digging through it.
4. **Split screen, good drawer vs junk drawer.** Side by side for the findability point.

## Section b-roll ideas (mapped to the chapter)

### Findability in everyday spots
- A towel hanging exactly where a wet hand reaches for it.
- Scissors pulled from a drawer without the person looking down.
- A phone charger living in one known spot, plugged in casually.
- (Contrast) Someone checking three rooms looking for the charger.

### The origin story
- Warm, archival-style footage of a postwar Japanese car factory floor. Keep it warm-toned, not cold or clinical.
- **Shadow board:** Tools outlined on a pegboard, every tool in its place. Then one tool missing, leaving an obvious gap. Highlight the gap.
- **Marked walkway:** A clean taped aisle, then a box left in the aisle that "looks wrong" immediately.
- A simple four-node animated timeline: Postwar Japan (1950s) to Toyota Production System to Spreads worldwide (hospitals, airlines, offices) to Into the home. Title it "From Factory Floor to Front Hall."

### The six steps (one real action per S)
- **Sort:** Hands pulling unused items out of a drawer into a discard pile.
- **Straighten:** Placing daily items into clear, easy-to-reach spots; rare items moved up or back.
- **Shine:** Wiping down a shelf, then a beat where a hand finds a loose hinge or a small drip. Cleaning as inspection.
- **Safety:** A dim hallway walked at night; a trip hazard on the floor glows red, a hand moves it, the glow turns green. Also: a wobbly shelf, an overloaded outlet, cleaning chemicals stored above cereal (then corrected).
- **Standardize:** A clearly labeled basket or hook; a second person finding something easily in a space they have never used.
- **Sustain:** A quick, light evening tidy. Small habit, not a deep clean.

### Home is not a factory (reassurance)
- Quick, slightly comic flash of an over-the-top "warehouse kitchen": yellow floor tape, every spatula traced on a pegboard, a runaway label maker.
- Warm reveal of a real lived-in kitchen: a plant, a fruit bowl, a mug, kids' art on the fridge, the worn spot on the couch.
- **Split illustration (from the book):** factory bench with floor tape on the left, warm kitchen counter on the right, the same green calm-dots glowing under both. Caption: "Same logic underneath. Completely different feeling on top."

### One method, any size
- Nested-targets animation: a drawer, a shelf, a room, a garage, each stamped with the same small six-step loop badge. Title it "One Method, Any Scale."
- Time labels: Drawer (15 minutes), Shelf (30 minutes), Room (an afternoon), Garage (a few weekends).

## Captioning / accessibility notes

- Keep on-screen text to one short line at a time so it is readable on a phone.
- The green-dot and red-spark moments should not be the only way meaning is carried; pair them with text or narration so colorblind viewers do not miss the point.
- Burn in captions for the spoken narration on all short-form cuts.

---

## Tone reminders for the editor

- Warm light, lived-in spaces, real homes. Avoid cold, glossy, showroom looks.
- Calm pacing on the teaching beats. Faster, satisfying cuts only on the six-steps montage.
- Music soft and friendly, never driving or hype. Let the bottle-opener moment breathe in near silence.
