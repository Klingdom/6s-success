# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions

*Chapter 2: What Is 6S?*

These questions are open-ended on purpose. There are no right answers, only your own home and how it actually works.

1. The chapter separates findability from tidiness, and argues that a drawer can be neat and still useless. Where in your home is something neat but hard to actually find or use? What makes the difference?

2. Think of a space in your home that already answers your questions, the one spot where you reach without looking and the thing is there. What did someone do, on purpose or by accident, to make it behave that way?

3. The method came out of postwar Toyota, where the goal was to win by wasting less rather than spending more. Does knowing the factory origin make 6S feel more credible to you, or less suited to a home? Why?

4. The visual workplace works by making normal visible, so abnormal stands out, like a missing tool leaving an obvious gap. Where could that idea help at home, and where might it feel like too much?

5. The English names for the steps wobble between books, partly because they started as Japanese words that translators have wrestled with for decades. Does the imperfect, slightly awkward translation bother you, reassure you, or not matter at all?

6. The book places Safety as the fourth S and argues it may be the most important step at home, because "workplaces have inspectors and rules. Your home has you." What is one safety thing in your home that no one but you is going to catch?

7. The chapter worries aloud that some readers picture yellow floor tape and a label maker gone rogue. Did you carry any version of that fear about organizing? Where did it come from?

8. "Organized does not mean bare or cold." The fruit bowl, the kids' art, the worn spot on the couch are described as the life 6S exists to protect. What in your home would you never want an organizing method to erase?

9. The chapter claims one method scales from a fifteen-minute drawer to a garage that takes a few weekends, the same six steps either way. Does that make a big project feel more approachable, and which small space would you use as practice first?

10. The chapter ends by saying 6S was built so ordinary people could do good work under pressure, without being heroes about it, and that this also describes running a home. Where in your week do you most wish things were a little easier instead of needing a hero?
