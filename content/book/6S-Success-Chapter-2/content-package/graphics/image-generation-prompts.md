# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 2, "What Is 6S?"

Five prompts for hero and supporting imagery. Each is written for the book's warm,
editorial, hand-made feel and against generic AI-art clichés. Each prompt specifies
subject, composition, lighting, materials, palette, and mood, and carries a short
"avoid" note.

**Palette to feed into prompts when the tool supports it:** warm paper cream `#F7F2E9`,
soft panel cream `#FBF7EF`, dark warm ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey
gold `#DDA63A`, muted slate blue `#3C5A6B`, sage green `#6E8B5B`. Keep saturation
gentle. Think printed cookbook and well-made home magazine, not glossy advertising.

---

## Prompt 1, Hero: the bottle-opener drawer (photographic)

**Use:** Chapter opener and the lead social image.

> A top-down photograph of a single shallow kitchen drawer, open, shot straight from
> above. Inside, a small wooden tray holds three bar tools resting together: a flat
> metal bottle opener, a corkscrew, and a can opener. Beside the tray, forks, spoons,
> and butter knives rest in their own tidy compartments, each utensil in its own lane.
> Real but simple, a lived-in home kitchen rather than a showroom. Warm late-afternoon
> daylight raking in from the left, soft shadows, a little honest wear on the wood.
> Materials: pale oak drawer, natural linen tray liner, aged stainless utensils. Color
> mood built on warm cream, terracotta, honey gold, and muted slate blue, low
> saturation. Calm, reassuring, the feeling of a space that answers a question before
> you finish asking it. Editorial still-life, shot on a 50mm lens, gentle depth.
- **Avoid:** sterile stock-photo gloss, plastic organizers, neon or cold blue tones,
  perfect symmetry, oversaturation, lens flare, text or logos, cluttered background.

## Prompt 2, Supporting: the warm kitchen counter (photographic)

**Use:** The "your home is not a factory" reassurance scene; pairs with that section.

> A relaxed eye-level photograph of one corner of a warm kitchen counter. A small potted
> herb plant in a terracotta pot, a shallow ceramic bowl with a few apples and lemons, a
> single well-used stoneware mug. A child's crayon drawing is held to the side of the
> fridge with a magnet, just visible at the frame edge. Honest morning light from a
> window, soft and diffuse. Surfaces clean and uncluttered but clearly part of a real
> home, with a couple of small signs of daily life. Materials: butcher-block counter,
> hand-thrown ceramics, terracotta, a sage green plant. Palette of warm cream, honey,
> terracotta, slate, and sage, gently muted. Mood: cozy, calm, comfortable, evidence
> that real people live here. Editorial lifestyle photography, natural and unstaged.
- **Avoid:** minimalist showroom emptiness, museum sterility, cold gray tones, granite
  and chrome luxury-kitchen look, heavy clutter, harsh flash, busy patterns, text.

## Prompt 3, Hero illustration: top-down drawer line art (illustration)

**Use:** A drawn alternative to Prompt 1, matching the chapter's SVG style.

> A hand-drawn top-down illustration of an organized kitchen drawer, flat editorial
> style with clean dark ink outlines and flat color fills, no shading or gradients. A
> small tray in the upper left holds a bottle opener drawn in slate blue, a corkscrew in
> terracotta, and a can opener in honey gold. To the right, three compartments hold
> forks, spoons, and knives in neat rows. Four small sage green dots sit along the top
> edge of the compartments, each marking an item that has a clear home. Background a warm
> cream paper tone with a faint paper grain. The look of a thoughtful printed book
> diagram, warm and friendly, slightly imperfect linework as if drawn by hand.
- **Avoid:** 3D rendering, glossy vector slickness, photorealism, drop shadows,
  gradients, neon colors, busy textures, watermark, generic clip-art look.

## Prompt 4, Conceptual: factory logic to home warmth (illustration)

**Use:** Header art for the origin-story or "borrow the logic, not the look" section.

> A split editorial illustration in flat hand-drawn style with dark ink outlines. On the
> left half, a tidy factory workbench: a pegboard with tool outlines, a strip of floor
> tape, a small hazard triangle, drawn cool and orderly in slate blue and honey. On the
> right half, the same care expressed as a warm home counter: a potted plant, a fruit
> bowl, a mug, drawn in terracotta, honey, and sage. A soft dashed divider runs down the
> middle. Along the bottom of both halves, a shared row of small sage green dots ties the
> two scenes together. Warm cream paper background. Mood: the quiet realization that the
> same logic underneath can wear completely different clothes on top. Printed-book
> diagram feel, gentle and clear.
- **Avoid:** literal robots or assembly lines, corporate infographic gloss, cold steel
  blues dominating, photorealism, heavy 3D, neon, dense labeling, stock-icon style.

## Prompt 5, Texture / background: warm paper field (texture)

**Use:** A flexible backdrop for quote cards and section dividers.

> A close, slightly raking photograph of a sheet of warm cream paper with a subtle
> natural fiber grain and the faintest soft shadow at one edge. Even, gentle daylight, no
> hard highlights. A single small sage green dot sits low in one corner, hand-printed,
> like a quiet bookmark. Otherwise empty, calm, with plenty of open space for text.
> Color a warm cream tone, very low contrast, soft and inviting. The feel of good
> uncoated book stock under window light.
- **Avoid:** busy patterns, watermarks, visible text, harsh shadows, cool gray paper,
  digital noise, vignette, glossy or plastic surface, distracting detail.

---

## Notes for whoever runs these

- Render at high resolution, then crop to 1080 x 1350 (portrait) and 1080 x 1080
  (square) for social. Prompt 1 and Prompt 3 also need a landscape crop for the main
  infographic and web header.
- Keep saturation gentle across the set so all five sit together as one warm family.
- The sage green dot is the book's "this has a clear home" signal. Use it sparingly and
  only where it means something, never as decoration.
- If a tool drifts toward a cold or glossy look, push the prompt back toward "printed
  cookbook," "uncoated paper," and "soft daylight."
