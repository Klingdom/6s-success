# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 2, "What Is 6S?"

Five diagrams for social and infographic use. Four remix the chapter's existing
diagrams for standalone sharing. The fifth is new. All stay inside the book palette
(paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`,
slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark `#CB4B36`), Fraunces for display, Newsreader for
body.

---

## 1. From Factory Floor to Front Hall (timeline)

- **Purpose:** Tell the origin story in one glance. Show that a method born in a car
  factory walked all the way into the home, and that the idea never changed, only the
  setting.
- **What it shows:** Four nodes connected by a horizontal line, honey arrows between
  them.
  1. **Postwar Japan**: "Rebuild. Waste nothing." (terracotta ring)
  2. **Toyota system**: "5S is born." (terracotta ring)
  3. **Spreads worldwide**: "Hospitals, offices, airlines." (terracotta ring)
  4. **Into your home**: "Same idea. Your scale." (filled slate node, the destination)
- **Rough layout:** Landscape band, roughly 1000 x 280. Title eyebrow centered at top in
  soft sans. Line at mid-height, four circular nodes evenly spaced, each with a simple
  ink line-icon (factory roofline, gear, globe, house). Label in Fraunces under each
  node, one-line caption in Newsreader below that. The final house node is filled slate
  to mark arrival. Footer caption: *It was never really about cars.*

## 2. Five Japanese Words, Five English Ones (bilingual mapping)

- **Purpose:** Give readers the plain-English meaning of each original S, and quietly
  reassure them that the English labels vary between books.
- **What it shows:** Five rows. Each row: the Japanese word (italic, soft gray) on the
  left, a honey arrow, the English name (Fraunces, terracotta) in the middle, and the
  one-line home meaning (Newsreader, ink) on the right.
  - Seiri to **Sort**: Keep what belongs. Remove what does not.
  - Seiton to **Straighten**: A home for everything, by how often you use it.
  - Seiso to **Shine**: Clean, and notice problems while you do.
  - Seiketsu to **Standardize**: Make the good way the normal, visible way.
  - Shitsuke to **Sustain**: Keep it up. Build the habit.
- **Rough layout:** Portrait or square table, alternating row fills (`#fff` and
  `#F6EEDD`) with hairline borders. Eyebrow title at top. Italic footnote at bottom in
  Newsreader: *Different books use slightly different English words. The ideas, and the
  order, stay the same.* For social, this also works as a clean five-row table for a
  featured-snippet style carousel slide.

## 3. How 5S Became 6S (growth)

- **Purpose:** Make the math of the method obvious. The original five, plus the one
  pillar nobody will leave to chance, equals 6S.
- **What it shows:** A left-to-right equation.
  - Left: a cluster of five honey tiles under a big Fraunces **5S**, caption "The
    original five."
  - A Fraunces **+** in soft gray.
  - Middle: a single slate `#3C5A6B` **Safety** tile with a small shield-and-check
    glyph, caption "The one we refuse to leave to chance."
  - A Fraunces **=**.
  - Right: six tiles under a big **6S**, five honey and one slate, caption "The whole,
    safer set."
- **Rough layout:** Wide band, roughly 1000 x 240. Tiles are rounded squares with ink
  outlines. The slate Safety tile is the visual anchor, larger than the small tiles.
  Footer caption: *In this book, Safety is the fourth S, placed right after Shine.*

## 4. One Method, Any Scale (nested targets)

- **Purpose:** Show that the same six steps point at anything, from a drawer to a garage,
  so every small project is practice for the big ones.
- **What it shows:** Four objects growing larger left to right, each stamped with the
  same small green "6S" loop badge.
  - **A drawer**: 15 minutes
  - **A shelf**: 30 minutes
  - **A room**: An afternoon
  - **A garage**: A few weekends
- **Rough layout:** Landscape band, roughly 1000 x 260. Four simple ink line-icons on a
  shared baseline, each clearly bigger than the last to read as a rising scale. Honey
  step-up. Each item carries a green `#6E8B5B` circular "6S" badge in the same spot, so
  the eye sees the badge repeat while the object grows. Caption: *Learn the loop once,
  point it anywhere.*

## 5. The Findable Drawer vs the Interrogating Drawer (new, before and after)

- **Purpose:** Dramatize the chapter's core feeling. The opening "answers your question"
  versus "asks more" line, made into a single side-by-side picture. This is the new idea,
  and it ties directly to the chapter's closing Quick Win ("does this drawer answer me,
  or interrogate me?").
- **What it shows:** Two top-down drawers side by side, same outer frame, a dashed
  `#D9CDB8` divider down the middle.
  - **Left, "Interrogates you":** a jumble. Tangled cords, loose menus, a dead battery, a
    lone chopstick, a stray warranty card. No trays, no lanes. One small friction spark
    `#CB4B36` dot in the corner as the tension mark. Header in friction spark.
  - **Right, "Answers you":** the chapter's clean drawer. The bottle opener, corkscrew,
    and can opener together in a tray, forks in a lane, spoons in a lane, knives in a
    lane. Four green calm-dots `#6E8B5B` marking the items with a clear home. Header in
    green.
- **Rough layout:** Square or portrait, two equal panels. Identical drawer outline on
  both sides so only the contents differ, which keeps the comparison honest. Centered
  caption strip below in Newsreader italic: *The difference is two seconds, every single
  day.* Optional footer line, the canonical definition, to turn it into a self-contained
  share.

---

## Shared production notes

- All five reuse the chapter's flat-fill, ink-outline drawing style. No gradients or
  photo textures; keep the hand-made editorial feel.
- Green calm-dots only mark items that have a clear home. Do not use them as decoration.
- Friction spark appears only in Diagram 5 (the "interrogates you" panel). Everywhere
  else stays calm.
- Each diagram should hold up as a single social image and as a slide in a carousel.
  Export at 1080 wide minimum, with the eyebrow title and a one-line caption so the
  diagram explains itself without surrounding copy.
