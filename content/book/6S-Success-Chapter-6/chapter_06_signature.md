## Signature Overview

**Length:** 16 pages (8 spreads)
**Part:** Part 2, Prepare (the middle chapter of Part 2; it follows Chapter 5's audit baseline and hands off to Chapter 7, Define the Purpose of the Area)
**Reading time:** 9 to 12 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 58% text, 42% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Convince the reader to take an honest before photo of the target they audited in Chapter 5, teach them how to take one that will actually be useful later, and explain why the picture matters as much as the number. Pay off Chapter 5's closing promise ("the eye is generous, and the eye forgives, and the eye quietly edits out the pile you have stopped noticing; the camera does not; in the next chapter we take the picture"). Establish the before photo as the visual baseline that pairs with the audit's numeric baseline. Do not start the work. The sorting begins in Part 3. This chapter records the starting point.

**Throughline space:** the entryway drop zone again, carried through from Chapters 4 and 5, so the reader photographs the same space they chose and scored. Continuity makes the before/after payoff land harder when the room-by-room work begins.

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapters 1 to 5. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data. Green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns (the camera makes the sparks visible that the eye had faded out). The friction meter appears at the close.

**New hero motif introduced:** **the Eye vs the Camera.** A split image. On the left, the space as the eye actually sees it after months of habituation: soft, forgiving, the clutter faded into the background. On the right, the same space as the camera sees it: sharp, honest, every friction-spark in focus. The point in one picture: your eye has stopped seeing the mess, and the camera is the witness that has not. A second, practical device, **the Matched Pair**, shows the technique: shoot the before and the future after from the same marked spot, same frame, same light, so the comparison is honest and dramatic.

**Friction meter call:** holds steady, now documented. No objects move in this chapter, so the needle holds (book rule). Chapter 4 added the GOAL crosshair on the calm side; Chapter 5 added the NOW baseline marker on the friction side. Chapter 6 frames that NOW marker with small photo-corner brackets, signaling that the starting point is now recorded in two ways, a number and a picture. The needle still has not moved. The starting line is simply, finally, on the record.

## Page-by-Page Layout

### PAGE 1 (recto) | Chapter Opener
- **Spread objective:** Open on the small, slightly awkward act this chapter is about: holding up a phone to photograph a mess you would rather not photograph.
- **Layout:** Full-bleed image. Title lower third. Kicker "Part 2 · Prepare."
- **Visual:** [VISUAL: Full-page photo] A hand holding up a phone to photograph the entryway drop zone. On the phone screen the clutter is crisp and obvious; in the soft-focus room beyond, the same clutter half-disappears into the everyday. Warm light.
- **Caption:** "Your eye made peace with this mess months ago. The camera never did. Before you change a thing, let the honest witness take one good look."

### PAGE 2-3 (Spread A) | The Mess You Stopped Seeing
- **Spread objective:** Name the strange truth that the eye edits out clutter it lives with, and that the camera is the cure. Set up the before photo as the visual baseline that pairs with the audit number.
- **Layout:** Left page narrative. Right page a small spot illustration of the same drop zone twice, once soft (eye) and once sharp with red sparks (camera).
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Opening" plus the top of "Why Your Eye Lies to You," roughly 480 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > You stop seeing a mess about three days after it arrives. The camera never habituates. A photo shows you your space the way a guest sees it, which is the way it actually is.

### PAGE 4-5 (Spread B) | Why Your Eye Lies to You
- **Spread objective:** Explain habituation plainly and kindly, then add the second gift of the photo: it flattens the scene and reveals problems you walk past, the same way Shine reveals a leak.
- **Layout:** Text with the hero split infographic dominating the spread.
- **Body copy:** "Why Your Eye Lies to You," continued, roughly 420 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The Eye vs the Camera." Left, the space soft and forgiving, clutter faded, a calm green wash. Right, the same space sharp, every friction-spark in focus, framed like a phone photo. Caption line: the eye forgives, the camera does not.
- **Boxed definition:**
  > **The honest frame in one line**
  > A before photo is not vanity and it is not for show. It is evidence. It is the visual baseline that, paired with your audit number, proves the change you are about to make actually happened.

### PAGE 6-7 (Spread C) | The Before Is for the After
- **Spread objective:** Make the motivational case. The before/after pair is the single most powerful artifact in this whole method, and the after means nothing without an honest before.
- **Layout:** Text with a small before/after thumbnail spot of the drop zone.
- **Body copy:** "The Before Is for the After," roughly 420 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Skipping the before because you are embarrassed by it. The embarrassing photo is the valuable one. A flattering before steals your own future proof, and the proof is what carries you into the next room.

### PAGE 8-9 (Spread D) | How to Take a Useful Before Photo
- **Spread objective:** Teach the technique, fast and practical. The matched pair: same spot, whole area, good light, a few shots, and the floor mark that makes the after line up.
- **Layout:** Text with the practical "Matched Pair" infographic.
- **Body copy:** "How to Take a Useful Before Photo," roughly 460 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The Matched Pair." A simple diagram: a phone shooting the drop zone from a marked spot on the floor (a small X), a wide shot and a detail shot, and two framed thumbnails, before and after, lined up. Rule labels: same spot, whole area, good light, do not tidy first.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > Mark where you stood. A scuff on the floor, a tile line, a note in your phone. The after photo only lands if it is shot from the exact same spot, so the room, not the camera, is what changed.

### PAGE 10-11 (Spread E) | Do Not Tidy First
- **Spread objective:** The firm, friendly rule, carried over from Chapter 1. The urge to straighten up before the picture is powerful and must be defeated, because the messy truth is the entire point.
- **Layout:** Text with a small spot of a hand reaching to "fix" the shot, with a gentle stop mark.
- **Body copy:** "Do Not Tidy First," roughly 380 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Straightening up "just a little" before the before. A staged before is a lie you tell only yourself, and it quietly robs the after of everything that would have made it satisfying.

### PAGE 12-13 (Spread F) | Keep It, and Keep It Yours
- **Spread objective:** Handle saving and privacy. Save it where you will find it, name it with the space and the date, and know that the before is yours. You never have to post it. One trusted person, if you want accountability, is plenty.
- **Layout:** Text with a small spot of a phone album labeled "6S Before."
- **Body copy:** "Keep It, and Keep It Yours," roughly 380 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Make a photo album called "6S Before" right now, before you shoot anything. One named place to put these means you will actually find them on the day you want to feel proud.

### PAGE 14-15 (Spread G) | Take the Picture
- **Spread objective:** Turn it into the single small action. Stand on your spot, frame the whole audited space, do not tidy, take three shots, save them, note the date. Done.
- **Layout:** A short, encouraging section and a compact "Take the before photo" checklist.
- **Body copy:** "Take the Picture," roughly 340 words.
- **Checklist:**
  > **Take the before photo**
  > - Open a photo album named "6S Before."
  > - Stand in one fixed spot, and remember it.
  > - Frame the whole audited area, then take one detail shot.
  > - Do not tidy first. The mess is the point.
  > - Save the shots and note the space and the date.
- **Callout:**
  > **Family Challenge**
  > Everyone photographs their own target space, no tidying allowed, and drops the before photos in a shared album. In a month, the family revisit is its own small celebration. Worst before plus best after wins.

### PAGE 16 (verso) | Chapter Close and What's Next
- **Spread objective:** Land the recorded starting point and tee up Chapter 7.
- **Layout:** Short close, "One Idea to Keep" box, friction-meter spot (holds, NOW documented), "Next" strip.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Close," roughly 240 words.
- **Boxed recap:**
  > **One Idea to Keep**
  > The after only means something next to an honest before. Take the picture you would rather not take. It is the one that will prove, later, that you did this.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Friction-meter spot] The meter holding where it sat, with the NOW baseline marker now framed by small photo-corner brackets, and the GOAL crosshair still on the calm side. The starting point is recorded twice, in numbers and in a picture.
- **Next teaser:** "Next: Chapter 7 · Define the Purpose of the Area. You have measured it and pictured it. Before you change a thing, one short, clarifying question: what is this space actually for?"

## Production Notes / Review Flags
- This chapter records; it does not fix. Keep the reader from starting the work. Sorting begins in Part 3 (Chapter 8). Part 2 is Prepare: audit (Ch 5), photograph (Ch 6), purpose (Ch 7).
- The before photo first appeared as Chapter 1's "first move." Confirm the relationship reads as intended: Chapter 1 introduced the before photo as a gentle first action to prove you have started; Chapter 6 gives it the full treatment as the visual baseline for your chosen, audited target. The two should feel like a deliberate setup and payoff, not a repeat.
- The friction meter gains photo-corner brackets on the NOW marker. Confirm this read. If review prefers the meter unchanged from Chapter 5, the brackets drop easily.
- Keep the privacy note. Some readers will not want to photograph or keep images of their home. Honor that; the before is for them, optional to share.
- Reuse Chapters 1 to 5's exact type, palette, and callout styling. The Eye vs the Camera split should be drawn so the camera side can be lifted out as a standalone social graphic.
