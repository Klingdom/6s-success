# Chapter 6 Summary: Photograph Before You Fix

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Two, Prepare*

## One-line summary

Before you fix the space you audited, take an honest before photo, because your eye has stopped seeing the mess but the camera never habituates, and the picture is the visual baseline that proves the change later.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Chapter 6 explains why you stop seeing the messes you live with, and what to do about it. Your brain habituates to anything that stays the same, so the pile you walk past every day becomes invisible to you and obvious to a guest. The camera is the cure: it never habituates, so a photo shows your space the way it really is. The chapter pairs the visual baseline with Chapter 5's numeric one, teaches the matched-pair technique (shoot the before and the future after from the same marked spot), repeats the firm rule "do not tidy first," and keeps the before private if you wish.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 6 is the middle chapter of Part Two, Prepare. The reader has chosen and audited a target. Now they record it visually.

It opens on habituation. Your eye is a generous editor that trims familiar detail into the background, so a mess that has stayed put for a few days stops registering as a problem and becomes scenery. The trouble is that "stopped being news" feels identical to "stopped being a problem," and they are not the same. A photograph breaks the spell because it does not edit. It flattens the space into a rectangle you can see the way a stranger would, which is why people are routinely a little shocked by the first before photo of a room they see every day. The camera also works as a second kind of audit, revealing problems you walk past, the way Shine reveals a leak.

The chapter's deeper case is motivational: the before and after pair is the most powerful artifact in the method, and its power lives entirely in the gap between the two images, a gap that only exists if you captured an honest before. So the embarrassing before is the valuable one.

It then teaches the matched-pair technique: pick a spot and mark it, frame the whole area plus a detail shot, get good light, and above all do not tidy first. It covers saving the photos in a "6S Before" album and keeping them private if preferred. The reader takes the picture, and the starting line is now drawn in numbers and in an image. Next, Chapter 7 asks what the space is for.
