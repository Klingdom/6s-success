# Chapter 6 Outline: Photograph Before You Fix

A section-by-section map of the chapter as written in the final HTML and manuscript. This chapter records the chosen, audited space; it does not yet fix it.

## Opener: The same clutter, twice
A full-bleed image of a phone held up to the entryway drop zone: sharp on the screen, soft in the room. Caption: "Your eye made peace with this mess months ago. The camera never did. Before you change a thing, let the honest witness take one good look."

## 1. Chapter Opening: The Mess You Stopped Seeing
There is a pile you cannot see anymore, in plain sight, that your brain filed under "normal, ignore." Habituation is a feature, but it makes your mess invisible to you and obvious to a guest. The camera is the cure: it does not habituate. Before you fix the audited space, photograph it, for proof. The audit gave a number; this gives a picture.

## 2. What You Will Learn (objectives)
- Why you stop seeing the messes you live with
- How a photo works as an honest witness, and as a second audit
- How to take a before photo that will be useful later
- How to shoot a matched pair so the after lines up with the before
- How to save and protect your before photos, on your own terms

## 3. Why Your Eye Lies to You
The eye is a generous editor; it trims familiar detail into the background. "Stopped being news" feels like "stopped being a problem" but is not. A photo does not edit; it flattens the space so you see it like a stranger. The small shock is accurate vision returning. The camera is also a second inspection: it reveals the cord, the drawer that does not close, the colonized chair. The audit named the weakest S; the photo shows where it lives.
- Hero infographic: The Eye vs the Camera (soft forgiving eye view vs sharp honest camera view)
- Definition box: A before photo is evidence, the visual baseline that paired with your audit number proves the change happened.

## 4. The Before Is for the After
The before/after pair is the most motivating artifact in the method. Its power is the gap between the images, which only exists if the before is honest. It is the receipt for your effort and your rebuttal on the day the old voice says you are not organized. So the embarrassing before is the valuable one; a modest before makes a modest after.
- Callout (Common Mistake): Skipping the before because you are embarrassed. The embarrassing photo is the valuable one.

## 5. How to Take a Useful Before Photo
You are taking the first half of a matched pair. Pick your spot and mark it (a tile, a scuff, a note), because the drama comes from before and after sharing the same frame. Frame the whole area plus a detail shot of the worst friction. Get good light. Same spot, whole area, good light, a couple of shots.
- Infographic: The Matched Pair (before and after thumbnails from one marked spot; rules: same spot, whole area, good light, do not tidy first)
- Callout (6S Tip): Mark where you stood. The after only lands if shot from the exact same spot.

## 6. Do Not Tidy First
The same rule from Chapter 1, broken every time. The urge to straighten before the before is self-sabotage dressed as good manners. Every item you tidy is transformation you erase in advance. A staged before makes the after look like barely any change. The most satisfying afters sit next to befores that made their owners cringe.
- Callout (Common Mistake): Straightening up "just a little" before the before. A staged before is a lie you tell only yourself.

## 7. Keep It, and Keep It Yours
Decide where the photos live before you shoot: a "6S Before" album, named with the space and date. And the before is yours: you never have to post it. Public accountability is optional; a private album used as your own evidence is completely valid, and one trusted person is plenty if you want accountability.
- Callout (Quick Win): Make a "6S Before" album now, before you shoot anything.

## 8. Take the Picture
Go to the audited space, open the album, choose and notice your spot, frame the whole space, resist tidying, take a wide shot and a detail or two, save and date them. You now have a number and a picture: a complete, truthful record of the start. Do not study it or feel bad; it is a tool, not a verdict.
- Checklist (Take the before photo): open the album, stand in a fixed spot, frame the whole area plus a detail, do not tidy first, save and note the space and date.
- Callout (Family Challenge): Everyone photographs their target, no tidying, into a shared album. Revisit in a month. Worst before plus best after wins.

## 9. Chapter Close: The Starting Line, in Ink
The photo changed nothing in the room, but it made the starting point real, outside forgetful memory, as a number and an image. "Prepared" means measured, pictured, and impossible to deny. One step left in Part 2: what is this space for? A space organized for the wrong purpose is tidy in a way that will not last.
- Boxed recap (One Idea to Keep): The after only means something next to an honest before. Take the picture you would rather not take. It is the one that will prove, later, that you did this.
