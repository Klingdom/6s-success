# Chapter 6 Calls to Action: Photograph Before You Fix

This chapter records the starting point; it does not fix anything. Its strongest CTA is the before photo itself. The CTAs match the chapter's encouraging, low-pressure, privacy-respecting tone.

## Primary CTA
Take the before photo. "Open a photo album called '6S Before.' Stand in one fixed spot you can find again, frame the whole space you audited, and take a wide shot and a detail shot. Do not tidy first. Save them with the space and the date. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Your eye stopped seeing the mess months ago. Chapter 6 shows you why, and how to take a before photo that makes the after worth it. Read it free online, then Chapter 7 asks what the space is actually for."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Make a "6S Before" photo album right now, before you shoot anything.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone photographs their target space, no tidying, into a shared album. Revisit in a month. Worst before plus best after wins.

## Soft CTAs for social posts (rotate these)
- "This is from Chapter 6 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online."
- "Take the before photo you would rather not take. The chapter shows you how."
- "The camera never habituates. The full chapter is free in the online book."
- "Grab the free Before and After Photo Guide and shoot a matched pair."
- "Next after the photo: Chapter 7, what is this space actually for?"

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the signature)
- **The Before and After Photo Guide:** a printable of the matched-pair technique, the floor-mark and same-spot rules, and a place to log the date and the audit score.
- **The "6S Before" album setup card:** how to make and name the album and a simple naming convention (space plus date), so the photos are findable later.

## CTA writing notes
- The before photo is the action. Lead with "stand on your spot, frame the whole space, do not tidy, shoot," which is concrete and about a minute.
- Respect privacy. The before is the reader's own; never imply they must post it. "Keep it private if you want" is part of the message.
- "The embarrassing before is the valuable one" is the most counterintuitive, shareable hook in this chapter. Lead social with it.
- Avoid hype. The claim is honest: the picture is proof, not magic.
