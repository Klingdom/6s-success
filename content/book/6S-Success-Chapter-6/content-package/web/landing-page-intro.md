# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 6, Photograph Before You Fix

There is a pile somewhere in your home that you cannot see anymore. It is not hidden. It sits in plain sight, somewhere you walk past a dozen times a day, and your brain has quietly filed it under "normal, ignore." This is not a flaw. Your eye is a generous editor. It softens what stays the same so it can save your attention for what is new. The trouble is that the mess you live with becomes invisible to the one person most able to fix it, which is you. A guest walks in and sees it instantly. You have not really seen it in months.

There is a simple cure, and you are holding it. A camera. The lens does not habituate. It has no fond tolerance built up over a hundred busy mornings. It just records what is in front of it, sharply and honestly, with all the small frictions your eye learned to smooth away. So before you fix the space you audited, you photograph it. Not to shame yourself. To record an honest starting point, the visual baseline that pairs with your audit number and proves, later, that you did this.

It takes about sixty seconds. Read the chapter free in the online book, then go take the picture you would rather not take.
