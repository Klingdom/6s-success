# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions

### Chapter 6: Photograph Before You Fix

These questions are open-ended. Use them on your own or with a group. There are no right answers.

1. The chapter opens by saying there is a pile in your home you cannot see anymore. What is yours? Where is it, and how long do you think it has been invisible to you?

2. The chapter calls habituation a feature, not a flaw, because your brain saves attention for what is new. Where in your life does this same blindness help you, and where does it cost you?

3. The author says "stopped being news" and "stopped being a problem" feel identical from the inside but are not the same thing. Can you think of a mess, a habit, or even a situation where you confused the two?

4. A guest sees your clutter instantly, but you have not seen it in months. Has someone else ever pointed out something in your home you had completely stopped noticing? How did it feel to suddenly see it again?

5. The chapter describes the small shock of seeing a first before photo as "accurate vision returning." If you have ever photographed a familiar space, did it look different in the picture than in your memory? Why do you think that is?

6. The book argues the embarrassing before is the valuable one, and that the power of a before and after lives entirely in the gap between them. Do you agree? What makes you want to skip or stage a before photo?

7. Why do you think so many people who "try to get organized" never capture an honest starting point? What does that cost them later?

8. "Do not tidy first" is called the one rule that matters more than all the technique. Why is the urge to straighten up before documenting the mess so strong, and who is the staged before really fooling?

9. The matched-pair technique asks you to stand in the same marked spot both times. Beyond photos, where else in life does keeping the conditions the same help you see what actually changed?

10. The chapter is firm that the before is yours, private if you want it, and that you owe no one your drop zone. Where do you land on public accountability versus private evidence, and what would actually motivate you to follow through?
