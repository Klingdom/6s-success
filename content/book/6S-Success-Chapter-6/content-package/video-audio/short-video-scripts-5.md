## SHORT 1: You Stop Seeing Your Own Mess
**Topic: habituation. Runtime: about 45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** "There is a pile in your home you literally cannot see anymore."

[VISUAL: Host walks past a cluttered drop zone without glancing at it, then stops and turns back.]

[SCRIPT]
There is a pile in your home you literally cannot see anymore. Not hidden. In plain sight. You walked past it this morning and your brain filed it under "normal, ignore," the same way it ignores the hum of the fridge. This is not laziness. Your brain habituates to anything that stays the same. It takes about three days. The problem is the mess becomes invisible to the one person who can fix it, which is you. A guest sees it the second they walk in. So how do you see it again? You hold up the thing in your hand. The camera never habituates.

[ON SCREEN TEXT beats:]
- "You stop seeing your own mess."
- "About 3 days."
- "Invisible to you. Obvious to a guest."
- "The camera never habituates."

[VISUAL: Phone lifts to the drop zone. Clutter on screen is sharp, room behind is soft.]

[CTA]: Hold your phone up to the messiest corner in your home right now. From Chapter 6 of 6S Success: Home Edition.

## SHORT 2: The Embarrassing Before Is the Valuable One
**Topic: the embarrassing before. Runtime: about 50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** "Stop cleaning up before you take the before photo."

[VISUAL: Host's hand reaches to straighten a messy pile, then freezes mid-air.]

[SCRIPT]
Stop. Do not clean up before you take the before photo. I know the urge. You lift the phone, the mess looks awful through the lens, and you want to straighten it just a little. Here is why that is a mistake. The whole power of a before and after is the gap between the two pictures. A modest before makes a modest after. But an honest, slightly mortifying before makes an after that genuinely moves you. The most satisfying afters in the world sit next to befores that made their owners cringe. So cringe a little. You are not taking this for anyone else. You are taking it for the you in a month who wants to feel how far they came.

[ON SCREEN TEXT beats:]
- "Do not clean up first."
- "The power is in the gap."
- "Modest before = modest after."
- "The embarrassing before is the valuable one."

[VISUAL: A genuinely messy before slides next to a calm after. The contrast lands.]

## SHORT 3: The Matched Pair
**Topic: the matched-pair technique. Runtime: about 60 seconds.**

**Hook line:** "Your before and after photos never line up. Here is the fix."

[VISUAL: Two mismatched photos of a room, shot from different angles, shaking side by side. A red X over them.]

[SCRIPT]
Your before and after photos never line up, and here is why. You shot the before from the doorway and the after from across the room. That is not a comparison. That is two unrelated photos. The trick is the matched pair. First, decide where you stand, and remember it. Notice what you are standing on. A tile, a scuff, the edge of the rug. Or drop a note in your phone. Then frame the whole area, not just a flattering corner. Wide shot first. Then one detail shot of the worst friction. Good light, which just means turn on the lights and open the blinds. When you finish the space, you stand on that exact spot again. Now the room is the only thing that changed, and the comparison tells the truth.

[ON SCREEN TEXT beats:]
- "Same spot."
- "Whole area."
- "Good light."
- "Mark where you stood."

[VISUAL: A phone icon on a floor mark with dashed lines up to a matched before and after thumbnail.]

[CTA]: Mark your spot before you shoot. Grab the matched-pair technique in Chapter 6.

## SHORT 4: Do Not Tidy First
**Topic: do not tidy first. Runtime: about 40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** "Tidying before the before photo is stealing from yourself."

[VISUAL: Hand reaches toward a pile to fix it. Hard stop. Pulls back.]

[SCRIPT]
Tidying before the before photo is stealing from yourself. Every item you quietly straighten is a piece of your eventual transformation, and you are erasing it in advance. A staged before is a lie, and the only person it fools is you. It fools you in the worst direction, by making your real, hard-won after look like barely any change at all. So when your hand twitches toward the pile to push the worst of it out of frame, do not. The messy truth is not a side effect of this photo. It is the entire point of it.

[ON SCREEN TEXT beats:]
- "Do not tidy first."
- "A staged before is a lie you tell yourself."
- "The mess is the point."

[VISUAL: Phone takes the honest, messy shot. Shutter sound.]

[CTA]: Shoot it exactly as it is. Chapter 6 of 6S Success: Home Edition.

## SHORT 5: Keep It Private
**Topic: keep it private. Runtime: about 45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** "You never have to post your messy before. Ever."

[VISUAL: Host holds phone, then turns the screen face-down on the table, calm and reassuring.]

[SCRIPT]
You never have to post your messy before. Ever. The internet is full of people sharing their befores and their afters, and if that kind of public accountability lights a fire under you, wonderful, use it. But it is completely optional. Plenty of people find photographing their mess private, or uncomfortable, or simply nobody else's business, and all of that is valid. You can take a before photo, keep it in a private album no one will ever see, use it as your own evidence, and delete it the moment you want to. The photo serves you, not an audience. If you do want a little accountability, one trusted person you text the pair to is plenty.

[ON SCREEN TEXT beats:]
- "You never have to post it."
- "Keep it private if you want."
- "The photo serves you, not an audience."
- "One trusted person is plenty."

[VISUAL: A private album titled "6S Before" on the phone, with a small lock icon.]

[CTA]: Make a private "6S Before" album and take the shot just for you. Chapter 6, free online.
