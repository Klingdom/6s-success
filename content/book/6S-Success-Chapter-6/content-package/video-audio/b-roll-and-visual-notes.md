# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Photograph Before You Fix

**Chapter 6 of 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions.**
**Slug: photograph-before-you-fix**

Visual language pulled from the chapter design: warm paper tones, a soft forgiving "eye" view versus a sharp framed "camera" view, green calm-dots for the after, red friction-sparks for the before, and the friction meter that holds steady while the NOW marker becomes a documented photo.

---

## CORE SHOT LIST

### 1. The phone held up to a drop zone (signature opener)
- [B-ROLL] A hand lifts a phone toward a cluttered entryway drop zone. Critical detail: the clutter on the phone screen is razor sharp and in focus, while the actual room behind the phone is soft and slightly blurred. This is the hero image of the whole chapter.
- Mirrors the chapter opener SVG: "the same clutter, twice."
- Shoot variants: wide (whole entryway visible), and tight (just the phone screen and the hand).
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "The same clutter, twice."

### 2. The eye-vs-camera split
- [VISUAL] Split frame. Left labeled WHAT YOUR EYE SEES, rendered soft, faded, with a faint green calm wash over the room. Right labeled WHAT THE CAMERA SEES, sharp, enclosed in a phone frame, with small red friction-sparks marking the cord, the overflow, the colonized chair.
- Animate: left side fades up gently, right side snaps into focus with a subtle shutter click.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "The eye forgives. The camera does not."
- Lower third available here: see lower-third list below.

### 3. The floor mark where you stand
- [B-ROLL] A foot or shoe next to a specific, identifiable floor feature: a tile line, a scuff, the edge of a rug. Then a quick insert of a note being typed into a phone, for example "drop zone, stand on tile."
- [VISUAL] Optional graphic overlay: a small terra-colored X or crosshair on the floor where the person stands, with dashed sight lines rising to the framed shot.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "Mark where you stood."

### 4. The matched before/after pair
- [VISUAL] A before thumbnail (red BEFORE tag, friction-sparks, messy) and an after thumbnail (green AFTER tag, green calm-dots, clear) shown side by side, both obviously shot from the same position. A small phone icon sits on the floor mark below, with dashed sight lines to both frames.
- Footer strip: SAME SPOT, WHOLE AREA, GOOD LIGHT, DO NOT TIDY FIRST.
- Animate: before and after slide together; hold on the gap between them.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "The power is in the gap."

### 5. The hand that reaches to tidy, then stops
- [B-ROLL] A hand reaches toward a messy pile to straighten it, hesitates, and pulls back. Shoot in slow motion if possible. This is the visual anchor for "do not tidy first."
- Pair with the messy phone screen still showing the untouched mess.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "Do not tidy first. The mess is the point."

### 6. The "6S Before" phone album
- [B-ROLL] A phone screen showing a photo album titled "6S Before." A finger creates the album, then drops a freshly shot photo into it. Show a small date and space label on the photo, for example "Entryway drop zone, June."
- Optional reassurance beat: the album shown with a small lock icon, or the phone turned face-down on a table, to signal privacy.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "Make a '6S Before' album."

### 7. The friction meter, holding, now documented
- [VISUAL] The friction meter gauge. The needle sits where it was, pointing toward the friction side, because no objects have moved. CALM and GOAL on the green left, FRICTION and NOW on the red right.
- Key change from earlier chapters: the NOW marker is now framed by small photo-corner brackets, signaling it has been documented. The GOAL crosshair stays on the calm side.
- Caption under it: "Measured, and now photographed."
- [ON SCREEN TEXT moment]: "Nothing has moved yet. But the starting line is drawn."

## SUPPORTING B-ROLL (cutaways)

- Someone grabbing keys off a cluttered table without looking at the surrounding objects (illustrates habituation and the editing eye).
- A slow pan across an ordinary lived-in room: stack of papers, tangle of cords, a chair that became a wardrobe.
- A phone push-in on a single friction point: a cord to trip on, a drawer that will not fully close, three bags on one chair.
- A hand turning on a light and opening blinds; soft natural light filling the space (good light).
- A finger scrolling a camera roll and stopping, surprised, on an old photo of a cluttered counter (the personal-aside beat).
- A messy before and a calm after held up side by side, the contrast doing the work.
- A phone shutter moment: the screen flashes, the shot is captured, the phone is set down.

## COLOR AND TONE NOTES

- Keep the palette warm and paper-toned, matching the book design. Avoid cold, clinical white.
- Before / friction = red sparks. After / calm = green dots. Use these consistently so the audience reads the language without explanation.
- The "eye" view should feel soft, warm, and slightly hazy. The "camera" view should be crisp, framed, and a touch cooler, like a phone photo.
- Privacy beats should feel reassuring, not stern. A face-down phone, a soft smile, an unhurried pace.
- No hype graphics, no fast aggressive cuts. The chapter's voice is warm, encouraging, and honest. The edit should match it.
