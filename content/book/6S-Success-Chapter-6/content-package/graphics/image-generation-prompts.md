# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 6, "Photograph Before You Fix"

Five prompts for hand-made, editorial, cozy-but-clean photography that matches the
book's warmth. Each is specific about subject, composition, light, materials, palette,
and mood, and each ends with a short "avoid" note. The palette to steer toward in every
image: warm paper cream, soft oatmeal, muted terracotta, honey gold, dusty slate blue,
calm sage green, all under warm natural light. The feel is a real home, lived in and
honest, photographed with care rather than staged for a catalog.

---

## Prompt 1: The opener, a phone held up to the drop zone

A photograph taken over a person's shoulder as they hold a smartphone up to an
entryway drop zone by the front door. On the phone screen the clutter is crisp and in
focus: a small shelf piled with mail, a tangle of keys, a couple of canvas bags on a
hook. In the room beyond the phone, the same clutter is gently soft and out of focus.
Morning light comes low through a nearby window, warm and a little golden, raking
across the wall. Materials read as real: a painted wood shelf, a woven jute basket,
brass hooks, cream plaster wall. Color palette of warm paper cream, oatmeal, muted
terracotta, and honey. Composition slightly off-center, the phone on the right third,
the soft room filling the left. Mood is honest and a little tender, the moment before
someone decides to change a space. Editorial lifestyle photography, natural grain, no
heavy retouching.

**Avoid:** glossy real-estate staging, a spotless showroom hallway, neon screen glow,
fisheye distortion, oversaturated colors, a model smiling at the camera.

---

## Prompt 2: The Eye vs the Camera, a diptych

A two-panel image of the exact same corner of a home, a small console table by a door
with everyday clutter on it. Left panel: the scene rendered soft and hazy, slightly
faded, with a faint sage-green cast over it, as if remembered rather than seen,
familiar and forgiven. Right panel: the identical scene, same objects in the same
spots, but sharp, clear, and honestly lit, every detail crisp, the way a phone photo
looks. The two panels share one warm color palette of cream, oatmeal, slate blue, and
terracotta. Soft daylight from a window on the left. Real materials: a wooden table, a
ceramic dish of keys, a stack of post, a folded scarf. Composition perfectly mirrored
so the only difference between the panels is the softness versus the sharpness. Mood is
quiet and revealing, a gentle before-the-fix feeling. Editorial, hand-made, warm.

**Avoid:** harsh split-screen graphic borders, dramatic color grading, a messy versus
clean comparison (both panels show the same clutter), HDR halos, plastic textures.

---

## Prompt 3: The matched pair on a phone, with the floor mark

A close, warm photograph looking down at a person's feet and the lower half of a
smartphone held out to frame a room, with a small piece of low-tack painter's tape
marking an X on the wood floor where they are standing. The phone shows a wide shot of
the space being documented. The framing teaches a quiet lesson about standing in the
same spot. Light is soft and indoor, warm afternoon through a window, gentle shadows.
Materials: worn oak floorboards, a sock-foot or simple slipper, the matte back of a
phone, a strip of cream tape. Color palette of warm cream, honey, oatmeal, with one
small terracotta accent on the tape mark. Composition from a high angle looking down,
the floor mark in the lower center, the phone upper right. Mood is practical and
calm, a small careful habit. Documentary lifestyle photography, natural and unstaged.

**Avoid:** a tripod or professional rig, a perfectly clean styled floor, studio
lighting, top-down flat-lay symmetry, bright white balance.

---

## Prompt 4: The 6S Before phone album

A cozy overhead photograph of a smartphone resting on a warm wooden table, the screen
showing a photo album titled "6S Before" filled with a grid of before-photo thumbnails
of different rooms and corners of a home. Around the phone, a few honest props: a mug
of tea, a pen, a small notebook with a handwritten date. Light is warm and low, a soft
lamp glow plus a little daylight, the kind of evening light you tidy your photos in.
Materials read real: brushed wood grain, ceramic mug, paper notebook, the phone's
matte case. Color palette of warm paper cream, honey gold, oatmeal, with a dusty slate
blue notebook cover. Composition a relaxed overhead, the phone slightly angled, props
arranged loosely rather than styled. Mood is content and quietly organized, a small
ritual. Editorial still life, warm and inviting.

**Avoid:** a clinical white desk flat-lay, perfectly aligned props, cold blue screen
light, fake app interface that looks like a stock template, glossy reflections.

---

## Prompt 5: The friction meter, framed as a photo on the wall

A still life of a single framed print hanging on a warm cream plaster wall, the print
showing a simple half-circle gauge that runs from calm sage green on the left to muted
terracotta and spark red on the right, with a needle resting toward the friction side
and a small "NOW" marker wrapped in photo-corner brackets. Below the frame, a narrow
wooden shelf holds a phone face up and a sprig of dried eucalyptus in a small vase. Warm
side light from the left casts a soft shadow of the frame onto the wall. Materials: a
thin oak frame, textured matte paper print, plaster wall, dried botanicals, a worn
wooden shelf. Color palette of warm cream, sage green, honey, slate blue, and a single
spark-red accent on the gauge. Composition centered and calm, plenty of negative space
on the plaster around the frame. Mood is settled and reflective, a measured starting
point made real. Editorial, hand-made, gallery-warm.

**Avoid:** a sterile gallery wall, glass glare across the print, a busy gallery cluster
of frames, cold gray walls, an infographic that looks like a corporate dashboard.

---

## Shared direction for all five

- Real homes and real materials, not showrooms. A little wear is good.
- Warm natural light, low and soft, never hard studio flash or cold daylight balance.
- The book palette throughout: cream paper, oatmeal, terracotta, honey, slate blue,
  sage green. One accent doing the work per frame.
- Composition with breathing room and an off-center subject, the way a careful editor
  would crop it, not a stock template.
- Natural grain and honest texture over heavy retouching. The mood is cozy, clean, and
  a little tender, matching the chapter's warm voice.
