# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 6, "Photograph Before You Fix"

Five diagrams drawn from the chapter's own figures. Each is described as a clean
vector concept in the book palette: paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark
`#CB4B36`. Fraunces for display, Newsreader italic for mood captions, Inter for small
labels. All four diagrams already exist as SVGs in `chapter_06_final.html`; these notes
adapt them for standalone social use.

---

## 1. The Eye vs the Camera (the split)

**Purpose:** Explain habituation in one image. Show that the eye softens your own mess
into scenery while the camera reports it honestly.

**What it shows:** The same entryway drop zone, twice, on a panel background split by a
vertical dashed rule.
- Left, labelled `WHAT YOUR EYE SEES`: the shelf, legs, stacked items and a hook ring
  drawn in muted oatmeal line (`#C9B79A`) under a soft green wash (`#6E8B5B`, about 7
  percent). Newsreader italic caption: *Forgiving. Faded. Familiar.*
- Right, labelled `WHAT THE CAMERA SEES`: identical objects in identical positions, now
  inside a phone frame in crisp ink, with three friction sparks (`#CB4B36`) on the worst
  points.
- Beneath both, centered in Fraunces slate: *The eye forgives. The camera does not.*

**Rough layout:** Landscape, roughly 1000 x 470. Two equal halves, the center divider a
dashed `#D9CDB8` line. Labels top, art middle, the closing line full-width at the
bottom. This is the strongest single image in the chapter; treat it as the flagship.

---

## 2. The Matched Pair

**Purpose:** Teach the technique that makes a before and after actually line up: one
marked spot, shot twice.

**What it shows:** Two thumbnails of the same drop zone.
- A `BEFORE` thumbnail tagged spark red `#CB4B36`, showing clutter with one friction
  spark.
- An `AFTER` thumbnail tagged calm green `#6E8B5B`, same framing, shelf now clear, three
  small green dots where the clutter was.
- Between and below them, a phone icon standing on a floor line, with a terracotta floor
  mark (`#BC4B2A`, an X) beneath it and two dashed sight lines fanning up to the two
  thumbnails. Newsreader italic: *you stand here, both times.*
- Bottom rule row in Inter slate: `SAME SPOT . WHOLE AREA . GOOD LIGHT . DO NOT TIDY FIRST`

**Rough layout:** Landscape, roughly 1000 x 470. Two thumbnails across the top two
thirds, the phone-on-floor-mark and dashed sight lines forming a shallow triangle in the
center, the four-rule strip across the bottom. Header label `THE MATCHED PAIR` at the top.

---

## 3. Before and After pair (clean, no annotation)

**Purpose:** The payoff image. Show the gap between the two photos, which is where the
chapter says all the motivation lives. This one is meant to be felt, not studied.

**What it shows:** Two equal frames side by side, drawn like phone photos with rounded
corners and a thin ink border.
- Left frame, small spark-red `BEFORE` tag: the drop zone cluttered, the colonized hook,
  a stack on the shelf, a cord on the floor. Honest, a little much.
- Right frame, small green `AFTER` tag: the same spot from the same angle, shelf clear,
  hook holding one item, floor clean, three calm green dots.
- No sparks, no sight lines, no rules. Just the two frames and a thin honey hairline
  between them.

**Rough layout:** Square 1080 x 1080 or landscape. Two frames of equal size, centered,
small breathing room between. Optional tiny Newsreader caption beneath, soft gray: *the
power is in the gap.* Keep it spare so the comparison does the talking.

---

## 4. What the Camera Reveals (annotated frame)

**Purpose:** Make the "second audit" idea concrete. A photo is also an inspection; it
surfaces frictions you walk past in the flesh.

**What it shows:** A single phone photo of the drop zone, drawn in crisp ink inside a
phone frame, with three or four callout lines pulling out to labels in the margin. Each
label is small Inter text with a spark-red `#CB4B36` leader line and dot:
- "the cord you would trip on"
- "the drawer that never fully closes"
- "three bags on one chair"
- "the overflow you stopped noticing"

(The first three labels are taken directly from the chapter; the fourth is a stand-in,
swap in whatever the real photo shows. The chapter's examples are the cord, the drawer
that never fully closes, and three bags that colonized one chair.)

**Rough layout:** Portrait or square. The phone photo centered or slightly left, the
four labels stacked down the right margin with thin spark leader lines reaching to each
flagged object. Header in Inter slate: `WHAT THE CAMERA REVEALS`. Footer Newsreader
italic: *the flat frame hands the friction back to you.*

---

## 5. The Friction Meter, NOW framed as a photo

**Purpose:** Close the chapter's arc. The needle has not moved because no objects moved,
but the starting point is now on the record, measured and photographed.

**What it shows:** A half-circle gauge, calm green on the left running through honey to
spark red on the right, with a black needle resting on the friction side (matching the
Chapter 5 meter, unchanged).
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair on the calm side.
- A slate `NOW` diamond marker on the friction side, this time wrapped in four
  photo-corner brackets (`#2B2622`), so the baseline reads as "photographed."
- `CALM` label in green at the lower left, `FRICTION` label in spark red at the lower
  right.
- Newsreader italic caption beneath: *Measured, and now photographed.*

**Rough layout:** Compact, roughly 420 x 260, portrait-friendly. Gauge arc across the
top two thirds, GOAL and NOW markers sitting on the arc, labels along the baseline, the
italic caption at the very bottom. The only change from the Chapter 5 version is the
photo-corner brackets around NOW, which carry the whole chapter's point: the starting
line is now drawn in ink.
