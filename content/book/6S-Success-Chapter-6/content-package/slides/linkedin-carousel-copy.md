# LinkedIn Carousel Copy: Photograph Before You Fix

**Book:** 6S Success: Home Edition · Chapter 6
**Slug:** photograph-before-you-fix
**Format:** 10-slide carousel. On-slide copy written out below, ready to set.

Design notes:
- Palette: paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`. Accents: terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`. Motif: green calm-dot `#6E8B5B`, red friction-spark `#CB4B36`.
- Fonts: Fraunces for headlines, Newsreader for body.
- Keep one idea per slide. Big type, lots of paper showing.

---

## Slide 1 (Hook)

**There is a pile in your home you cannot see anymore.**

Not hidden. In plain sight. You walked past it this morning and your brain filed it under "normal, ignore."

[VISUAL] A hand holding a phone up to a cluttered drop zone. Clutter sharp on the screen, soft in the room behind. Warm dawn gradient. Title in Fraunces.

---

## Slide 2

**Your eye made peace with the mess months ago.**

Your brain habituates to anything that stays the same, so it can save attention for what is new. You stop seeing a mess about three days after it arrives.

[VISUAL] A faded room under a soft green wash, clutter dimmed into scenery. One calm green dot.

---

## Slide 3

**A guest sees it instantly. You have not really seen it in months.**

This is not a flaw. It is a useful feature with one bad side effect: the mess goes invisible to the one person most able to fix it. You.

[VISUAL] Split frame. Left, your view, faded green wash. Right, the guest's view, sharp with a few red spark marks.

---

## Slide 4

**The eye forgives. The camera does not.**

The lens has no history with your home. No fond tolerance built over a hundred busy mornings. It just records what is there, sharply, with every friction your eye learned to smooth away.

[VISUAL] The Eye vs the Camera split. "What your eye sees" (soft, green wash) beside "What the camera sees" (phone-framed, sharp, red sparks on the clutter). Dashed divider.

---

## Slide 5

**A before photo is not vanity. It is evidence.**

Flattened into a frame, your space reveals the cord you would trip on, the drawer that never closes, the chair three bags have colonized. The photo is a second audit.

[VISUAL] A camera-style frame with three red spark marks pinned to specific friction points, thin slate label lines.

---

## Slide 6

**The before is for the after.**

The before and after pair is the most motivating thing in this whole method. The power is not the tidy after. It lives entirely in the gap between the two images.

[VISUAL] Messy BEFORE (red tag) and calm AFTER (green tag) side by side, with a glowing gap labeled "the power is here." Sparks become calm-dots in the after.

---

## Slide 7

**The embarrassing before is the valuable one.**

A modest before makes a modest after. An honest, slightly mortifying before makes an after that genuinely moves you. Cringe a little. It is worth it.

[VISUAL] A cringe-worthy before thumbnail with a red spark and a small honey "keep this" tag. Newsreader italic caption.

---

## Slide 8

**Same spot. Whole area. Good light. Do not tidy first.**

You are not taking a photo. You are taking the first half of a matched pair. Mark where you stand, a tile or a scuff or a note in your phone, so the after clicks into place later.

[VISUAL] The Matched Pair diagram. Before and after thumbnails above a phone on a marked floor spot, terracotta floor mark, dashed sight lines, slate rule strip along the bottom.

---

## Slide 9

**Do not tidy first. The mess is the point.**

Every item you quietly straighten is a piece of the transformation you erase in advance. A staged before is a lie, and the only person it fools is you. Keep it private if you want. The photo serves you, not an audience.

[VISUAL] A hand reaching for a pile, stopped by a slate bracket. Red spark stays lit on the pile. Small lock icon to signal "yours to keep private."

---

## Slide 10 (Soft CTA)

**The starting line, in ink.**

The picture changes nothing in the room. But your starting point is now real: measured, pictured, and impossible to deny. Take the picture you would rather not take. It is the one that proves, later, that you did this.
