## Slide 1 · Title
**Photograph Before You Fix**
- Your eye made peace with the mess months ago. The camera never did.
- Record an honest starting point before you change a thing.
- About sixty seconds of work, and it makes the whole project feel worth it later.

[VISUAL] The opener art: a hand holding a phone up to a cluttered entryway drop zone. On the phone screen the clutter is sharp and framed; in the room beyond it is soft and faded. Warm dawn gradient behind it.

## Slide 2 · The Pile You Cannot See
- There is a pile in your home you cannot see anymore. Not hidden, just unreported.
- The papers, the cords, the chair that became a wardrobe, the drop zone by the door.
- You looked right at it this morning and your mind filed it under "normal, ignore."

[VISUAL] A faded, low-contrast room scene under a soft green wash. The clutter is present but dimmed, the way habit dims it. One calm green dot in the corner.

## Slide 3 · Habituation: Why Your Eye Lies to You
- Your brain habituates to anything that stays the same, to save attention for what is new.
- This is a feature, not a flaw. The cost is that the mess goes invisible to you and stays obvious to a guest.
- You stop seeing a mess about three days after it arrives.

[VISUAL] A simple timeline: Day 1 (mess sharp and noticed) fading to Day 3 (mess greyed into scenery). The fade runs from ink to soft green wash across three frames.

---

## Slide 4 · The Eye vs the Camera
- The eye is a generous editor. It trims familiar detail into the background.
- The camera has no history with your home and no fond tolerance. It just records what is there.
- The eye forgives. The camera does not.

[VISUAL] The hero split. Left side "What your eye sees" under a soft green wash, faded and forgiving. Right side "What the camera sees" framed like a phone photo, sharp, with red friction-spark marks on the clutter. Vertical dashed divider down the middle.

---

## Slide 5 · A Photo Is a Second Audit
- A flat frame strips away your forgiveness and hands the friction back to you, sharply.
- In a photo you notice the cord you would trip on, the drawer that never closes, the chair three bags have colonized.
- The audit number told you which S was weakest. The photo often shows exactly where it lives.

[VISUAL] A single camera-style frame of a drop zone with three red spark marks pointing at specific friction points (cord, drawer, overflowing chair), each with a thin slate label line.

---

## Slide 6 · The Before Is for the After
- The real reason to shoot it is not today. It is a month from now.
- The before and after pair is the most motivating artifact in this whole method.
- The power is not the after. It lives entirely in the gap between the two images.

[VISUAL] Two thumbnails side by side, a messy BEFORE (red tag) and a calm AFTER (green tag), with a glowing gap between them labeled "the power is here." Green calm-dots replace red sparks in the after.

---

## Slide 7 · The Embarrassing Before Is the Valuable One
- The urge to clean up first is the urge to dodge a moment of mild discomfort.
- That discomfort is the raw material of your future pride.
- A modest before makes a modest after. An honest, slightly mortifying before makes an after that moves you.

[VISUAL] A cringe-worthy before thumbnail marked with a red spark and a small honey-colored "keep this" tag. Caption in Newsreader italic: "Cringe a little. It is worth it."

---

## Slide 8 · The Matched Pair
- You are not taking a photo. You are taking the first half of a matched pair.
- The drama comes from both shots sharing one spot, one frame, one light, so the room is the only thing that changed.
- Same spot. Whole area. Good light. Do not tidy first.

[VISUAL] The Matched Pair diagram: a BEFORE thumbnail and an AFTER thumbnail above a phone resting on a marked floor spot, with a terracotta floor mark and dashed sight lines to both frames. Footer rule strip in slate: SAME SPOT · WHOLE AREA · GOOD LIGHT · DO NOT TIDY FIRST.

---

## Slide 9 · Mark Where You Stood
- Pick your spot deliberately, then remember it. This is the part everyone forgets and regrets.
- Notice what you are standing on: a tile, a scuff, the edge of a rug, or just drop a note in your phone.
- Frame the whole area for context, then take one or two detail shots of the worst friction.

[VISUAL] Close-up of a phone over a floor mark (terracotta X on a tile line) with a green calm-dot tag reading "you stand here, both times." Light, instructional, minimal.

---

## Slide 10 · Do Not Tidy First
- This is the one rule that matters more than all the technique, and the rule people break every time.
- Every item you quietly straighten is a piece of the transformation you erase in advance.
- A staged before is a lie, and the only person it fools is you.

[VISUAL] A hand twitching toward a pile, halted by a slate "stop" bracket. Red spark on the pile stays lit. Small mistake-style callout bar in friction-spark color.

---

## Slide 11 · Keep It, and Keep It Yours
- Make an album now and call it "6S Before." Drop each shot straight in, noted with the space and the date.
- The before is yours. You are under no obligation to show it to anyone.
- A private album used purely as your own evidence is completely valid. One trusted person is plenty if you want accountability.

[VISUAL] A phone showing a labeled album "6S Before" with a small lock icon in slate. Calm green wash, no sparks. Reassuring and quiet.

---

## Slide 12 · Take the Picture
- Go to the space you audited. Open your "6S Before" album.
- Choose your spot, frame the entire space, and resist the urge to fix even one thing.
- Take the wide shot. Take a detail shot or two. Make sure one is sharp. Save, date, put the phone down.

[VISUAL] The five-step checklist as a clean card: open the album, stand in one fixed spot, frame the whole area plus a detail, do not tidy first, save and note the space and date. Slate checkbox squares, honey accent on the header.

---

## Slide 13 · The Starting Line, in Ink (Close + CTA)
- Nothing in the room moved. But your starting point is now real, written as a number and captured as an image.
- That is what prepared finally means: measured, pictured, and impossible to deny.
- The after only means something next to an honest before. Take the picture you would rather not take.
