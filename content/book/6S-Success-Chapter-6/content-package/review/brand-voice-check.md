# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 6 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 6 adds two specific jobs: it makes an awkward act (photographing your mess) feel safe and worthwhile, and it respects the reader's privacy. The voice must stay encouraging and never preachy or pushy.

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," uses a recognizable scene (the drop zone you scored last chapter).
- **Reassuring, not pushy.** "The photo serves you, not an audience. You do not owe the world your drop zone."
- **Plain over technical.** Habituation becomes "your brain quietly stopped reporting it," not a psychology lecture.
- **Concrete and specific.** The chair that became a wardrobe, a scuff on the floor, a "6S Before" album.
- **Gently witty.** "The way it ignores the hum of the refrigerator and the feeling of your own socks."
- **Honest about scope.** No objects move, so the friction meter holds. The chapter records, it does not fix.

## Specific voice wins
- The privacy thread is preserved everywhere, which matters because the chapter exists partly to reassure camera-shy readers. No asset pressures anyone to post their mess.
- "The eye forgives. The camera does not." survives intact as the chapter's sharpest line, across the thread, a quote card, and the article.
- "The embarrassing before is the valuable one" carries the encouraging, anti-shame thread that runs from Chapter 1's "rooms are not messy people."

## Voice risks to watch
- **Contractions on X.** The book uses few; X uses several. Deliberate platform choice. Revise only if brand guidelines forbid contractions everywhere.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the sign-offs so they do not feel templated.
- **Keep the privacy note visible.** A few "post your before and after" angles could read as pressure. Spot-checked: the assets keep public sharing optional. Keep it that way.
- **Emoji on Facebook.** Used sparingly. Confirm it matches your brand's emoji policy.

## Banned-language audit
- No em dashes anywhere (verified after fixing two heading/label files, zero hits). No en dashes used as separators.
- No prohibited hype words as filler. "Transform" appears only as the chapter's own nouns from the source and as SVG code attributes in the publishable HTML.
- No "in today's fast-paced world" or similar.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all twelve packages, and the make-it-safe-and-private job of Chapter 6 is honored throughout. Approve the voice. The chapter itself still needs author sign-off, since it was authored rather than supplied.
