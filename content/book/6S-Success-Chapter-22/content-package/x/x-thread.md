2/
The hunt was real, but it does not hold. "The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks." New cords, new heights, dead batteries: they flow quietly back in. A home that has been made safe still drifts.

(233 chars)

3/
And some harm cannot be removed in advance at all. A fire can still start, a person can still fall. "Those are not failures of your search. They are simply the part of safety that removing cannot reach."

(206 chars)

---

4/
"You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet."

(271 chars)

---

5/
"Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming." Improving overall safety changes the boat, so less water gets in and you are ready for the water that does. You still bail now and then. It just matters less.

(259 chars)

6/
Raising the baseline has two halves. One looks forward and keeps the hazards that have not arrived yet from taking hold: prevention. The other readies the home to meet the harm no prevention can stop: preparation. Together they turn a safe home into one that stays safe.

(273 chars)

---

7/
"A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way."

(259 chars)

---

8/
Prevention is three small habits, not more willpower. Choose the safer default the moment a thing arrives. Give the dangerous things a home, so away and safe are the same place. And re-hunt lightly now and then, to catch drift while it is small.

(248 chars)

---

9/
However well you prevent, a fire can start in a wall you cannot see, and a person can fall on a night when nothing was wrong with the floor. "You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps."

(256 chars)

10/
The emotional core, frozen:

"Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes."

(256 chars)

---

11/
Preparation has a center: a working smoke alarm, and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel, on every floor and near the bedrooms. It is the one protection that has to work while you are asleep, so its whole value is in being tested and kept alive.

(263 chars)

---

12/
The rest is short and finite. A fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen. A way out of every room, and an exit plan the household has actually walked, from the bedrooms to one meeting spot outside. "Not a bunker. A prepared home."

(239 chars)

13/
"Finding hazards makes a home safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good." That is the whole chapter. Safety is four moves: see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it, and the home is safe.
