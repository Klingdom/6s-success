### Slide 1 (hook)

**Your home is hunted and its hazards are gone. But a home does not hold still, and some harm cannot be prevented, only prepared for.**

Last chapter you carried five questions through the whole home and removed the dangers hiding in it. That was real good, done. But two honest facts remain. "The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks." New cords arrive, a child grows two inches and reaches a shelf that was safe last month, batteries die.

And some harm no hunt could ever reach: a fire can still start, a person can still fall. "Those are not failures of your search. They are simply the part of safety that removing cannot reach." This chapter completes Safety with the two moves a hunt cannot make. Swipe.

### Slide 2

**You can chase hazards forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor.**

The frozen reframe the whole chapter rests on:

"You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet."

Removing hazards lowers the danger that is here. Improving overall safety raises the floor under everything, so the home is safer even against the hazards you have not met.

### Slide 3

**Bailing versus fixing the leak.**

Everything in this part so far has been subtraction: find a danger, take it out. The right place to start, and not the place to stop.

"Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming." Improving overall safety changes the boat itself, so less water gets in and so you are ready if it does. You still bail now and then; the hunt is not retired. But once the baseline is higher, the bailing gets rarer and matters less.

RIGHT, "fixing the leak," the boat's hull sealed and its life jackets stowed, a green protection-dot #6E8B5B, ready. Slate #3C5A6B label: "removing is bailing; overall safety changes the boat."

### Slide 4

**Raise the baseline: two forward moves a hunt cannot make.**

The frozen definition:

"Improving overall safety is the work beyond removing today's hazards: preventing new ones from taking hold, and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent. Removing hazards makes a home safe now. Prevention and preparation are what keep it safe."

Raising the baseline has two halves. One looks forward and keeps new hazards from taking hold: prevention. The other gets the home ready for the harm no prevention can stop: preparation. Together they turn a home that has been made safe into a home that stays safe.

### Slide 5

**Prevent: make the safe way the easy way.**

The frozen prevention reframe:

"A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way."

This is the opposite of willpower. "You make the safe way the easy way, so that staying safe is what naturally happens when everyone just does the convenient thing." Three small habits: choose the safer default when a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so away and safe are the same place, and re-hunt lightly now and then to catch drift while it is small.

### Slide 6

**Prepare: ready for what you cannot prevent.**

The frozen preparation reframe, the emotional core:

"Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes."

However well you prevent, some harm sits outside your reach: a fire in a wall you cannot see, carbon monoxide with no warning your body can catch, a fall on a night when nothing was wrong with the floor. "You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps." What you can do is prepare.

### Slide 7

**The alarm is the center of preparation.**

The Prepare hero line, frozen:

"Put the protections in place for the harm you cannot prevent: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms you keep alive, a fire extinguisher within reach, an exit everyone has walked, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows."

The alarm comes first, because it is the one protection that has to work while you are unconscious. A working smoke alarm, and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel, on every floor and near the bedrooms, is the highest-value thing in the chapter, and its whole value depends on being tested and its battery kept alive. The rest is short and finite. "Not a bunker. A prepared home."

### Slide 8

**Finding hazards makes a home safer once. Preventing and preparing keep it safe for good.**

The rule, frozen, and the whole reason Safety needs these last two moves:

"Finding hazards makes a home safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good."

Removing hazards makes a home safe now. Prevention keeps the next one from taking hold, and preparation stands ready for the harm no prevention can stop. That is the difference between a home made safe on one day and a home that stays safe.

### Slide 10

**Safety is four moves, and you now have them all.**

Step back and look at the fourth S whole. It was four moves all along, each the answer to a question the one before it could not ask. See it (Chapter 20), remove it (Chapter 21), prevent the next one, and prepare for the harm you cannot stop.

"That is not a checklist to run once; it is a way of holding your home that, once you have it, you do not really put down." And it is the opposite of vigilance: each move is work you do deliberately, once or now and then, precisely so you can stop watching.

Family Challenge: "Walk the exit plan together, calmly, once. From each bedroom, how do you get out, and where does everyone meet outside? Children who have walked it once remember it, and walking it turns a frightening idea into a known, ordinary thing."
