# Read Next Recommendations: Chapter 22, Improve Overall Safety

1. Chapter 23, Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today
   The next step, and where Part Seven and the fifth S begin. Chapter 22 completed Safety by preventing new hazards from taking hold and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent; but everything you have built so far, all four S's, lives largely in your head and your hands, and works because you keep it going. Standardize is how you write down the best way you have found, so the home holds steady, does not drift back, and no longer depends on you alone. Chapter 22 completes the fourth S; Chapter 23 opens the fifth.

2. Chapter 21, Find and Remove Hazards
   The setup, and the second-Safety-step position just behind the needle. Chapter 21 made Safety thorough with the hazard hunt, a calm, room-by-room walk carrying the five safety questions and removing each hazard the moment it was found. This chapter is the forward-looking half that a hunt cannot reach, because a home does not hold still and some harm cannot be prevented at all, only prepared for. Read how the hunt cleared today's dangers, then see how prevention and preparation keep the home safe for good.

3. Test every alarm in the home today (the chapter action)
   Test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. Working alarms are the highest-value protection in the house, and the only one that has to keep working while you are asleep. It costs a minute, and it is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea.

4. The Home Safety Baseline card (free resource)
   A printable and phone wallpaper carrying the two moves, prevent and prepare, with the short prevent list and the short prepare list, so the whole overall-safety idea is always in reach.

5. The Family Fire and Exit Plan (free resource)
   A printable, a simple home map to draw two ways out of each room, the outside meeting spot, and the few emergency numbers and steps everyone should know, so the plan lives in more than one head and a household can walk it together, calmly, once.
