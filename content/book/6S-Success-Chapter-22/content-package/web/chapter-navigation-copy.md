## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 22, the third and final chapter of Part Six, Safety, and it completes the fourth of the six S's, the "plus one" American practice added to 5S. Chapter 20 opened Safety and taught you to see hazards, by changing the question from is this in order to could this hurt someone. Chapter 21 made Safety thorough and taught you to remove them, room by room, fixing each danger the moment you found it. Between them, those two skills made your home meaningfully safer, and if you stopped there you would have done real good. But there is a reason this part has one more chapter, and it is a pair of honest facts. The first is that a home does not hold still: the hunt was a snapshot, and after the shutter clicks new cords, new heights, and dead batteries flow quietly back. The second is harder and gentler: some harm cannot be removed in advance at all, because a fire can still start and a person can still fall, and no amount of hunting will have prevented it. So this chapter completes the fourth S by adding the two forward-looking moves a hazard hunt cannot make: it keeps new dangers from taking hold, and it makes the home ready for the harm you cannot prevent. The answer is not a longer list of dangers removed but a higher floor. You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's baseline so it is harder to get hurt in at all, safer even against the hazards you have not met yet. Raising the baseline has two halves. Prevention keeps new hazards from taking hold by making the safe way the easy way, which is design and not willpower, in three small habits: choose the safer default when a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so away and safe are the same place, and re-hunt lightly now and then so drift is caught while it is small; it stays light, never a documented schedule, because that belongs to a later S. Preparation readies the home for the harm you cannot prevent, the harder and gentler half: some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for, and pretending you can remove it in advance is not safety, it is just anxiety with extra steps. Its center is the alarm, the one protection that has to keep working while you are asleep, a working smoke alarm and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel, on every floor and near the bedrooms, kept tested and alive; beyond it, a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a way out of every room and an exit plan the household has actually walked to one meeting spot outside, and the few plain numbers and steps everyone knows, including the children. Not a bunker, a prepared home. Seen whole, Safety turns out to have been four moves all along, each the answer to a question the one before it could not ask: see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it, not a checklist to run once but a way of holding your home you do not really put down, and the opposite of the exhausting vigilance the last chapter warned against, since each move is work you do deliberately, once or now and then, precisely so you can stop watching. Because completing Safety adds prevention and preparation rather than clearing everyday friction, the friction meter reaches a marked SAFETY COMPLETE milestone, the fourth on the dial after Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete; the step onto the mark is small and honest, because the big daily friction was cleared long ago and what these three safety chapters removed was the friction of the worst day, quiet on a dial that mostly measures ordinary days and one of the most valuable things in the whole book. Four of the six S's are now done. This chapter does not re-teach the safety lens or the triage (Chapter 20), does not re-teach the hazard hunt or re-list the five questions (Chapter 21), does not write documented standards or maintenance schedules (Standardize), and does not build long-term adherence or audits (Sustain). It is a real milestone, the fourth S finished: Chapter 21's second-Safety-step position is now the dashed ghost just behind the live needle, the Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, and Sort-complete milestones sit further back as passed markers, and the goal, out on the calm side, stays ahead, because the needle reaches it only at the very end, when everything is not just built but made to last, and Standardize and Sustain still follow.
