## SHORT 1: Raise the baseline
**Topic: the hero reframe, stop chasing hazards one at a time and raise the whole home's floor. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You hunted your home for hazards and removed them. That made it safe for exactly one day.

**Script:**
Here is the honest next thing. A hunt makes a home safe as of that day, and then the day ends and things flow back. A new appliance with a new cord. A child grows two inches and reaches a shelf that was safe last month. Batteries die. The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks. So instead of chasing hazards one at a time forever, you raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Think of bailing a boat versus fixing the leak. Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming. Improving overall safety changes the boat itself, so less water gets in and you are ready if it does. That is the whole of this chapter, and it has two halves: prevent, and prepare.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "You removed your home's hazards."
- 12s: "A home does not hold still."
- 28s: "Stop bailing. Fix the leak."
- 40s: "Raise the whole home's floor."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Bailing a boat with a bucket, then fixing the leak and changing the boat itself, then the whole-home floor visibly rising. Alongside, a hunted home at ease and ordinary life flowing back: a new cord arriving, a child reaching a once-safe shelf, a battery blinking dark. Warm daylight, calm.

## SHORT 2: Prevent
**Topic: prevention is design, not willpower, in three small habits. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You will not keep your home safe by resolving to be more careful. You proved that back when you tried to be neater.

**Script:**
A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way. That is the opposite of willpower. It is design. You steer the ordinary, convenient decision toward safe, once, so that staying safe is just what naturally happens. Three small habits. One: choose the safer default when something new arrives, the one moment it is easiest, the heater that switches itself off if it tips, the cleaner that lives behind a latch. Two: give the dangerous things a home, so away and safe are the same place, the medicines in one latched box, the button batteries up high. Three: re-hunt lightly, now and then, a slow walk with the five questions, so drift is caught while it is small. None of it is a schedule to write down. It is just the safe way, made easy.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Prevention is not willpower."
- 14s: "Make the safe way the easy way."
- 30s: "Safer default. A home for the dangerous things. A light re-hunt."
- 44s: "Keep it light. No schedule needed."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Three quiet ordinary moments: a new heater set down (the one that switches itself off if it tips), a replaced cleaner going straight into a latched cupboard; the medicines going back into one latched box, the button batteries up high; a calm, unhurried walk with the five questions catching a small drift. Warm, calm, no chore-energy.

## SHORT 3: Prepare
**Topic: readiness for the harm you cannot prevent, the emotional core. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise the alarm will wake you.

**Script:**
Here is the harder, gentler half, and it is not about hazards at all. However well you prevent, some harm sits outside your reach. A fire can start in a wall you cannot see. Carbon monoxide gives no warning your body can catch. A person can fall on a night when nothing was wrong with the floor. You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety, it is just anxiety with extra steps. What you can do is prepare. Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes. And it is a short list, not a state of dread. Working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms you keep alive, on every floor. A fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen. An exit everyone has walked, to one meeting spot outside. And the few numbers and steps everyone knows. Not a bunker. A prepared home.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Some harm you cannot prevent."
- 14s: "Only prepare for."
- 32s: "Alarms. An extinguisher. A walked exit."
- 44s: "Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The center first: one alarm on the ceiling and a single press of the test button, a small green light. Then the short list drawn calm: a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a soft dashed exit line walked from the bedrooms to a single tree outside, a household including children calmly knowing the few numbers and steps. Warm, tender, never alarmist.

## SHORT 4: The four moves of safety
**Topic: Safety as one whole arc, see, remove, prevent, prepare. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Safety was never one thing. It was four moves, and you now have them all.

**Script:**
Step back and look at the fourth S whole, because you have just finished it, and it turns out to have been four moves all along, each the answer to a question the one before it could not ask. You learned to see the hazard, by changing the question from is it tidy to could this hurt someone. You learned to remove it, room by room, fixing each danger the moment you found it. You just learned to prevent the next one, by making the safe way the easy way so the home stops drifting back. And you learned to prepare for the harm none of that can stop, so the home is ready on its worst day. See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it. That is not a checklist to run once. It is a way of holding your home that, once you have it, you do not really put down. And it is the opposite of vigilance: each move is work you do once or now and then, precisely so you can stop watching. A safe home is not one where nothing can ever go wrong. It is one that is ready when it does.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Safety is four moves."
- 12s: "See it. Remove it. Prevent it. Prepare for it."
- 30s: "Not a checklist to run once."
- 42s: "Ready when something goes wrong."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Four stepping stones left to right, labeled See, Remove, Prevent, Prepare. See and Remove drawn slightly faded, as done and behind you (Chapter 20 and 21); Prevent and Prepare in full color, as this chapter. A soft bracket under all four reads "the fourth S, complete". Warm, calm, slate labels, one warm accent on Prevent and Prepare.

## SHORT 5: The needle reaches Safety Complete
**Topic: the honest milestone, the fourth S finished. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The needle just reached a milestone that was a long time coming. And the step onto it was small.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, because it has reached a mark. The needle has moved to the fourth milestone on the dial, the one labeled Safety complete, beside Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete. Four of the six S's are done. Look at how far it has traveled from where it started, back in the clutter: the home is cleared, arranged, kept clean, and now safe, its hazards gone, new ones kept from taking hold, and its worst days prepared for. The step onto this mark was small, and that is honest, and it does not diminish the milestone. It was small because the big daily friction was cleared long ago, in Sort and Straighten and Shine. What these three safety chapters removed was a different kind of friction: not the friction you feel every day, but the friction of the worst day, the harm kept from happening. That is one of the most valuable things in the whole book. The mark is a true arrival. And the goal is still ahead, because two S's remain to make everything last. For now, the fourth S is complete, and your home is safe. Take the win.
