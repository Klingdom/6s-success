## SECTION 1: RAISE THE BASELINE

[HOST]
Let me start with the shift the whole chapter turns on.

[VISUAL: Bailing a boat with a bucket, then fixing the leak and changing the boat itself, then the whole-home floor visibly rising so it is harder to get hurt in at all.]

[HOST, voice over]
Everything so far in this part has been subtraction: find a danger, take it out. That is the right place to start, and not the place to stop, because subtraction alone leaves you doing the same work forever. So here is the reframe. You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet. Think of bailing a boat versus fixing the leak. Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming. Improving overall safety changes the boat itself, so less water gets in and you are ready if it does.

[HOST, on camera]
Here is the whole idea in one line. Improving overall safety is the work beyond removing today's hazards: preventing new ones from taking hold, and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent. Removing hazards makes a home safe now. Prevention and preparation are what keep it safe. Two halves. Let me take them one at a time.

## SECTION 2: PREVENT

[HOST]
Start with the leaks.

[VISUAL: A hunted home at ease, then ordinary life quietly flowing new things back in: a new cord arriving, a child reaching a once-safe shelf, a battery blinking dark. Then the three prevention habits as three quiet, ordinary moments.]

[HOST, voice over]
A hunted home drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life brings new things in. So here is the reframe. A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way. That last phrase is the whole of prevention, and it is the opposite of willpower. You will not keep your home safe by resolving to be more careful, any more than you kept it tidy by resolving to be neater. What works is design.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Make the safe way the easy way.]

[HOST, on camera]
In practice, three small habits. One: choose the safer default when something new arrives, the one moment it is easiest, the heater that switches itself off if it tips, the cleaner that lives behind a latch. Two: give the dangerous things a home, the Straighten move aimed at safety, so away and safe are the same place, the medicines in one latched box, the button batteries up high. Three: run the hunt again, lightly, now and then, a slow walk with the five questions, so drift is caught while it is small. And notice, none of this is a schedule to write down and enforce. That documented standard is a later S.

## SECTION 3: PREPARE

[HOST]
Now the harder, gentler half, and it is not about hazards at all.

[VISUAL: The center first, a single alarm on the ceiling and one press of the test button, a small green light. Then the short list drawn calm: a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a soft dashed exit line walked from the bedrooms to a single tree outside, a household including children calmly knowing the few numbers and steps.]

[HOST, voice over]
However well you prevent, some harm sits outside your reach. A fire can start in a wall you cannot see. Carbon monoxide gives no warning your body can catch. A person can fall on a night when nothing was wrong with the floor. You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps. What you can do is prepare. Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.]

[HOST, on camera]
And it is a short, concrete list, not a state of dread. The center is the alarm, because it is the one protection that has to work while you are unconscious. A working smoke alarm, and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel, on every floor and near the bedrooms, is the single highest-value thing in this chapter, and its whole value depends on being tested and its battery kept alive. Beyond the alarm, the list is calm and finite. A fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen. A way out of every room, and an exit plan the household has actually walked, to one meeting spot outside. And the few plain numbers and steps everyone should know, including the children. That is the whole of it. Not a bunker. A prepared home.

## SECTION 4: THE WHOLE OF SAFETY

[HOST]
Now step back and look at the fourth S whole, because you have just finished it.

[VISUAL: Four stepping stones left to right, labeled See, Remove, Prevent, Prepare. See and Remove drawn slightly faded, as done and behind you; Prevent and Prepare in full color, as this chapter. A soft bracket under all four reads "the fourth S, complete".]

[HOST, voice over]
It turns out to have been four moves all along, each the answer to a question the one before it could not ask. You learned to see the hazard, by changing the question from is it tidy to could this hurt someone. You learned to remove it, room by room, fixing each danger the moment you found it. You have now learned to prevent the next one, by making the safe way the easy way so the home stops drifting back. And you have learned to prepare for the harm that none of that can stop. See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it.]

[HOST, on camera]
That is not a checklist to run once; it is a way of holding your home that, once you have it, you do not really put down. And notice what it is not. It is not the exhausting vigilance the last chapter warned against. Each of the four moves is the opposite of vigilance: a piece of work you do deliberately, once or now and then, precisely so that you can stop watching. The goal has a stark name, zero accidents and injuries, but read it as a direction, not a destination you tick off. Safety consciousness is not fear held at a high pitch. It is a steady, low, permanent habit of care that runs quietly in the background of an ordinary, happy home.
