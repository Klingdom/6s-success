# Teleprompter Script: Improve Overall Safety

**Chapter 22, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** improve-overall-safety
**Length:** about 740 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the last chapter of Safety.

By now you can do two things.

You can see the hazards in your home, because you have the lens.

And you can remove them, room by room, because you have the hunt.

That has made your home meaningfully safer.

So why one more chapter?

Because of two honest facts about homes.

The first: a home does not hold still.

The hunt made it safe as of that day. Then the day ended.

A new appliance arrives with a new cord.

A child grows two inches and reaches a shelf that was safe last month.

Batteries die.

The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks.

The second fact is harder, and gentler.

Some harm cannot be removed in advance at all.

A fire can still start. A person can still fall.

Those are not failures of your search.

They are the part of safety that removing cannot reach.

So this chapter adds two moves a hunt cannot make.

Prevent new hazards from taking hold.

And prepare for the harm you cannot prevent.

Here is the reframe.

You can chase hazards one at a time forever.

Or you can raise the whole home's floor.

So it is harder to get hurt in at all.

Think of bailing a boat versus fixing the leak.

Removing hazards is bailing. Necessary, effective, and endless.

Because the water keeps coming.

Improving overall safety changes the boat itself.

So less water gets in, and you are ready if it does.

First half: prevent.

A hunted home drifts, because ordinary life brings new things in.

Prevention keeps the next hazard from quietly taking the last one's place.

And the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way.

That is the opposite of willpower. It is design.

Three small habits.

One. Choose the safer default when a thing arrives.

The heater that switches itself off if it tips.

The cleaner that lives behind a latch.

Two. Give the dangerous things a home.

So away and safe are the same place.

The medicines in one latched box. The button batteries up high.

Three. Re-hunt lightly, now and then.

A slow walk with the five questions, so drift is caught while it is small.

None of it is a schedule to write down. That is a later S.

Second half: prepare. The harder, gentler one.

However well you prevent, some harm sits outside your reach.

A fire in a wall you cannot see.

Carbon monoxide, with no warning your body can catch.

A fall on a night when nothing was wrong with the floor.

You cannot remove these in advance.

And pretending you can is not safety. It is anxiety with extra steps.

So you prepare.

You cannot promise a fire will never start.

You can promise the alarm will wake you, and the way out is clear.

Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.

And it is a short list, not a state of dread.

The center is the alarm.

The one protection that has to work while you are unconscious.

A working smoke alarm, and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel.

On every floor, and near the bedrooms.

And its whole value depends on being tested and kept alive.

Beyond the alarm, the list is calm and finite.

A fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen.

An exit everyone has walked, to one meeting spot outside.

And the few numbers and steps everyone knows.

Not a bunker. A prepared home.

Now step back and look at the fourth S whole.

It turns out to have been four moves all along.

See the hazard. Remove it. Prevent the next one. Prepare for the rest.

See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it.

Not a checklist to run once.

A way of holding your home you do not really put down.

And it is the opposite of vigilance.

Each move is work you do once or now and then.

Precisely so you can stop watching.

Now go look at the friction meter.

It has reached the fourth milestone. Safety complete.

Beside Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete.

Four of the six S's are done.

The step onto the mark was small. And that is honest.

The big daily friction was cleared long ago.

What these three safety chapters removed is the friction of the worst day.

The mark is a true arrival.

The goal is still ahead, because two S's remain to make everything last.

So here is your job.

Test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now.

One press each. Note any floor that has none.

That is the highest-value protection in the house.

For now, the fourth S is complete, and your home is safe.
