# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 22

**"Improve Overall Safety"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Six · Safety*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_22_final.html`: the
whole-home opener, its hazards gone and the marks of readiness added, an alarm glowing green on each
floor, an extinguisher on a low shelf, a small safety shelf, and a soft dashed exit path out to a
meeting spot; the home-safety-baseline two-column card, PREVENT beside PREPARE with green check-dots
and the frozen rule in terracotta at the foot; the four-moves progression, See, Remove, Prevent,
Prepare, the first two faded as done and the last two in full color as this chapter; and the friction
meter, which reaches its FOURTH marked milestone this chapter, SAFETY COMPLETE, a small step past
Chapter 21's second-Safety-step position, with the goal still ahead.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`
(protections and calm-dots), friction spark / hazard-mark red `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`.
Incidental fills used in the figures: green wash `#EDF1E4`, gauge track `#EDE3CE`, cream `#FCF8F0`,
quiet fill `#F3EEE2`, wood-warm `#EFE7D6`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the THIRD and FINAL chapter of Part Six, and it COMPLETES Safety, the
fourth S, at the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone. It does one turn: it takes the home whose hazards were
seen (Chapter 20, the lens) and removed (Chapter 21, the hunt) and makes Safety whole by adding the
two moves removing cannot make, PREVENT (keep new hazards from taking hold) and PREPARE (be ready for
the harm you cannot prevent). It teaches the baseline reframe (chase hazards one at a time forever, or
raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all), the bailing-against-fixing-the-leak
metaphor (removing is bailing, necessary and endless; improving overall safety changes the boat),
prevention as design not willpower (make the safe way the easy way, in three small habits: choose the
safer default, give the dangerous things a home, re-hunt lightly now and then), preparation as the
short concrete list (working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive, an extinguisher within reach,
a walked exit plan and a meeting spot, the few numbers and steps everyone knows), the rule that finding
makes a home safe once while preventing and preparing keep it safe for good, Safety seen whole as four
moves (see, remove, prevent, prepare), and the fourth milestone on the meter. It USES the cleared,
arranged, cleaned home from Sort, Straighten, and Shine and the hazards seen and removed in Chapters 20
and 21, and does NOT re-teach any of those, and does NOT re-open the safety lens or the hazard hunt.
CRITICAL fence: this is making Safety LAST and complete, PREVENT and PREPARE, NOT the seeing of hazards
(Chapter 20, done) and NOT the thorough removal of them (Chapter 21, done, shown here only as the home
already cleared), and NOT the documented standard-work of Standardize or the long-term adherence of
Sustain (later S's, named only as still to come). It opens no new hazard tool; prevention and
preparation are habits and protections, not a schedule to be written down and enforced, which is a
later S. Because new hazards are kept from taking hold and the worst days are prepared for, not because
anything more was cleaned or removed, the friction needle reaches its FOURTH marked milestone off the
Shine-complete mark, SAFETY COMPLETE, honoring the book's rule that the meter moves only when the home
really changes; the step onto the mark is small and honest, because the big daily friction was cleared
long ago and what these three safety chapters add is the quieter gain of the worst day averted, and the
goal is still ahead. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 22 · SAFETY`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
carbon-monoxide, safety-shelf, exit-plan, meeting-spot, smoke-alarm, safer-default, safe-way,
easy-way, re-hunt, whole-home, home-scaled, line-and-flat-color, calm-dot, second-half, worst-day,
Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, Sort-complete, Safety-complete, four-moves, first-aid,
fire-extinguisher. Anti-shame throughout: a home drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is
careless but because ordinary life brings new things in; the fix is design, not vigilance or blame.
Safety is love, not fear, so no diagram carries an alarm tone, an injury statistic, or graphic harm;
preparation is love for the day you hope never comes, never dread. No photoreal harm, no fire, no
gore, no distress; the register is calm, warm, and reassuring, a home that has exhaled. No force or
violence framing anywhere; no bunker, no siege. No hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product
endorsements; teach the habit, not a shopping list.

## 1. The Whole Home, Prepared (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole idea in one image. Chapters 20 and 21 took the hazards out; this
home is no longer defined by dangers removed but by what has been put into it, the quiet marks of
readiness. A house that has exhaled, cared for and prepared, the way a boat looks with its life jackets
stowed. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A warm cutaway of the whole home, several rooms and floors visible at once, the
kitchen and the living room and the stairs and a bathroom and a child's room and the bedrooms, all
drawn lovely and at ease, its hazards from last chapter simply gone. Onto that calm home are added the
marks of preparation, each with a soft green calm-dot: a small smoke or carbon-monoxide alarm glowing
softly on the ceiling of each floor; a fire extinguisher standing on a low shelf near the kitchen; a
tidy little safety shelf holding a flashlight, spare batteries, and a first-aid kit; and a soft dashed
line tracing a path from the bedrooms, down the stairs, out the door, to a single tree in the yard
where a small family figure waits. Nothing about the house looks fearful.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm daylight wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint
  Newsreader italic line across the top, muted: `the same home, one chapter on · its hazards gone, now ready`.
- A single ink floor line across the base, faint `#E2D8C4` room and floor dividers, the whole home read
  as a warm cutaway at ease, lived-in and cared-for rather than staged or braced.
- The marks of readiness, each with a soft green calm-dot and a muted Newsreader italic tag: an alarm
  on each floor in cream `#FCF8F0` with a gentle lit indicator, `an alarm · on every floor, kept alive`;
  a fire extinguisher on a low shelf near the kitchen in wood-warm `#EFE7D6`, `an extinguisher · within
  reach`; a small safety shelf with a flashlight, spare batteries, and a first-aid kit, `one shelf ·
  flashlight, batteries, first-aid`; a soft dashed exit path from the bedrooms out to a tree in the
  yard, `a way out · everyone has walked`, ending at `a meeting spot · one tree, one place`.
- A small sans caps line low across the scene, terracotta: `NOT A FEARFUL HOUSE · A PREPARED ONE`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "The same home, one chapter on. The hazards are gone, and now something
  has been added: the quiet marks of a home that is ready. An alarm on every floor, an extinguisher
  within reach, a way out that everyone has walked. Not a fearful house. A prepared one. This is the
  fourth S, complete."

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is the shift from dangers removed to protections added, so
draw the home genuinely at ease, its hazards gone, and let the new marks read as readiness, not alarm.
Hold the green discipline: the calm-dots are soft green `#6E8B5B` where the protections sit, and there
is no spark-red anywhere in this image, because there are no live hazards left. Draw no fire, no smoke,
no injury and no accident; the extinguisher and alarms sit quiet and unused, the dashed exit path is a
gentle line not a fire drill, and any family figure is calm and safe. The register is a home that has
exhaled, cared for and prepared, the way a boat looks with its life jackets stowed. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, the register of quiet readiness.

## 2. The Home Safety Baseline (the two-column card)

**Purpose:** Put the whole of overall safety on one page: the two forward moves and their short,
concrete lists, so the reader has a complete, doable baseline rather than a vague sense that they
should be safer. Prevention on the left, preparation on the right, and the rule at the foot. Mirrors
Figure 2 (the infographic) in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A clean card titled "The Home Safety Baseline", divided into two columns. The left
column, PREVENT, carries three habits; the right column, PREPARE, carries four protections; each item
sits beside a small green check-dot. At the foot, in terracotta, the frozen rule.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a Fraunces slate title `The Home Safety Baseline`, with a
  small sans caps subtitle, letter-spaced, soft gray: `TWO MOVES THAT KEEP A HUNTED HOME SAFE`.
- Left column, a slate heading `PREVENT · keep new hazards from taking hold`, three rows, each a small
  green check-dot and a short line with a plain sketched icon:
  - `Choose the safer default (at the moment a thing arrives)`.
  - `Give the dangerous things a home (away equals safe)`.
  - `Re-hunt lightly, now and then (catch drift while it is small)`.
- Right column, a slate heading `PREPARE · ready for what you cannot prevent`, four rows, each a small
  green check-dot and a short line with a plain sketched icon:
  - `Working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms on every floor (kept alive)`.
  - `A fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen`.
  - `A walked exit plan and one meeting spot outside`.
  - `The few emergency numbers and steps everyone knows`.
- At the foot, a terracotta strip carrying the frozen rule in Fraunces: `Finding hazards makes a home
  safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good.`
- Caption (from the chapter): "The whole baseline on one page. The left column keeps new hazards from
  taking hold; the right stands ready for the harm you cannot prevent. Neither is long, and neither is
  a project. Together they are what keeps a hunted home safe after the hunt is over."

**Note for the illustrator:** the card must read as complete and doable, not as a warning poster, so
keep the two lists short and finite and the icons ordinary and calm. Set the PREVENT and PREPARE item
lines as they read in the chapter; the two column intros and the foot rule are frozen wording and must
not be trimmed or reworded. Hold the color discipline: slate for the two headings and the labels, soft
green for the check-dots beside every item, one warm terracotta for the rule strip at the foot; no
spark-red anywhere, because this is a baseline of protections, not a page of live hazards. No injury
imagery, no scare, no bunker. Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate headings, the register of a calm,
usable baseline, the whole of overall safety on one card.

## 4. Bailing, or Fixing the Leak (the metaphor, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's central turn as a picture: removing hazards is bailing, necessary and
endless, while improving overall safety is the other kind of work, the kind that changes the boat so
less water gets in and so you are ready if it does. This diagram has no direct HTML figure twin; it
renders the "From Removing to Improving" section as a calm contrast, and carries the boundary line
between prevention and preparation without turning into a scare.

**What it shows:** Two calm facing panels of the same small boat on a calm sea, read left to right as
the two kinds of work. Panel one, BAILING: a hand lifts water out with a pail, the work honest and
real and never quite done, a muted line reading that this is necessary and effective and endless
because the water keeps coming. Panel two, RAISING THE BASELINE: the same boat changed, its seams
sealed so less water gets in, and a life jacket stowed neatly under the seat for the water that comes
anyway, the two halves of the chapter drawn as one calm move, prevent and prepare.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `FROM REMOVING TO IMPROVING`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath
  carrying the section's spine: "Overall safety is not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home
  that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet."
- Two warm cream `#FCF8F0` panels with ink outlines, left to right, each headed with a small slate tab.
  Panel `BAILING`: a hand and a pail lifting water, the boat unchanged, a muted Newsreader italic note,
  `necessary, effective, and endless · the water keeps coming`. Panel `RAISING THE BASELINE`: the same
  boat with its seams sealed and a life jacket stowed, a muted italic note, `less water gets in · and
  you are ready if it does`.
- Beneath, two small slate-bordered sub-strips, side by side, carrying the two halves in ink: `PREVENT
  · make the safe way the easy way, so hazards stop coming back` and `PREPARE · ready for the harm you
  cannot prevent`. Between them, a quiet italic line, the boundary: `Some harm you cannot prevent, only
  prepare for.`
- To the right, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `STANDARDIZE · SUSTAIN · NEXT` in muted
  honey sans caps, a muted italic note beneath, `Making all of it last, so the home holds without
  leaning on any one person`, with a fainter panel outline behind it for the second of the two S's.
  Hold the right group quiet and low-contrast, clearly ahead, not yet reached.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "You will still bail now and then. But once the home's baseline is
  higher, the bailing gets rarer and matters less."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the chapter's central turn, so keep it calm and plain, never a
scare; the point is not that bailing was wrong but that it never ends on its own, so you also change
the boat. Draw the bailing panel honest and even a little tiring, and make the raised-baseline panel
visibly eased, the boat sound and the life jacket stowed. Hold the color discipline: slate for the tabs
and the two sub-strips, one warm terracotta reserved for an optional rule footer, honey for the dim
Standardize-and-Sustain panel; no spark-red, and no real storm, no sinking, no drowning, the sea is
calm and the boat is fine. Keep the next-S panel dim, unlit, and clearly ahead; you may name it as
next, but teach nothing of it, no standard and no audit, only that the work turns to making all of it
last. The register is calm resolve, not alarm: a home made safe, now being kept that way.
