# Infographic Spec: Improve Overall Safety

**Chapter 22, "Improve Overall Safety"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Six · Safety**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: removing
hazards makes a home safe once, and two forward moves keep it safe for good, and the whole of overall
safety fits on one card as a short, doable baseline. The concept pairs the hero rule, **Finding hazards
makes a home safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good.**, with the
picture at its heart: **the home safety baseline**, two columns, PREVENT on the left with its three
habits and PREPARE on the right with its four protections. So the viewer sees the whole move at once,
the two halves of overall safety and the short concrete list under each, and understands that neither
is long and neither is a project.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_22_final.html`. Slate carries the two
column headings, the definition, the headings, and the labels; a soft green marks the check-dots beside
every baseline item; the single small warm red is held back for marks only and is barely used, if at
all, on this calm image, because a baseline is protections, not live hazards. "Tidy" is not the point,
and neither is "hunted"; a home can be cleared, arranged, spotless, and freshly hunted and still drift
back toward hazard, so the target here is a home that is also durably safe, its baseline raised.
Anti-shame throughout: a home drifts back not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life
brings new things in, and the fix is design, not vigilance or blame. Safety is love, not fear, so the
register is calm and reassuring, never an alarm; preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: finding makes a
  home safe once, preventing and preparing keep it safe for good. The eye should read the rule, then see
  the baseline card make it usable, the two short lists that keep a hunted home safe after the hunt is
  over.
- The home-safety-baseline card is the hero, so make it clear and complete: two columns, PREVENT with
  three habits and PREPARE with four protections, each item beside a green check-dot, calm and hand-made
  rather than clinical. Set the PREVENT and PREPARE intros and the foot rule byte-for-byte as they read
  in the chapter; they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: slate carries the two headings, the definition, and the labels; a soft
  green marks the check-dot beside every baseline item; the single small warm red is reserved for marks
  and is barely used, if at all, because this image is a baseline of protections, not a page of live
  hazards. This image is calm, so no fill-line red anywhere.
- This image makes the tool, the home safety baseline and its rule and its two halves, prevent and
  prepare. Do NOT re-teach Sort, Straighten, or Shine, and do NOT re-open the safety lens or the hazard
  hunt from Chapters 20 and 21; the home is already clean, kept, and cleared of its hazards, and this
  chapter makes its safety last. Do NOT build documented standard-work (Standardize, a later S) or solve
  long-term adherence or audits (Sustain, a later S); this is the raising of the baseline that completes
  the fourth S. And name no brand and draw no specific product; the alarms, extinguisher, flashlight,
  batteries, and first-aid kit are plain and generic, teaching the habit, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: a home drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life
  brings new things in, and the fix is design, not vigilance or blame. What you are building is not fear,
  it is a raised floor. Keep the register calm and reassuring, safety is love, not fear; preparation is
  love for the day you hope never comes; no alarm cues, no injury imagery, no fire or smoke, no scare
  framing, no force or bunker framing anywhere. A hunted home is a fine thing to have made; this image
  adds the baseline that keeps it safe.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the card and the icons
  drawn by hand rather than as a clinical chart, the check-dots and glyphs sketched rather than typeset,
  the whole image the calm, warm register of a good idea rather than a warning poster.
