# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 22

**"Improve Overall Safety"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Six · Safety*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to
hold across all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single warm
red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only and used barely, if at all, on these calm images.
Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the finished home the reader has cleared, arranged, and cleaned across
Chapters 12 to 19, then seen with the safety lens in Chapter 20 and hunted thoroughly in Chapter 21:
its hazards are gone. This chapter, the THIRD and FINAL of Part Six, COMPLETES Safety, the fourth S,
and does one turn: it makes Safety whole by adding the two moves removing cannot make, PREVENT (keep
new hazards from taking hold) and PREPARE (be ready for the harm you cannot prevent). It teaches the
baseline reframe (chase hazards one at a time forever, or raise the whole home's floor so it is harder
to get hurt in at all), the bailing-against-fixing-the-leak metaphor (removing is bailing, necessary
and endless; improving overall safety changes the boat so less water gets in and so you are ready if it
does), prevention as design rather than willpower (make the safe way the easy way, in three small
habits: choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, re-hunt lightly now and then),
preparation as a short concrete list (working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive, a fire
extinguisher within reach, a walked exit plan and a meeting spot, the few numbers and steps everyone
knows), the rule that finding makes a home safe once while preventing and preparing keep it safe for
good, Safety seen whole as four moves (see, remove, prevent, prepare), and the fourth milestone on the
meter. Nothing of Sort, Straighten, or Shine is re-taught here, and neither is the safety lens or the
hazard hunt from Chapters 20 and 21; the home is already clean, kept, and cleared of its hazards, and
this chapter makes its safety last. No documented standard or long-term audit is built here (Standardize
and Sustain, later S's, named only as still to come). Keep the tone calm, warm, and reassuring: a home
drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life brings new things in;
the fix is design, not vigilance or blame. Safety is love, not fear, so the register is relief and quiet
readiness, a home that has exhaled, never lecture, never guilt, never a scare tone; preparation is love
for the day you hope never comes, not dread. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated house
style: no photoreal harm, no fire, no smoke, no injury, no accident, no blood, no gore, no distress; the
alarms and the extinguisher sit quiet and unused, the exit path is a gentle dashed line and not a fire
drill, and any family figure in frame is calm and safe. No force or violence framing anywhere, no
bunker, no siege. No alarmist injury statistics. No hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product
endorsements; the alarms, extinguisher, flashlight, batteries, and first-aid kit are plain and generic,
teaching the habit, not a shopping list.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical
object in the scene, a hand-lettered card, a handwritten line on a real sheet, or the reading on a real
alarm indicator, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered
label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 22 · SAFETY`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot
between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: carbon-monoxide, safety-shelf, exit-plan,
meeting-spot, smoke-alarm, safer-default, safe-way, easy-way, re-hunt, whole-home, calm-dot, worst-day,
first-aid, fire-extinguisher, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, Sort-complete, Safety-complete,
four-moves.

## 1. The whole home, prepared (the opener)

A warm, wide editorial illustration of a finished, cared-for home drawn as a gentle cutaway with
several rooms and floors visible at once, the kitchen and the living room and the stairs and a bathroom
and a child's room and the bedrooms, all clean and arranged and lovely and at ease, in soft daylight,
its hazards from the last chapter simply gone. Onto this calm home are added the quiet marks of
readiness, each with a small soft green calm-dot: a small smoke or carbon-monoxide alarm glowing softly
on the ceiling of each floor; a fire extinguisher standing on a low shelf near the kitchen; a tidy
little safety shelf holding a flashlight, spare batteries, and a first-aid kit; and a soft dashed line
tracing a path from the bedrooms, down the stairs, out the door, to a single tree in the yard where a
small family figure waits. Nothing about the house looks fearful; it looks cared for and prepared, the
way a boat looks with its life jackets stowed. Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown
shadows, soft green for the calm-dots where the protections sit, no warm-red anywhere because no live
hazard remains. Editorial cookbook-style illustration. The same home, one chapter on, its hazards gone
and now ready.

- Mood: the home is no longer defined by the dangers taken out of it; it is defined by what has been put
  into it, the protections that prevent new harm and stand ready for the harm no one can prevent; an
  alarm on every floor, an extinguisher within reach, a way out that everyone has walked; not a fearful
  house but a prepared one; this is what a finished, safe home looks like, the fourth S complete.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating typeset text overlays, any fire, smoke,
  injury, accident, blood, gore, or a distressed or endangered person, a fire-drill or emergency tone,
  a bunker or fortified look, any warm-red accent, any live hazard, brand names or recognizable product
  labels; keep the home genuinely at ease and lovely, the marks of readiness quiet and green, the whole
  image a calm, prepared home that has exhaled rather than a warning.

## 2. The home safety baseline (the two-column card)

A warm editorial illustration of a calm, hand-made card titled the home safety baseline, drawn by hand
rather than as a clinical checklist, divided into two columns. The left column, headed prevent, keep new
hazards from taking hold, carries three small habits, each beside a soft green check-dot with a plain
sketched icon: choose the safer default at the moment a thing arrives; give the dangerous things a home,
so away and safe are the same place; re-hunt lightly, now and then, to catch drift while it is small.
The right column, headed prepare, ready for what you cannot prevent, carries four protections, each
beside a soft green check-dot with a plain sketched icon: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms on
every floor, kept alive; a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen; a walked exit plan and one
meeting spot outside; the few emergency numbers and steps everyone knows. At the foot of the card,
hand-lettered in terracotta, the rule: finding hazards makes a home safer once, preventing them and
preparing for them keep it safe for good. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, slate for
the two hand-lettered headings, soft green for every check-dot, one warm terracotta for the rule at the
foot, no warm-red anywhere. A picture of one plain, doable idea: the whole of overall safety on one
card.

- Mood: the whole baseline on one page, the two forward moves and their short, concrete lists; the left
  column keeps new hazards from taking hold and the right stands ready for the harm you cannot prevent;
  neither is long and neither is a project; together they are what keeps a hunted home safe after the
  hunt is over; complete, doable, unfrightening.
- Avoid: a busy clinical checklist, cold clinical light, floating typeset text overlays, an alarmist or
  statistical tone, any fire, smoke, or injury imagery, any warm-red, more than three prevent items or
  four prepare items, brand names or recognizable products; keep it a calm, hand-made card, the two
  lists short and the icons ordinary, the register a usable baseline rather than a warning poster.

## 3. The four moves of safety (the progression)

A warm editorial illustration of a calm, hand-drawn left-to-right progression of four steps, like
stepping stones across a quiet space, labeled see, remove, prevent, prepare, drawn by hand rather than
as a technical chart. Under each stone, one short hand-lettered line: see, the safety lens, could this
hurt someone, chapter twenty; remove, the hazard hunt, find it and fix it now, chapter twenty-one;
prevent, make the safe way the easy way, chapter twenty-two; prepare, ready for what you cannot prevent,
chapter twenty-two. The first two stones, see and remove, are drawn slightly faded and soft, as done and
behind you; the last two, prevent and prepare, are drawn in full warm color, as this chapter's work. A
soft hand-drawn bracket spans all four and reads, in small letters beneath, the fourth S complete. Warm
paper cream background, ink-brown line work, slate for the labels and the bracket, the first two stones
faded to soft gray, the last two in full color with one warm accent on prevent and prepare, optional
soft green calm-dots on the completed stones, no warm-red anywhere. A picture of one plain idea: Safety
is four moves, and with the last two the fourth S is whole.

- Mood: Safety as a single complete arc across the three chapters of Part Six; see and remove cleared
  the home of its dangers, prevent and prepare keep it safe and ready; the four moves are one whole, and
  with the last two the S is finished; each move is the answer to a question the one before it could not
  ask; calm, complete, quietly satisfying.
- Avoid: four disconnected icons with no throughline, a cold technical diagram, gridlines everywhere,
  floating typeset text, any fire, smoke, or injury imagery, any warm-red, drawing all four stones the
  same weight so the done and the new do not read apart, brand names or recognizable products; keep the
  four moves one continuous arc, the first two faded and the last two full color, the bracket closing
  the whole, the register a calm map of a finished S rather than a warning.

## 4. Bailing, or fixing the leak (the metaphor)

A calm, warm illustration of the chapter's central turn, drawn as two gentle facing panels of the same
small boat on a calm sea, read left to right as the two kinds of work, in soft daylight. The first
panel, bailing: a hand lifts water out of the boat with a pail, the work honest and real and never quite
done, the register that of necessary, effective, endless effort, because the water keeps coming. The
second panel, raising the baseline: the same boat changed, its seams sealed so less water gets in, and a
single life jacket stowed neatly under the seat for the water that comes anyway, the two halves of the
chapter drawn as one calm move, prevent and prepare. Between or beneath the two, a quiet hand-written
line carries the boundary: some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for, so you make the safe way the
easy way and you keep the life jacket stowed. To the right, a quieter, dimmer, unlit panel outline
stands for the next S's, where all of it is made to last, with a fainter outline behind it, both kept
low-contrast and clearly ahead. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, soft slate for the
tabs, the sea calm throughout, the right group dim and unlit, no warm-red and no storm. The image reads
as one plain idea: removing is bailing, necessary and endless, and improving overall safety changes the
boat so less water gets in and so you are ready if it does.

- Mood: removing hazards is bailing, necessary and effective and endless because the water keeps coming;
  improving overall safety is the other kind of work, the kind that changes the boat so less water gets
  in and so you are ready if it does; you will still bail now and then, but once the baseline is higher
  the bailing gets rarer and matters less; prevent is the sealed seam and prepare is the stowed life
  jacket; calm resolve, not alarm.
- Avoid: a real storm, a sinking or swamped boat, anyone drowning or in distress, any fire or injury,
  a guilt-heavy or scolding tone on the bailing panel, drawing the raised-baseline panel no calmer than
  the bailing panel, any warm-red, teaching any next-S content in the dim right panel, floating typeset
  text, hype; keep the sea calm and the boat fine throughout, the bailing panel honest and a little
  tiring and the raised-baseline panel visibly eased, the register calm resolve rather than alarm.

- Mood: as before the needle moves not because daily friction dropped but because the chance of harm

- Avoid: the live needle reaching or passing the goal crosshair, no room left between the needle and the
