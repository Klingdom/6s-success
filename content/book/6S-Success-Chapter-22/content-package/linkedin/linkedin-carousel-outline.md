# Chapter 22 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Improve Overall Safety

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, calm, quietly complete voice; preparation is love, not fear; a home that is ready, not a bunker; no dread, no graphic emergency detail, no alarmist numbers, no brand names.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You cleared the hazards. A home does not hold still.

**Body:** Finding and removing hazards makes a home safer today. But things flow back in, and some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. Swipe for the two moves that complete Safety.

---

## Slide 2: What removing cannot do

**Headline:** The hunt is a snapshot. The home keeps moving.

**Body:** After the shutter clicks, a new cord, a child grown two inches, dead batteries. And some harm cannot be removed in advance at all. Those are not failures of your search. They are the part of safety that removing cannot reach.

---

## Slide 3: Raise the baseline

**Headline:** Stop chasing hazards one at a time. Raise the floor.

**Body:** You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet.

---

## Slide 4: Bailing versus fixing the leak

**Headline:** Removing hazards is bailing. It never ends.

**Body:** Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming. Improving overall safety changes the boat itself, so less water gets in and so you are ready if it does.

---

## Slide 5: Two forward moves

**Headline:** Prevent, and prepare.

**Body:** Raising the baseline has two halves. One keeps new hazards from taking hold (prevent). The other gets the home ready for the harm no prevention can stop (prepare). Together they turn a home made safe into a home that stays safe.

---

## Slide 6: Prevent

**Headline:** Make the safe way the easy way.

**Body:** A home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way. Not willpower. Design.

---

## Slide 7: Prevention is three small habits

**Headline:** Choose the safer default. Give it a home. Re-hunt lightly.

**Body:** Make the safe way the easy way, so hazards stop coming back: choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, and re-hunt now and then to catch what has drifted in.

---

## Slide 8: Prepare

**Headline:** Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for.

**Body:** You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps. What you can do is prepare, and preparation is a short, concrete list, not a state of dread.

---

## Slide 9: The prepare list

**Headline:** Working alarms, an extinguisher, a walked exit.

**Body:** Working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms you keep alive, a fire extinguisher within reach, an exit everyone has walked, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows. Not a bunker. A prepared home.

---

## Slide 10: The emotional core

**Headline:** Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.

**Body:** You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. That is the center of the whole chapter.

---

## Slide 11: Safety is four moves

**Headline:** See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it.

**Body:** Safety is four moves, and you now have them all. A safe home is not one where nothing can ever go wrong. It is one that is ready when it does.

---
