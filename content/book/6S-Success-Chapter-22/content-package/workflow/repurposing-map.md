# Chapter 22 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- Finding and removing hazards makes a home safer today, but a home does not hold still, and some harm cannot be prevented at all, only prepared for; this chapter completes the fourth S by adding what removing cannot
- Two honest facts drive it: a home does not hold still (the hunt is a snapshot, and after the shutter clicks new cords, new heights, and dead batteries flow quietly back), and some harm cannot be removed in advance at all (a fire can still start, a person can still fall, and those are not failures of your search, they are the part of safety that removing cannot reach)
- The baseline reframe: you can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all; overall safety is a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet
- Bailing versus fixing the leak: removing hazards is bailing, necessary and endless, while improving overall safety changes the boat itself so less water gets in and so you are ready if it does; you still bail now and then, but it matters less once the baseline is higher
- Prevention, keep new hazards from taking hold by making the safe way the easy way (the opposite of willpower; you make the safe way the easy way so staying safe is what naturally happens when everyone just does the convenient thing), in three small habits: choose the safer default at the moment a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so away and safe are the same place, and re-hunt lightly now and then to catch drift, none of it a documented schedule (that is a later S)
- Preparation, ready for the harm you cannot prevent (the emotional core): working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive on every floor (the single highest-value protection, the one that has to work while you are unconscious), a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a walked exit plan to one meeting spot outside, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows; preparation is love for the day you hope never comes; not a bunker, a prepared home
- The rule, frozen: finding hazards makes a home safer once; preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good
- Safety is four moves: see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it, each the answer to a question the one before could not ask, and each the opposite of the exhausting vigilance the last chapter warned against, work you do deliberately so you can stop watching
- Zero accidents and injuries is a direction, not a destination; safety consciousness is not fear held at a high pitch but a steady, low, permanent habit of care that runs quietly in the background of an ordinary, happy home
- The friction meter reaches the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone, the fourth mark on the dial, beside Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete; the step onto it was small and that is honest, but the mark is a true arrival, and the goal stays ahead because two S's remain to make everything last

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the third and final chapter of Part Six and it COMPLETES Safety, the fourth S, at the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone. It USES the finished home the first three S's built, the safety lens Chapter 20 opened, and the hazard hunt Chapter 21 ran, but it does not re-open any of them. Do not re-teach cleaning or inspection (Chapters 16 to 18) or the reset (Chapter 19); prevention and preparation are a new, forward-looking layer over the same home.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 20, Protect People First): the safety lens ("could this hurt someone?"), the protect-people-first triage, Heinrich's pyramid, the 5S+1 history, and safety-is-love-not-fear are already done. Chapter 22 USES the lens; a brief one-line callback ("you learned to see") is fine, a re-teach or re-derivation is not.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 21, Find and Remove Hazards): the hazard hunt, the five safety questions, and find-it-and-fix-it-now are already done. Chapter 22 USES the hunt (the re-hunt habit and "give the dangerous things a home" call back to it) but does not re-teach the hunt or re-list the five questions as a lesson; removing today's hazards is last chapter's job, and this chapter is what keeps the next one from taking hold and readies the home for what none of it can stop.
- CRITICAL fence (Standardize and Sustain, later S's): keep prevention and preparation LIGHT and habit-shaped. Do NOT write documented standards, written schedules, or checklists to be enforced (Standardize), or long-term adherence and review systems (Sustain). The manuscript is explicit: "that kind of documented standard is a later S." Chapter 22 only completes the fourth S; the next chapter opens the fifth.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life brings new things in; the four moves are the opposite of vigilance, not a verdict on the reader. Keep it calm, loving attention and relief, not lecture.
- Safety is love, not fear: no alarmist statistics, no graphic injury, no fear-mongering, and no anxiety framing of preparation (pretending you can remove all harm in advance is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps). Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes; a prepared home is not a fearful one, the way a boat looks with its life jackets stowed. No hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "a smoke alarm", "a fire extinguisher", or "a cabinet latch", not a brand); teach the baseline, the three habits, and the preparation list, not a shopping list.
- The source's Principles of the Safety Step (establish a goal of zero accidents and injuries, develop safety consciousness, integrate safety into all the S's) and the Tools for Improving Safety are drawn from the 6S source, home-scaled into prevention and preparation and the four moves, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed. "Zero accidents and injuries" is read as a direction, not a destination you will ever tick off.
- The friction-meter movement is specific to Ch 22: it reaches the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone, the FOURTH marked tick on the dial, beside Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete, so FOUR of the six S's are done. Be honest that the step onto the mark was small (the big daily friction was cleared long ago in Sort, Straighten, and Shine, and what the three safety chapters added was the removal of the friction of the worst day, quiet on a dial that mostly measures ordinary days) but that the mark is a true ARRIVAL, the fourth S finished, NOT the goal. Chapter 21's second-Safety-step position is now the dashed ghost just behind, the earlier milestones sit further back as passed markers, and the goal is still ahead on the green calm side, because Standardize and Sustain still follow to make everything last.
- Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical; point syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium) back to it.
- Keep the framing plain: "raise the baseline", "make the safe way the easy way", "prevent and prepare", "the four moves", "see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it". Finding hazards makes a home safer once; preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good.
