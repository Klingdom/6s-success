## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
Finding and removing hazards makes a home safer today, but a home does not hold still, and some harm cannot be prevented at all, only prepared for. Chapter 20 taught the reader to see hazards and Chapter 21 taught the reader to remove them thoroughly, room by room; this chapter completes the fourth S by adding what removing cannot. It opens on an honest pair of facts: a home does not hold still (the hunt was a snapshot, and after the shutter clicks new cords, new heights, and dead batteries quietly flow back), and some harm cannot be removed in advance at all (a fire can still start, a person can still fall, and no amount of hunting will have prevented it). So the chapter raises the whole home's baseline rather than chasing hazards one at a time, the baseline reframe, and think of the difference between bailing a boat and fixing the leak: removing hazards is bailing, necessary and endless, while improving overall safety changes the boat itself. Raising the baseline has two halves. Prevention keeps new hazards from taking hold by making the safe way the easy way, in three small habits: choose the safer default at the moment a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so away and safe are the same place, and re-hunt lightly now and then to catch drift, none of it a documented schedule (that is a later S). Preparation gets the home ready for the harm you cannot prevent, and it is the emotional core: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive on every floor (the single highest-value protection, the one that has to work while you are unconscious), a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a walked exit plan to one meeting spot outside, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes. Stepped back, Safety turns out to have been four moves all along, see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it, each the answer to a question the one before could not ask, and each the opposite of the exhausting vigilance the last chapter warned against: work you do deliberately so you can stop watching. Zero accidents and injuries is a direction, not a destination, and safety consciousness is a steady, low, permanent habit of care. The friction meter reaches the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone, the fourth mark on the dial beside Sort complete, Straighten complete, and Shine complete; the step onto it was small and that is honest (the big daily friction was cleared long ago), but the mark is a true arrival, and the goal stays ahead because two S's remain to make everything last. Hands off to Chapter 23 · Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today, which opens Part Seven and the fifth S.

- The definition box (frozen): **Improving overall safety is the work beyond removing today's hazards: preventing new ones from taking hold, and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent. Removing hazards makes a home safe now. Prevention and preparation are what keep it safe.**
- Prevent (frozen): **Make the safe way the easy way, so hazards stop coming back: choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, and re-hunt now and then to catch what has drifted in.**
- Prepare (frozen): **Put the protections in place for the harm you cannot prevent: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms you keep alive, a fire extinguisher within reach, an exit everyone has walked, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows.**
- The baseline reframe (frozen): **You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet.**
- The prevention reframe (frozen): **A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way.**
- The preparation reframe (frozen, the emotional core): **Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.**
- The four moves (frozen): **See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it.**
- One Idea to Keep: **Safety is four moves, and you now have them all: see the hazard, remove it, prevent the next one, and prepare for the harm you cannot stop. A safe home is not one where nothing can ever go wrong. It is one that is ready when it does.**
