# Facebook Longform Post: A Hunt Finds Today's Hazards. Two More Moves Keep a Home Safe.

Conversational, like talking to a friend. Preparation is framed as calm love, never alarm. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes. Body hard-capped 300 to 450 words.

Last spring I finished a proper hazard hunt of my house. Room by room, five questions in each, every danger I found fixed on the spot. It felt done, and the relief was real.

Then, months later, I noticed the house had quietly moved on without me. A new extension cord snaking under a rug. A heavy thing on a high shelf that had not been there before. And a smoke alarm I had put up with great pride and never once tested since. That was the sentence that got me.

"You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet."

A hunt is a snapshot. The home keeps moving after the shutter clicks. So instead of hunting the same dangers over and over, I worked on two things a hunt cannot do.

The first is prevention.

"A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way."

Three small habits, no willpower required. Choose the safer option at the moment a new thing arrives, when it is easiest. Give the dangerous things a home, so away and safe are the same place. And re-walk the house lightly now and then, to catch drift while it is small.

The second half is the one I did not expect to move me.

"Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes."

So I did the short, finite list. Tested every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm, one press each, and put one on the floor that had none. Set a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen. Walked the way out of each bedroom with everyone, to one tree in the yard where we all meet. Wrote the few numbers and steps somewhere the whole house knows.

None of it made the place tidier. It made it ready. Four of the six S's are done, and my home is safe.
