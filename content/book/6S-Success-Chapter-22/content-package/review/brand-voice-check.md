# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 22 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 22 is the THIRD and FINAL chapter of Part Six and COMPLETES Safety, the fourth of the six S's. Its job is to finish the fourth S by adding the two forward moves that removing cannot make: prevent new hazards from taking hold so the home does not quietly undo the hunt, and prepare for the harm no prevention can stop so the home is ready on its worst day. Chapter 20 taught the reader to SEE hazards (the safety lens), Chapter 21 to REMOVE them (the room-by-room hunt), and this chapter raises the whole home's baseline: prevention and preparation. Because the subject includes fire, falls, carbon monoxide, and children, the register must stay calm love, not alarm: not "your home is full of danger you missed," but "raise the floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all, and stand ready for the day something goes wrong anyway." The voice must stay anti-shame (a hunted home drifts back toward hazard "not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life brings new things in"), frame preparation as love rather than fear ("Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes"), and keep the goal honestly ahead (the fourth S completes here at a milestone, but the needle reaches the goal only when Standardize and Sustain make everything last).

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored manuscript and final HTML)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," stands the reader in the home built across the book, and pictures the ordinary marks of readiness: a smoke or carbon-monoxide alarm glowing softly on each floor, an extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a safety shelf with a flashlight and spare batteries and a first-aid kit, a walked exit path from the bedrooms to one tree in the yard where a small family figure waits.
- **Concrete over abstract.** The idea is taught through the bailing-versus-fixing-the-leak image, the three small prevention habits (choose the safer default the moment a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so "away" and "safe" are the same place, re-hunt lightly now and then), and the short, finite preparation list (a tested alarm on every floor, an extinguisher by the kitchen, a walked exit plan to one meeting spot, the few numbers everyone knows).
- **Anti-shame.** "A hunted home drifts back toward hazard not because anyone is careless but because ordinary life brings new things in." The four moves are named as "the opposite of vigilance," a deliberate piece of work done once or now and then "precisely so that you can stop watching," not a verdict on the reader.
- **Calm love, not fear (the register the chapter turns on).** "You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps." A prepared home "looks cared for and prepared, the way a boat looks with its life jackets stowed." Danger is named plainly ("Not a bunker. A prepared home") and never dramatized.
- **Honest about the milestone and the meter.** The needle reaches the fourth mark, Safety complete, and the manuscript says the step onto it "was small, and that is honest, and it does not diminish the milestone," because the big daily friction was cleared long ago and what these three safety chapters added was "the removal not of everyday friction but of the friction of the worst day." The goal is kept openly ahead because "two S's remain to do that."

## Voice risks to watch
- **Hero vocabulary is dense.** "safe," "safety," "hazard," "prevent," "prepare," "home," "alarm" recur because they are the subject; expected, not a tic, but vary the surrounding language when excerpting, and watch "prevent," "prepare," "baseline," and "ready" as the payoff words most likely to stack.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the LinkedIn and X sign-offs; several close on "Read the free chapter" / "Read it free online" / "Read the free chapter in the online book."
- **Keep the register calm love, not alarm.** The chapter names real fears (fire, falls, carbon monoxide, children), so any asset must not tip from "raise the floor and stand ready" into fear or guilt. The frozen framing keeps it anti-shame (drift is ordinary life, not carelessness) and frames preparation as love, not dread ("anxiety with extra steps" is explicitly named as the thing preparation is NOT).
- **"Prevent" and "remove / eliminate" are topic words, not force words.** They are used calmly throughout (prevention is "three small habits, not more willpower"; the four moves are "the opposite of vigilance"); watch that excerpts never let them tip into a war-on-hazards metaphor.

## Banned-language audit (scans actually run, across the content-package files)
- **Widened dash scan** (em dash, en dash, and spaced-hyphen " - " used as punctuation, excluding markdown list bullets): em **0**, en **0**, spaced-hyphen-as-punctuation **0**. - **Force / violence scan:** a scan for "attack" / "blitz" / "blast" / "purge" / "war on" / "waged war" returned **0 files** across the entire content-package, reader-facing and production files alike. Prevention and preparation are framed as calm, deliberate care ("Not a bunker. A prepared home"), never as a war on hazards. Recorded for full honesty.
- **Alarmist-safety check:** no alarmist injury statistics, no graphic injury, no graphic emergency detail. Dangers (fire, falls, carbon monoxide, choke) are named plainly and met with readiness, never dramatized. Preparation is explicitly framed as love, not dread; the "zero accidents and injuries" goal from the source is read "as a direction rather than a destination," not as a scare.
