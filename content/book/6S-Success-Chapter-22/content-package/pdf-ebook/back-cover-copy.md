# Back Cover Copy: Improve Overall Safety

## Back-cover text

Last chapter you carried the five questions through the whole home, one room at a time, and removed each hazard the moment you found it: the button battery, the blind cord, the unanchored bookcase, the heater by the curtain. You made your home genuinely safer, and if you stopped there you would have done real good. Here is the honest reason this part has one more chapter, said not to take that away but to finish it: a home does not hold still, and some harm cannot be removed in advance at all. "The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks." A new cord arrives, a child grows two inches, batteries die. And you can clear every hazard you can find and a fire can still start, a person can still fall. "Those are not failures of your search. They are simply the part of safety that removing cannot reach."

Chapter 22 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the third and final chapter of Part Six, and it completes Safety, the fourth of the six S's, at the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone. Chapter 20 taught you to see hazards and Chapter 21 taught you to remove them; this chapter adds the two forward-looking moves a hazard hunt cannot make. "You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet." Think of the difference between bailing a boat and fixing the leak. "Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming." Raising the baseline has two halves. Prevention keeps new hazards from taking hold by making the safe way the easy way, in three small habits: choose the safer default when a thing arrives, give the dangerous things a home so away and safe are the same place, and re-hunt lightly now and then to catch what has drifted in. "You make the safe way the easy way, so that staying safe is what naturally happens when everyone just does the convenient thing."

Then comes the harder, gentler half, and the emotional core of the chapter. "Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes." Preparation is a short, concrete list, not a state of dread: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive on every floor, a fire extinguisher within reach, an exit everyone has walked to one meeting spot outside, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows, including the children. "Not a bunker. A prepared home." That is the whole rule of the chapter: "Finding hazards makes a home safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good." Then the friction meter reaches the fourth milestone on the dial, Safety complete, beside Sort, Straighten, and Shine. Four of the six S's are done, and the step onto the mark was small and honest, because what these three safety chapters removed was not everyday friction but the friction of the worst day. The goal is still ahead, because two S's remain, Standardize and Sustain, the work of making all of it last. Today, test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. "For now, the fourth S is complete, and your home is safe. Take the win." Keep the whole idea in reach with **The Home Safety Baseline card**, and ready your household with **The Family Fire and Exit Plan**. Next, Chapter 23 · Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today.
