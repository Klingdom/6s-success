## How to read this chapter

This is the third and final chapter of Part Six, Safety, and it completes the fourth S. It picks up from a home you can already see the hazards in (Chapter 20, the safety lens) and have already hunted thoroughly (Chapter 21, the hazard hunt), and it uses both of those without re-teaching them. It is not another hunt and it opens no new way to tidy. It is about the two moves a hunt cannot make: keeping new dangers from taking hold, and being ready for the harm you cannot prevent. Read it through once for the idea, then do the two small, concrete things it asks: raise the home's baseline going forward, and put the few plain protections in place.

The chapter rests on a pair of honest facts. A home does not hold still, so the hunt you ran was a snapshot and things quietly flow back in after it. And some harm cannot be removed in advance at all, only prepared for. So instead of chasing hazards one at a time forever, you raise the whole home's floor. The first half is prevention, and its whole content is making the safe way the easy way, which is the opposite of willpower: not resolving to be more careful, but design. In practice that is three small habits: choose the safer default at the one moment it is easiest, which is when a thing arrives; give the dangerous things a home, the Straighten move aimed at safety, so away and safe are the same place; and run the hunt again, lightly, now and then, so drift is caught while it is small. Keep prevention light. It is habits and defaults, not a documented schedule or standard, and it is not the general adherence-and-audit system either; those are later S's. The second half is preparation, the emotional core, and it is a short and finite list, not a state of dread. The center is the alarm, the one protection that has to work while you are unconscious: a working smoke alarm, and a carbon-monoxide alarm where anything burns fuel, on every floor and near the bedrooms, tested and kept alive. Beyond it: a fire extinguisher within reach of the kitchen, a way out of every room and an exit plan the household has actually walked to one meeting spot outside, and the few plain numbers and steps everyone should know, including the children. The register throughout is calm, warm, and quietly complete. Preparation is love, not fear. This is not a scare campaign, and there is no dread and no graphic emergency detail in it.

A word on the scope fence, so the chapter stays the thing that completes Safety rather than drifting into the S's that follow. It uses Chapter 20's lens and Chapter 21's hunt and does not re-teach either. Its prevention is light, habits and safer defaults, not documented maintenance schedules or written standards, which are Standardize, Chapter 23 to 25. It does not build the general adherence-and-audit system that keeps everyone doing all of it over years, which is Sustain, Chapter 26 to 30. And it does not re-open Sort, Straighten, or Shine; the earlier S's already cleared, arranged, and cleaned the home, and this chapter works on the home they left. Because completing Safety adds prevention and preparation rather than clearing everyday friction, the friction meter reaches its fourth milestone in a small but honest step. Small, because the big daily friction was cleared long ago in Sort, Straighten, and Shine, and what these three safety chapters removed was the friction not of the everyday but of the worst day, quiet on a dial that mostly measures ordinary days and one of the most valuable things in the whole book. The mark is a true arrival, the fourth S finished, and the goal, out on the calm side, is still ahead, because two S's remain, Standardize and Sustain, the work of making all of it last.

If you only do one thing: test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. Working alarms are the highest-value protection in the house, and the only one that has to keep working while you are asleep. That one minute is the smallest possible proof of the whole chapter, and it is what turns a home you have made safe into a home that is ready. For now, the fourth S is complete, and your home is safe. Take the win.
