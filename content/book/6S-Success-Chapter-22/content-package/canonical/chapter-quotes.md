## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "The hunt is a snapshot, and the home keeps moving after the shutter clicks."

2. "Those are not failures of your search. They are simply the part of safety that removing cannot reach."

3. "See it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it."

4. "Removing hazards is bailing: necessary, effective, and endless, because the water keeps coming."

5. "You make the safe way the easy way, so that staying safe is what naturally happens when everyone just does the convenient thing."

6. "You cannot remove these in advance, and pretending you can is not safety; it is just anxiety with extra steps."

7. "Not a bunker. A prepared home."

8. "That is not a checklist to run once; it is a way of holding your home that, once you have it, you do not really put down."

9. "What is left is not more building; it is holding."

10. "For now, the fourth S is complete, and your home is safe. Take the win."

## The rule (frozen, hero)

"Finding hazards makes a home safer once. Preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good."

## The baseline reframe (frozen, pull-quote)

"You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet."

## The prevention reframe (frozen, pull-quote)

"A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way."

## The preparation reframe (frozen, pull-quote, the emotional core)

"Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes."

## One Idea to Keep (frozen close)

"Safety is four moves, and you now have them all: see the hazard, remove it, prevent the next one, and prepare for the harm you cannot stop. A safe home is not one where nothing can ever go wrong. It is one that is ready when it does."

## Quick Win (canonical)

"Test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. Working alarms are the highest-value protection in the house, and the only one that has to keep working while you are asleep."

## Family Challenge (canonical)

"Walk the exit plan together, calmly, once. From each bedroom, how do you get out, and where does everyone meet outside? Children who have walked it once remember it, and walking it turns a frightening idea into a known, ordinary thing."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"Keep the tools of preparation in one known place, a single shelf or box: the spare alarm batteries, the fire extinguisher, a flashlight, the first-aid kit. Preparation you cannot find in the dark is not preparation."

## Common Mistake (canonical)

"Fitting a smoke alarm, feeling safe, and never testing it again, so the one protection you were counting on is silent the night it matters. A protection you do not keep alive is only decoration. Give the button a press whenever the clocks change."

## The friction-meter caption (for graphics)

"The needle reaches the Safety-complete mark, the fourth milestone on the dial, after Sort, Straighten, and Shine. Four of the six S's are done. The home is cleared, arranged, kept clean, and now safe: today's hazards gone, new ones kept from taking hold, and the worst days prepared for. The goal is still ahead, because two S's remain, Standardize and Sustain, the work of making all of it last."

## Frozen canonical design-block strings (from CH22_CANONICAL_STRINGS_AND_BRIEF.md)

These three are the frozen source-of-truth wording for the chapter's definition box and the Prevent and Prepare hero lines. They are canonical strings from the brief (not running manuscript prose), reproduced here verbatim so every downstream package copies them exactly.

### The definition box (frozen)

"Improving overall safety is the work beyond removing today's hazards: preventing new ones from taking hold, and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent. Removing hazards makes a home safe now. Prevention and preparation are what keep it safe."

### Prevent (frozen)

"Make the safe way the easy way, so hazards stop coming back: choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, and re-hunt now and then to catch what has drifted in."

### Prepare (frozen)

"Put the protections in place for the harm you cannot prevent: working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms you keep alive, a fire extinguisher within reach, an exit everyone has walked, and the few numbers and steps everyone knows."
