## Search intent
Keeping a home safe for good, after the hazards have been found and removed. Readers arriving here already have a home that is cleared, arranged, kept clean, and hunted room by room for hazards (Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Chapters 20 and 21). They sense, correctly, that a home does not hold still and that removing today's dangers does not settle tomorrow's, and that some harm cannot be prevented at all. They want the forward-looking half of safety: how to keep new hazards from taking hold, and how to be ready for the emergencies no one can prevent, without turning the home into a place of dread. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: raise the whole home's baseline with two forward moves, prevention (make the safe way the easy way: choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, re-hunt lightly now and then) and preparation (working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms kept alive, a fire extinguisher within reach, a walked exit plan and meeting spot, the few numbers and steps everyone knows); and finding hazards makes a home safer once, preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good. It brings the whole of Safety together as four moves (see, remove, prevent, prepare) and completes the fourth S at a SAFETY COMPLETE milestone, with the goal still ahead because Standardize and Sustain remain.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Separates prevention from preparation, naming that keeping new hazards from taking hold and being ready for the harm you cannot prevent are two different moves, rather than blurring them into a vague sense that one should "be safer"
- Frames prevention as design, not willpower: you make the safe way the easy way (choose the safer default, give the dangerous things a home, re-hunt lightly), rather than resolving to be more careful
- Names the honest limit of removal: a home does not hold still and some harm cannot be prevented at all, so removing hazards alone is not enough, correcting the assumption that a single hunt settles safety for good
- Frames preparation as calm love, not fear: the alarm that wakes you and the exit everyone has walked reduce dread by being ready, rather than stoking it with alarmist statistics or graphic detail
- Gives a concrete, finite preparation list (working alarms on every floor kept alive, an extinguisher within reach, a walked exit plan and one meeting spot, the few numbers and steps everyone knows) instead of an open-ended sense of never being ready enough
- Names the alarm as the highest-value protection and insists it be kept alive, correcting the common mistake of fitting an alarm once and never testing it again
- Keeps prevention light (safer defaults and simple habits, testing alarms whenever the clocks change), not a documented maintenance schedule, holding the fence to Standardize
- Keeps the register calm and anti-shame, with no fear-mongering, no graphic emergency detail, no alarmist numbers, no force or violence framing, and no brand names
- Completes Safety honestly at a milestone: four of the six S's are done, the step onto the mark is small because the big daily friction was cleared long ago, and the goal is still ahead because Standardize and Sustain remain to make everything last
