## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. Working alarms are the highest-value protection in the house, and the only one that has to keep working while you are asleep.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the exit plan together, calmly, once. From each bedroom, how do you get out, and where does everyone meet outside? Children who have walked it once remember it, and walking it turns a frightening idea into a known, ordinary thing.
- **6S Tip:** Keep the tools of preparation in one known place, a single shelf or box: the spare alarm batteries, the fire extinguisher, a flashlight, the first-aid kit. Preparation you cannot find in the dark is not preparation.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: press the test button on every alarm today, and note any floor that has none. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of calm love and relief. A home that has drifted, or that lacks a plan, is the normal case; the fix is two calm moves, prevent and prepare, not guilt or fear.
- The rule (finding hazards makes a home safer once, preventing them and preparing for them keep it safe for good), the four moves (see it, remove it, prevent it, prepare for it), and the preparation reframe (preparation is love for the day you hope never comes) are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype, alarmist statistics, graphic emergency detail, and any force or violence framing. Preparation is framed as calm love, a way to reduce dread by being ready, never a scare campaign. Keep the calm register throughout, and avoid brand names (say "a fire extinguisher" or "a carbon-monoxide alarm," not a brand).
- Scope note: this completes Safety with the forward-looking moves, prevention (light: safer defaults and simple habits, not a documented schedule) and preparation (working alarms, an extinguisher, a walked exit plan); it does not re-teach the safety lens or triage (Chapter 20) or the hazard hunt (Chapter 21), write documented standards or maintenance schedules (Standardize), or build long-term adherence and audits (Sustain). Four of six S's are done, and the goal is still ahead because Standardize and Sustain remain.
