## The baseline reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> You can chase hazards one at a time forever, or you can raise the whole home's floor so it is harder to get hurt in at all. Overall safety is the second thing: not a longer list of dangers removed, but a home that is safer even against the ones you have not met yet.

## The prevention reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> A hunt clears the hazards that are here. Prevention is what keeps the next one from quietly taking their place, because a home does not stay hunted. It drifts, and things flow back in, and the only defense that scales is making the safe way the easy way.

## The preparation reframe (frozen, pull-quote, the emotional core)
> Some harm you cannot prevent, only prepare for. You cannot promise a fire will never start. You can promise that the alarm will wake you and that the way out is clear. Preparation is love for the day you hope never comes.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Test every smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm in the home right now, one press each, and note any floor that has none. Working alarms are the highest-value protection in the house, and the only one that has to keep working while you are asleep.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the exit plan together, calmly, once. From each bedroom, how do you get out, and where does everyone meet outside? Children who have walked it once remember it, and walking it turns a frightening idea into a known, ordinary thing.
- **6S Tip:** Keep the tools of preparation in one known place, a single shelf or box: the spare alarm batteries, the fire extinguisher, a flashlight, the first-aid kit. Preparation you cannot find in the dark is not preparation.
- **Common Mistake:** Fitting a smoke alarm, feeling safe, and never testing it again, so the one protection you were counting on is silent the night it matters. A protection you do not keep alive is only decoration. Give the button a press whenever the clocks change.
