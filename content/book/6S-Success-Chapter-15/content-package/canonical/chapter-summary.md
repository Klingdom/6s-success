# Chapter 15 Summary: The Room Map

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four, Straighten*

## One-line summary

Chapter 15 completes Straighten by gathering the already-made, well-placed, self-explaining homes into a handful of zones grouped by activity, then turning the whole arrangement into one shared picture, the room map, so the entire room can be read at a glance and belongs to the household instead of living in one person's head.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Every keeper has a home, each home sits where your hand wants it, and each home now speaks, yet the entryway is still a row of separate clever spots, and the only picture that ties them together lives behind your eyes. Chapter 15 closes that gap without moving a thing. Group the homes into zones by what you do, not by what things are: a coffee zone at the machine, a launch pad by the door. Reveal the entryway as one zone all along. Then draw a simple map and walk the household through it. The friction meter reaches the Straighten-complete mark.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 15 is the fourth and final chapter of Part Four, and it completes Straighten, the second S. Chapter 12 gave every keeper a home; Chapter 13 placed each home by how often you reach for it; Chapter 14 made each home explain itself. Yet the arrangement still lives as scattered pieces, because each home was built one at a time and arranged around itself, not around the errands you actually run, and the picture that connects them exists only in the head of the person who built it. That private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a failing. The chapter fences itself off: it does not re-choose, re-place, or re-mark homes, does not clean or teach cleaning, does not build routines or standards, and does not re-open Sort.

A room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there. The hero move is zones: group by what you do, not by what things are, so a coffee zone at the machine beats a kitchen things cupboard. The running entryway example is revealed as one zone all along, the launch pad, and the home turns out to be just a handful of zones: coffee, homework corner, charging station, mail-and-admin. Then two small acts make the map shared: sketch it as a napkin diagram, and walk the household through it, from your room to their room. The friction meter reaches the Straighten-complete milestone, the second mark after Sort complete. Two of the six S's are done, and the goal is still ahead.
