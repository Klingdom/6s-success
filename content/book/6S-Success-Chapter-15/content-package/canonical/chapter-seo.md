## Search intent
Gathering a well-organized home into a single, shared picture, not decluttering or re-placing. Readers arriving here have already given their things homes, placed those homes by how often they use them, and made each home explain itself, but the arrangement still reads as separate clever spots, and the picture that ties them together lives only in one person's head. They want a way to see the whole room at once and to hand that view to the household. The chapter answers with a device and a rule: a room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there, and the rule is to group by what you do, not by what things are. It teaches zones (group by the verb, not the noun), reveals the entryway as one zone, the launch pad, zooms out to the home as a handful of zones (coffee, homework, charging, mail-and-admin), and shows the two small acts that make the map shared: sketch it, and walk the household through it.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Reframes organizing as gathering good homes into a whole-room view and sharing it, instead of one-more-round of decluttering or bin-buying
- Gives one clear rule, group by what you do, not by what things are, rather than category-based "a place for everything" advice that scatters a single task across the room
- Teaches zones as activity clusters (name by the verb, not the noun), with the coffee zone as a concrete before-and-after
- Names the launch pad by the door as a zone anyone can adopt, and shows the home as just five or six zones, not dozens
- Puts the emphasis on shared: a map only counts when more than one person holds it, and walking the household through it matters more than the drawing
- Delivers the anti-shame framing directly (a private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a failing) instead of implying the reader is disorganized
- Keeps scope tight: this gathers and shares; it does not re-choose, re-place, or re-mark homes (Chapters 12 to 14), clean or teach cleaning (Chapter 16, Shine), build routines or standards (the later Standardize S), or re-open Sort
