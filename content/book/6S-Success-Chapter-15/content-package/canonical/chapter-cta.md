# Chapter 15 Calls to Action: The Room Map

This is the fourth and final chapter of Part Four, and it completes Straighten, the second S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible first move: pick one busy corner, name the activity in a single word, and gather what that activity needs into the spot. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive tone, carrying a note of earned pride without hype: a private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a failing, and the fix costs nothing but a sheet of paper and ten honest minutes at the kitchen table.

## Primary CTA
Draw your first zone. "Pick one busy corner and name, in a single word, the activity that happens there. Gather everything that activity needs into that spot, and move out anything that belongs to a different activity. You just drew your first zone. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA

"Every home is well placed and self-explaining now, but the arrangement still reads as separate spots, and the picture that ties them together lives only in your head. Chapter 15 gathers the homes into zones by activity and turns the whole arrangement into one shared map, so the entire room reads at once. Read it free online, then Chapter 16 opens Shine, where the third S cleans and cares for what Straighten arranged."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one busy corner and name, in a single word, the activity that happens there. Gather everything that activity needs into that spot, and move out anything that belongs to a different activity. You just drew your first zone.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the house together and give each area a one-word job: launch pad, homework, coffee, charging. Then sketch a simple map of where those zones sit. When everyone can point to the same picture, the map is shared.
- **6S Tip:** Zone by the verb, not the noun. A coffee zone (mug, beans, grinder, filters, all at the machine) beats a "kitchen things" cupboard, because you live by what you are doing, not by what category a thing happens to fall in.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable first move, not a burst of effort: name one corner by its activity and gather what it needs, then draw the map and walk the household through it. Lead with "draw your first zone," which is concrete and doable in a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, with a quiet note of pride, because this closes the second S. A private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a family's failing.
- The rule (group by what you do, not by what things are), the launch pad reveal, the shared-map completion, and the reframe (the arrangement was yours) are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is gathering good homes into a shared picture; nothing moves house.
- Scope note: this gathers and shares; it does not re-choose, re-place, or re-mark homes (Chapters 12 to 14), clean or teach cleaning (Chapter 16, Shine), build routines or standards for keeping order (the later Standardize S), or re-open Sort. Straighten is complete; Shine opens next.
