# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 15 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 15 is the fourth and final Straighten chapter, and it closes the second S. Its job carries a note of earned pride the earlier chapters did not: the room is now fully arranged. The voice must let that pride sit quietly, stay anti-shame, and be honest that the milestone is real but not the finish line.

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," returns to the entryway one last time, and imagines a friend locking up or a child returning the leash to the launch pad without being told.
- **Concrete over abstract.** The concept is taught through the garden or museum "you are here" diagram, the morning coffee scattered across four cupboards (mug, beans, grinder, filters), and a napkin sketch of the rooms with the zones blocked in.
- **Anti-shame.** "A map that lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach; it is where every well-run home sits right before this step." A private, in-your-head map is a stage, not a failing.
- **Handing-over reframe.** "The arrangement was yours." The chapter's kindest move is handing the design to the whole household, so it stops being your room that they live in and becomes their room that they run.
- **Honest about scope.** Nothing left the house to earn the milestone; the friction move is justified on a real basis: the room's logic stopped being private and became shared, so nobody hunts and nobody holds the whole thing alone.

## Specific wins
- "Group by what you do, not by what things are" is the most portable line in the chapter and travels intact across channels.
- "It was one zone all along" delivers the launch-pad payoff in five words and lands as the shareable reveal.
- The private-map / shared-map pull-quote gives the emotional turn a clean, quotable form.

## Voice risks to watch
- **Hero vocabulary is dense.** "map," "zone," "room," "home," "shared," "activity" recur because they are the subject; expected, not a tic, but vary the surrounding language when excerpting, and watch "whole," "picture," and "logic" as the payoff words most likely to stack.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the LinkedIn and X sign-offs; several close on "Free online" or "the online book."
- **Keep the pride quiet.** The chapter closes a whole S, so a note of accomplishment is right; do not let any asset tip into fanfare or a "two-thirds done" victory-lap register. The frozen framing keeps the goal honestly ahead (four S's remain).

## Banned-language audit (scans actually run, LC_ALL=C, across manuscript, final HTML, publishable HTML, and all 52 package files)
- Widened dash scan (em dash, en dash, and spaced-hyphen " - " used as a separator, excluding markdown list bullets): **zero** in every category. Dash HTML entities (&mdash;, &ndash;, numeric): **zero**. Labels use the middot "·" (present in 30 files).
- Hype-word scan (unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, seamless, game-changing, "almost magical", "fast-paced", "dive in", "in conclusion"): **zero** in all files.
- Force / violence scan: "war on clutter" and "pull the trigger" **zero**. "purge" and "attack" each return **2 hits, both in `video-audio/b-roll-and-visual-notes.md` as negated production guidance** ("No force or violence framing. Nothing is purged or attacked"). Neither appears in any reader-facing asset. Not a voice violation; noted for full honesty.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all twelve packages, and the quiet-pride, anti-shame, honest-milestone job of Chapter 15 is honored throughout. Approve the voice. The chapter itself still needs author sign-off, since it was authored in the book voice rather than supplied from source.
