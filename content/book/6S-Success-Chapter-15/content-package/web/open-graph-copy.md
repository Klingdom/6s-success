# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 15, The Room Map

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (the rule)

- og:title: Group by What You Do, Not by What Things Are
- og:description: Good, self-explaining homes can still add up to a scatter, because each was built one at a time. Gather them into a few zones by activity and the whole room reads at a glance.
- twitter:title: Group by What You Do, Not by What Things Are
- twitter:description: A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens. A coffee zone at the machine beats a "kitchen things" cupboard. From Chapter 15 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A flattened floor plan of a home with a few labeled zones blocked in by activity, calm and legible at a glance: a rectangle by the door marked "launch pad," a square at the counter marked "coffee," a patch at the table marked "homework," a spot by the beds marked "charging," a shelf near the hall marked "mail." A friendly "you are here" dot sits at the door. Along the bottom: Group by what you do, not by what things are.

## Variant B (the shared map)

- og:title: The Entryway Was a Zone All Along
- og:description: The keys, leash, bag, and umbrella by the door were never six separate spots. They were one zone all along: the launch pad, everything leaving and arriving needs, clustered where that activity happens.
- twitter:title: The Entryway Was a Zone All Along
- twitter:description: A map that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person steps out. Draw it together instead. From Chapter 15 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: The entryway drawn as one coherent zone, all its keepers grouped and labeled: keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, shoes, gathered inside a single soft outline marked "launch pad." A small note points to the outline: "everything leaving and arriving needs, in one spot." A second hand reaches in to sketch the outline alongside the first, so the drawing reads as shared. Footer: Naming it as one zone is the last stitch that completes Straighten.
