## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
Every keeper has a home (Ch 12), each home sits where your hand reaches for it (Ch 13), and each home now speaks (Ch 14), yet the entryway is still a row of separate clever spots and the picture that ties them together lives behind one person's eyes. Chapter 15 closes that gap without moving a thing. A **room map** is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there. The hero move is zones: **group by what you do, not by what things are**, so a coffee zone at the machine beats a kitchen-things cupboard. The running entryway example is revealed as one zone all along, the launch pad, and the home turns out to be a handful of zones: launch pad, coffee, homework, charging, mail-and-admin. Then two small acts make the map shared: sketch it, and walk the household through it, so it stops being your room that they live in and becomes their room that they run. The friction meter reaches the marked STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE tick, the second milestone on the dial after SORT COMPLETE. Two of the six S's are done, and the goal is still ahead. Hands off to Chapter 16 (Shine: Cleaning as Inspection), which opens Part Five.

## Frozen strings (copy verbatim; do not paraphrase)
- The rule: **Group by what you do, not by what things are.**
- The zone (woven definition): **A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens, so a task is served by one spot instead of scattered across the room.**
- The definition box: **A room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there, so anyone in the household can see where everything lives, and why, all at once.**
- One Idea to Keep: **Group the homes into a few zones by what you do, and draw the map once with everyone, so the arrangement stops living in your head and starts living in the whole household. A room whose logic is shared is a room that runs itself, which is exactly where Straighten set out to go.**
