## The Room Map

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 15. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with the teaching deck and the visual storyboard.*

### Slide 1 (hook)

**Every home is placed, and every home now speaks. So why does the house still only make full sense to you?**

Because good, separate spots do not add up to a picture. The one thing that ties them together lives behind your eyes.

Swipe for the final Straighten step: gather the homes into zones, and share the map.

Floating faintly above the row, sketched in light pencil in soft, a large "?" where a single connecting picture would sit. Warm paper, slate chapter label. Zone colours not shown yet; the point is the missing picture.

### Slide 2

**A newcomer meets your homes the way you meet strangers at a party: one at a time.**

Every spot is finished and correct. There is still no single view of the space, nothing you could point a camera at to say, in one breath, here is how this corner works.

The view exists. It just exists in exactly one place, behind your eyes.

[VISUAL] The same tidy row of homes on paper ground. A newcomer stands at the door, meeting each spot alone in the moment they reach for it, a soft "?" over each in turn. Above, in soft pencil, the whole layout floats as a tiny sketch chained to the head of the person who built it. Slate footnote: "the only place the picture lives."

---

### Slide 3

**The keys, the leash, the bag, and the umbrella were never really separate spots. They are one cluster: the leaving-the-house cluster.**

You know that without thinking, because you are the one who leaves. Nobody else can see the grouping, because you never drew it.

A map that lives in one head is a completely natural stage, not a failing. It is simply the last thing that has not been made shareable.

[VISUAL] The four entryway keepers softly ringed as one cluster in soft, with a dashed thread running up to a single thought-bubble over one person's head. A slate tag: "real, and invisible, and yours alone." No verdict colours; nobody is behind here.

---

### Slide 4

**A room map is that you-are-here diagram, shrunk to fit your home.**

It does not list every item. It shows the shape of the place, so in one glance you hold the whole layout and can find anything without being led by the hand.

"A room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there, so anyone in the household can see where everything lives, and why, all at once."

[VISUAL] A flattened, friendly floor plan of a home with a few blocks of colour labeled by activity: launch pad terracotta, coffee honey, homework slate, charging green, mail-and-admin soft. A small "you are here" dot. Calm and legible at a glance. Slate caption: "Grouped by what you do, not by what things are."

---

### Slide 5

**Group by what you do, not by what things are.**

Most homes get organized by category, and it quietly fails you every day, because you do not live by category. You live by activity.

Grouping by what things are spreads a single task across the room. Grouping by what you do pulls it back into one spot.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the frozen rule set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Beneath it, two small tags: "by category · scattered" in soft, and "by activity · one spot" with a green calm-dot. Paper ground.

---

### Slide 6

**Coffee by category lives in four places. Coffee by activity is one reach.**

Mugs on the crockery shelf, beans in the dry-goods cupboard, grinder in the appliance drawer, filters wherever they landed. Making a cup is a small circuit before the kettle boils.

Gather all four at the machine and the circuit collapses. Nothing was bought, nothing thrown out. That one spot is a zone.

[VISUAL] A side-by-side. Left, a kitchen with the four coffee items scattered across three cupboards and a shelf, a soft looping path between them. Right, the same four gathered at the machine inside a single honey coffee-zone boundary, the path collapsed to a reach, a green calm-dot. Caption: "same objects, gathered around what you do with them."

---

### Slide 7

**The row by the door was one zone all along: the launch pad.**

Everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs, clustered right where that activity happens. On the way out it hands you what you cannot leave without; on the way in it catches it back.

Naming it as one zone is the last stitch, the one that turns six good homes into a single thing you can understand in a word.

[VISUAL] The entryway keepers, keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, shoes, drawn inside one terracotta boundary labeled "LAUNCH PAD." A soft note: "what four chapters were quietly building." Paper ground, warm daylight.

---

### Slide 8

**Your home is really just a handful of zones.**

Launch pad by the door. Coffee at the machine. Homework corner at the end of the table. Charging station by the sofa. Mail-and-admin near the front hall.

Five or six named zones describe almost any home. You need only the few your life actually runs on.

[VISUAL] The full floor plan again, now with all five zones lit in their fixed colours and named by verb. A slate tag: "a logic you could explain from the door to the kitchen." Calm, uncluttered, readable in one look.

---

### Slide 9

**Take a sheet of paper and sketch the home no straighter than a napkin diagram.**

Block in the zones: a rectangle by the door for the launch pad, a square at the counter for coffee, a patch at the table for homework.

You are not drafting a blueprint. You are making the map in your head visible, so it exists somewhere other than behind your eyes.

[VISUAL] A hand sketching a rough floor plan on a single sheet, zones blocked in loosely and labeled in their colours, corners deliberately crooked. The soft pencil thought-bubble from earlier slides now redrawn on the page. Caption: "the moment it is on paper, it can be pointed to and improved."

---

### Slide 10

**A private map fails the moment that person steps out. So do not draw it alone.**

"A map that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person steps out. A map the whole household has drawn together is the one that finally makes the room belong to everyone who lives in it."

Walk the household through it. Stand in each zone, say its name out loud. A child who has walked the map once can return the leash to the launch pad without being told.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the frozen shared-map line set large in Fraunces, slate rules. Beside it, a family gathered over one sheet at the kitchen table, hands pointing to zones, a green calm-dot on the shared map. Warm and light.

---

### Slide 11

**Two of the six S's are done. The room is cleared and fully arranged.**

You gave every keeper a home, placed each where your hand wants it, taught each to speak, and today gathered them into one shared picture.

You did not buy a system or add an ounce of storage. A room whose arrangement is shared asks less of every person in it, including you.

[VISUAL] The entryway, now calm and readable, with the launch pad zone named and a small green calm-dot on the shared map beside it. A slate strip shows the six S's, with Sort and Straighten ticked and four still ahead. Footer: "the second S, standing finished in a hallway."
