# Chapter 15 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: The Room Map

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, no force metaphors.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** Every home in your house is placed and self-explaining. So why does the whole place still live in your head?

**Body:** Because good separate homes are not the same as one shared picture. Swipe for the final Straighten step: gathering the homes into zones and handing the map around.

---

## Slide 2: Good homes, no shared picture

**Headline:** The entryway is still a row of separate clever spots.

**Body:** Keys on a hook, leash on a hook, bags in a basket, each finished and good, but nothing says they belong to one another. The one view that ties them together exists in exactly one place: behind your eyes.

---

## Slide 3: You live by activity, not by category

**Headline:** Nobody thinks, "time to interact with my kitchen things." They think, "I want coffee."

**Body:** And then they need the mug, the beans, the grinder, and the filters, four items a category-minded house scatters across three cupboards and a shelf.

---

## Slide 4: The rule

**Headline:** Group by what you do, not by what things are.

**Body:** Gather the four coffee items at the machine and the circuit collapses to a reach. Nothing was bought and nothing was thrown out. The same objects gathered around the thing you do with them.

---

## Slide 5: What a zone is

**Headline:** A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens.

**Body:** A task is served by one spot instead of scattered across the room. Name it by the verb, not the noun: the coffee zone, not "the cupboard of coffee-related objects."

---

## Slide 6: What a room map is

**Headline:** A single shared picture of a space, organized into zones.

**Body:** A room map is that friendly "you are here" diagram, shrunk to fit your home. In one glance, anyone can see where everything lives, and why, all at once.

---

## Slide 7: The reveal

**Headline:** The entryway was one zone all along.

**Body:** The keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, and shoes were never six separate spots. They are the launch pad: everything the leaving-and-arriving activity needs, at the threshold between inside and out.

---

## Slide 8: A home is a handful of zones

**Headline:** Five or six named zones will describe almost any home.

**Body:** The coffee zone. The homework corner. The charging station. The mail-and-admin spot. You do not need dozens. You need the few your life actually runs on.

---

## Slide 9: Draw it

**Headline:** Making the map in your head visible.

**Body:** Sketch the rough shape of your home, no straighter than a napkin diagram, then block in the zones. You are not drafting a blueprint. You are moving the map out from behind your eyes.

---

## Slide 10: A private map fails

**Headline:** A map that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person steps out.

**Body:** This is not a failing. A private, in-your-head map is a natural stage. Making it shareable is the small, satisfying last job.

---

## Slide 11: From your room to their room

**Headline:** It stops being your room that they live in and becomes their room that they run.

**Body:** Draw the map together and walk everyone through it. It costs nothing but a sheet of paper and ten honest minutes at the kitchen table.

---

**Headline:** The zones are named, the picture is shared, and Straighten is complete.
