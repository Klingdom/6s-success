# Infographic Spec: The Room Map

**Chapter 15, "The Room Map"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a house
of good, well-placed, self-explaining homes is still only half arranged if the sole record of how it
all fits together is one person's head, so the room map gathers the homes into a few activity zones,
drawn once and shared, and the whole space becomes readable at a glance and belongs to everyone. The
concept pairs the hero rule, **Group by what you do, not by what things are**, with the **zoned floor
plan** beneath it, the launch pad, coffee, homework, charging, and mail-and-admin, so the viewer sees
both the rule that turns pieces into a picture and the handful of zones it produces, and sees at once
that a scatter of clever separate spots has become one thing anyone can read.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_15_final.html`. Wood fill marks the
launch pad by the door; honey, slate, green, and terracotta-tint mark the other zones so they read
apart at a glance; slate carries the rule, the definition, and the tags; the single small
friction-spark is held back and used nowhere on this calm image. A handful of zones, each named by
the verb, is the discipline the image itself should model.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest element on the page. Draw it as the one plain move that turns a scatter of
  separate homes into a picture, so the eye grasps the rule before the plan: you live by activity, not
  by category, so the homes gather around what you do.
- Hold the color discipline: wood fill marks the launch pad by the door; honey, slate, green, and
  terracotta-tint mark the other zones so they read apart at a glance; slate carries the rule, the
  definition, and the tags; the green you-are-here dot marks the threshold. The friction-spark is held
  back entirely; this image is calm, so no fill-line red anywhere.
- The zones are exactly the handful the chapter names: launch pad, coffee, homework, charging, and
  mail-and-admin, each named for one activity. Keep the mapping one to one and plain, five or six
  zones and no more, and model the chapter's own rule by naming every zone with the verb, not the
  noun.
- This image gathers the already-made, well-placed, self-explaining homes into shared zones and draws
  the whole-room picture. Do NOT re-assign homes or reopen that decision (that was Chapter 12). Do NOT
  re-place a home by frequency of use (that was Chapter 13). Do NOT re-mark a home with a cue (that was
  Chapter 14). Do NOT clean or teach cleaning (Chapter 16, Shine). Do NOT build a routine, schedule, or
  written standard for keeping order over time (the later Standardize and Sustain S's). Do NOT reopen
  Sort.
- Anti-shame: a map that lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach, not a
  verdict on the person, and a scatter of good separate spots is information about a picture not yet
  drawn, not a failing. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no guilt cues, no pressure framing, and no
  force framing anywhere.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the plan drawn as a
  napkin sketch rather than a drafted blueprint, the rule set as if jotted rather than typeset.
