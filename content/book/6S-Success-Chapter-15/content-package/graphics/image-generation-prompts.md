# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 15

**"The Room Map"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to
hold across all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single warm
red `#CB4B36` reserved for rare small accents only. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy
depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the settled entryway, and later a whole-home floor plan and a family
around a kitchen table. Across Chapters 12, 13, and 14, Straighten gave every keeper one clear home,
placed each home by how often your hand goes to it, and made each home self-explaining: the keys, the
dog's leash, the reusable shopping bag, the folding umbrella, the outgoing mail, and the shoes. Those
homes are done, good, well placed, and able to explain themselves. This chapter, the fourth and final
of Part Four, does one narrow thing: it gathers those homes into a handful of ZONES grouped by
activity, and turns the whole arrangement into one SHARED picture, the room map, that the household
holds together. Nothing is re-decided, re-placed, re-marked, or removed. No home is re-assigned (that
was Chapter 12), no home is re-placed by frequency (Chapter 13), no home is re-marked with a cue
(Chapter 14), nothing is cleaned (Chapter 16, Shine), and no routine, schedule, or written standard
for keeping order over time is built (the later Standardize and Sustain S's). Sort stays closed. Keep
the tone calm and kind: a map that lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach,
not a fault, and the fix is a drawn, shared picture, not willpower. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no
shame cues, and no force or pressure framing.

No text is baked into these images. Any label named below is a physical object in the scene, a
hand-lettered name-plate a household would actually write, not a typeset overlay. Separator
discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 15 ·
STRAIGHTEN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their
internal hyphen: self-explaining, mail-and-admin, whole-room, at-a-glance, one-word, Sort-complete,
Straighten-complete, walk-through.

## 1. Good spots, no picture (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of a settled entryway in soft morning daylight. Along the wall by the
door runs a handful of good, finished spots, each clearly complete and clearly separate: a small
hooked key rack, a hook holding a coiled dog leash, a woven basket for shopping bags, a slim umbrella
stand, and a shallow tray for outgoing mail. A little gap of bare wall sits between each spot and the
next, so they read as separate clever homes rather than one arrangement. Every spot is tidy, correct,
and quietly finished. Nothing in the scene ties the spots together, no shared shelf, no drawn frame,
no connecting line. Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a soft slate blue wall.
Editorial cookbook-style photography, shallow focus running along the row of separate spots. The room
looks settled and well kept, with one quiet, unspoken thing missing: any single picture that shows
how the whole corner works.

- Mood: every home is done, good, placed, and able to explain itself, yet they stand as separate good
  spots with a gap of wall between each one, and the only place a whole-room picture exists is inside
  the person who built them; that gap is what the chapter closes.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, any clutter or mess
  drama, any sense of re-deciding or moving what is placed, price tags, hurry or stress, and any
  drawn zone outline, floor plan, or connecting frame tying the spots together; the opener's whole
  point is that the picture is still absent.

## 2. What a room map is (the zoned floor plan)

A warm editorial illustration of a simple, hand-drawn overhead floor plan of a small home, loose and
friendly like a napkin sketch rather than an architect's blueprint. A few activity zones are blocked
in as soft colored areas, each named by a small hand-lettered card sitting on it: a rectangle by the
front door for the launch pad where leaving and arriving happens, a square at the kitchen counter for
coffee, a patch at the end of the dining table for homework, a spot by the sofa for charging, and a
shelf near the hall for mail-and-admin. A small hand-drawn "you are here" dot marks the threshold. The
plan is calm and legible at a glance, five or six zones and no more, each an activity rather than a
category. Warm paper cream background, honey wood and soft-green and slate zone tints kept quiet and
distinct, ink-brown line work. Documentary and hand-made, as if a household had just sketched the
shape of their home and blocked in the zones by what they actually do.

- Mood: the little you-are-here diagram from a garden or museum, shrunk to fit a home, so the whole
  arrangement can be read in one glance as a handful of zones instead of discovered one home at a
  time.
- Avoid: a crisp architectural blueprint, dozens of tiny blocks, labeling individual objects, cold
  clinical light, floating typeset text, a launch-pad close-up (that is the next image), any cleaning
  or schedule, and any category-style naming; every zone is named for the activity, the verb, not the
  noun.

## 3. The launch pad was a zone all along (the payoff)

A warm editorial photograph of the same entryway from the first image, now read as one coherent zone.
The keys, the coiled dog leash, the reusable shopping bag, the folding umbrella, the outgoing mail,
and the shoes are grouped together at the threshold as a single cluster, everything the one activity
of leaving and arriving needs, gathered where that activity happens. A small hand-lettered card on the
cluster names it the launch pad. Where the first image showed gaps of bare wall between separate
spots, here the same keepers read as one thing, close together and clearly belonging to each other.
Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, soft daylight, ink-brown shadows, one small soft-green calm
note on the named cluster. Documentary and unstaged, as if a household had finally seen that the row
by the door was one zone all along.

- Mood: the six good homes by the door, built one keeper at a time across three chapters, revealed as
  a single named zone a person can understand in a word, everything leaving and arriving needs in one
  spot; a quiet, satisfied payoff, not a fanfare.
- Avoid: adding new objects, moving a home, re-marking anything, a full floor plan (that was the
  previous image), cold light, floating typeset text, celebratory drama, any cleaning or routine.
  Keep the same keepers in the same places; the only change is that they now read as one gathered
  zone.

## 4. Your room to their room (draw it, and share it)

A calm, warm photograph of a household gathered over a single sketched sheet of paper at the kitchen
table. A loose hand-drawn map of the home sits on the table, the same napkin-style floor plan with a
few named zones blocked in, and two or three people of different ages lean in together, each pointing
to a zone on the same sheet. The mood is shared and unhurried: the picture that once lived in one
person's head is now out on the table where everyone can see it, point to it, and improve it. Ordinary
kitchen light, real hands on a real sheet of paper, a pencil resting nearby. Warm paper cream
background, honey light, ink-brown shadows, one soft-green calm note on the shared sheet. The image
reads as a design being handed to the whole household, so the room stops being one person's and starts
being everyone's.

- Mood: a map the whole household has drawn together is the one that finally makes the room belong to
  everyone who lives in it; this is a weight handed over and a room shared, a relief, not a chore.
- Avoid: a staged or posed family photo, a slick printed blueprint on the table, one person lecturing
  the others, guilt or pressure cues, any framing of anyone as forgetful or at fault, a warm-red
  accent, harsh contrast, glossy retouching, floating typeset text. The sharing is the warm heart of
  the chapter; keep it gentle and real.

## 5. The needle reaches the mark (the friction meter, Straighten complete)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge dialing
from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right. The dark live
needle has moved past last chapter's spot and now reaches a clearly marked tick on the dial for
Straighten complete, the second milestone on the meter after the earlier Sort-complete mark, and it
sits notably calmer than Chapter 14. Just behind the live needle, a faint dashed ghost needle marks
that Chapter 14 position, showing the step just taken; fainter still, lighter dashed needles mark the
earlier Straighten positions from Chapters 12 and 13 further back. A small slate marker for the
Sort-complete milestone sits further back again, ground covered four chapters ago. A slate crosshair
labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag, still sits further out on the green calm side, clearly
ahead of the live needle, because four more disciplines remain. Warm paper cream background, ink line
work, consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest milestone, the second on the dial, nothing left the house yet the room grew calmer
  because the homes gathered into shared zones, so the whole space reads at once and no one carries
  the layout alone; the needle reaches a real mark, two of six S's done, with real road still ahead.
- Avoid: the live needle held in place with no move, the needle swung all the way to calm, the needle
  reaching or passing the goal crosshair, the dashed ghosts drawn solid or bright, celebratory
  confetti, hype, neon color, any sense of the whole journey being finished. Straighten is complete,
  but four more S's (Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain) still remain, so the goal stays clearly
  ahead.
