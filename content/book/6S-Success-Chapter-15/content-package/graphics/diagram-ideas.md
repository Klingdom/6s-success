# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 15

**"The Room Map"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_15_final.html`:
the settled entryway shown as a row of finished but separate good spots under a faint pencil
question, a simple overhead floor plan with a few activity zones blocked in and named, the entryway
redrawn as one coherent zone with all its keepers grouped and labeled launch pad, and the friction
meter reaching a marked Straighten-complete tick, the second milestone on the dial after
Sort complete.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the fourth and final Straighten chapter, and it completes the second S.
Chapter 12 already gave every keeper one clear home, Chapter 13 already placed each home by how often
your hand goes to it, and Chapter 14 already made each home self-explaining; all three are done and
stay. Chapter 15 does one narrow thing: it gathers the already-made, well-placed, self-explaining
homes into a handful of ZONES grouped by activity, and turns the whole arrangement into one SHARED
picture, the room map, that the household holds together. It does NOT re-assign homes (Chapter 12),
it does NOT re-place them by frequency (Chapter 13), it does NOT re-mark them with cues (Chapter 14),
and it does NOT clean anything (Chapter 16, Shine) or build routines, schedules, or written
standards to keep the arrangement up over time (the later Standardize and Sustain S's). It does not
reopen Sort. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 15 · STRAIGHTEN`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
launch-pad label glyphs, mail-and-admin, whole-room, self-explaining, at-a-glance, one-word,
Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, walk-through, anti-shame. Anti-shame throughout: a map that
lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach, not a failing; the scatter of
good separate spots is information about a picture not yet drawn, not a verdict on anyone. Keep it
calm and non-coercive, no guilt cues, no pressure framing, and no force or violence framing anywhere.

## 1. Good Spots, No Picture (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's starting point in one image. Every keeper now has a well-placed,
self-explaining home, and that is real progress, but the homes stand as separate clever spots with
no connecting logic, and the only place a whole-room picture exists is inside the person who built
them. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The entryway from the last three chapters, seen all at once. Along the wall by the
door sit a handful of good, finished spots, each clearly complete and clearly separate: a labeled
hook for the keys, a labeled hook for the leash, a marked basket for the bags, an umbrella stand, a
tray marked out for the mail. A little gap of blank wall sits between each spot and the next. Every
home is right; nothing yet binds them into one thing. Floating faintly above the whole row, sketched
in light pencil, is a large question mark where a single connecting picture would sit.

**Rough layout:**
- Warm daylight wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient), a low floor line in ink, the wall
  calm and settled.
- A row of five finished spots along a soft wood-fill `#E7C58B` wall, each drawn clean and correct
  and each carrying the Chapter 14 cue it already earned: the keys hook with a small `KEYS`
  name-plate, the leash hook with a `LEASH` name-plate, a basket labeled `BAGS`, an umbrella stand
  whose shape says its job, a tray marked `MAIL`. Draw a clear blank-wall gap between each spot.
- Above the row, a large question mark sketched in light pencil `#D9CDB8`, floating where a single
  connecting picture would sit, so the eye reads it as absent, not drawn.
- A small faint sans note beneath the row in muted `#6A625A`: `every spot is finished; the picture
  lives in one head`.
- Caption idea: "Every home here is finished: chosen, placed, and able to explain itself. But they
  stand as separate good spots with no picture that ties them together, and the only place that
  picture exists is inside the person who built them. That gap is what this chapter closes."

**Note for the illustrator:** the spots here are good, not a mess, so draw the row as tidy, finished,
and settled, never as clutter. The quiet tension is that every correct spot stands alone, with a gap
of blank wall between it and its neighbor and no line, frame, or color tying them together. Do not
move, re-rank, re-assign, or re-mark anything; that work is finished. Do not yet draw any zone
outline, floor plan, or connecting logic; the whole point of the opener is that the picture is
absent, held only by the faint pencil question. Keep it warm and matter-of-fact, no scolding about
the scatter, just a quiet noticing.

## 2. What a Room Map Is (the concept, the zoned floor plan)

**Purpose:** Make the room map concrete by borrowing the friendly "you are here" diagram at a garden
or museum entrance, shrunk to fit a home. It shows the hero move in one glance: the scattered homes
gathered into a few activity zones, blocked in and named, so the whole space can be read at once
instead of discovered piece by piece. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A simple overhead or flattened floor plan of a home, no straighter than a napkin
diagram, with a few labeled activity zones blocked in as soft colored rectangles. A rectangle by the
door reads `LAUNCH PAD`, a square at the counter reads `COFFEE`, a patch at the table reads
`HOMEWORK`, a spot by the sofa reads `CHARGING`, a shelf near the hall reads `MAIL`. Each zone is
named for the activity that happens there, and the whole plan is calm and legible at a glance.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label in soft gray: `WHAT A ROOM MAP IS`.
- A loose overhead floor outline in soft ink, rounded and hand-drawn, with a door marked on one wall
  and a "you are here" dot in calm green `#6E8B5B` at the threshold.
- Five soft-filled zone blocks, each a different quiet tint from the palette so they read apart at a
  glance: launch pad in wood fill by the door, coffee in honey at the counter, homework in slate at
  the table, charging in soft green by the sofa, mail in terracotta-tint near the hall. Each block
  carries a plain sans caps name and a tiny helper in Newsreader italic: `launch pad · leaving and
  arriving`, `coffee · the morning cup`, `homework · four o'clock`, `charging · overnight`, `mail ·
  post and bills`.
- A small legend strip along the bottom: `each block is a zone, named for what you do there`.
- The frozen rule set as if jotted above or beside the plan, Fraunces in ink: "Group by what you do,
  not by what things are."
- Caption idea: "A room map is the little you-are-here diagram, shrunk to fit your home. It does not
  list every object; it shows the shape of the place as a handful of zones, each named for the
  activity that happens there, so the whole arrangement can be read in one glance."

**Note for the illustrator:** keep the plan loose and friendly, a napkin sketch, not a blueprint or a
drafted floor plan; the point is that anyone could draw this in a few minutes. Five or six zones is
plenty; do not crowd the plan with dozens of blocks or label individual objects. Name every zone by
the verb, not the noun, so the labels read as activities, not categories. Do not draw the launch-pad
close-up here; that is the next figure. Do not add cleaning, routines, or a schedule. Keep the mood
quietly confident and the whole thing legible at a glance.

## 3. The Launch Pad Was a Zone All Along (the payoff)

**Purpose:** Deliver the chapter's quiet reveal as a picture: the row of separate homes by the door,
built one keeper at a time across three chapters, was one coherent zone all along, the launch pad,
everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs, clustered where that activity happens.
Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The same entryway from Figure 1, now redrawn as one thing. A single soft zone
outline gathers all six keepers into one cluster at the threshold: keys, leash, bag, umbrella,
outgoing mail, shoes, each in its home but now visibly held inside one named area. A clear label,
`LAUNCH PAD`, sits on the zone, and a small note points to the whole cluster: everything leaving and
arriving needs, in one spot.

**Rough layout:**
- Panel background, top sans caps label in soft gray: `THE LAUNCH PAD`.
- The entryway wall from Figure 1, but with a single soft-filled zone band in wood fill `#E7C58B`
  drawn around the whole row, rounded corners, a calm green border, so the six separate spots now
  read as one bounded area. Where Figure 1 showed gaps of blank wall between spots, this figure shows
  them held together inside one outline.
- The six keepers grouped and labeled inside the band: `keys`, `leash`, `bag`, `umbrella`, `outgoing
  mail`, `shoes`, each a small clean glyph in its home, in ink sans.
- A slate name-plate on the zone reading `LAUNCH PAD`, larger than the item labels, and a Newsreader
  italic helper beneath it: `everything leaving and arriving needs, where it happens`.
- A small faint sans note off to the side in muted `#6A625A`: `built one keeper at a time across
  three chapters; named as one zone here`.
- Along the bottom, a slate Newsreader italic line: "Six good homes become a single thing a person
  can understand in a word."
- Caption idea: "The row by the door was never six separate spots. It was one zone all along, the
  launch pad: everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs, clustered exactly where
  that activity happens. Naming it is the last stitch that turns six good homes into one thing."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a reveal, so the visual echo of Figure 1 matters; use the same
keepers in the same places, and let the one new element, the zone outline gathering them, do all the
work. Do not add new objects, move a home, or re-mark anything; the keepers keep the homes and cues
they already earned. The single label `LAUNCH PAD` names the whole; keep the item labels small and
quiet beneath it. Do not draw the full floor plan here (that is Figure 2) and do not add cleaning or
a maintenance routine. Keep it warm and quietly satisfied; this is a payoff, not a fanfare.

## 4. Your Room to Their Room (draw it, and share it)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's central reframe as a picture: the arrangement that lived in one
person's head becomes a shared map the whole household has drawn together, so it stops being your
room that they live in and becomes their room that they run. This diagram has no direct HTML twin; it
renders the "Draw It, and Share It" section as a paired-panel figure, then the shared-map completion
as the line beneath.

**What it shows:** Two facing panels. The left is the private map: the room's logic drawn only inside
one person's head, a single figure holding the whole picture while the rest of the household stands
a little apart, guests inside a logic they never quite saw. The right is the shared map: the same
household gathered over one sketched sheet on the kitchen table, everyone pointing to the same zones,
the picture now out in the open where all can hold it.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `DRAW IT, AND SHARE IT`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath: "lift
  the map out of one head and onto the table".
- Left panel, muted header bar `A PRIVATE MAP`: one ink figure with a small thought-bubble carrying a
  tiny floor plan, the household drawn faint and a little apart, a Newsreader italic note `the
  picture lives in one head`. Draw it calm and neutral, not sad; a small slate tag at the base
  `FAILS THE MOMENT THAT PERSON STEPS OUT`.
- Right panel, green header bar `A SHARED MAP`: the same household leaning over one sketched sheet on
  the kitchen table, each hand pointing to a named zone, the sketch showing the same loose plan from
  Figure 2, a green calm-dot on the shared sheet. Newsreader italic beneath: `everyone has stood over
  the same picture`. A green sans caps line at the base `BELONGS TO EVERYONE WHO LIVES IN IT`.
- Beneath the two panels, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen shared-map completion: "A map
  that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person
  steps out. A map the whole household has drawn together is the one that finally makes the room
  belong to everyone who lives in it."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "It stops being your room that they live in and becomes their
  room that they run."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a relief and a handing-over, not a reproach, so draw the left
panel as an ordinary, natural stage, never as a person failing; a map in one head is where every
well-run home sits right before this step. Keep the two panels balanced and calm, no friction-spark
on the private map and no scolding cue. The right panel is the warm one: real people around a real
sheet, pointing. Draw the sketch on the table as a napkin diagram, not a blueprint. Green marks the
shared map; slate carries the completion line. Do not add cleaning, a schedule, or a written standard;
the reframe stays at the level of one sheet of paper and ten honest minutes at the kitchen table.

## 5. The Needle Reaches the Mark (the Straighten-complete milestone)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest Straighten-complete move. Nothing left the house this
time either, but gathering the homes into shared zones removed the last of the arranging friction, so
the live needle reaches a marked tick on the dial, the second milestone after Sort complete, with the
goal still ahead because four S's remain. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML, and drives the live
data-driven gauge.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle reaching a clearly marked

milestone sitting further back as a passed marker, and the goal crosshair still ahead on the calm
side because four more S's remain.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark (right),

- Fainter still, lighter dashed needles at the earlier Straighten steps (Chapters 12 and 13), tagged

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a MILESTONE, the second on the dial, not a hold and not
the finish line. The live needle must REACH the marked Straighten-complete tick, notably calmer than
Chapter 14, with Chapter 14's spot drawn as the faint dashed ghost just behind it, the earlier
Straighten steps fainter still, and the Sort-complete milestone sitting further back again. The
needle must NOT reach the goal crosshair; the goal stays clearly ahead on the green side, because four
more S's (Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain) remain. Two of the six are now done, so this reads as
a real, earned arrival at a mark, but the road ahead is real too, and both are true at once. No
confetti, no completion glow, no swing all the way to calm.
