# B-Roll and Visual Notes: The Room Map

**Chapter 15, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-room-map
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero device is the room map: the homes gathered into a handful of zones by activity, drawn once and shared, so the whole space reads at a glance and belongs to everyone. Show it as an overhead or flattened floor plan with a few labeled zones blocked in, calm and legible.
- A zone is the secondary device: everything one activity needs, gathered into the place that activity happens, named by the verb, not the noun. Show it as a labeled boundary around a cluster of already-placed homes (coffee at the machine, launch pad at the door).
- The starting point is the finished, well-placed, self-explaining entryway from Chapters 12 to 14. A neat row of good, complete homes by the door: a labeled hook for keys, a labeled hook for the leash, a marked basket for bags, an umbrella stand, a tray marked out for mail. Each spot is clearly finished and clearly separate, with a little gap of blank wall between one and the next. Floating faintly above the row, in light pencil, a large question mark where a single connecting picture would sit.
- The homes are already assigned, already placed, and already speaking. The only action this chapter is gathering them into zones and drawing the whole arrangement as one shared picture. Do NOT stage re-choosing a home, re-placing by frequency, re-marking a home with a cue, or re-Sorting. Nothing moves house.
- The garden or museum "you are here" diagram is the everyday image: colored blocks, a friendly dot, the shape of the place readable in one glance. Home-scale it. The workshop overall-layout provenance is referenced lightly, not overclaimed.
- The friction meter REACHES A MILESTONE this chapter, and nothing leaves the house. The live needle arrives at a clearly marked tick labeled STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE, the second milestone on the dial after SORT COMPLETE, notably calmer than Chapter 14 but not touching the goal. Chapter 14's position becomes a faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle; the earlier Straighten steps and the Sort-complete milestone sit further back. The GOAL crosshair stays out on the green calm side, because four more S's remain (Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain).
- Warm daylight, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.
- Keep the whole thing calm and unhurried, and let it carry a note of quiet, earned pride, because this closes the second S. No force or violence framing. Nothing is purged or attacked. The tone is anti-shame: a private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a family's failing.

## CORE SHOT LIST

### Shot 1: Good separate spots, no picture (hero opener)
- The finished entryway seen all at once. A row of good, complete homes by the door: a labeled hook for keys, a labeled hook for the leash, a marked basket for bags, an umbrella stand, a tray marked out for mail. Every spot is finished and every spot is clearly separate, a little gap of blank wall between each. Floating faintly above the whole row, sketched in light pencil, a large question mark where a single connecting picture would sit.
- Critical detail: the parts are all here and all correct; they simply do not yet form one thing. The only place the connecting picture exists is inside the person who built them.
- Coverage: one wide of the finished, separate row; one slow push along the little gaps between good spots; one tilt up to the faint pencil question mark.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Every home is finished. Nothing yet ties them together."
- **Lower third:** Chapter 15 · The Room Map

### Shot 2: The picture lives behind your eyes (the turn)
- The owner moving through the entryway in easy certainty, sweeping past the row and gathering keys, leash, bag on the way out, as if the whole cluster were one thing. Then a newcomer in the same spot, meeting each good spot one at a time, the way you meet strangers at a party. Overlay a faint line quietly linking the keys, leash, bag, and umbrella into one group, visible only over the owner.
- The point: you know, without thinking, that these are really one cluster, the leaving-the-house cluster, because you are the one who leaves. Nobody else can see that grouping, because you never drew it. It is real, and it is invisible, and it belongs to you alone.
- **On-screen text moment:** "The view exists, but it exists in exactly one place, behind your eyes."
- **Lower third:** A private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a failing

### Shot 3: What a room map is (the you-are-here diagram)
- The little diagram by the entrance of a big garden or a museum: colored blocks, a friendly "you are here" dot, this wing the sculpture, that corner the cafe, the gardens out the back. Hold the whole layout in one glance. Then dissolve to a home-scaled echo: a flattened floor plan of a home with a few labeled zones blocked in (launch pad, coffee, homework, charging, mail-and-admin), calm and legible.
- The point: the diagram does not list every plant or painting; it shows the shape of the place, so you can find anything without being led by the hand. A room map is that diagram, shrunk to fit your home.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A room map shows the shape of the place, so the whole layout reads in one glance."
- **Lower third:** Grouped by what you do, not by what things are

### Shot 4: Zones, group by what you do (the coffee before-and-after)
- Split screen. On the left, organized by category: mugs on the crockery shelf with the plates, beans in the dry-goods cupboard with the flour, the grinder in a drawer with the blender, filters somewhere else. A hand makes a small circuit of the kitchen before the kettle boils. On the right, organized by activity: mug, beans, grinder, filters all gathered at the machine, and the circuit collapses to a single reach.
- The point: nothing was bought and nothing thrown out; the same objects simply gathered around the thing you actually do with them. That one spot is a zone, named by the verb, not the noun.
- **On-screen text moment:** "By category, coffee lives in four places. By activity, it collapses to a reach."
- **Lower third:** Name the zone by the verb, not the noun

### Shot 5: The entryway was a zone all along (the payoff)
- The entryway row from Shot 1, but now a single labeled boundary is drawn softly around the whole cluster, keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, shoes, and the label lands: "launch pad." The little gaps of blank wall between the good spots quietly close into one coherent thing. On the way out, a hand sweeps past and it hands over keys, leash, bag in one motion; on the way in, it catches them all back.
- The point: the row was never six separate spots. It was one zone all along, everything the leaving-and-arriving activity needs, clustered at the threshold. We built it home by home across three chapters; naming it as one zone is the last stitch.
- **On-screen text moment:** "It was one zone all along. The launch pad."
- **Lower third:** Six good homes become one thing you can name in a word

### Shot 6: Your home is a handful of zones (the zoom out)
- Move through the calm house and let a soft labeled boundary settle over each activity cluster in turn: the kitchen counter by the machine becomes the coffee zone; the end of the dining table becomes the homework corner; the drawer and power strip by the sofa become the charging station; the shelf near the front hall becomes the mail-and-admin spot. Five or six named blocks, no more.
- The point: your home is really just a handful of zones, each named for what happens there. You do not need dozens; you need the few your life actually runs on.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Five or six zones, each named for what happens there, describe almost any home."
- **Lower third:** The groups are already there. You just have not named them.

### Shot 7: Draw it, and share it
- A sheet of paper on the kitchen table. A hand sketches the rough shape of the home, no straighter than a napkin diagram, then blocks in the zones: a rectangle by the door labeled launch pad, a square at the counter labeled coffee, a patch at the table labeled homework. Then the household gathers over the same sheet. Someone points and pushes back (the charging station is wrong, nobody charges by the sofa, they charge by their beds), and the map gets better. Then the walk-through: standing in each zone in turn, saying its name out loud, a child putting a hand on the launch pad and returning the leash there without being told.
- The point: a map only counts when more than one person holds it. Drawing makes the map in your head visible; walking the household through it is what makes it shared. The sharing matters more than the drawing.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Draw it once with everyone. The sharing matters more than the drawing."
- **Lower third:** From your room that they live in to their room that they run

### Shot 8: The needle reaches the mark (close)
- The recurring friction meter, reaching a milestone. The live needle has moved past Chapter 14's position to a clearly marked tick on the dial labeled STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE, the second milestone after SORT COMPLETE, notably calmer than last chapter but not touching the goal. Chapter 14's needle position is a faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle; the earlier Straighten steps are fainter still, and the Sort-complete milestone sits further back as a small passed marker. - Emphasize the honesty and the quiet pride: nothing left the house today, yet the needle reaches the mark, because the homes gathered into shared zones and the room's logic became public. Two of the six S's are done. The goal is still ahead, because four S's remain.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Nothing was added or removed. The homes just gathered into shared zones." then "Straighten is complete. The goal is still ahead."
