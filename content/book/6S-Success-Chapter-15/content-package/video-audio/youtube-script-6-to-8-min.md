# YouTube Script: The Room Map

## COLD OPEN

[B-ROLL: Slow push along the finished, well-placed entryway seen all at once. A row of good, complete homes by the door: a labeled hook for keys, a labeled hook for the leash, a marked basket for bags, an umbrella stand, a tray marked out for mail. Every spot is finished, and every spot is clearly separate, a little gap of blank wall between each. Floating faintly above the row, in light pencil, a large question mark where a single connecting picture would sit. Warm daylight.]

[HOST, voice over]
Every keeper has a home. Every home sits where your hand reaches for it. And every one can explain itself now, so a friend locking up can put a thing back without asking. Three chapters of patient work are standing in that hallway. And yet, step back and squint, and the entryway is still a row of separate clever spots. Nothing says they belong to one another.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Every home is finished. Nothing yet ties them together.]

[HOST, on camera]
A newcomer meets them the way you meet strangers at a party, one at a time. You know, without thinking, that the keys and the leash and the bag are really one cluster, because you are the one who leaves the house. But nobody else can see that grouping, because you never drew it. The view exists, but it exists in exactly one place, behind your eyes. This chapter draws that view, gives it a name, and hands it around. Then the second S is done.

[VISUAL: Title card. "The Room Map." Lower third: Chapter 15.]

## SECTION 1: GOOD HOMES, NO SHARED PICTURE

[HOST]
Let me gather up what the last three chapters built, because it is the ground this one stands on.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: A private, in-your-head map is a natural stage, not a failing.]

[HOST, voice over]
We gave every keeper a single home. We placed each home by how often your hand goes there. We made each home speak. Three real gains, and none of them unwinds today. But notice the shape of all three: every one of them works on a home, one at a time. And a house is not experienced one hook at a time. You move through it in errands: leaving, making coffee, getting the kids out the door. Each errand touches several homes at once, and nothing so far has arranged the homes around the errands. They are arranged around themselves.

[HOST, on camera]
So the arrangement still lives as scattered pieces, and the thing that connects them lives in your head. And I want to say this plainly: that is not a failing. A map that lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach. It is where every well-run home sits right before this step. It is simply the last thing that has not been made shareable, and making it shareable is a small, satisfying job.

---

## SECTION 2: WHAT A ROOM MAP IS

[HOST]
Think of the little diagram by the entrance of a big garden or a museum.

[VISUAL: The garden or museum "you are here" board, colored blocks, a friendly dot, this wing the sculpture, that corner the cafe. Then dissolve to a flattened home floor plan with a few labeled zones blocked in: launch pad, coffee, homework, charging, mail-and-admin.]

[HOST, voice over]
The one with the colored blocks and the friendly "you are here" dot. It does not list every plant or painting. It shows you the shape of the place, so in one glance you hold the whole layout and can find anything without being led by the hand. A room map is that diagram, shrunk to fit your home. The idea comes, like much of Straighten, from the workshop floor, where a good shop keeps an overall layout that anyone can read.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: A room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there.]

[HOST, on camera]
Two words in that are doing the heavy lifting. One is shared, which we come to soon. The other is zones, which is how a scatter of separate homes becomes one readable thing. That is where we go next.

---

## SECTION 3: ZONES, GROUP BY WHAT YOU DO

[HOST]
Here is the move that turns pieces into a picture, and it is a small change in how you look.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Group by what you do, not by what things are.]

[VISUAL: Split screen. Left, by category: mugs with the plates, beans with the flour, grinder in a drawer, filters elsewhere, a hand making a small circuit of the kitchen. Right, by activity: mug, beans, grinder, filters all gathered at the machine, one reach.]

[HOST, voice over]
Most homes get organized by category. All the kitchen things in the kitchen cupboard. And it quietly fails you every day, because you do not live by category, you live by activity. Nobody thinks, time to interact with my kitchen things. They think, I want coffee, and then they need the mug, the beans, the grinder, and the filters. By category, the coffee lives in four places and making a cup is a small circuit of the kitchen. By activity, all four sit at the machine and the circuit collapses to a reach. Nothing was bought and nothing thrown out.

[HOST, on camera]
That one spot is a zone. A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens, so a task is served by one spot instead of scattered across the room. You name it by the verb, not the noun: the coffee zone. And the groups are already there; you just have not named them.

---

## SECTION 4: THE ENTRYWAY WAS A ZONE ALL ALONG

[HOST]
Now for the quiet reveal, the one four chapters have been building toward.

[B-ROLL: The entryway row, then a single soft labeled boundary drawn around the whole cluster, keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, shoes, and the label lands: "launch pad." The little gaps of blank wall close into one thing. A hand sweeps past on the way out and gathers keys, leash, bag in one motion.]

[HOST, voice over]
That row of homes by the door, the keys and the leash and the bag and the umbrella and the outgoing mail and the shoes, was never really six separate spots. It was one zone all along. It is the launch pad: everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs, clustered exactly where that activity happens. We built it one keeper at a time across three chapters. Naming it as one zone is the last stitch, the one that turns six good homes into a single thing a person can understand in a word.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Five or six zones, each named for what happens there, describe almost any home.]

[HOST, on camera]
And once you can see one zone, you can see the rest of the house the same way. The coffee zone at the machine. The homework corner at the end of the table. The charging station by the sofa. The mail-and-admin spot near the front hall. You do not need dozens. You need the few your life actually runs on.

---

## SECTION 5: DRAW IT, AND SHARE IT

[HOST]
A zone you can see in your mind is only half the job, because the other word was shared.

[B-ROLL: A hand sketching a napkin-simple floor plan on a sheet at the kitchen table, blocking in a few labeled zones. Then the household gathered over the same sheet, someone pointing and pushing back, a child putting a hand on the launch pad and returning the leash.]

[HOST, voice over]
Drawing it is almost absurdly simple. Take a sheet of paper, sketch the rough shape of your home no straighter than a napkin diagram, and block in the zones. You are not drafting a blueprint. You are making the map in your head visible. So do not draw it alone. Walk the household through it, and let them push back. Someone will tell you the charging station is in the wrong place, and they will be right, and the map will get better.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The sharing matters more than the drawing.]

[HOST, on camera]
The arrangement was yours. You chose it, you carried it, and everyone else lived as a guest inside a logic they never quite saw. Drawing the map together hands the design to the whole household, so it stops being your room that they live in and becomes their room that they run. And it costs nothing but a sheet of paper and ten honest minutes at the kitchen table.

## SECTION 6: THE NEEDLE REACHES THE MARK

[VISUAL: The friction meter. The live needle has moved past Chapter 14's position to a clearly marked tick labeled STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE, the second milestone after SORT COMPLETE, notably calmer than last chapter but not touching the goal. Chapter 14's position is a faint dashed ghost just behind; the earlier Straighten steps and the Sort-complete milestone sit further back. The GOAL crosshair still sits out on the green calm side.]

[HOST, voice over]
Go and look at the friction meter, because this time it reaches something. The needle does not just nudge, it arrives at a marked tick on the dial, the second milestone after Sort complete. Straighten is done. And, exactly as before, nothing left the house to earn it. The homes simply gathered into shared zones, so the room's logic stopped being a private thing and became a public one. Every task now sits in one spot, nobody carries the layout in their head as a standing duty, and the room reads at a glance.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Straighten is complete. The goal is still ahead.]

[HOST, on camera]
And now the honest part. Reaching the Straighten mark is a genuine milestone, not the finish line. Two of the six S's are done: the room is cleared and it is fully arranged. Four remain, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain, and their job is to clean what you have, protect it, and hold it steady over time.

## CLOSE AND SOFT CTA

[HOST]
Stand at the door one last time and take in the four chapters together.

[B-ROLL: A slow move from the shared map on the kitchen table to the calm whole house, each zone quietly named, then to the friction meter resting on the Straighten-complete tick.]

[HOST, voice over]
You gave every keeper a home, placed each where your hand wants it, taught each to speak, and today gathered them into zones and drew them into one shared picture. Sort emptied the room of what did not belong; Straighten arranged what stayed until the space almost runs itself. You did not buy a system or add an ounce of storage across these four chapters. A room whose arrangement is shared asks less of every person in it, including you, and it keeps asking less tomorrow.

[HOST, on camera]
So here is your job, and it is small on purpose. Pick one busy corner and name, in a single word, the activity that happens there. Gather everything that activity needs into that spot, and move out anything that belongs to a different activity. You just drew your first zone.

[HOST]
The full chapter is free in the online book. The room is cleared and arranged, but it is not yet cared for. Next chapter, Chapter 16 opens Shine, the third S, which cleans and cares for what remains, and turns the simple act of cleaning into a way of noticing what needs attention before it becomes a problem.
