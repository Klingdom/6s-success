## SHORT 1: The map lives behind your eyes
**Topic: the starting gap, good separate homes with no shared picture. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Your home might be perfectly organized and still only work when you are the one standing in it.

**Script:**
Every keeper has a home. Every home sits where your hand reaches for it. Each one can even explain itself now. And yet the entryway is still a row of separate clever spots. The keys have their hook, the leash has its hook, the bags have their basket, and nothing says they belong to one another. A newcomer meets them the way you meet strangers at a party, one at a time. You know, without thinking, that the keys and the leash and the bag are really one cluster, because you are the one who leaves the house. Nobody else can see that grouping, because you never drew it. The view exists, but it exists in exactly one place, behind your eyes. And here is the kind part: a map that lives in one person's head is a completely natural stage to reach. It is simply the last thing that has not been made shareable.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Perfectly organized. Still only works with you there."
- 14s: "The keys, leash, and bag are really one cluster."
- 28s: "The view lives in one place: behind your eyes."
- 38s: "A private map is a natural stage, not a failing."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The finished entryway row, each good spot separated by a little gap of blank wall, a faint pencil question mark floating above. Then a faint line links keys, leash, and bag into one cluster, visible only over the owner, invisible to the newcomer beside them. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 2: Group by what you do
**Topic: the hero rule and the coffee before-and-after. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Most homes are organized in a way that quietly fails you every single day.

**Script:**
Group by what you do, not by what things are. Most homes get organized by category. All the kitchen things in the kitchen cupboard. And it fails you every day, because you do not live by category, you live by activity. Nobody thinks, time to interact with my kitchen things. They think, I want coffee, and then they need the mug, the beans, the grinder, and the filters, four items a category-minded house scatters across three cupboards and a shelf. Organized by category, making a cup is a small circuit of the kitchen. Organized by activity, all four sit at the machine and the circuit collapses to a reach. Nothing was bought and nothing thrown out; the same objects just gathered around the thing you actually do. That one spot is a zone. Name it by the verb, not the noun. The coffee zone.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Group by what you do, not by what things are."
- 12s: "You do not live by category. You live by activity."
- 26s: "By category, coffee lives in four places."
- 36s: "By activity, it collapses to a reach. That is a zone."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Split screen. Left, by category: mugs with the plates, beans with the flour, grinder in a drawer, filters elsewhere, a hand making a small circuit of the kitchen. Right, by activity: mug, beans, grinder, filters gathered at the machine, one reach. Calm, warm light.

## SHORT 3: It was one zone all along
**Topic: the launch pad reveal. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The keys, leash, bag, and umbrella by your door were never six separate spots.

**Script:**
That row of homes by the door, the keys, the leash, the bag, the umbrella, the outgoing mail, the shoes, was never really six separate spots. It was one zone all along. It is the launch pad: everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs, clustered exactly where that activity happens, at the threshold between inside and out. On the way out, it hands you what you cannot leave without. On the way in, it catches all of it back. We built it one keeper at a time across three chapters. Naming it as one zone is the last stitch, the one that turns six good homes into a single thing you can understand in a word. And once you can see one zone, you can see the rest. Your home is really just a handful of them: coffee, homework, charging, mail-and-admin. Five or six zones describe almost any home.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Never six separate spots."
- 12s: "It was one zone all along. The launch pad."
- 28s: "Naming it is the last stitch."
- 38s: "Your home is really just a handful of zones."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The entryway row, then a single soft labeled boundary drawn around the whole cluster, the label "launch pad" landing, the gaps of blank wall closing into one thing. Then a soft boundary settles over the coffee counter, the homework table, the charging drawer. Warm, home-scaled.

## SHORT 4: Draw it, and share it
**Topic: the two small acts, and the reframe. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A map in your head fails the moment you step out the door.

**Script:**
A map that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person steps out. So do two small things. First, draw it. Take a sheet of paper, sketch the rough shape of your home no straighter than a napkin diagram, and block in the zones: launch pad by the door, coffee at the counter, homework at the table. You are not drafting a blueprint. You are making the map in your head visible. Second, and this matters more, walk the household through it. Stand in each zone, say its name out loud, and let people put their hands on it. A child who has walked the map once can return the leash to the launch pad without being told. The arrangement was yours. Drawing the map together makes it stop being your room that they live in and become their room that they run.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A map in your head fails the day you step out."
- 12s: "Draw it. Napkin-simple. Block in the zones."
- 28s: "Walk everyone through it. The sharing matters more."
- 38s: "Their room that they run, not your room they live in."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand sketching a napkin-simple floor plan on a sheet at the kitchen table, blocking in a few labeled zones. Then the household gathered over the same sheet, someone pointing, a child putting a hand on the launch pad and returning the leash. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 5: The needle reaches the mark
**Topic: the STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE milestone, honest. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Nothing left the house, and the second of the six S's just finished.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, because this time it reaches something. Three chapters of Straighten stepped the needle toward calm: a home for everything, each home placed by reach, each home able to speak. This chapter takes the last step, and the needle does not just nudge. It arrives at a marked tick on the dial, the second milestone after Sort complete. Straighten is done. And, exactly as before, nothing left the house to earn it. The homes simply gathered into shared zones, so the room's logic stopped being a private thing and became a public one. Now the honest part: this is a real milestone, not the finish line. Two of the six S's are done, the room is cleared and fully arranged. The goal is still ahead, because four remain: Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Nothing left the house. The second S just finished."
- 14s: "The needle reaches a marked tick: Straighten complete."
- 30s: "A real milestone, not the finish line."
- 40s: "Two of six done. Four remain."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The friction meter. The live needle reaches a clearly marked tick labeled STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE, the second milestone after SORT COMPLETE, notably calmer than Chapter 14. Chapter 14's position is a faint dashed ghost just behind; the earlier Straighten steps and the Sort-complete milestone sit further back; the GOAL crosshair still sits ahead on the green calm side.
