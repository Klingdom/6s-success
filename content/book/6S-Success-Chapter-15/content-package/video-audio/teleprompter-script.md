# Teleprompter Script: The Room Map

**Chapter 15, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-room-map
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come stand at the door with me one last time.

Every keeper has a home now.

Every home sits where your hand reaches for it.

And every one can explain itself.

Three chapters of patient work, and it all holds.

And yet, step back and squint.

The entryway is still a row of separate spots.

The keys have their hook.

The leash has its hook.

The bags have their basket.

Each one is finished and good.

But nothing says they belong to one another.

A newcomer meets them one at a time.

You know they are really one cluster.

The leaving-the-house cluster.

Because you are the one who leaves.

But nobody else can see it.

Because you never drew it.

The view exists.

It just exists behind your eyes.

And that is not a failing.

A map in one person's head is a natural stage.

It is simply the last thing to be shared.

So today, one job.

Gather the homes into a few groups.

Draw the grouping. And share it.

Nothing moves house.

Think of the map at a garden or a museum.

Colored blocks. A friendly "you are here" dot.

It shows you the shape of the place.

In one glance you hold the whole layout.

A room map is that diagram, shrunk to your home.

And here is the whole move.

Group by what you do, not by what things are.

Most homes get organized by category.

All the kitchen things in one cupboard.

And it fails you every day.

Because you do not live by category.

You live by activity.

Nobody thinks, time to use my kitchen things.

They think, I want coffee.

Then they need the mug, beans, grinder, filters.

By category, those live in four places.

By activity, all four sit at the machine.

The circuit collapses to a reach.

That one spot is a zone.

Name it by the verb, not the noun.

The coffee zone.

Now the quiet reveal.

That row by the door was never six spots.

It was one zone all along.

The launch pad.

Everything leaving and arriving needs.

Clustered right where that happens.

On the way out, it hands you what you need.

On the way in, it catches it all back.

Naming it as one zone is the last stitch.

And once you see one zone, you see them all.

Your home is really just a handful of them.

Coffee. Homework corner. Charging. Mail-and-admin.

Five or six zones describe almost any home.

But a zone in your mind is only half the job.

The other word was shared.

So first, draw it.

Take a sheet of paper.

Sketch the shape of your home.

No straighter than a napkin diagram.

Then block in the zones.

You are making the map in your head visible.

Second, and this matters more.

Walk the household through it.

Stand in each zone. Say its name out loud.

Let people put their hands on it.

A child who has walked the map once

can return the leash to the launch pad.

Without being told.

The arrangement was yours.

You chose it. You carried it.

Everyone else lived as a guest inside it.

Drawing the map together hands it around.

It stops being your room that they live in.

And becomes their room that they run.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Because this time it reaches something.

The needle does not just nudge.

It arrives at a marked tick on the dial.

The second milestone, after Sort complete.

Straighten is done.

And nothing left the house to earn it.

The homes just gathered into shared zones.

But be honest about what the meter still shows.

This is a real milestone, not the finish line.

Chapter 14's position is the faint dashed ghost.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

Because two of the six S's are done.

And four remain.

Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain.

So here is your job.

Pick one busy corner.

Name, in a single word, the activity there.

Gather everything that activity needs.

Move out anything that belongs elsewhere.

You just drew your first zone.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go and draw your first zone.
