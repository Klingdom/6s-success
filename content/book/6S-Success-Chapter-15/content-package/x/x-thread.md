2/
The last three chapters worked on one home at a time: a hook, a shelf, a bin, each made good on its own. But you move through a house in errands, and each errand touches several homes at once. Nothing yet arranges the homes around what you actually do.

(approx. 250 chars)

3/
"So the arrangement, good as it is, still lives as scattered pieces, and the thing that connects those pieces still lives in your head."

That head-held map is a natural stage, not a failing. It is simply the last thing not yet made shareable.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

4/
So we draw one view of the whole room.

"A room map is a single shared picture of a space, organized into zones by the activities that happen there, so anyone in the household can see where everything lives, and why, all at once."

(approx. 232 chars)

---

5/
The move that turns pieces into a picture is a small change in how you look:

"Group by what you do, not by what things are."

You do not live by category. You live by activity, so arrange the room around what you actually do.

(approx. 226 chars)

---

6/
Morning coffee, grouped by category, lives in four places: mug with the plates, beans with the flour, grinder with the blender, filters wherever. Grouped by activity, all four sit at the machine. Nothing bought, nothing tossed. The circuit becomes a reach.

(approx. 254 chars)

---

7/
"A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens, so a task is served by one spot instead of scattered across the room."

Name it by the verb: the coffee zone, not the cupboard of cups.

(approx. 217 chars)

---

8/
Now the quiet reveal. That row by the door, keys, leash, bag, umbrella, mail, shoes, was never six separate spots.

"It was one zone all along."

The launch pad: everything leaving and arriving needs, right at the threshold.

(approx. 222 chars)

---

9/
"Naming it as one zone is the last stitch, the one that turns six good homes into a single thing a person can understand in a word."

Once you see one zone, you see the rest: coffee, homework, charging, mail. A home is a handful of zones.

(approx. 234 chars)

---

10/
"Drawing it is almost absurdly simple."

Sketch the rough shape of a room, no straighter than a napkin diagram. Block in the zones: launch pad by the door, coffee at the counter, homework at the table. The map in your head, made visible.

(approx. 237 chars)

---

11/
"A map that lives in one person's head is a private map, and a private map fails the moment that person steps out. A map the whole household has drawn together is the one that finally makes the room belong to everyone who lives in it."

(approx. 234 chars)

---

12/
So do not draw it alone.

"The sharing matters more than the drawing, so if you do only one thing, do this: walk the household through it."

Stand in each zone, say its name, let people put their hands on it.

(approx. 206 chars)

---

13/
The reframe:

"Drawing the map together hands the design to the whole household, so it stops being your room that they live in and becomes their room that they run."

The arrangement was yours. Now it belongs to everyone.

(approx. 216 chars)
