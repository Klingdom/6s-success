# Facebook Longform Post: The Map That Lived in My Head

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 15 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the step that completes Straighten. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes.

---

By my front door, every keeper finally had a home. The keys on their hook, the leash beside them, the bag in its basket, the umbrella in its stand, the mail in its tray. Each spot was chosen, placed, and able to explain itself. I was quietly proud of it.

Then a friend came to lock up while we were away, stood in the entryway, and I realized something. She was meeting a row of separate clever spots, one at a time. There was no single view of that corner, nothing she could take in at a glance and understand. The view existed, but it lived in exactly one place: behind my eyes.

That is the last small gap in arranging a home. The homes are right, the placement is right, the cues are right. What is missing is a way to see the whole arrangement at once, and a way to lift that view out of your head and into everyone else's.

The move that closes it is smaller than it sounds. You stop grouping by category and start grouping by activity.

"Group by what you do, not by what things are."

Nobody stands in the kitchen thinking, time to interact with my kitchen things. They think, I want coffee, and reach for the mug, the beans, the grinder, and the filters. Gather those four at the machine and you have a coffee zone. Do that for the handful of things your household actually does, and the room becomes a short list of zones anyone can name.

Which is when the reveal landed for me. That row by the door was never six separate spots. It was one zone all along, the launch pad, everything the single activity of leaving and arriving needs.

So I drew it. A napkin sketch of the rooms, the zones blocked in, then a walk through it with everyone at home. The map stopped being mine and started being ours.

Here is your low-pressure homework for the comments. Pick one busy corner, name in a single word the activity that happens there, and gather what it needs into that spot. You just drew your first zone.
