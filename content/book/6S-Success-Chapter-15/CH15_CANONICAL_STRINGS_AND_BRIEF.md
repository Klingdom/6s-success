## Hero device (frozen): the room map (zones + a shared picture)
Individual homes are good, but until they cohere into an overall logic and that logic is shared, the arrangement still lives as scattered pieces in one person's head. The room map is the whole-room view: the homes gathered into a few zones by activity, drawn once and shared, so the entire space reads at a glance and belongs to everyone.
> **The rule (frozen):** Group by what you do, not by what things are.

## Zones (frozen framing)
A zone gathers everything one activity needs into the place that activity happens. Group by the verb, not the noun: a launch pad by the door (keys, leash, bag, umbrella, outgoing mail, shoes), a coffee station at the machine (mug, beans, grinder, filters), a homework corner, a charging station, a mail-and-admin spot. The room stops being a scatter of clever individual homes and becomes a handful of zones anyone can name.

## The entryway was a zone all along (frozen payoff)

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> Group the homes into a few zones by what you do, and draw the map once with everyone, so the arrangement stops living in your head and starts living in the whole household. A room whose logic is shared is a room that runs itself, which is exactly where Straighten set out to go.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one busy corner and name, in a single word, the activity that happens there. Gather everything that activity needs into that spot, and move out anything that belongs to a different activity. You just drew your first zone.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the house together and give each area a one-word job: launch pad, homework, coffee, charging. Then sketch a simple map of where those zones sit. When everyone can point to the same picture, the map is shared.
- **6S Tip:** Zone by the verb, not the noun. A coffee zone (mug, beans, grinder, filters, all at the machine) beats a "kitchen things" cupboard, because you live by what you are doing, not by what category a thing happens to fall in.
- **Common Mistake:** Keeping the map in your own head and expecting the household to absorb it. If you never draw it and never walk everyone through it, you are still the map, and the room still stops working the day you are away.
