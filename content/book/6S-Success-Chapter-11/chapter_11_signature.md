- **Donate is the default (frozen):** Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.
- **The sell trap (frozen):** Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.
- **The store trap (frozen):** Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.
- **The throw reframe (frozen, sunk-cost callback to Ch 9):** Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** the five doors Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw / the out box (from Chapter 9). The out box is already decided; today you only route it.

## Hero device: The Five Doors Out
Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A quick question picks the door. The bias is deliberate and strong: most of the box goes through Donate, and Recycle takes most of the rest. Sell and Store are narrow side doors kept for the rare item that truly earns them. This is the disposition step of Sort, the home-scaled version of the Lean red-tag disposition, where dispositioned items are returned, relocated, sold, recycled, or scrapped. Reference lightly, do not overclaim.

Routing cascade (frozen order):
- Broken or truly unusable? → Recycle it if you can, otherwise Throw it.
- Genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell? → Sell (with a deadline).
- Genuinely needed later, just not now? → Store (labeled, dated, elsewhere).
- Still useful to someone? → Donate. This is where most of the box goes.

## Motifs
- **The best door is the one that empties the box.** A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Pick the door that gets it out of the house.
- **Two fast lanes, two slow doors.** Donate and Recycle clear the box quickly; Sell and Store are worth it only for the rare item that earns the extra work, each with a guardrail.
- **Not deciding in disguise.** The someday sale and the just-in-case storage both feel responsible and both keep the clutter while you tell yourself you dealt with it.
- **The needle moves.** The out box physically leaves the building, so the last of the Sort clutter is gone from the house. The meter takes its last Sort step to a marked Sort-complete milestone. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain.

## Emotional purpose
Permission to finish cleanly and without shame. You are not wasteful for giving something away; someone else gets the use. Nobody has to sell anything. Throwing a worn thing out is not the waste, because the waste, if any, happened at the store, not at the bin. The chapter protects the reader from the two responsible-feeling traps (the someday sale, the just-in-case storage) that quietly keep the clutter, and it gives the honest satisfaction of a first S actually finished.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to route an already-decided out box: run each item through the quick cascade, send most of it through Donate and Recycle, and reserve Sell (with a sell-by deadline) and Store (labeled and dated, elsewhere) for the rare item that earns them. Get the box out of the house in one trip. Do NOT re-decide necessary vs unnecessary (Ch 9), re-run red tags (Ch 10), or arrange the keepers (Ch 12).
