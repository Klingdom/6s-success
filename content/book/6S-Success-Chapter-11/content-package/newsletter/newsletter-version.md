# Newsletter Edition: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

**Subject line (placeholder):** Five doors out, and the box leaves today

**Preview text (placeholder):** The out box is already decided. Today you just pick each door.

---

Hi friend,

Come back to the entryway one last time for Sort. The console you cleared is still holding its five working things. The overflow basket is empty. And in the corner, full and settled, sits the out box, every item in it already judged unnecessary, waiting quietly for somewhere to go. That box is today's whole job, and it is a good one, because there is no more deciding left in it.

That last part is worth pausing on, because it changes the mood of the work. Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. What is left is the second, and it is smaller and calmer than it looks. You are not weighing attachment or use anymore. You are asking each thing one plain question: what is the cleanest way for you to leave? Answer that a handful of times and the corner is clear.

So this issue is a routing issue, and it is the last one in Sort. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, and it helps to see them named together: **Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw.** A short cascade picks the door for each item. Run each one down it and stop at the first door that fits.

- Broken or truly unusable? Then **Recycle** it if you can, and **Throw** it if you cannot.
- Genuinely valuable, and worth your time to sell? Then **Sell** it, with a deadline.
- Genuinely needed later, just not now? Then **Store** it, labeled and dated, out of the way.
- Still useful to someone? Then **Donate** it. This is where most of the box goes.

Read the order twice, because it is doing quiet work. The two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and the bottom, and between them they take the great majority of anything you route. The two doors in the middle, Sell and Store, are narrow on purpose. They are not bad doors, but they are slow ones, and a thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today; it takes a to-do with it.

Start with the door most of your box will take, and let me say plainly why it is the default.

**Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.**

Look at what that protects you from. The alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it, because you already decided you do not use it. The alternative is holding it to sell later, or to give to the perfect person eventually, or to find just the right charity. Each of those feels more careful than a plain donation, and each is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about your intentions. Donating asks nothing of the future. The bag goes to a place that will pass it on, and the transaction is over the same day. And the small guilt that sometimes shows up here, the sense that giving something away is a kind of waste, is worth waving off: the thing is not being destroyed, it is being handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite of waste.

Now the two slow doors, the ones to use sparingly. Both are legitimate, and each is exactly right for a certain rare item, but both keep the thing in your house while you tell yourself you have dealt with it. So each gets a guardrail. Here is the one for selling.

**Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.**

The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it. And here is the guardrail for storing.

**Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.**

A locker full of things you already decided you do not use is the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

That leaves Recycle and Throw, for the things nobody can use. Recycle is the second fast lane: paper, cardboard, old cables, a dead charger, a small broken gadget, all pointed at the right stream instead of the landfill. And Throw is the last door, the smallest, and the one people flinch at. Here the only real obstacle is a feeling, so let me take it apart. Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. The money was spent long ago, at the moment of purchase, and keeping the ruined object in a drawer does not bring one cent of it back. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.

Then carry the box out, drop the donation bag where it goes, put the recycling in its bins, and come back to the empty corner. And this time, go look at the friction meter, because it moves. For three chapters the needle held, because understanding is not the same as an emptier room. This issue the out box did not get tidied or relabeled. It left the building. So the needle steps forward, off its held position and onto a clearly marked tick on the dial: **Sort complete.** Sit with that, because it is a real milestone. The first of the six S's is done. What did not belong and what you do not need are both gone from the house now, not shuffled to another corner of it. The room is genuinely lighter, not merely arranged to look that way. The goal crosshair is still ahead, because five S's remain, but the room is lighter, not just tidier.

**To try this week:** Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes. And one small trick: put that donation bag in your car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked; a bag in the car gets dropped off.
