# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 11. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36, ghost needle #B9AC96. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader. Footer strip: DONATE · SELL · STORE · RECYCLE · THROW.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**The out box is full. Now it just needs to leave.**

Every thing in it has already been decided. Not one item is still in question.

Swipe for the five doors out, and the two that finish the job fastest.

[VISUAL] The full out box by the front door, its contents flowing toward five labeled doors: a wide green DONATE door, a honey RECYCLE door, two narrow slate SELL and STORE doors, and a small soft THROW door. A terracotta donation bag ready by the door, keys beside it. Warm paper, slate chapter label.

---

### Slide 2

**You are here to route.**

Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first.

Today is smaller and calmer than it looks. You are not here to decide anything today.

[VISUAL] A settled slate out box on paper ground, tagged "already decided." A soft rule reads "not another round of hard calls, just routing."

---

### Slide 3

**Five doors out, and only five.**

Donate · Sell · Store · Recycle · Throw. That is the whole set. Nothing bound for out needs a sixth option.

A quick question picks the door for each item.

[VISUAL] The five doors named together in a row, sized by how much of the box each takes: a wide green DONATE, a honey RECYCLE, two narrow slate SELL and STORE, a small soft THROW. Footer: "most of the box goes through two of them."

---

### Slide 4

**Stop at the first door that fits.**

Broken or truly unusable? Recycle, or Throw. Genuinely valuable and worth the work? Sell, with a deadline. Genuinely needed later? Store, labeled and dated. Still useful to someone? Donate.

That is the whole method.

[VISUAL] A vertical cascade of honey question diamonds, each with a soft exit arrow to its door. The "still useful?" branch runs to a wide green DONATE door, drawn largest, tagged "most of the box."

---

### Slide 5

**Two doors take most of the box.**

Donate and Recycle sit at the top and bottom of the cascade and carry the great majority of anything you route.

Sell and Store are narrow on purpose. A thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today; it takes a to-do with it.

[VISUAL] The wide green DONATE door and the honey RECYCLE door drawn open as the fast lanes; the slate SELL and STORE doors drawn nearly shut. Soft caption: "fast lanes wide, slow doors narrow."

---

### Slide 6

**Donate is the default.**

Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.

The transaction is over the same day. No listing to write, no buyer to meet. It is the exit that actually completes.

[VISUAL] A wide green DONATE door with a donation bag passing through it, a single green calm-dot beside a small tag: "the exit that actually completes."

---

### Slide 7

**You are not throwing value away.**

You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

The thing is not being destroyed; it is being handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite of waste.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A soft green glow, no sparks. Small tag: "the opposite of waste."

---

### Slide 8

**Selling turns clearing into a part-time job.**

Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.

The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it.

[VISUAL] A narrow slate SELL door with a sell-by tag: "sell only what earns it, then the donate bag." No sparks; slate does the work.

---

### Slide 9

**Storing is not deciding.**

Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.

A rented locker full of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

[VISUAL] A narrow slate STORE door with a labeled, dated box tag. Small caption: "label it, date it, and route everything else."

---

### Slide 10

**Some things are simply worn out.**

Throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store.

Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

[VISUAL] The honey RECYCLE door taking paper and e-waste, and a small soft THROW door for the truly finished. No sparks; the frame is plain, not scolding.

---

### Slide 11

**The needle takes its last Sort step.**

The out box did not get tidied or relabeled. It left the building, so the meter moves to a clearly marked tick: Sort complete.

The first S is done and the room is genuinely lighter. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain.

[VISUAL] The friction meter, moved. The live needle has stepped off Chapter 10's held position onto a marked "Sort complete" tick; Chapter 10's old position is now a faint dashed ghost needle behind it. The GOAL crosshair still ahead on the calm side. Footer: "the meter only moves when the room does, and today it did."

---

### Slide 12 (soft CTA)

**The best destination is the one that actually empties the box.**

A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished.
