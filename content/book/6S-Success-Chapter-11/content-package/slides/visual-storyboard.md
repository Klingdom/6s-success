# Visual Storyboard

## What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 11. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

---

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_11_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper #F7F2E9: slide and card backgrounds
- Panel #FBF7EF: card fill, definition and idea boxes
- Ink #2B2622: primary text, object and door outlines
- Soft #6A625A: captions, secondary labels, the small Throw door
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: the chapter kicker, the donation bag headed to the car
- Honey #DDA63A: the Recycle door, the neutral cascade question, the middle of the meter
- Slate #3C5A6B: chapter labels, the two slow doors Sell and Store, the GOAL flag
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: the wide Donate door, the completed outcome, the cleared spot
- Friction spark #CB4B36: the friction side of the meter only
- Ghost needle #B9AC96: the faint dashed ghost of Chapter 10's held position
- Rules and hairlines #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8: dividers, dashed lines
- Display font Fraunces: titles, pull quotes, door labels, column headers
- Body font Newsreader: worked pass, captions, running text
- Label font Inter: uppercase, wide letter-spacing, for kickers, tags, axis labels

**The recurring motif.** The hero object of the chapter is The Five Doors Out: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A wide green #6E8B5B Donate door and a honey #DDA63A Recycle door are the two fast lanes and take most of the box. Two narrow slate #3C5A6B doors, Sell and Store, are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that earns them. A small soft #6A625A Throw door takes only what is truly finished. Green marks the default and the completed outcome; slate marks the slow doors and the GOAL flag; honey stays warm and neutral, carrying the Recycle door, the cascade question, and the middle of the meter; terracotta is spent only on the chapter kicker and the donation bag. The red spark #CB4B36 belongs to the friction side of the meter only.

**Two fences to hold visually.** First, this is routing, not deciding: never redraw the Use Test or the red tags, because the out box is already decided and Chapter 11 only sends each thing through a door. Second, do not arrange the keepers or give them homes: the keepers stay where they landed, and giving them proper places is Part 4, Chapter 12. Keep the focus on the out box leaving, not on the keepers.

## Beat 1 · The five doors out

The chapter and both decks open the same way: the full out box by the front door, its contents flowing toward five labeled doors, with a deliberate bias toward the fast two.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm daylight. The entryway out box sits on the floor by the front door, full and settled. From it, a soft stream of items flows toward five labeled doors set into the wall. Most of the stream pours through a wide green #6E8B5B DONATE door, and a good share turns toward a honey #DDA63A RECYCLE door. Two narrow slate #3C5A6B side doors, SELL and STORE, sit almost closed, one small item hesitating at each. A small soft #6A625A THROW door takes only what is clearly finished. A terracotta #BC4B2A donation bag stands ready by the door, keys beside it, headed for the car.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, ink #2B2622 outlines, green on the wide Donate door, honey on the Recycle door, slate on the two narrow doors, soft on the small Throw door, terracotta on the donation bag. The room already lighter behind the box.
- Motif: five doors out, a deliberate lean toward the fast two, and a box that is about to leave the house.
- Caption line, from the book: "Five doors out, and most of the box goes through two of them. Donate takes the usable things, Recycle takes the worn ones, and the box leaves in a single trip."

## Beat 2 · Route, do not decide

The line that reframes the whole chapter, given the book's pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."
- Beneath it, a small Inter tag: "deciding and routing are two separate acts, and the first is done."
- Palette: no dots, no doors. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the sentence that settles the reader.
- Point: deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it goes are two separate acts, and Chapter 9 finished the first. What is left is smaller and calmer than it looks.

## Beat 3 · The Five Doors cascade

The chapter's hero device and the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: the five doors named together, sized by how much of the box each takes, with the quick cascade running down beside them. Four honey #DDA63A question diamonds in order: "Broken or truly unusable?" to a honey RECYCLE door and a small soft #6A625A THROW door; "Genuinely valuable, worth the work?" to a narrow slate #3C5A6B SELL door; "Genuinely needed later?" to a narrow slate STORE door; "Still useful to someone?" to a wide green #6E8B5B DONATE door, drawn largest.
- The bias, drawn: Donate is the wide default lane and Recycle is the second fast lane; Sell and Store are narrow side doors, nearly shut. Tag the Donate door in Inter "most of the box."
- Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "Stop at the first door that fits."
- This is the clearest single frame of the routing logic. Do not paraphrase the door names "Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw," nor the cascade order. The frozen definition may sit beneath: "Routing the out box means sending each unnecessary thing out through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and choosing the door that actually gets it gone. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes and take most of the box; Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them."

## Beat 4 · Donate is the default

The anchor line of the chapter, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months."
- Beneath, a single green calm-dot #6E8B5B and a small Inter tag: "the exit that actually completes."
- Palette: green marks the default door and the completed outcome; otherwise slate does the work. No sparks.
- Tone note: donating asks nothing of the future. The bag goes to a place that will pass the things on, and the transaction is over the same day. It is the door most of the box should take.

---

## Beat 5 · Giving it away is not waste

The anti-shame center of the Donate section, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the reframe in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain."
- Beneath, a small Inter tag: "the opposite of waste."
- Palette: a soft green #6E8B5B glow, no sparks, no scolding. This beat should feel like permission.
- Tone note: the thing is not being destroyed; it is being handed to someone who wants it. Someone who needs a second umbrella gets one, and you get your corner back, both now rather than eventually. Keep the frame light.

---

## Beat 6 · The two slow doors: Sell and Store

The two middle doors, paired so their guardrails read together. The sell trap and the store trap.

- Layout: two narrow slate #3C5A6B doors side by side on paper ground, each with its guardrail drawn as a small tag. Left, the SELL door with a sell-by tag: "sell only what earns it, then the donate bag." Right, the STORE door with a labeled, dated box tag: "label it, date it, never rent space for what you do not need."
- The two frozen lines carry the beat. Sell trap: "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date." Store trap: "Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need."
- Palette: slate on both doors, because both are slow and both take a to-do with them; soft #6A625A on the guardrail tags. Paper ground, no verdict colors.
- Motif: both doors are legitimate for the rare item that earns them, and both are easy to over-use because both feel responsible. The guardrail is what keeps each honest: a deadline on selling, a label and a date on storing.
- Fence: draw the slow doors as narrow and nearly shut, never as wide as Donate. The bias toward the fast lanes must stay visible.

---

## Beat 7 · Recycle and Throw

The two doors for the broken and the finished, and the sunk-cost reframe that quiets the guilt at the bin. Callback to Chapter 9.

- Composition: the honey #DDA63A RECYCLE door as the second fast lane, with a paper-and-cardboard stream and an e-waste stream (old cables, a dead charger, a small gadget) flowing into it; beside it the small soft #6A625A THROW door for the truly finished. Below, the pull-quote treatment carries the reframe.
- The frozen reframe, in Fraunces medium #2B2622 on panel #FBF7EF with slate #3C5A6B rules: "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done."
- Palette: honey on the Recycle door, soft on the small Throw door, no sparks. Keep the frame plain, not scolding.
- Motif: do the responsible thing where it is easy, and let go of the guilt that clings to the bin. The money was spent long ago, at the store; keeping the ruined object does not bring a cent of it back. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

## Beat 8 · The routing pass on the out box

The worked example, five items, kept short. Use it as a copyable template.

- Layout: the five out-box items in a row on paper ground, each mapped by a soft arrow to its door. Two umbrellas and a handful of tote bags to a wide green #6E8B5B DONATE door. Old takeout menus, a dead charging cable, some torn bags, and a spare key to the sold car to a honey #DDA63A RECYCLE door, the key carrying a small soft #6A625A "or Throw" branch. The two narrow slate SELL and STORE doors sit shut and empty.
- Tally box, Newsreader italic slate #3C5A6B: "Donate and Recycle took the whole box, one trip, nothing sold and nothing stored."
- Palette: green on the Donate door, honey on the Recycle door, soft on the small Throw branch, slate on the two empty slow doors. Paper ground.
- Note: keep it to these five items only. The pass is short on purpose; that is the normal shape of an out box, and it is why Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes the cascade leans on.

## Beat 9 · Sort complete, the friction meter moves

The landmark payoff, and the close of both decks. Rebuild from the HTML SVG, and make the MOVE unmistakable: the needle steps forward off Chapter 10's held position and onto a clearly marked "Sort complete" tick, with the old position now a dashed ghost and the goal still ahead.

- Composition: the semicircular friction meter. The arc runs green #6E8B5B (CALM) on the left, honey #DDA63A in the middle, red spark #CB4B36 (FRICTION) on the right.
- The move: the live needle has stepped off Chapter 10's held position and onto a clearly marked tick, labeled in ink #2B2622 "Sort complete," a real step further toward calm. This is the fulfillment of Chapter 10's dashed projection: the out box physically left the building, so the needle actually steps this time.
- The ghost: Chapter 10's old held position is drawn just behind the live needle as a faint dashed ghost needle #B9AC96, showing how far the needle came. It is the ground covered, not where the needle sits now.
- The GOAL marker: a slate #3C5A6B crosshair on the calm side, still further ahead of the live needle, carrying its slate PURPOSE flag, because five S's remain (Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain). Mark the Sort-complete milestone tick clearly on the dial, distinct from the green goal further on.
- Footer caption, verbatim: "The needle takes its last Sort step, because the out box left the building. The first S is done: what does not belong and what you do not need are both gone from the house now. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain, but the room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier."
- Motif close: a real milestone MOVE that finishes the first S, honest and not triumphant. The dashed ghost shows the ground covered; the goal ahead shows there is more to come.

## Beat 10 · Sort is finished

The close of the chapter and the soft handoff, given the One Idea to Keep pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the empty corner on paper ground, warm and light. The console holds its five working things, the overflow basket is empty, and a green calm-dot #6E8B5B marks the cleared spot where the out box used to sit. On a shelf out of the way, a single dated tag still runs out its clock.
- The frozen One Idea to Keep, in Fraunces medium #2B2622 on panel #FBF7EF with slate #3C5A6B rules: "The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished."
- A forward slate #3C5A6B arrow leads to a "CH 12" tab. Footer, from the book: "Next: Chapter 12 · Straighten: A Place for Everything. Only the keepers remain, sitting where they landed. Part 4 stops removing and starts arranging."
- Fence: do not arrange the keepers here. They stay exactly where the pass left them; giving them homes is the next part. This beat only closes Sort.

## Carrying it across the set
