# Chapter 11 Key Takeaways: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **This chapter closes Sort.** It is the fourth and final chapter of Part Three. By the end the corner is empty, the first of the six S's is genuinely done, and the only things left in the room are the ones you chose to keep.

2. **This is a routing chapter, not a deciding chapter.** Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route.

3. **Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors.** Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. That is the whole set. Nothing bound for out needs a sixth option.

4. **A quick cascade picks the door.** Broken or truly unusable, then Recycle it if you can and Throw it if you cannot. Genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell, then Sell it with a deadline. Genuinely needed later, just not now, then Store it labeled and dated. Still useful to someone, then Donate it. Stop at the first door that fits.

5. **The bias is deliberate and strong.** The two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom of the cascade and take the great majority of anything you route. Sell and Store are narrow on purpose, because a thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today; it takes a to-do with it.

6. **Donate is the default.** Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months. It is the exit that actually completes: the transaction is over the same day, with no listing to write and no buyer to meet.

7. **Donating a usable thing is not waste.** The thing is not being destroyed; it is being handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite of waste. You are moving value to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

8. **The sell trap: selling turns clearing into a part-time job.** Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date. The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it.

9. **The store trap: storing is not deciding.** Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need. A rented locker full of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

10. **Recycle is the second fast lane.** It is the door for the worn and the electronic: paper to paper recycling, and old cables, dead chargers, and small broken gadgets to an e-waste drop-off rather than the landfill. Do the responsible thing where it is easy, because so much of a worn-out box qualifies for it.

11. **Throw away without guilt.** Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. The money was spent long ago, and keeping the ruined object does not bring a cent of it back. Recycle what you can, bin the rest, and be done.

12. **The someday sale and the just-in-case storage are ways of not finishing.** Both feel responsible and both keep the clutter in your life while you tell yourself you dealt with it. That is why the guardrails matter.

13. **The normal shape of an out box is Donate and Recycle.** In the worked pass, two umbrellas and a handful of tote bags go through Donate; menus, a dead cable, some torn bags, and a useless key go through Recycle. Nothing was worth selling, nothing needed storing, and the whole box left in one trip.

14. **The friction meter takes its last Sort step.** The out box did not get tidied or relabeled; it left the building. So the needle steps off its held position and onto a clearly marked tick: Sort complete. Chapter 10's held position becomes a faint dashed ghost showing how far the needle came.

15. **The milestone is real, and the journey is not over.** The first S is done and the room is genuinely lighter, not merely arranged to look that way. But the goal crosshair on the green calm side is still ahead, because five S's remain: Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain. The meter marks the milestone plainly and refuses to oversell it.

16. **Sorting decided what stays; it did not decide where any of it lives.** The keepers are still sitting where they landed, without homes of their own. A keeper without a place is just tomorrow's clutter waiting to happen, which is why the work now turns from removing to arranging in Part Four (Chapter 12, Straighten).
