## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 11 is the fourth and final chapter of Part Three, and it closes Sort. It does not re-open whether anything belongs in the box; that was Chapter 9, and it is done. Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it goes are two separate acts, and only the second remains. So the work is smaller and calmer than it looks: route each already-decided thing through the right exit, and the corner is clear.

Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw, and a short cascade sorts them almost automatically: broken then Recycle or Throw, valuable and worth the work then Sell, needed later then Store, still useful then Donate. The order does quiet work, because the two fast doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom and take the great majority of anything routed. Donate is the default because it is the exit that actually completes, same day, with nothing left to do; donating a usable thing is not waste, it moves value to where it will finally get used.

Sell and Store are the slow doors, legitimate for the rare item but easy to over-use because both feel responsible. Each gets a guardrail: a sell-by deadline on selling, a label and date on storing, and never rent space for what you do not need. Recycle and Throw handle the worn and finished, with the sunk-cost reframe that the waste happened at the store. A short routing pass empties the entryway box, and the friction meter steps to Sort complete.
