# Chapter 11 Quotes: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

Pull quotes lifted directly from the manuscript and final HTML, ready for quote cards, social posts, and graphics. Every line below was grep-verified as present in chapter_11_manuscript.md. All are verbatim or lightly trimmed. Nothing here is invented.

## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."

2. "Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first."

3. "Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months."

4. "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain."

5. "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date."

6. "The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it."

7. "Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need."

8. "Store the genuine later-use items, labeled and dated, and route everything else."

9. "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done."

10. "There is no virtue in living among broken objects."

## The five doors (frozen)

"Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw."

## Definition box (canonical)

"Routing the out box means sending each unnecessary thing out through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and choosing the door that actually gets it gone. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes and take most of the box; Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them."

## One Idea to Keep (canonical close)

"The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished."

## Quick Win (canonical)

"Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"Put the donation bag in your car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked; a bag in the car gets dropped off."

## Common Mistake (canonical)

"The someday sale and the just-in-case storage. Both feel responsible and both are ways of not finishing, because they keep the clutter in your life while you tell yourself you dealt with it."

## Family Challenge (canonical)

"Everyone picks one thing they have been meaning to sell someday and says honestly whether they will do the work this week. If not, it goes in the donate bag. Nobody has to sell anything; everybody has to be honest about it."

## The friction-meter caption (for graphics)

"The needle takes its last Sort step, because the out box left the building. The first S is done: what does not belong and what you do not need are both gone from the house now. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain, but the room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier."

## The Five Doors Out (for graphics)

The hero device: every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A quick cascade picks the door, and the bias is deliberate: Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes and take most of the box, while Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them.
