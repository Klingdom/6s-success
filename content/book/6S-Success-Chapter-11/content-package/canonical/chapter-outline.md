## 1. Chapter Opening: The box is decided, today it leaves
The out box is full, settled, and every single thing in it has already been decided. Over the last three chapters you emptied the entryway of what did not belong, tested what was left against whether you actually use it, and gave the honest maybes a tag and a deadline. What settled to the bottom is the out box: things already judged unnecessary, ready to go somewhere. You are not here to decide anything today; you are here to route. Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. This is a routing chapter, and it is the last one in Sort. By the end the corner is empty and the first of the six S's is genuinely done.

## 2. What You Will Learn (objectives)
- Route an already-decided out box through five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw
- Run each item through a quick cascade that picks its door in a second
- Send most of the box through the two doors that finish quickest, Donate and Recycle
- Keep Sell and Store for the rare item that earns them, with a deadline and a label
- Carry the whole box out in a single trip, cleanly and without guilt

## 3. The Last Loose End
Chapter 10 closed with the entryway almost done and one thing left standing in the corner: the out box, full and quiet, holding the things you had already released. Keep the fences honest, the same way we have all through Sort. We are not reopening whether anything belongs in the box; that was Chapter 9. We are not re-running the red tags; the tagged maybes stay on their timers, and if one expires untouched it drops into an out box and routes exactly the way today's items route. And we are not giving the keepers homes; that is the whole of the next part. Today has one narrow task: take the things already bound for out, and send each one through the right door. Because the hard part is behind you, routing is quick; you are only asking what is the cleanest way for this thing to leave.

## 4. The Five Doors
Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, named together: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. Run each item down the cascade and stop at the first door that fits: broken or truly unusable, then Recycle it if you can and Throw it if you cannot; genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell, then Sell it with a deadline; genuinely needed later, just not now, then Store it labeled and dated; still useful to someone, then Donate it, which is where most of the box goes. The order does quiet work: the two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom and take the great majority of anything routed. Sell and Store are narrow on purpose, not bad doors but slow ones, and a thing that goes through either takes a to-do with it. The bias is deliberate and strong.
- Definition box (Routing the out box): Routing the out box means sending each unnecessary thing out through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and choosing the door that actually gets it gone. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes and take most of the box; Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them.

## 5. Donate Is the Default
Start here, because most of your box ends here. A thing that still works, that someone could use tomorrow, has an easy and honorable exit: you give it to someone who will use it. The alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it; it is holding onto it to sell later, or to give to the perfect person eventually, each of which is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about your intentions. Donating asks nothing of the future: the bag goes to a place that will pass things on, and the transaction is over the same day. It is the exit that actually completes. And the small guilt that giving is a kind of waste is answered plainly: the thing is not destroyed, it is handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite of waste.
- Pull-quote (frozen): Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.
- Callout (Quick Win): Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes.
- Callout (6S Tip): Put the donation bag in your car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked; a bag in the car gets dropped off.

## 6. The Two Slow Doors: Sell and Store
The two middle doors, to use sparingly. Sell and Store are both legitimate and each is right for a certain rare item, but both are slow, both keep the thing in your house while you tell yourself you have dealt with it, and both are easy to over-use because both feel responsible. So each gets a guardrail. Selling is work: photographs, a listing, a price, messages, a no-show, a relisting; worth it for a hundred-dollar item and a waste of a week for a five-dollar one. Only genuinely valuable things go through this door, and every one gets a sell-by date; if it has not sold by then, it goes straight into the donate bag. Store has the same shape: winter coats in July, camping gear you truly use, things you genuinely need later. The guardrail is a label and a date, and the harder rule, worth reading twice: never pay to store what you do not need.
- Pull-quote (frozen, Sell): Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.
- Pull-quote (frozen, Store): Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.
- Callout (Common Mistake): The someday sale and the just-in-case storage. Both feel responsible and both are ways of not finishing, because they keep the clutter in your life while you tell yourself you dealt with it.
- Callout (Family Challenge): Everyone picks one thing they have been meaning to sell someday and says honestly whether they will do the work this week. If not, it goes in the donate bag. Nobody has to sell anything; everybody has to be honest about it.

## 7. Recycle and Throw
The two doors for the things nobody can use, the broken and the finished. They matter mostly for two small habits: do the responsible thing when it is easy, and let go of the guilt that clings to the bin. Recycle is the door for the worn and the electronic: paper and cardboard to paper recycling; old cables, dead chargers, a battery, a small broken gadget are e-waste, and most towns have a drop-off, which keeps the metals and toxins out of the landfill. It is the second fast lane precisely because so much of a worn-out box qualifies for it. Throw is the last door, the smallest, for things truly finished, and the only real obstacle is a feeling, the guilt that says throwing something away is wasteful. That is the same knot untied in Chapter 9: the money was spent long ago, at the store, and keeping the ruined object does not bring a cent back.
- Pull-quote (frozen, the throw reframe): Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.

## 8. A Routing Pass on the Out Box
Route the entryway box, because the method is easier to trust once you have watched it run. The two extra umbrellas: perfectly good, not worth selling, not needed later, still useful to someone, so Donate, into the bag. The wad of surplus tote bags: same answer, Donate, with the torn or stained few stepping over to Recycle. The spare key to the car sold two summers ago: opens nothing, worth nothing, usable by no one, so the bottom of the cascade, Recycle as scrap metal or Throw if simpler. The fan of old takeout menus: paper, so Recycle. The dead charging cable: e-waste, so the electronics drop-off, which is Recycle. Tally: the whole box split cleanly between the two quick doors and left the house in one trip. Nothing was valuable enough to sell, nothing needed later enough to store, which is the normal shape of an out box.

## 9. The Out Box Leaves the Building
Carry the box to the car, drop the donation bag, put the recycling in its bins, and come back to the empty corner. Then look at the friction meter, because this time it moves and it has earned the move. For three chapters the needle held, through the deciding, the tagging, and the quieting of second thoughts, because it only moves when the room does. Last chapter it held once more but drew a faint dashed arrow just ahead, a promise of the step it would take when things actually left. This chapter is that step. The out box did not get tidied or relabeled; it left the building. So the needle steps forward, off its held position and onto a clearly marked tick: Sort complete. Then be honest in both directions: the old held position is now a faint dashed ghost, and the goal crosshair on the green calm side is still ahead, because five S's remain, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain.

## 10. Chapter Close: Sort Is Finished
Look at the entryway one last time. The console holds its five working things, the overflow basket is empty, the out box has left in a single trip, and on a shelf out of the way a tag or two still runs out its clock. What is left in the room is only what you chose to keep. Across these four chapters you took a space that had filled with the wrong things and the unused things and removed both, all the way out of the building, without force and without shame. The first discipline of the six is now a finished thing you can point to. But the empty corner also reveals that the keepers are still where the pass left them, without proper places of their own. Sorting decided what stays; it did not decide where any of it lives. So the work turns now: we stop removing and start arranging. That is the next part.
- Boxed recap (One Idea to Keep): The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished.
