## Search intent
Disposition, not decision. Readers arriving here have already decided what to let go and now have a pile, box, or bag of unnecessary things and need to know where each thing actually goes. Many are stalled by guilt (giving something away feels wasteful, throwing it out feels worse) or by the well-meaning trap of saving things to sell or store someday. The chapter answers with a simple five-door system (Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw), a quick cascade that picks the door in a second, a strong default toward Donate and Recycle as the fast lanes, and guardrails that stop Sell and Store from becoming permanent (a sell-by deadline, a label and date, and never renting space for what you do not need). The throw reframe answers the guilt directly: the waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Gives one clear routing system (five doors, one quick cascade) instead of a scattered list of drop-off options
- Names Donate and Recycle as the fast lanes and makes Donate the explicit default, rather than treating all five options as equal
- Exposes the two common ways decluttering stalls at the disposition step: the someday sale and the just-in-case storage, and fences each with a guardrail
- Puts a hard sell-by deadline on selling and a label-and-date rule on storing, and adds the harder rule to never rent space for what you do not need
- Reframes the guilt around throwing things away as sunk cost: the waste, if any, happened at the store, not at the bin
- Keeps scope tight: this routes the already-decided out box; it does not re-decide what to keep (Chapter 9) and does not arrange the keepers or give them homes (Chapter 12, Part Four)
