# X Thread: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

Chapter 11 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. One thread, 16 posts. Each post is at or under 280 characters and stands on its own. Plain "1/" numbering on its own line.

---

1/
The out box is full, it is settled, and every single thing in it is already decided. Today it leaves, and when it does, Sort is finished. Chapter 11 of 6S Success: Home Edition is a routing chapter, the last one in Sort. No more hard calls. Just exits.

(approx. 250 chars)

2/
Draw the line first. Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it goes are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. You are not weighing attachment or use today. You are asking a smaller question of each thing: what is the cleanest way out?

(approx. 260 chars)

---

3/
Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, and it helps to see them named together: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. That is the whole set. Nothing bound for out needs a sixth option, and a short cascade sorts almost everything for you.

(approx. 252 chars)

---

4/
Run each item down the cascade, stop at the first door that fits:

Broken or unusable? Recycle it, or Throw it.
Valuable and worth your time? Sell it, with a deadline.
Needed later, just not now? Store it, labeled and dated.
Still useful to someone? Donate it.

(approx. 261 chars)

---

5/
The order does quiet work. The two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom and take the great majority. Sell and Store are narrow on purpose: a thing that goes through either does not leave your life today. It takes a to-do.

(approx. 260 chars)

---

6/
Start where most of the box ends.

"Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months."

It is the one exit that actually completes, the same day.

(approx. 254 chars)

---

7/
And head off the small guilt at this door. Giving a usable thing away is not waste. The thing is not destroyed; it is handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite. You are moving value to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back too.

(approx. 258 chars)

---

8/
The two middle doors are for rare items only. Take selling first.

"Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date."

The deadline is the guardrail.

(approx. 256 chars)

---

9/
Store has the same shape and the same danger: using it to hide what you have not got the nerve to route.

"Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need."

(approx. 246 chars)

---

10/
Recycle is the second fast lane, for the worn and the electronic. Paper to paper recycling. Old cables, dead chargers, small broken gadgets to an e-waste drop-off, not the landfill. It costs almost nothing to point them at the right stream, so do it where it is easy.

(approx. 265 chars)

---

11/
Throw is the last door, the one people flinch at.

"Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done."

(approx. 245 chars incl. number)

---

12/
Watch the method run. Two extra umbrellas: usable, so Donate. A wad of tote bags: Donate, the torn ones Recycle. A spare key to a car sold two summers ago: opens nothing, so Recycle or Throw. Old takeout menus: paper, Recycle. A dead charging cable: e-waste, Recycle.

(approx. 264 chars)

---

13/
Tally the pass. Everything split cleanly between Donate and Recycle. Nothing was valuable enough to sell, nothing needed later enough to store, and the whole box left the house in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box, and why the cascade leans on the fast two.

(approx. 269 chars)

14/
One habit makes the exit stick: put the donation bag in your car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked. A bag in the car gets dropped off. Grab three things from the box and get them out today. Three things gone for good in five minutes.

(approx. 251 chars)
