# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 11

**"What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are
working specs, not finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in
`chapter_11_final.html`: the out-box opener streaming toward five labeled doors, the Five
Doors routing cascade with Donate as the wide default lane, the routing pass mapping the
out-box items to doors with a tally, and the friction meter moved to a Sort-complete
milestone.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`,
ink `#2B2622`, soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`,
calm green `#6E8B5B`, friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill
`#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter routes the already-decided out box from Chapter 9, sending each
unnecessary thing through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw. It does
not re-open whether anything belongs in the box or re-run the Use Test (Chapter 9), it does
not re-run the red tags or the holding area (Chapter 10), and it does not give the keepers
homes or arrange them by frequency of use (Part 4, Straighten, Chapter 12 onward). Nothing is
re-decided here; the box is settled, and today it only gets routed. Separator discipline:
labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 11 · SORT`, never a floating hyphen or a floating
dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: out-box, sell-by, later-use,
e-waste. Anti-shame throughout: giving a usable thing away is not waste, nobody has to sell
anything, and a worn-out thing in the bin is not a failure. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no
guilt cues, no force or violence framing anywhere.

## 1. The Five Doors Out (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's whole idea in one image. The out box is full and settled,
and every thing in it leaves through one of five doors, with a deliberate lean toward the two
fast ones. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The entryway out box on the floor by the front door, its contents flowing
as a soft stream toward five labeled doors set into the wall. The stream widens toward Donate
and Recycle and thins to almost nothing at Sell and Store.

**Rough layout:**
- Warm dawn wash background, a floor line low in the frame, the room already lighter behind
  the box.
- Lower left: the out box, full and settled, a small terracotta panel label `THE OUT BOX`, a
  few items poking out (an umbrella handle, a folded shape, a small card). A Newsreader italic
  note beneath, soft gray: "already decided, waiting to be routed".
- By the front door: a green donation bag with a set of keys, a small green sans note "to the
  car", ready to leave in one trip.
- Along the wall, five doors sized by how much of the box each takes:
  - `DONATE`, the widest, green header and green-bordered panel, a green calm-dot, Newsreader
    italic "most of the box".
  - `RECYCLE`, medium, green, a calm-dot, italic "worn, paper, e-waste".
  - `SELL`, narrow, slate, nearly empty, small italic "slow".
  - `STORE`, narrow, slate, nearly empty, small italic "slow".
  - `THROW`, small, spark-bordered, italic "finished".
- The stream: a thick green ribbon to Donate, a lighter green ribbon to Recycle, and thin
  dashed slate threads to Sell and Store, a faint spark thread to Throw, so the eye reads the
  fast-lane bias instantly.
- Caption idea: "Five doors out, and most of the box goes through two of them. Donate takes
  the usable things, Recycle takes the worn ones, and the box leaves in a single trip."

**Note for the illustrator:** the two fast lanes, Donate green and Recycle, are visibly wide;
Sell and Store are visibly narrow and nearly closed, each with at most a single small item
hesitating at it. No re-deciding is happening in this frame; the box is already settled and
the items are simply on their way out. Keep the light warm and the mood calm, a box about to
leave the house, not a scene of stress.

## 2. The Five Doors Routing Cascade (the hero device)

**Purpose:** Make the hero device legible at a glance: a short cascade of questions, and the
order is doing quiet work, so that everything still usable falls through to the wide default
lane, Donate. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** Four questions down a spine, each branching to a narrow side door, with
everything that does not branch falling through to one wide Donate lane at the bottom.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE FIVE DOORS`.
- A vertical spine on the left, starting at a soft dot labeled in Newsreader italic "an item
  from the box".
- Question 1, spark-bordered: "Broken or unusable?" branches right with a "yes" to a
  spark panel `Recycle, else Throw`, italic helper "e-waste and paper to the right stream".
- Question 2, slate-bordered: "Valuable and worth selling?" branches right to a slate panel
  `Sell`, italic "with a sell-by deadline", a small sans note "a slow door".
- Question 3, slate-bordered: "Truly needed later?" branches right to a slate panel `Store`,
  italic "labeled, dated, elsewhere", a small sans note "a slow door".
- The default: below the last question the spine turns green, labeled "everything else", and
  opens into one wide green lane, a green-bordered bar with a calm-dot, a green sans caps tab
  `THE DEFAULT DOOR`, and in Fraunces `Donate`, with a Newsreader italic tail "still useful to
  someone, today".
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "One quick cascade, and everything still usable falls
  through to the wide default lane."

**Note for the illustrator:** the two middle side doors, Sell and Store, are drawn narrow on
purpose; the Donate lane at the bottom is the widest element on the page. The order is the
lesson: the fast lanes sit at the top and the bottom, and the slow doors are narrow branches a
thing takes only when it makes a genuine case. Stop at the first door that fits. Keep the two
slow doors calm slate, not alarming; the Recycle-or-Throw branch is the honest exit for the
broken, not a scolding.

## 3. The Routing Pass on the Out Box (the worked example)

**Purpose:** Make the method trustworthy by watching it run on the real entryway box, and show
in a tally how the whole box splits between the two fast lanes. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The specific out-box items on the left, each with an arrow to its door, and
a tally panel on the right totaling the doors.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE ROUTING PASS`.
- A left column of item rows, each a Newsreader item name, a colored arrow, and a rounded door
  chip:
  - "two extra umbrellas" green arrow to a green `Donate` chip with a calm-dot.
  - "a wad of tote bags" green arrow to a green `Donate` chip, italic aside "torn ones to
    Recycle".
  - "old takeout menus" green arrow to a green `Recycle` chip, italic "paper".
  - "a dead charging cable" green arrow to a green `Recycle` chip, italic "e-waste, not the
    landfill".
  - "key to the sold car" slate arrow to a slate `Recycle/Throw` chip, italic "scrap metal,
    opens nothing".
- Right column: a `THE TALLY` panel, five rows with a leading dot each:
  - Donate 2 (green), Recycle 3 (green), Sell 0 (muted), Store 0 (muted), Throw 0 (muted).
  - A rule, then a Newsreader italic line "the whole box, in one trip".
- Caption idea: "The whole box splits between the two fast doors. Nothing was valuable enough
  to sell, nothing was needed later enough to store."

**Note for the illustrator:** the tally is the payoff, so make Donate and Recycle read as full
and green and Sell, Store, Throw read as a calm empty zero, not a red failure. The tally
counts are Donate 2, Recycle 3, Sell 0, Store 0, Throw 0. This ordinary box did not hold a
Sell or a Store item, and that is the normal shape of an out box, not a mistake to correct.

## 4. The Two Slow Doors and Their Guardrails (the discipline)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's middle section as a picture: Sell and Store are legitimate
for a rare item, but each is slow and each gets one guardrail that keeps it from quietly
becoming permanent. This diagram has no direct HTML twin; it renders the "Two Slow Doors"
section as a paired-card figure.

**What it shows:** Two facing slate cards, one for Sell and one for Store, each with the rare
case it is right for and the single guardrail that fences it.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE TWO SLOW DOORS`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath:
  "two real doors, two guardrails".
- Left card, slate header bar `SELL`: a small price-tag glyph, sans helper "the good bicycle
  nobody rides, the appliance you replaced". Newsreader italic beneath: "genuinely worth the
  work". The guardrail chip below, slate-bordered: `A SELL-BY DEADLINE`, small line "if it has
  not sold by the date, it goes straight to the donate bag".
- Right card, slate header bar `STORE`: a small labeled-box glyph, sans helper "the winter
  coats in July, the camping gear you truly use". Newsreader italic beneath: "genuinely needed
  later". The guardrail chip below, slate-bordered: `A LABEL AND A DATE`, small line "never
  rent space to keep what you do not need".
- Between the two, a slate rule and the Fraunces line, ink: "Storing is not deciding, and
  selling is not finishing."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "Both feel responsible, and both are ways of not
  finishing, so each one earns its place or it does not go through."

**Note for the illustrator:** keep it kind and matter-of-fact. These are not bad doors, only
slow ones, so draw them as calm and legitimate, not as traps to feel ashamed of. Nobody has to
sell anything; the point is that the rare item earns the door and the ordinary item does not.
Do not draw a rented locker as a villain, just as a cost worth naming. No force or pressure
cues.

## 5. The Friction Meter, Moved to Sort Complete (the milestone)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest milestone move. The out box physically left the
building, so the last of the Sort clutter is gone from the house, and the live needle steps
off Chapter 10's held position onto a clearly marked Sort-complete tick, with the goal still
ahead. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle moved forward to a
labeled Sort-complete tick, Chapter 10's held position drawn behind it as a faint dashed
ghost, and the goal crosshair still further ahead on the calm side.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark
  (right), on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- The live needle in ink, moved a real step further toward calm than last chapter, pointing to
  a small slate tick on the band crowned with a solid slate chip `SORT COMPLETE`.
- Behind and just below the live needle, a faint dashed gray needle at Chapter 10's old
  position, tagged in small sans "held last chapter", set to about 55 percent opacity so it
  clearly reads as the ground already covered, not the live reading.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still
  clearly ahead of the live needle, because five S's remain.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The needle steps to Sort complete. The goal is still
  ahead."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a real MOVE, not a hold, but it is a milestone,
not the finish. The live needle must sit at the marked Sort-complete tick, a clear step past
where Chapter 10 held it and fulfilling last chapter's dashed projection. Chapter 10's old
position is now the dashed ghost behind it. The needle must not reach the goal crosshair; the
goal stays clearly ahead on the green side, because Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize,
and Sustain all still lie ahead. Mark the Sort-complete tick plainly and distinct from the
green goal beyond it. No confetti, no completion glow, no swing all the way to calm; the first
S is done and the room is genuinely lighter, and that is exactly what the meter should say, no
more.
