# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 11

**"What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across
all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single
warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest, most final door. Natural soft daylight, shallow
but not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the entryway drop zone. Across Chapters 8 to 10 the console was
cleared, the overflow basket emptied, the unused set aside, and the honest maybes given a tag
and a date. What settled to the bottom of all that is the out box: things already decided
unnecessary, sitting together, ready to go. This chapter routes that box. Every unnecessary
thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, with a
deliberate lean toward the two fast lanes, Donate and Recycle. Nothing is re-decided here, no
keeper is arranged or given a home (that is Part 4). Keep the tone calm and kind: giving a
usable thing away is not waste, nobody has to sell anything, and a worn-out thing in the bin
is not a failure. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no shame cues, no force or pressure framing.

Separator discipline: any incidental label uses the middot, for example `CHAPTER 11 · SORT`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal
hyphen: out-box, sell-by, later-use, e-waste.

---

## 1. The five doors out (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of an entryway in early morning light: a full, settled cardboard
box on the floor by the front door, and set into the wall beyond it, five labeled doors of
different widths. Most of the box's contents drift as a soft visual stream toward a wide,
welcoming green door and a second medium door, while two narrow doors sit almost closed with a
single small item hesitating at each, and one small door takes only what is clearly finished.
A ready donation bag with a set of keys waits by the front door, headed for the car. Warm
paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a gentle slate blue wall, soft-green calm
notes at the two wide doors. Editorial cookbook-style photography, shallow focus on the box
and the wide green door. The room already looks lighter behind the box.

- Mood: a settled box about to leave the house in one trip, a clear bias toward the two fast
  ways out.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, a scene of
  stress or hurry, an overflowing mess, price tags on ordinary things, any sense of
  re-deciding what is in the box.

## 2. The five doors, named and sized (the hero device)

A warm editorial still-life or flat-lay of five labeled destinations laid out in a row, each
sized to how much of a box it takes: a wide, open Donate basket clearly the largest and most
inviting, a medium Recycle bin beside it, then two small, nearly empty containers marked for
selling and for storing, and one small bin for the truly finished. A few ordinary usable
household things rest near the Donate basket; the two small containers hold at most a single
item each. Warm paper cream background, honey light, ink-brown shadows, a soft-green note on
the wide Donate basket, calm slate on the two small slow ones. Shallow focus on the wide
Donate basket, which is plainly the default.

- Mood: a simple set of five exits with an obvious, calm lean toward giving usable things away
  today.
- Avoid: five equal bins in a sterile row, a factory or recycling-plant look, harsh warning
  colors, clutter drama, any pressure or hard-sell framing, floating typeset text.

## 3. The routing pass on the out box (the worked pass)

A warm editorial still-life of the specific entryway out-box items laid out and sorted toward
two groups: two folding umbrellas and a small wad of tote bags gathered toward a donation bag,
and a fan of old takeout menus, a dead charging cable, and a single orphan car key gathered
toward a recycling area. Soft daylight, honey and paper tones, ink-brown shadows, shallow
depth on the two small groups. Documentary and unstaged, as if the box were just tipped out
and quickly sorted. The point is visible: almost everything falls into just two groups, the
give-away group and the recycle group, with nothing set aside to sell and nothing set aside to
store.

- Mood: a quick, trustworthy sort that finishes, the whole box splitting cleanly between two
  fast ways out.
- Avoid: an overfull junk pile, urgency, clutter drama, cold clinical light, a sense of
  agonizing over items, a garbage-strewn scene, shame cues, floating typeset text.

## 4. The two slow doors, kept honest (the discipline)

A calm, warm photograph of two small, tidy set-asides side by side: one a single item with a
handwritten sell-by date card resting on it, the other a labeled, dated storage box with its
contents named on a clear tag. Both are neat, deliberate, and few, a rare item each rather
than a pile. Warm paper cream background, honey light, ink-brown shadows, calm slate notes on
the two cards, shallow focus shared between the date card and the box label. The image should
read as two legitimate but disciplined choices, each fenced by a small written promise.

- Mood: quiet discipline, a rare item earning a slow door, each kept honest by a date or a
  label.
- Avoid: a crowded shelf of maybe-sell items, a rented locker stuffed full, urgency or hard
  selling, guilt cues, a chaotic garage, harsh contrast, glossy retouching, floating typeset
  text.

## 5. The needle moves to Sort complete (the friction meter, the milestone)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge
dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the
right. The dark live needle has stepped a clear notch further toward the calm side than last
chapter and now points to a plainly marked tick on the dial labeled the Sort-complete
milestone. Just behind and below the live needle, a faint dashed ghost needle marks the exact
spot the meter held last chapter, showing the ground the needle just covered. A slate crosshair
labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag, still sits further out on the green calm side,
clearly ahead of the live needle, because five of the six disciplines remain. Warm paper cream
background, ink line work, consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest, earned milestone, the first discipline finished and the room genuinely
  lighter, with more road plainly ahead.
- Avoid: the live needle held in place with no move, the needle swung all the way to calm, the
  needle reaching or passing the goal crosshair, the dashed ghost drawn solid or bright,
  celebratory confetti, hype, neon color, any sense of the whole journey being done. Sort is
  complete, but five disciplines still remain.
