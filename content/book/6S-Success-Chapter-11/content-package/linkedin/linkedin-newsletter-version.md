# Five Doors Out, and Most of the Box Goes Through Two

*A 6S Success: Home Edition newsletter*

Last chapter we built a calm system for the honest maybes: a tag, a holding area, a deadline, and time to make the call. That left one loose end standing in the corner, the out box, finally full and settled. Today it leaves, and when it does, Sort is finished.

Here is the good news hiding in that. Every single thing in the out box has already been decided. You emptied the space of what did not belong, you tested what was left against whether you actually use it, and you gave the maybes their tags. What settled to the bottom is a collection of things you already judged unnecessary. So today is not another round of hard calls.

Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route.

## The five doors

Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. That is the whole set. A short cascade picks the door almost automatically, so run each item down it and stop at the first that fits.

- Broken or truly unusable? Recycle it if you can, and Throw it if you cannot.
- Genuinely valuable, and worth your time to sell? Sell it, with a deadline.
- Genuinely needed later, just not now? Store it, labeled and dated, out of the way.
- Still useful to someone? Donate it. This is where most of the box goes.

The order does quiet work. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes, sitting at the top and bottom, and between them they take the great majority of anything you route. Sell and Store are narrow on purpose, because a thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today; it takes a to-do with it.

## Donate is the default

**Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.**

The alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it, because you already decided you do not use it. The alternative is holding onto it to sell later, or to give to the perfect person eventually. Each feels careful, and each is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about your intentions. Donating asks nothing of the future. The bag goes, and the transaction is over the same day. And you are not throwing value away; you are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

## The two slow doors

Sell and Store are both legitimate, each right for a certain rare item. But both are slow, and both feel responsible, which is why each needs a guardrail.

**Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.** The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it.

**Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.** A rented locker of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

## Recycle and Throw

Recycle is the second fast lane: paper to paper recycling, and old cables, dead chargers, and small broken gadgets to an e-waste drop-off rather than the landfill. Do the responsible thing where it is easy. Throw is the last and smallest door, and the only obstacle is a feeling.

**Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.** There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

## The box leaves the building

Carry the box to the car, drop the donation bag, put the recycling in its bins, and come back to the empty corner. For three chapters the friction meter held, because it only moves when the room does. This time it moves. The out box did not get tidied; it left the building, so the needle steps onto a clearly marked tick: Sort complete. The first of the six S's is done, and the room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain, but the milestone is real.

## The assignment

Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes.

The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished.
