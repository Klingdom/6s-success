# Editorial Review: Chapter 11 Content Package

Reviewed against the brief's checklist: repetition, AI-sounding phrasing, unsupported claims, weak hooks, unclear CTAs, source alignment, overlong social posts, tone mismatch, formatting. Every quoted line and named object below was grep-verified in `chapter_11_manuscript.md` before being cited here. Every count below was measured, then written.

## Overall verdict
Publish-ready, with one standing caveat: the chapter itself is a draft. The package holds the warm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame voice across all channels, the facts trace cleanly to the authored manuscript and final HTML, and the hooks are concrete (the out box already decided, five doors out, Donate as the default, two guardrails on the slow doors, the waste that happened at the store, the needle that finally moves). The validation pass is clean. Findings below are minor polish, not blockers.

## Standing caveat: the chapter is a draft
Chapter 11 had no supplied source files. The signature, manuscript, and final HTML were authored in the established book voice and design, then packaged. Everything here is faithful to that draft, but the draft is awaiting author approval. If the author revises the chapter (especially the five-door set and its order, the two guardrails, the worked out-box pass, or the friction-meter move to "Sort complete"), refresh the canonical layer and re-run the affected packages.

## Unsupported claims
- No invented statistics, names, or dates.
- The chapter does not overclaim originality. It presents the five-door routing plainly and never calls it the book's invention, and it does not attribute the method to any outside source or authority. For transparency: unlike Chapter 10, which lightly attributed the red tag to its Lean factory-floor origin, Chapter 11 makes no such attribution because it introduces no new borrowed tool. The word "disposition" appears only in the SEO metadata (`canonical/chapter-seo.md`), not in the manuscript, and is used there as a category label, not as a sourced claim. So there is nothing to over-attribute and nothing over-claimed; the scope note simply holds.
- The sunk-cost reframe is framed as a plain observation, not a statistic: "The money for a worn-out thing was spent long ago, at the moment of purchase, and keeping the ruined object in a drawer does not bring one cent of it back." (verified). No figure is invented.
- Testimonials in `pdf-ebook/back-cover-copy.md` are clearly marked placeholders ("PLACEHOLDERS · do not publish as real quotes"), with bracketed replace-before-print instructions and no invented attributions (three placeholders, counted).

Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months." open on a sharp, calm claim (both verbatim).

(verbatim), is the most distinctive framing in the chapter and is used well in the thread (post 11), the LinkedIn "Throw it out without guilt" post, the Facebook longform, and the quote cards.
