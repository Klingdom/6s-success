# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 11 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 11 is the fourth and final Sort chapter, and it carries the closing job of the four. Chapter 8 sorted by belonging, Chapter 9 sorted by use, and Chapter 10 tagged the honest maybes; this one takes the out box those passes filled and does not re-decide a single thing in it. It routes. The voice has to make "you are not here to decide anything today, you are here to route" read as a relief rather than a shrug, and it does. The routing is framed as the smaller, calmer act that follows a decision already made, the fast doors are honored without shaming the slow ones, and the reader is never told they were foolish to keep what they are now letting go.

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," returns to the same scene the reader already knows (the entryway, the console with its five working things, the emptied basket, the out box waiting in its corner). "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route." (verbatim)
- **Non-prescriptive and anti-shame.** The permission runs through the disposal doors: "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain." (verbatim). The throw reframe answers the guilt at the bin directly: "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store." (verbatim). Letting go is treated as an honest close, not a failure.
- **Plain over clever.** The hero device is stated in five plain words, verbatim in the manuscript: "**Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw.**" The bluntest lines carry the load without a flourish: "There is no virtue in living among broken objects." and "Storing is not deciding." (both verbatim).
- **Concrete and specific.** Real objects carry the writing: two extra umbrellas, a wad of surplus tote bags "that breed in a drawer," old takeout menus, a dead charging cable, and the spare key to the car you sold two summers ago. Each of these strings was grep-verified in the manuscript.
- **Gently witty, never mean.** "the ones that breed in a drawer" and "a rented locker full of things you have already decided you do not use is the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly" (both verbatim) keep the tone light rather than stern.
- **Honestly framed, no invented origin.** Chapter 11 does not claim the five-door routing as the book's own invention, and it does not attribute it to any outside source either. It presents the doors plainly as the sensible exits and lets the cascade do the work. The red tag appears only as a callback to Chapter 10 ("We are not re-running the red tags"), not re-explained or re-claimed here.
- **Honest about the gauge.** The friction meter moves this chapter, and the voice earns it rather than declaring victory: "the needle steps forward, off its held position and onto a clearly marked tick on the dial: **Sort complete.**" (verbatim), immediately followed by the honest counterweight, "the goal, the crosshair out on the green calm side, is still ahead of where the needle now sits. Sort is finished; the journey is not." (verbatim).

## Specific voice wins
- **The five doors survive intact everywhere.** "Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw" carries the thread, the quote cards, the article, and the infographics as the same five names in the same order, not a paraphrase. The order itself does quiet work (the two fast doors at the top and bottom), and the assets keep it.
- **Donate as the default holds.** The central rule, "Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months." (verbatim), rides through the LinkedIn posts, the X thread, the Facebook longform, and the quote cards in the same register, and the anti-guilt reframe rides with it.
- **The two guardrails read as care, not rules.** The sell-by deadline and the label-and-date-never-rent-space fence are each stated once, plainly, and reused without hardening into commands: "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job..." and "Storing is not deciding..." (both verbatim). The voice keeps both in kitchen-table language.
- **The friction-meter move is handled with restraint and honesty.** The chapter has the meter step to a marked "Sort complete" tick because the out box physically left the building, and it refuses to oversell the milestone in the same breath: five S's remain, the goal crosshair is still ahead, and Chapter 10's held position is now drawn as a faint dashed ghost so you can see the ground covered. Both the "Sort complete" tick and the dashed ghost are present in `chapter_11_final.html` and `chapter-11-publishable.html` (verified). Marking a real milestone without pretending it is the finish line is the brand, and the move is written as a genuine payoff, not a victory lap.

## Voice risks to watch
- **The door names run hot.** In the manuscript "door" and "doors" appear 51 times, "box" 43 times, "sell/selling/sold/sale" word-forms 34 times, "donate" word-forms 28 times, "recycle/recycling" 27 times, "store/storing/storage" word-forms 25 times, "out box" 18 times, "route/routing" word-forms 14 times, and "throw" word-forms 13 times (all counted). This is the chapter's frozen working vocabulary, so the repetition is structural rather than filler, but watch it in the social assets so no single post stacks the same door name four or five times.
- **Contractions on X.** The book uses few; X uses several. Deliberate platform choice. Revise only if brand guidelines forbid contractions everywhere.
- **CTA sameness.** The soft-CTA bank in `canonical/chapter-cta.md` holds six rotate phrasings (counted). Rotate them so LinkedIn and X sign-offs do not feel templated.
- **Emoji on Facebook.** One emoji sits at the end of the longform post (counted: one, U+1F642); the other two Facebook files carry one and two more (four across the Facebook folder, counted). Confirm they match your brand's emoji policy.

## Banned-language audit
- **Widened dash scan, run for this review.** I ran a scan for em dash (U+2014), en dash (U+2013), and the spaced-hyphen " - " used as a separator (excluding markdown list bullets at line start) across all 52 package files plus `chapter_11_manuscript.md` and `chapter_11_final.html` (54 files scanned; the publishable HTML is one of the 52 package files; the review folder was empty at scan time). Result: zero em dashes, zero en dashes, zero spaced-hyphen separators. The package uses the middot ("·") for masthead and label separators (137 middot occurrences counted across the scan). For full transparency, the only em dashes anywhere in the chapter folder are three inside the working brief `CH11_CANONICAL_STRINGS_AND_BRIEF.md` (counted), which is not a shipped package file; `chapter_11_signature.md` has none.
- **Hype-word scan.** Zero hits across the package for "unlock," "leverage," "seamless," "elevate," "supercharge," "game-changing," "delve," "dive in," "in conclusion," "fast-paced," "in today's," "effortless," "revolutionize" (scan run, each returned zero files).
- **No force or violence framing.** No "attack the pile" or "pull the trigger" anywhere. The words "purge" and "war on clutter" each appear once, both inside `video-audio/b-roll-and-visual-notes.md` as instructions to the videographer to AVOID that framing ("no purge montage, no aggressive dumping, no 'war on clutter'"), not as copy. The whole move is framed as calmly routing already-decided things out through the door that gets them gone.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all channels, and the two things that make this chapter distinct (the five-door routing with its deliberate lean on Donate and Recycle, and the honest friction-meter move to a marked "Sort complete" tick that closes the first S) are honored throughout. Approve the voice. The chapter itself still needs author sign-off, since it was authored in the book voice rather than supplied from source.
