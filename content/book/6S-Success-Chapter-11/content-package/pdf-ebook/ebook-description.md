# 6S Success: Home Edition (Ebook Description)

Most decluttering advice ends at the decision and leaves you standing over a full box. You worked out what you do not need; now there is a pile of it in the corner, and it is not leaving on its own. That last step, getting the already-decided things out of the house, is where good passes quietly stall. Chapter 11 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is where the box finally leaves.

"What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away" is the fourth and final Sort chapter, and it closes Sort. It does not re-open whether anything belongs in the box; that was Chapter 9, and it is done. "Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first." So this is a routing chapter, not another round of hard calls. As the chapter puts it, "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."

The method is the Five Doors Out. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and a short cascade picks each door in a second: broken then Recycle or Throw, valuable and worth the work then Sell, needed later then Store, still useful then Donate. The order does quiet work. The two fast lanes, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom and take the great majority of any box, while Sell and Store are narrow on purpose. Donate is the default for anything still usable, because it is the exit that actually completes, same day, with no listing to write and no buyer to meet. And donating is not waste: "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain."

The two slow doors each get a guardrail. On selling: "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date." On storing: "Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need." Recycle and Throw handle the worn and the finished, with the sunk-cost reframe that answers the guilt: "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done."

A short worked pass routes the entryway out box, item by item, and the whole thing splits cleanly between Donate and Recycle and leaves in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box. When it leaves the building, the friction meter takes its last Sort step to a Sort-complete milestone: the first of the six S's is done, and the room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain, and the chapter marks the milestone honestly without overselling it. Sorting decided what stays; giving the keepers homes is the next part. For now, grab three things, get them out today, and finish Sort.
