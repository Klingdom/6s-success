# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

*Chapter 11, 6S Success: Home Edition. Use these for personal reflection or a group discussion. They are open-ended, with no single right answer.*

1. The chapter draws a firm line: "Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first." Where in your own clearing do the two acts blur together, so that a full box sits undealt with because the last, smaller step feels like more deciding?

2. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw. Think of the last pile you cleared. If you routed each item down the cascade now, how many would land at Donate or Recycle, and how many would you have been tempted to hold for Sell or Store?

3. "Donate is the default." The chapter argues the alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it, but holding onto it to sell later or give to the perfect person eventually. Which of those well-meaning delays do you use most, and what does the thing actually cost you while it waits?

4. Donating a usable thing is framed as the opposite of waste: "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain." Where has a quiet sense that giving something away is wasteful kept an object in your house longer than it deserved?

5. "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job." The chapter allows selling only for the genuinely valuable item, with a sell-by date, and donates whatever has not sold. Have you ever set something aside to sell that never sold? What would a hard deadline, written down the day you list it, have changed?

6. "Storing is not deciding." The store trap is using storage as a hiding place for what you have not got the nerve to route. Where in your home, or in a rented space, are you paying to keep things you already decided you do not need?

7. The two slow doors each get a guardrail: a sell-by deadline on Sell, a label and date on Store, and the harder rule to never rent space for what you do not need. Which guardrail would you most need to actually write down, and why does saying it out loud make it stick?

8. The throw reframe: "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store." How does moving the moment of waste back to the purchase, and off the bin, change the guilt you feel when you finally let a worn-out thing go?

9. Recycle is named the second fast lane, for the worn and the electronic, worth doing where it is easy. Where do your old cables, dead chargers, and small broken gadgets actually end up now, and what is the smallest change that would point them at the right stream instead of the trash?

10. In the worked pass, the whole entryway box splits cleanly between Donate and Recycle and leaves in one trip. Nothing was worth selling and nothing needed storing. Does that match the normal shape of your own out box, or do you tend to route more items into the two slow doors than they deserve?

11. When the box leaves the building, the friction meter takes its last Sort step to a Sort-complete milestone, but the goal crosshair is still ahead because five S's remain. Why might a gauge that marks the milestone plainly and refuses to oversell it earn more of your trust than one that swings to celebrate?

12. The chapter closes by noting that sorting decided what stays but not where any of it lives, and "a keeper without a place is just tomorrow's clutter waiting to happen." Now that Sort is finished, which of your keepers are sitting where they landed with no home of their own, ready for the next part?
