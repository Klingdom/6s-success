# 6S Success: Home Edition (Sales Page Copy)

## The box is full, and every call in it is already made.

You reached the end of a Sort pass and something is sitting in the corner: the out box. It holds the things you decided you do not need. Two extra umbrellas. A wad of tote bags that breed in a drawer. A spare key to a car you sold two summers ago. A fan of old takeout menus. A dead charging cable. Not one of them is still in question. The hard part, the deciding, is behind you.

So why is the box still in the house?

Because the last step of Sort is where good passes quietly stall. The usable things get set aside to sell someday, or to give to the perfect person eventually, and they sit in a bag by the stairs for a season while the plan never quite arrives. The worn things get held onto out of a vague guilt that throwing them out is wasteful. The box stays, and the corner you meant to clear is still occupied.

## This is a routing chapter, not a deciding one.

"Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first." Chapter 11 handles only the second, and it is smaller and calmer than it looks. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and a quick question picks the door for each item in a second. "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."

The bias is deliberate and strong. Two of the five doors finish quickest, Donate and Recycle, and between them they take the great majority of any box. The other two, Sell and Store, are narrow on purpose, because a thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today; it takes a to-do with it.

## What you get in this chapter

- The Five Doors Out, named together, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw, with a short cascade that sorts each item almost automatically
- Donate as the default for anything still usable, because it is the exit that actually completes: the bag goes out and the transaction is over the same day
- The sell trap and the store trap, each fenced with a guardrail: a sell-by deadline on selling, and a label and date on storing, plus the harder rule to never rent space to keep what you do not need
- The Recycle and Throw reframe for the worn and the finished, so the guilt that clings to the bin can stay in the bin
- A short worked pass that routes the whole entryway out box, item by item, and carries it out in a single trip

## Donating is not waste, and nobody has to sell anything.

The chapter meets the guilt at each door plainly. Giving a usable thing away is not an admission you should not have had it: "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain." Selling is optional, and a worn-out thing belongs in the bin without apology, because the waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store, not at the bin. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

## The first S finishes here.

When the box leaves the building, the friction meter takes its last Sort step, off the position it held for three chapters and onto a clearly marked tick: Sort complete. It is a real milestone, and the chapter refuses to oversell it. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain. But the room is genuinely lighter now, not merely arranged to look that way, and the first of the six disciplines is a finished thing you can point to.

Read the chapter, grab three things from the out box, and get them out of the house today. Then the corner is clear, and Sort is done.
