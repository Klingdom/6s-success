# Facebook Longform Post: The Box That Finally Left

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 11 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA to route three things out today. No em dashes.

---

The out box by my front door was full and settled, and every single thing in it was already decided. Two extra umbrellas. A wad of tote bags that breed in a drawer. The spare key to a car I sold two summers ago. A fan of old takeout menus. A dead charging cable.

I was not there to decide anything. That part was done. I was only there to route.

Here is the idea that made it quick. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A short question picks the door, and most of the box goes through one of them.

Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months. So the umbrellas and the good tote bags went straight into a donation bag. That is not waste. You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

The two slow doors, Sell and Store, I left almost closed. Selling turns clearing into a part-time job, and nothing in my box was worth the photographs and the listing and the stranger at the door. And storing is not deciding, so I was not about to hide a decision on a shelf.

That left the worn ones. The menus are paper, so Recycle. The dead cable is e-waste, so the electronics drop-off, not the landfill. The old key opens nothing and helps nobody, so Recycle if the program takes small metal, or the bin if not. Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store.

Tally the pass. Everything went through Donate or Recycle. Nothing sold, nothing stored, and the whole box left the house in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box.

And then Sort was finished. The first of the six S's is done, and the corner is empty. Not tidier, actually empty.

So here is your low-pressure homework for the comments, if you want it. Grab three things from your own out box and get them out of the house today. Choose the door that gets it gone, and do not overthink the rest.
