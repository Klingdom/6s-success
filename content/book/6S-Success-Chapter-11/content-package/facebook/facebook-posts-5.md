## Post 1 · Five doors out

The out box by your front door is full, and every thing in it is already decided. You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route.

Here is the whole method. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A quick question picks the door, and you stop at the first one that fits.

Broken or truly unusable? Recycle it if you can, and Throw it if you cannot. Genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell? Sell it, with a deadline. Genuinely needed later, just not now? Store it, labeled and dated. Still useful to someone? Donate it. This is where most of the box goes.

Two of those doors, Donate and Recycle, are the fast lanes, and between them they take the great majority of any box. The other two are slow on purpose. That is the entire chapter: five doors, one quick question, and a firm lean toward the two that finish the job.

## Post 2 · Donate is the default

Start with the door most of your box takes.

Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.

Look at what that protects you from. The alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it, because you already decided you do not use it. The alternative is holding onto it to sell it later, or to give it to the perfect person eventually. Each of those feels careful, and each is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about your intentions.

Donating asks nothing of the future. The bag goes to a place that will pass it on, and the transaction is over the same day. And the small guilt that shows up here, the sense that giving something away is a kind of waste, is worth answering plainly. You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

## Post 3 · The two slow doors

Sell and Store are both legitimate, and both are easy to over-use, because both feel responsible. So each one gets a guardrail.

Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date. Selling is real work: photographs, a listing, a price, messages, a stranger at your door. Worth it for a hundred-dollar item, a waste of a week for a five-dollar one. The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it.

Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need. The winter coats in July belong in storage. A locker full of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

Both slow doors are fine for the rare item that earns them. Just do not let them become a place to hide a decision.

## Post 4 · Recycle and throw, without guilt

That leaves the two doors for the things nobody can use, the broken and the finished.

Recycle is the second fast lane. Paper and cardboard go to paper recycling. Old cables, dead chargers, a battery, a small broken gadget, all of that is e-waste, and most towns have a drop-off that takes it. It costs almost nothing to point these things at the right stream instead of the trash, so do it where it is easy.

Throw is the last door, the smallest, and the one people flinch at. The only real obstacle here is not logistics but a feeling: the guilt that says throwing something away is wasteful.

Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.

The money was spent long ago, and keeping a dead cable in a drawer does not bring one cent of it back. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

## Post 5 · The whole box, one trip

Let me run the method on a real box so you can see how fast it goes.

By my front door: two extra umbrellas, a wad of tote bags, the spare key to a car I sold two summers ago, a fan of old takeout menus, and a dead charging cable.

The umbrellas are perfectly good, so Donate. The tote bags are usable, so Donate, with the torn ones over to Recycle. The menus are paper, so Recycle. The dead cable is e-waste, so the electronics drop-off. The old key opens nothing and helps nobody, so Recycle if the program takes small metal, or the bin if not.

Tally the pass. Everything went through Donate or Recycle. Nothing was worth selling, nothing needed storing, and the whole box left the house in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box, and it is exactly why Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes.

And with that, Sort is finished. The first of the six S's is done, and the corner is empty.
