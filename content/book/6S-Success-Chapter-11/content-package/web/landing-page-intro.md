# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 11, What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

The out box is full, it is settled, and every single thing in it has already been decided. Over the last three chapters you emptied the entryway of what did not belong there, you tested what was left against the plain question of whether you actually use it, and you gave the honest maybes a tag and a deadline. What settled to the bottom of all that is the out box: a collection of things you have already judged unnecessary, sitting together, ready to go somewhere. You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route.

That word draws the line this chapter runs on. Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. What is left is the second, and it is smaller and calmer than it looks. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, and a quick question picks the door for each one. Most of the box goes through a single door, quickly, and the whole thing can leave in one trip. This is the last chapter in Sort, and by the end of it the corner is empty and the first of the six S's is genuinely done.

The five doors are Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, and Throw. Run each item down a short cascade and stop at the first door that fits: broken or truly unusable, then Recycle it if you can and Throw it if you cannot; genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell, then Sell it with a deadline; genuinely needed later, just not now, then Store it labeled and dated; still useful to someone, then Donate it. The order does quiet work. The two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and bottom, and between them they take the great majority of anything you route.

So the bias of the whole system is deliberate and it is strong, and it starts here: Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months. Donating asks nothing of the future. The bag goes to a place that will pass things on, and the transaction is over the same day. And the small guilt that sometimes shows up at this door is answered plainly: the thing is not being destroyed, it is being handed to someone who wants it, which is the opposite of waste.

The two middle doors, Sell and Store, are narrow on purpose, because both are slow and both keep the thing in your house while you tell yourself you have dealt with it. So each gets a guardrail. Selling turns clearing into a part-time job, so sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date. Storing is not deciding, so store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need. Recycle and Throw handle the worn and the finished, and the guilt that clings to the bin gets the same honest answer it got in Chapter 9: the waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store, not at the bin.

Read Chapter 11 free in the online book, then grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes, and the last of the Sort clutter starts leaving the building.
