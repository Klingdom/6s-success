## SECTION 1: YOU ARE HERE TO ROUTE

[HOST]
Let me draw the line clearly before we start, because it changes how heavy today feels.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route.]

[HOST, voice over]
Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first. What is left is the second, and it is smaller and calmer than it looks. You are not weighing attachment or use anymore. You are asking a much simpler question of each thing: what is the cleanest way for you to leave the house?

[HOST, on camera]
Answer that once, twice, however many times the box holds, and the corner is clear. That is the whole shape of the work. Not another round of deciding. Just a sorting of already-decided things into the right exits.

---

## SECTION 2: THE FIVE DOORS

[HOST]
Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors, and it helps to see them named together before we use them.

[VISUAL: The five doors infographic. A wide, welcoming green door marked DONATE and a door marked RECYCLE take most of the stream. Two narrow side doors, SELL and STORE, sit almost closed. A fifth door, THROW, takes only what is clearly finished.]

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw.]

[HOST, voice over]
Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. That is the whole set. And you run each item down a short cascade, stopping at the first door that fits. Broken or truly unusable? Recycle it if you can, and Throw it if you cannot. Genuinely valuable, and worth your time to sell? Sell it, with a deadline. Genuinely needed later, just not now? Store it, labeled and dated. Still useful to someone? Donate it. This is where most of the box goes.

[HOST, on camera]
Read that order again, because the order is doing quiet work. The two doors that clear a box quickest, Donate and Recycle, sit at the top and the bottom, and between them they take the great majority of anything you route. The two doors in the middle, Sell and Store, are narrow on purpose. They are not bad doors. They are slow ones, and a thing that goes through either does not actually leave your life today. It takes a to-do with it.

---

## SECTION 3: DONATE IS THE DEFAULT

[HOST]
Start with the door most of your box will take.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Donate is the default.]

[HOST, voice over]
Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months. The alternative to donating a usable thing is almost never keeping it, because you already decided you do not use it. The alternative is holding onto it to sell it later, or to find just the right charity eventually. Each of those feels careful, and each is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about your intentions.

[HOST, on camera]
And let me head off the small guilt that shows up at this door, the sense that giving something away is a kind of waste. It is not. You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

---

## SECTION 4: THE TWO SLOW DOORS

[HOST]
Now the two middle doors, the ones to use sparingly. Sell and Store are both legitimate, each exactly right for a certain rare item. But both are slow, both keep the thing in your house while you tell yourself you have dealt with it, and both feel responsible. So each one gets a guardrail.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Selling turns clearing into a part-time job.]

[HOST, voice over]
Take selling first. Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date. Photographs, a listing, a price, a stranger at your door, a no-show, a relisting. That work is worth it for a hundred-dollar item and a waste of a week for a five-dollar one. So every thing you list gets a sell-by date, and if it has not sold by then, it goes straight into the donate bag. The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it.

[VISUAL: A narrow SELL door with a single item at it, a written sell-by date beside it, an arrow looping to the donate bag if the date passes.]

[HOST, on camera]
The Store door needs the same kind of fence. Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need. The winter coats in July, the camping gear you truly use every summer, those belong in storage. But a rented locker full of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly. Store the genuine later-use items, labeled and dated, and route everything else.

---

## SECTION 5: RECYCLE AND THROW

[HOST]
That leaves the two doors for the things nobody can use, the broken and the finished.

[HOST, voice over]
Recycle is the door for the worn and the electronic. Paper and cardboard to paper recycling. Old cables, dead chargers, a small broken gadget, all of that is e-waste, and most towns have a drop-off that takes it. It costs you almost nothing to point these at the right stream, and it is the decent thing to do, so do it where it is easy. Recycle is the second fast lane precisely because so much of a worn-out box qualifies for it.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Throwing a worn-out thing away is not the waste.]

[HOST, on camera]
Throw is the last door, and the one people flinch at, because of a feeling: the guilt that says throwing something away is wasteful. Let me take that apart.

[HOST, voice over]
Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done. The money was spent long ago, and keeping a dead cable in a drawer does not bring one cent of it back. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

---

## SECTION 6: A ROUTING PASS

[HOST]
Let us route the entryway box, because the method is easier to trust once you have watched it run.

[B-ROLL: A hand tips the out box out and takes items one at a time. Two umbrellas and a handful of tote bags go into the donation bag. Menus, a dead cable, and a small key go into a recycling bin.]

[HOST, voice over]
Two extra umbrellas: perfectly good, not worth selling, not needed later, useful to someone. Donate. A wad of surplus tote bags: same answer, Donate, and the few torn ones step over to Recycle. The old takeout menus are paper, so Recycle. The dead charging cable is e-waste, so Recycle. And the spare key to the car you sold two summers ago opens nothing and is worth nothing, so it takes the bottom of the cascade: Recycle if your program takes small metal, or Throw if not.

[HOST, on camera]
Tally the pass. Donate took the umbrellas and the good tote bags. Recycle took the rest. Nothing was valuable enough to sell. Nothing was needed later enough to store. The whole box split cleanly between the two quick doors, and it left the house in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box.

Carry the box to the car, drop the donation bag, put the recycling in its bins, and come back to the empty corner. Then go look at the friction meter, because this time it moves, and it has earned the move. For three chapters the needle held, because the meter only moves when the room does. [ON SCREEN TEXT: Sort complete. The first S is done.]

Sit with that, because it is a real milestone. The first of the six S's is done. What did not belong and what you do not need are both gone from the house now, not shuffled somewhere else in it. And then be honest about what the meter still shows. The goal, out on the green calm side, is still ahead. Five S's remain: Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain. Sort is finished. The journey is not.

## CLOSE AND SOFT CTA

[HOST]
Look at the entryway one last time. The console holds its five working things. The out box has left, carried out in a single trip. What is left in the room is only what you chose to keep. Nothing else.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The best destination is the one that actually empties the box.]

[HOST, on camera]
So here is your job, and it takes five minutes. Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes. And put that bag in the car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked. A bag in the car gets dropped off.
