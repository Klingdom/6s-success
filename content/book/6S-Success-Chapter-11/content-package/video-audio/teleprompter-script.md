# Teleprompter Script: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

**Chapter 11, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** what-to-donate-sell-store-recycle-or-throw-away
**Length:** about 700 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come back to the entryway one last time.

The out box is full, and it is settled.

Every single thing in it has already been decided.

Today it leaves.

And when it does, Sort is finished.

So let me draw one line before we start.

You are not here to decide anything today.

You are here to route.

Deciding a thing is unnecessary,

and deciding where it should go,

are two separate acts.

You already finished the first.

What is left is smaller and calmer than it looks.

You are only asking one thing of each object.

What is the cleanest way for you to leave the house?

Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors.

Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw.

That is the whole set.

And a quick question picks the door.

Broken or truly unusable?

Recycle it if you can, and Throw it if you cannot.

Genuinely worth your time to sell?

Sell it, with a deadline.

Genuinely needed later, just not now?

Store it, labeled and dated.

Still useful to someone?

Donate it. This is where most of the box goes.

Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes.

Sell and Store are the slow doors.

They are narrow on purpose.

So start with the default.

Donate is the default.

Most usable things you do not need

should go to someone who does, today.

Not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.

And giving it away is not waste.

You are not throwing value away.

You are moving it to where it will finally get used,

and taking your space back in the bargain.

Now the two slow doors, and their guardrails.

Selling turns clearing into a part-time job.

Sell only what is genuinely worth the work.

Put a deadline on it.

And donate whatever has not sold by the date.

Storing is not deciding.

Store only what you will truly use later.

Label it and date it.

And never rent space to keep what you do not need.

Then the last two doors.

Recycle is for the worn and the electronic.

Paper to paper recycling.

Old cables and dead chargers to e-waste.

Do the responsible thing where it is easy.

And Throw is the last door, the one people flinch at.

But some things are simply worn out,

and throwing them away is not the waste.

The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store.

Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.

There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

Now let us route the box.

Two extra umbrellas: usable, so Donate.

A wad of tote bags: usable, so Donate.

Old takeout menus: paper, so Recycle.

A dead charging cable: e-waste, so Recycle.

A spare key to a car you sold two summers ago,

worth nothing to anyone: Recycle, or Throw.

Nothing was worth selling.

Nothing needed storing.

The whole box split between two doors,

and it left the house in one trip.

Now carry it out, and come back to the empty corner.

Then go look at the friction meter.

Because this time, it moves.

For three chapters the needle held.

The meter only moves when the room does.

This chapter, the out box left the building.

So the needle steps forward,

onto a clearly marked tick: Sort complete.

The first of the six S's is done.

The room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier.

But be honest about what the meter still shows.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

Five S's remain.

Sort is finished. The journey is not.

So here is your job.

Grab three things from the out box.

Get them out of the house today.

The usable ones into a donation bag in your car.

The broken one to recycling or the bin.

Three things gone for good in five minutes.

The best destination is the one that actually empties the box.

Choose the door that gets it out of the house,

and Sort is finished.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go route three things.
