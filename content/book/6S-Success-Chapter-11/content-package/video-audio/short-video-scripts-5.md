## SHORT 1: The five doors out
**Topic: the hero device, the Five Doors Out. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The out box is already decided. Now it just needs the right door.

**Script:**
The out box is full, and every thing in it is already decided. You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route. Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A quick question picks the door. Broken or unusable? Recycle it, or Throw it. Genuinely worth selling? Sell it, with a deadline. Truly needed later? Store it, labeled and dated. Still useful to someone? Donate it, and that is where most of the box goes. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes. Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them.

- 10s: "Five doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw"
- 26s: "Donate and Recycle = the fast lanes"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A wide green DONATE door and a RECYCLE door take most of the stream from the out box. Two narrow side doors, SELL and STORE, sit almost closed. Warm morning light.

## SHORT 2: Donate is the default
**Topic: the default door, anti-shame. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The someday sale is just a way of keeping it.

**Script:**
Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months. The alternative to donating is almost never keeping it, because you already decided you do not use it. It is holding on to sell it later, or to find the perfect charity eventually. Each feels careful, and each is really just a way of keeping the thing in your house while you feel good about it. And giving it away is not waste. You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Donate is the default"
- 10s: "A someday sale keeps it in your house for months"
- 22s: "Giving it away is not waste"
- 30s: "You are moving value, and taking your space back"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand drops usable things into a donation bag, then sets the bag in a car. The corner of the room clears behind it. Warm, unhurried.

## SHORT 3: The two slow doors
**Topic: the sell trap and store trap, with guardrails. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Sell and Store both feel responsible. Both are how you avoid finishing.

**Script:**
Sell and Store are the two slow doors, and both feel responsible, which is exactly why they are easy to over-use. So each gets a guardrail. Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date. That deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it. And storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need. A locker full of things you already decided you do not use is just the clutter you cleared, moved offsite and charged to you monthly.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Sell and Store both feel responsible"
- 10s: "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job"
- 24s: "Storing is not deciding"
- 34s: "Never rent space to keep what you do not need"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A narrow SELL door with a single item and a written sell-by date; a narrow STORE door with a labeled, dated box. An arrow loops the unsold item to the donate bag.

## SHORT 4: Throw it away without guilt
**Topic: the sunk-cost reframe. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The waste did not happen at the bin. It happened at the store.

**Script:**
Here is the guilt that clings to the trash can: throwing something away feels wasteful, like you failed by not using it up. So let me take that apart. Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done. The money was spent long ago, and keeping a dead cable in a drawer does not bring one cent of it back. Holding on to a broken thing does not honor what you paid. It just adds a small daily weight to a cost already paid. There is no virtue in living among broken objects.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Throwing it away feels wasteful"
- 10s: "The waste, if any, happened at the store"
- 22s: "A dead cable in a drawer brings back nothing"
- 30s: "No virtue in living among broken objects"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand turns a frayed cable over, then drops it calmly into an e-waste box; a cracked item goes into the bin. Nothing dramatic. Warm light.

## SHORT 5: Sort complete
**Topic: the worked pass and the friction-meter milestone. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Watch the whole box leave through just two doors.

**Script:**
Watch a normal out box route itself. Two extra umbrellas: usable, so Donate. A wad of surplus tote bags: usable, so Donate, and the torn ones to Recycle. Old takeout menus: paper, so Recycle. A dead charging cable: e-waste, so Recycle. And the spare key to a car you sold two summers ago, worth nothing to anyone: Recycle or Throw. Nothing was worth selling. Nothing needed storing. The whole box split between Donate and Recycle, and it left the house in one trip. Then go look at the friction meter, because this time it moves. The out box left the building, so the needle steps onto its Sort-complete tick. The first S is done. The goal is still ahead, because five S's remain, but the room is genuinely lighter, not just tidier.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A normal out box, routed"
- 12s: "Umbrellas + tote bags = Donate"
- 22s: "Menus, cable, key = Recycle or Throw"
- 34s: "Sort complete · the first S is done"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand routes each item into a donation bag or a recycling bin, then the empty corner. Cut to the friction meter: the needle steps onto a marked "Sort complete" tick, a faint dashed ghost just behind it, the GOAL crosshair still ahead on the green side.
