# B-Roll and Visual Notes: What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away

**Chapter 11, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** what-to-donate-sell-store-recycle-or-throw-away
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero device is the Five Doors Out: five labeled doors set into a wall, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw. A soft stream of items flows from the out box toward them.
- The bias is drawn, not just stated. DONATE is a wide, welcoming green door; RECYCLE is a good-sized door beside it. Most of the stream pours through those two. SELL and STORE are two narrow side doors, almost closed, with a single small item hesitating at each. THROW takes only what is clearly finished.
- The out box from Chapter 9 sits full and settled in the corner by the front door, holding the already-decided items: two extra umbrellas, a wad of surplus tote bags, a spare key to a car sold two summers ago, a fan of old takeout menus, and a dead charging cable. A donation bag stands ready by the door, keys on top, headed for the car.
- Everything in the box is already decided. Nothing is re-judged on camera. The only action is routing, sending each thing through its door.
- The friction meter MOVES this chapter, for the first time in Sort. The live needle steps off Chapter 10's held position and onto a clearly marked tick labeled "Sort complete." Chapter 10's held position becomes a faint dashed ghost just behind it. The GOAL crosshair stays further out on the green calm side, because five S's remain.
- Warm daylight, the room already lighter behind the box. Lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.
- Keep the whole thing calm and unhurried. No force or violence framing: nothing is purged, attacked, or dumped. Items are carried out gently, in a single trip.

- Split or dissolve: on the left, the hard deciding of past chapters, a hand weighing an object; on the right, today's calm routing, the same hand simply carrying a thing to a door. The left panel dims; the right stays lit.
- The point: deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it goes are two separate acts, and the first is already finished.
- **On-screen text moment:** "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."
- **Lower third:** Deciding is done · today you route

### Shot 3: The Five Doors, with the bias drawn in (hero device)
- The five labeled doors, sized to show the lean. DONATE is wide and green; RECYCLE is a good door beside it; the stream from the out box pours mostly through those two. SELL and STORE are narrow side doors, nearly closed. THROW is smallest, taking only the clearly finished.
- Overlay the quick cascade as a branch: broken → Recycle or Throw; genuinely worth selling → Sell, with a deadline; truly needed later → Store, labeled and dated; still useful → Donate, the wide default lane.

- **Lower third:** The Five Doors: Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw

### Shot 4: Donate is the default
- A hand drops usable things into a donation bag, then carries the bag out to the car. The corner clears behind it. Optional contrast insert: the same object left in a bag by the stairs, gathering a little dust, dimming, to sell "a plan that never quite arrives."
- The point: the usable exit that actually completes the same day. Giving it away is not waste; it is value moving to where it gets used.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Donate is the default." then "You are not throwing value away."
- **Lower third:** Donate: the exit that actually completes

### Shot 5: The two slow doors, each with its guardrail
- The narrow SELL door with a single item at it and a written sell-by date beside it; an arrow loops the unsold item to the donate bag when the date passes. Then the narrow STORE door with one labeled, dated box going in, and a rented locker crossed out.
- Sell the rule: both feel responsible, both are slow, and each needs a fence, a deadline on selling and a label and date on storing.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job." then "Storing is not deciding."
- **Lower third:** The slow doors: a deadline on Sell, a label and date on Store

### Shot 6: Recycle and Throw, without guilt
- A hand turns a frayed cable over, then sets it calmly into an e-waste box; old menus go to paper recycling; a cracked item goes into the bin. Nothing dramatic, nothing flinched at.
- Sell the reframe: do the responsible thing where it is easy, and let the guilt stay in the bin. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store, not at the trash can.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt." then "The waste happened at the store."
- **Lower third:** Recycle and Throw: do the easy right thing, without guilt

### Shot 7: A routing pass on the out box (worked example)

## ON-SCREEN TEXT BANK (pull quotes, verbatim from the chapter)

- "You are not here to decide anything today. You are here to route."
- "Deciding a thing is unnecessary and deciding where it should go are two separate acts, and you already finished the first."
- "Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months."
- "You are not throwing value away. You are moving it to where it will finally get used, and taking your space back in the bargain."
- "Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date."
- "The deadline is what keeps a sale from quietly becoming permanent storage with a price tag on it."
- "Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need."
- "Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done."
- "There is no virtue in living among broken objects."
- "The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished."

## EDITING NOTES

- This is the closing chapter of Sort, and the payoff is physical: the out box actually leaves the house. Build toward that departure, then let the empty corner and the moving needle land the milestone.
- Everything in the box is already decided. Do NOT stage any re-deciding, any Use Test, or any red-tagging on camera; that was Chapters 8 through 10. The only action this chapter is routing what is already in the out box.
- Do NOT arrange the keepers or give anything a home. The keepers stay where they landed; giving them places is Chapter 12, Part Four (Straighten). Use the Chapter 12 teaser as a forward look only.
- Draw the bias, do not just say it. In every doors shot, DONATE and RECYCLE should visibly take most of the stream, while SELL and STORE stay narrow and nearly empty.
- Keep the tone anti-shame and non-prescriptive. Donating is not waste, nobody has to sell anything, and worn-out things belong in the bin without guilt. Never frame any door as a failure.
- Avoid force or violence framing: no purge montage, no aggressive dumping, no "war on clutter." The box is carried out calmly, in a single trip.
- The friction-meter shot in Shot 9 must read as a real MILESTONE MOVE, not a jump to the finish. The needle steps to a marked "Sort complete" tick; Chapter 10's held position becomes the dashed ghost behind it; the GOAL crosshair stays ahead on the green side, because five S's remain.
- Color and warmth should match Chapters 1 through 10: warm paper tones, honey accents, green for the wide Donate door and the calm side of the meter, slate for data overlays. Keep the Recycle and Throw beats matter-of-fact and light.
