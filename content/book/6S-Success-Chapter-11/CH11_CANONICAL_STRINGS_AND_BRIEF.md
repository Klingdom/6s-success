## Hero device (frozen): The Five Doors Out
Every unnecessary thing leaves through one of five doors: **Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw.** A quick question picks the door for each item. The bias is deliberate and strong: most things go through the Donate door, fast.
Routing cascade (frozen order):
- Broken or truly unusable? → **Recycle** it if you can, otherwise **Throw** it.
- Genuinely valuable and worth your time to sell? → **Sell** (with a deadline).
- Genuinely needed later, just not now? → **Store** (labeled, dated, elsewhere).
- Still useful to someone? → **Donate.** This is where most of the box goes.

## The three frozen teaching lines
- **Donate is the default (frozen):** Donate is the default. Most usable things you do not need should go to someone who does, today, not to a someday sale that keeps them in your house for months.
- **The sell trap (frozen):** Selling turns clearing into a part-time job. Sell only what is genuinely worth the work, put a deadline on it, and donate whatever has not sold by the date.
- **The store trap (frozen):** Storing is not deciding. Store only what you will truly use later, label it and date it, and never rent space to keep what you do not need.
- **The throw reframe (frozen, sunk-cost callback to Ch 9):** Some things are simply worn out, and throwing them away is not the waste. The waste, if there ever was any, happened at the store. Recycle what you can, bin the rest without guilt, and be done.

## Worked example (routing the out box)
Route the entryway out box, item by item, and notice how few need the slow doors:
- **The two extra umbrellas:** still perfectly usable, so → **Donate.**
- **The wad of surplus tote bags:** usable, so → **Donate** (the worn ones → Recycle).
- **The spare key to the sold car:** opens nothing, worth nothing, not usable by anyone → **Recycle** (scrap metal) or **Throw**.
- **The old takeout menus:** paper → **Recycle.**
- **The dead charging cable:** e-waste → **Recycle** (electronics/e-waste, not the landfill).
Result: everything goes through Donate or Recycle. Nothing was worth selling, nothing needed storing, and the whole box is out of the house in one trip. That is the normal shape of an out box, and it is why Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes.
(Name when Sell/Store genuinely apply, briefly: a real-value item you are replacing, or gear you truly use next season, each with its guardrail. But the entryway box needed neither.)

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> The best destination is the one that actually empties the box. A thing given away today beats a thing you mean to sell someday. Choose the door that gets it out of the house, and Sort is finished.

## Definition box (frozen)
> Routing the out box means sending each unnecessary thing out through one of five doors, Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw, and choosing the door that actually gets it gone. Donate and Recycle are the fast lanes and take most of the box; Sell and Store are the slow doors, worth it only for the rare item that truly earns them.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Grab three things from the out box and get them out of the house today. The usable ones into a donation bag in your car; the broken one to recycling or the bin. Three things gone for good in five minutes.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone picks one thing they have been meaning to sell someday and says honestly whether they will do the work this week. If not, it goes in the donate bag. Nobody has to sell anything; everybody has to be honest about it.
- **6S Tip:** Put the donation bag in your car now, not by the door. A bag by the door gets unpacked; a bag in the car gets dropped off.
- **Common Mistake:** The someday sale and the just-in-case storage. Both feel responsible and both are ways of not finishing, because they keep the clutter in your life while you tell yourself you dealt with it.
