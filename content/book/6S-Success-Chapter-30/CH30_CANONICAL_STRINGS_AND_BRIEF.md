## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The rule (frozen):** A 6S home is never finished, only renewed. Put the next event on the calendar before this one ends, and keeping becomes a loop that comes back around on its own instead of a project that quietly stops.

- **The three cadences (frozen):** Keeping a home runs on three clocks: the daily times that take a few minutes, the regular check-in that catches drift in one, and the occasional 6S event that renews the whole area from the top. The small ones hold the line day to day; the event is what lifts the home back to its best and sets the bar a little higher.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A 6S event is a scheduled return to an area you have already done: a short, whole-area re-run of the six S's that clears what has crept back in, refreshes the standards, and raises the bar a little. It is the occasional renewal that keeps a good home from slowly becoming an old one, and its most important step is choosing the date of the next one.

- **The loop-not-line reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** You have been picturing 6S as a line with an end: you sort, you straighten, you shine, and one day you are done. But a home is never done, because life keeps arriving. 6S is not a line but a loop, and the last step of every turn is simply deciding when the next turn begins.

- **The pursue-perfection reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Perfection in a home is not a place you arrive at and stay. It is a direction you keep facing. Each 6S event does not only restore the home to where it was, it lifts it a little higher, because you always notice something this time that you were not ready to notice last time. You do not chase a finish line, you keep raising the one you have.

- **The full-circle reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The first area you ever chose was the hardest, because all of it was new. Choosing your next one is different, because you already know how. The six S's are no longer a book you are following but a way of seeing that you carry into any room, any drawer, any garage, for as long as you live there.

- **The friction caption (frozen, governs the meter figure):** The needle rests on the goal at last. Six S's are complete, the home sits in the calm it was always meant to have, and the anti-backslide catch holds it there. The dial does not so much stop here as begin to turn again, because the goal, once reached, becomes the bar you renew and gently raise, one 6S event at a time.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A home worth living in is not one you finish once. It is one you know how to renew. You know how now, so put the next turn on the calendar and keep going.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** a loop, not a line / renew, do not finish / schedule the next one before this one ends. Chapters 26 to 29 gave keeping its mindset and its daily and regular rhythms; Chapter 30 gives it the periodic renewal that closes the loop and completes all six S's.

## The four callouts (frozen)

- **Quick Win:** Before you close this book, open your calendar and put one date on it: your next 6S event. Pick an area you have already done, name the day, and the loop has begun.
- **Family Challenge:** Together, choose the next area and the next date, and write both where the whole household can see them. Let whoever is keenest own the invitation, and plan something small and good at the end of the event, because a renewal the family looks forward to is one that actually comes back around.
- **6S Tip:** Schedule the next event at the end of this one, while the area is fresh in everyone's mind and the calendar is open. Tie it to a season or a repeating date so it never has to be remembered, only kept.
- **Common Mistake:** Treating 6S as finished the moment the home looks good. With no next event booked, the home drifts quietly back over a year, and the whole loop has to be started cold again instead of simply renewed. The opposite mistake is making the event so large and so rare that it becomes a dreaded overhaul rather than a light, regular return.
