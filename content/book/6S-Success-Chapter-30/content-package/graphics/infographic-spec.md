# Infographic Spec: Your Next 6S Event

**Chapter 30, "Your Next 6S Event"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain · THE FINALE**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter, and the closing image of the whole book's main arc. It
carries the whole idea in one glance: you have been picturing 6S as a line with an end, but a home is never
done because life keeps arriving, so 6S is not a line but a loop, and the last step of every turn is simply
deciding when the next turn begins. The concept pairs the hero rule, **A 6S home is never finished, only
renewed. Put the next event on the calendar before this one ends, and keeping becomes a loop that comes back
around on its own instead of a project that quietly stops.**, with the picture at its heart: **the same six
steps, two shapes**, the same six-S journey drawn twice, on the left as a line, a straight arrow through the
six S's to a planted "DONE" flag with the home graying and drifting behind it, on the right as a loop, the same
six S's around a gently turning circle whose final step curves back to the first with a small returning arrow
"choose the next area", the home bright and settled at the center. So the viewer sees the whole move at once,
that a home treated as a project has an end and past that end it drifts, and a home treated as a loop is renewed
each turn and stays.

It stays consistent with the four inline figures in `chapter_30_final.html`. The two sides are unmistakably the
same six steps; cool slate carries the ending line and the graying home, warm terracotta and soft green carry
the loop and its bright home, and the returning arrow is terracotta so the eye reads the last step curving home
to the first; the single small warm red is held back for the smallest marks only, because this is a gift of a
loop, not a hazard. "Finished" is not the point, and neither is "discipline"; a home whose keeping runs as a
loop is renewed again and again rather than declared done once and left to age. Anti-shame throughout: the
drift past the flag is not a failing of the reader's systems, it is simply what a lived-in home is, because
life keeps arriving; the danger is the single quiet thought "I am finished", and the whole method removes it by
turning keeping into a loop. The register is warm, proud, and hopeful, never corporate, never triumphant: you
have not arrived at the end of the work, you have arrived at the point where the work renews itself, which is a
far better place to be. Renew, do not finish.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: a 6S home is never
  finished, only renewed, so put the next event on the calendar before this one ends, and keeping becomes a
  loop that comes back around on its own. The eye should read the rule, then see the same six steps, two shapes
  make it real, the left side a line that ends at a planted flag with the home graying behind it, the right side
  a loop whose last step curves home to the first.
- The same six steps, two shapes is the hero, so make the two sides unmistakably the same six steps: the same
  six-S journey, only the shape different, a line that ends on the left and a loop that renews on the right.
  Keep the "as a line" home only gently aging past the flag, never ruined or filthy, a cushion askew, a surface
  filming over, the quiet drift of a home nobody comes back to; keep the "as a loop" home bright, calm, and
  settled at the center of the turning circle, with the returning arrow the emphasised thing, the last step
  curving home to the first. Set the rule, the definition, and the three cadences byte-for-byte as they read in
  the chapter; they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: cool slate carries the ending line and the graying home, warm terracotta and green
  carry the loop and its bright home, the returning arrow terracotta so the eye reads the loop closing; the
  single small warm red is reserved for the smallest marks and is barely used, because this image is a gift of
  a loop and not a live hazard.
- This image makes the concept, the reframe that closes the book: 6S is not a line that ends but a loop that
  renews. Do NOT re-teach the first five S's, do NOT re-teach the mindset, audit, problem-solving, or daily
  rhythm of Chapters 26 to 29 (used but not re-explained), and do NOT teach the specifics of Part Nine
  (Room-by-Room Playbooks), which is named only as where the reader points the loop next. And name no brand and
  no calendar app and draw no specific product; the flag, the calendar, the loop, and the home are plain and
  generic, teaching the idea, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: the drift past the flag is not a failing of the reader's systems, it is simply what a lived-in
  home is, because life keeps arriving; the danger is the single quiet thought "I am finished", and the whole
  method removes it by turning keeping into a loop. What you are building is not a stricter finish but a gentler
  return, a loop that comes back around on its own. Keep the register warm, proud, and hopeful, a home renewed
  again and again rather than declared done and left to age; no force, control, or discipline-as-punishment
  language, no scolding, no hype, no smugness or victory-lap swagger. Use "finished" ONLY as the quiet thought
  the loop removes.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the two sides drawn by hand as
  the same six-S journey rather than clinical thumbnails, the line side a plain arrow to a planted flag with the
  home quietly graying behind it and the loop side a gently turning circle with a bright, settled home at the
  center, the returning arrow a small terracotta accent, the whole image the calm, warm, proud, and hopeful
  register of a finale: not a home you finish once, but a home you know how to renew.
