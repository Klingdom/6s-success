seeing you carry into any room for as long as you live there). CRITICAL fence: this is the PERIODIC RENEWAL
EVENT, the slowest clock, an afternoon every season or so that renews the whole area from the top, NOT the
daily rhythm of Chapter 29, NOT the friendly audit of Chapter 27, NOT the shared problem-solving of Chapter
28. It USES the mindset, audit, problem-solving, and daily rhythm of Chapters 26 to 29 without re-teaching any
of them, and it names Part Nine (Room-by-Room Playbooks) only as where the reader points the loop next.
Because keeping has finally become a loop that renews itself, the friction needle takes its LAST tiny move and
comes to rest EXACTLY on the goal: this IS the milestone, "SUSTAIN COMPLETE", all six S's, the calm the dial
has pointed at since the first chapter. The register is celebratory but quiet, warm, proud, and hopeful: a
finale with no hype and no smugness, the calm relief of a home that now stays good on its own, and the gentle
turn that the goal, once reached, becomes the bar the loop renews and raises a little each time. Separator
discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 30 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating
dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: line-vs-loop, loop-not-line, six-S, whole-area,
re-run, day-to-day, check-in, launch-pad, full-circle, pursue-perfection, self-renewing, three-cadences,
rising-bar, last-gap, three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete,

## 1. As a Line, As a Loop (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole argument in one image, before a word is read. The same six-S journey,
drawn twice, so the only variable the eye reads is its shape: a line that ends and quietly decays, or a loop
that renews itself. On the LEFT, as a line: the six S's laid out as a straight left-to-right arrow to a small
planted flag marked `DONE`, and past the flag, where nothing more happens, the home drawn beginning to gray
and drift, a cushion askew, a surface filming over, because the line simply stopped. On the RIGHT, as a loop:
the same six S's arranged around a gently turning circle, each flowing into the next, the final step curving
back to the first with a small returning arrow `choose the next area`, and at the center the home bright and
settled, kept fresh because the loop keeps coming around. Same six steps, two shapes. Mirrors Figure 1 in the
HTML.

**What it shows:** The same six-S journey, drawn two ways. The LEFT scene, `AS A LINE`, shows Sort, Straighten,
Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain as a straight arrow ending at a small planted `DONE` flag, and behind the
flag the home quietly graying and drifting because the line has ended. The RIGHT scene, `AS A LOOP`, shows the
same six S's around a gently turning circle, each flowing into the next, the final step curving back to the
first with a small returning arrow `choose the next area`, the home at the center bright and settled, renewed
each turn.

**Rough layout:**
- A soft warm wash on paper cream `#F7F2E9` falling into panel `#FBF7EF`. A faint Newsreader italic line,
  muted `#6A625A`: `the same six steps, two shapes`.
- LEFT scene eyebrow, cool slate sans caps: `AS A LINE`. The six S's as a straight arrow to a planted `DONE`
  flag, the home behind it graying and drifting, with a Newsreader italic line beneath in slate: `you finish,
  you plant the flag, and behind it the home begins its slow drift`.
- RIGHT scene eyebrow, warm terracotta sans caps: `AS A LOOP`. The six S's around a gently turning circle, the
  final step curving back to the first with a small terracotta returning arrow `choose the next area`, the home
  bright and settled at the center, with a Newsreader italic line beneath in green: `the last step curves back
  to the first, the home stays bright, and keeping never has to be started cold again`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "The same six steps, drawn two ways. On the left, as a line: you finish,
  you plant the flag, and behind it the home begins its slow drift because nothing follows 'done'. On the
  right, as a loop: the last step curves back to the first, the home stays bright, and keeping never has to be
  started cold again. This whole book has been teaching you the loop. This chapter closes it."

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is that the identical six-S journey can take two completely different
shapes, so draw the two scenes unmistakably the same six steps, only the shape different, a line that ends on
the left and a loop that renews on the right. Keep the "as a line" home only gently aging past the flag, never
ruined or filthy: a cushion askew, a surface filming over, the quiet drift of a home nobody comes back to, not
a disaster. Keep the "as a loop" home bright, calm, and settled at the center of the turning circle, and draw
the returning arrow clearly, the last step curving home to the first. Hold the color discipline: cool slate
for the ending line and the graying home, warm terracotta and green for the loop and its bright home, the
returning arrow terracotta; spark-red barely appears if at all. Warm line-and-flat-color style, the register
of a quiet before-and-after that turns entirely on whether keeping is drawn as a line that stops or a loop
that comes back around.

## 2. The Event, and the Next One (the 6S event, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's hero as one picture: a 6S event is simply one more trip around the six S's
the reader already knows, on an area they have already done, so it is light rather than daunting, and its
decisive final step is booking the next event, which is what turns a single pass into a self-renewing loop.
The value is in the enlarged final step and the quiet rising bars. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A single 6S event drawn as a compact, friendly loop badge: the six S's, Sort, Straighten,
Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain, arranged around a small circle, each a little icon, showing an area
carried briskly around all six once more. The final position on the circle, where Sustain hands back to the
start, is highlighted and enlarged as a small calendar page with the next date circled in warm terracotta and
labelled `schedule the next one`. Beside the loop, a small stepped motif of three short rising bars, each
slightly higher than the last, labelled lightly `each turn lifts the bar`, pictures pursue-perfection without
words. The loop is drawn in calm slate, the highlighted final step and the rising bars in warm accent.

  being carried briskly around all six once more.

## 3. The Three Cadences (the synthesis of Sustain, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the whole of Sustain in one picture: three practices, each on a different clock, together
covering every rate at which a home can drift. The value is that the reader sees the daily times, the
check-in, and the event not as three competing chores but as one layered rhythm, tiny-and-frequent at the top
down to bigger-and-rare at the bottom. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The three cadences of keeping drawn as three nested rhythms, stacked to show how they
relate. At the TOP, the fastest and lightest: a short repeating tick labelled `Daily times, a few minutes,
every day`, drawn small and frequent. In the MIDDLE: a slower beat labelled `The check-in, a few minutes,
weekly or monthly`, drawn as a gentle regular pulse. At the BOTTOM, the slowest and deepest: a single broad
mark labelled `The 6S event, an afternoon, seasonal or occasional`, drawn larger but rare. A simple vertical
axis hints at depth (how much each renews) and a horizontal one at frequency (how often), so the eye reads
tiny-and-frequent at the top down to bigger-and-rare at the bottom. Across the foot, a single line reads
`three clocks, one calm home`.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`, warm and homely. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft: `THE
  THREE CADENCES`.
- Three nested rows, top to bottom, faster-and-lighter down to slower-and-deeper:
  - top, lightest honey `#DDA63A`, a short frequent tick: `Daily times, a few minutes, every day`
  - middle, a mid tone, a gentle regular pulse: `The check-in, a few minutes, weekly or monthly`
  - bottom, settled slate `#3C5A6B`, a single broad rare mark: `The 6S event, an afternoon, seasonal or
    occasional`

- A line across the foot in Fraunces slate: `three clocks, one calm home`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "Three clocks, one calm home. The daily times catch the drift of

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the synthesis figure of the whole part, so keep it calm and legible, the
