2/
The moment you decide you are finished is the moment you stop, and a home that is no longer being renewed is a home that has quietly begun to age. It is not clutter or a missed hazard that starts the slow slide back. It is a single ticked checkbox.

3/
"You have been picturing 6S as a line with an end: you sort, you straighten, you shine, and one day you are done. But a home is never done, because life keeps arriving. 6S is not a line but a loop, and the last step of every turn is simply deciding when the next turn begins."

(279 chars)

4/
Life keeps arriving: a birthday brings a flood of new toys, a new job changes what waits by the door, a child moves up a shoe size and the boots no longer fit the shelf. None of it is a crisis. A line has no answer for it, because a line has ended. A loop does.

(264 chars)

5/
"A 6S event is a scheduled return to an area you have already done: a short, whole-area re-run of the six S's that clears what has crept back in, refreshes the standards, and raises the bar a little." Its most important step is choosing the date of the next one.

(265 chars)

6/
The word "event" can sound daunting, but it is not the dreaded whole-weekend overhaul. Because the area is already done and the daily rhythm has held it, an event is light: an afternoon, or even an hour, carrying one area briskly back around the six S's you know by heart.

(275 chars)

7/
Picture an event on the entryway, a Saturday in early autumn. Summer hats go to storage, dead post is recycled, the outgrown bag gets a bigger hook at the child's new height, the standard photo is updated. In under an hour it is not merely tidy, it is renewed.

(263 chars)

8/
"That is a 6S event: not a reckoning, just a good turn around a loop you already know." Light rather than daunting, because you have walked this loop before, on this very area, and this time you are only renewing it.

(219 chars)

9/
"Keeping a home runs on three clocks: the daily times that take a few minutes, the regular check-in that catches drift in one, and the occasional 6S event that renews the whole area from the top." Together they cover every rate at which a home can drift.

(257 chars)

10/
Each clock catches what the faster one lets through. The daily times keep a surface clear but never notice a category outgrowing its shelf. The check-in flags it but cannot re-home it. Only the event does that deeper work. "They are one rhythm, layered."

(258 chars)

11/
The habit the whole chapter turns on is so small it is easy to skip: schedule the next event before you finish this one, in the last minutes while everyone stands in the renewed space. "We should do this again sometime" is a line hoping to bend. A written date is a loop.

(275 chars)

12/
Hang each event on a marker the year already has: the first warm weekend of spring, the week before school. "Perfection in a home is not a place you arrive at and stay. It is a direction you keep facing." Each turn lifts the bar, because you notice more.

13/
