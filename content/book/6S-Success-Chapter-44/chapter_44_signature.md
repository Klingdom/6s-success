# Chapter 44 Signature: The Home Office

The Part 9 recurring close. The friction meter is retired (it reached GOAL at Chapter 30); each room playbook ends instead on a matched before-and-after room signature: the before kept per Chapter 6, the after held as the ideal-state standard per Chapter 25.

## The before-and-after room signature
Stand in the doorway. In one frame, capture the desk, the shelves, and the filing cabinet. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and do not clear the desk first, because the desk as it actually is on an ordinary Tuesday is the whole point of the picture. Take the after from the same spot.

## The variation unique to this room
This room's after photo does not go on a wall. It is printed and taped **inside the top drawer**, and it is matched **every evening when the laptop lid closes** rather than at the change of season. That makes it the highest-frequency standard in the book, and it is not an invention: it is the Manual's own Zone 1 Standardize pass ("photograph the cleared desk and tape the print inside the top drawer, so the word clear stops being a matter of opinion in this household") and its Sustain trigger ("reset the surface to match the photo before you stand up. Doing it next morning is too late, because by then the pile has been sitting there since last night and reads as normal").

Compare with the variations used so far: Ch 39 the child takes the after photo; Ch 40 the after is the nightly safe state; Ch 43 the after contains one detail that expires. This one is about **frequency**, not content.

## The before-and-after caption (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> This is the home office the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the desk clear enough to start work on without moving anything, the power strip up off the floor, the drawers holding pens that all write, the folders named for the question you would ask, a hand's width of empty shelf at the end of every row, and nothing in the room that is not office work. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard. Then do what this room asks that no other room in the book does: print the after and tape it inside the top drawer, because in here the standard is not checked at the change of season, it is checked every evening when you close the laptop lid.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> A home office is not clear because you tidied it. It is clear because you stopped storing decisions in it: you ruled on the cables in one sitting, you let the wet cloth nowhere near the electronics, you cleared one surface and defended it, and you made everything that was not office work leave through the door it came in. Do one zone this week, and make it the desk, because every other zone gets emptied onto it. Then photograph it clear and tape the picture inside the top drawer.
