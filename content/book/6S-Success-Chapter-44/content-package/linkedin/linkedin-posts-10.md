# Chapter 44 LinkedIn Posts: The Home Office

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. It is not dirty, it is undecided
Nothing in your home office is heavy. Nothing in it is dirty. No grease, no lint, no damp. By every measure that made the other rooms hard, it should be the easiest room in the house.

It is almost certainly the one you have left the longest.

"This room is not made of objects. It is made of decisions you have not taken."

## 2. The heaviest object in a house
A cable is not hard to lift. It is hard to rule on. A book from a job you left weighs nothing and costs an enormous amount to release. This is why the advice to just tidy up fails here: tidying moves things, and nothing in this room is waiting to be moved. It is waiting to be judged.

## 3. Why the cable drawer survives
Every home office has one, and it survives because each individual cable is too cheap to think about and too specific to throw away. That decision cannot be made one cable at a time. Empty the whole lot onto the desk at once, pick up each one, name the device it powers, and point at where that device physically is in this house. No device, no keep.

## 4. Batch, or it never happens
The move that unlocks this room is refusing to decide one item at a time. The whole cable drawer in one sitting. Every book off the shelf in one go. The entire supply cabinet lined up so you can see the true count. Made in one batch at a cleared surface, these are an afternoon. Made one item at a time, they never get made at all.

## 5. The one room where the damp cloth is the hazard
In every other room, water is your ally. Here it is the risk, because everything is electronics or paper. Power down and let it cool. Spray onto the cloth, never onto a screen, glass, or vent, because liquid sprayed at a machine runs down inside and finds the electronics. Use a dry cloth on toner dust, because water turns loose toner into ink.

## 6. The desk is a tool, not just a zone
It is the only zone in the book that is also a piece of equipment. Every other zone gets emptied onto it: the drawer you tip out, the shelf you strip, the cabinet you count. So it is not first because it is the most visible. It is first because nothing else in the room can be done properly without it.

## 7. The door that closes
Look at what is in your office that is not office work. The broken lamp. The unopened box from the last move. None of it is there because the office needed it. It is there because this is the room with a door that can be shut, and shutting it makes a decision disappear from view without ever being made.

## 8. Information is not a record
Most of a filing drawer is neither obviously permanent nor obviously rubbish, and that middle band is why the job stalls. Here is the test that clears it: if the issuer will reprint it on request, or it already lives inside an online account, it is information, not a record. And information does not get a folder.

## 9. The shelf that says who you used to be
The textbooks from a degree. The professional library from a job you left. These are the hardest books in the house to release, because they stand for a version of you that you have not formally retired. Ask when you last took it down, not whether you might one day.

## 10. Taped inside a drawer
"A home office is not clear because you tidied it. It is clear because you stopped storing decisions in it."

Photograph the cleared desk and tape it inside the top drawer, because this standard is not checked at the change of season. It is checked tonight.
