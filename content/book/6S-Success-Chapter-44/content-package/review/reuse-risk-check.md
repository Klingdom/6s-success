# Chapter 44 Reuse Risk Check: The Home Office

## Source provenance
Every zone, its purpose, its passes, its hard call, its watch-for items, and its shine detail are folded from the Micro Zone Manual's Home Office entry, which is the project's own material. The six calls are the Manual's `the_call` entries. The room's intro and its three tips supplied the layer: judgments rather than lifts, the wet cloth as the risk, clear the desk first, and the door that closes. Nothing in this chapter is drawn from an outside author, method, or publication.

## Third-party IP
- No quotations from any external book, article, or speaker.
- No named methodology other than 6S itself and the Toyota lineage the book already establishes.
- No rival step-systems introduced.
- No brand names, product names, model numbers, or retailer names anywhere in the package.
- No logos, no QR codes, no readable screen content or packaging text called for in any image prompt.
- The b-roll notes explicitly forbid capturing real names, addresses, account numbers, or identifiable documents in the filing sequence.

## The legal-adjacent content, and how it is fenced
This is the first room chapter that touches document retention, which is jurisdictional and changes over time. The fence is absolute and worth restating:

- **No retention period is ever stated.** Not in the manuscript, not in the HTML, not in any of the 52 platform files.
- **No tax rule, no jurisdiction, and no document class is declared legally required.** The chapter says tax paper gets one folder per year "held for whatever your local retention period is, which you look up once and write on the card".
- The list of permanent originals (certificates, deeds, titles, wills, immigration papers) is offered as the category most people would want in a fireproof box, not as a legal schedule.
- The reprint test ("if the issuer will reprint it on request, or it already lives inside an online account, it is information, not a record") is a practical heuristic, framed as such.

If the book is ever localised, this chapter needs no rewriting, only a companion card per territory. That was the design intent.

## Claims that need to stay defensible
1. Liquid sprayed at a screen, scanner, or vent can run inside and reach the electronics. Stated qualitatively.
2. Water turns loose toner into ink and makes the mess worse. Stated as a cleaning fact.
3. Paper is a significant fuel load and belongs clear of heaters and power strips. Stated qualitatively, with no statistics.
4. Tall loaded furniture tips forward when a top drawer is pulled fully out. Stated as the reason for anchoring.
5. A swallowed button battery is a medical emergency. Stated plainly as the reason for a storage rule, with no treatment advice given.

No safety statistic was invented to strengthen any of these.

## Liability posture
- No device or appliance repair instructions anywhere. Cleaning, looking, and flagging only.
- No electrical work. The power strip is moved and hooked, never rewired.
- No instruction to stand on anything other than a stable step stool, and the b-roll notes forbid depicting anyone on a rolling chair.
- No medical advice beyond naming a button battery as an emergency.

## Verdict
Low risk, and the retention fence is the most important thing in this file. Own source material throughout, no external IP, no invented statistics, no legal or jurisdictional claims, and no instruction that crosses from cleaning into repair.
