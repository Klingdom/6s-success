# Chapter 44 Facebook Longform Post: The Home Office

---

Here is why the home office beats almost everybody.

Nothing in it is heavy. Nothing in it is dirty. There is no grease, no lint, no damp, none of the things that made the kitchen or the bathroom hard work. By every ordinary measure it should be the easiest room in the house.

And it is almost certainly the one you have left the longest.

The reason is that this room is not made of objects. It is made of decisions nobody has taken. Every single thing in it asks a question before it will move. What does this cable actually power, and is that thing still in the house? Are you the person who reads this book now? That project in the corner you have not touched in six weeks: is it live, or is it over?

None of those questions are hard to lift. All of them are hard to answer, and an unanswered question is the heaviest object a house can hold.

Which leads to the one move that unlocks this room: stop deciding one item at a time.

The cable drawer is the perfect example. It survives for years because each cable is too cheap to think about and too specific to throw away, so you never rule on any of them. Empty the whole lot onto a cleared desk at once instead. Pick up each one, name the device it powers, and point at where that device physically is in this house. No device, no keep.

Made in one batch, that is a single sitting. Made one cable at a time, it never gets made at all. The same goes for the bookshelf, the supply cabinet, and the filing drawer.

Two more things worth knowing.

First, this is the one room in the book where the damp cloth is the hazard rather than the remedy. Everything here is electronics or paper. Power it down, let it cool, and spray onto the cloth, never onto a screen, glass, or vent, because liquid sprayed at a machine runs down inside and finds the electronics.

Second, look at what is in your office that is not office work. The broken lamp. The unopened box from the last move. It is there for one reason: this is the room with a door that closes, and shutting a door makes a decision disappear from view without ever being made.

Do one zone this week, and make it the desk, because every other zone gets emptied onto it. Then photograph it clear and tape the picture inside the top drawer.
