# Chapter 44 CTA and Companion Resources: The Home Office

## Primary call to action
Do one zone this week, and make it the desk, because every other zone in this room gets emptied onto it. Then photograph it clear and tape the picture inside the top drawer.

## Companion resources
- **The Home Office Reset card** (printable and phone wallpaper: the six zones, the done-looks-like for each, and the desk-first order of work).
- **The Home Office Kit list** (printable: the cleaners, tools, and storage types the room needs, grouped by job, with the spray-the-cloth rule called out).
- **The Batch Decision card** (the four batching calls in one page: the cable drawer, the shelf, the supply count, and the paper split, each with its test).
- **The Retention Card** (the three-way paper split to fill in once and tape inside the top drawer: permanent originals, tax years, and the reprint test).

## Register and fences
- Warm, practical, expert, and non-judgemental about a room almost everyone has lost. Product types only, never brand names. No invented statistics or prices.
- Never give appliance or device repair instructions. This chapter cleans, looks, and flags.
- Retention periods are named as a thing the reader looks up for their own country, never stated as a number.
- Friction meter retired for Part 9; close on the before-and-after room signature, which in this room is taped inside a drawer and matched nightly.

## Words to avoid
- No brand names (product types only). No specific legal retention periods, tax rules, or jurisdictions. No advice to open, dismantle, or service a device.
