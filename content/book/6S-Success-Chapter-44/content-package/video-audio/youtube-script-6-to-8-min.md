# Chapter 44 YouTube Script: The Home Office

*Six to eight minutes.*

---

**[Cold open, on the room]**

The home office should be the easiest room in your house to sort out. Nothing in it is heavy. Nothing in it is dirty. And it is almost certainly the one you have left the longest.

**[Title]**

**[Section 1: why it stalls]**

The reason is that this room is not made of objects. It is made of decisions nobody has taken.

Every thing in here asks you a question before it will move. What does this cable power, and is that thing still in the house. Are you the person who reads this book now. Are you required to keep this piece of paper.

None of those are hard to lift. All of them are hard to answer. And an unanswered question is the heaviest object a house can hold.

That is also why normal tidying advice fails here. Tidying moves things. Nothing in this room is waiting to be moved. It is waiting to be judged.

**[Section 2: batching]**

So here is the move that unlocks it. Stop deciding one item at a time.

Take the cable drawer. It survives for years because each cable is too cheap to think about and too specific to throw away, so you pick one up, think "not sure what that goes to", and put it back. Every time.

Instead: empty the entire drawer onto a cleared desk in one heap. Pick up each cable, name the device it powers, and point at where that device physically is in this house. No device, no keep. Then one spare of each connector you still use, and the rest to electronics recycling.

Same with the shelf. Every book off in one go. Same with the supply cabinet. Everything out and lined up, because a dozen packs of sticky notes look like nothing until they are side by side.

**[Section 3: six zones]**

Six zones: the desk, the drawers and pedestal, the file storage, the bookshelf, the printer station, the supply cabinet.

The desk comes first, and not because it is the most visible. It is the only zone in this book that is also a tool. Every other zone gets emptied onto it. Clear it completely before you open a single drawer, then defend it, because the moment it refills you have lost your only workbench.

And one pacing warning: give the file storage a fresh morning of its own. It is hundreds of keep-or-shred calls in a row, and tired judgment is what stalls it.

**[Section 4: the wet cloth]**

Now the practical bit that saves your equipment.

This is the one room in the whole book where the damp cloth is the hazard rather than the remedy, because everything here is electronics or paper.

Power down and let it cool. Spray onto the cloth, never onto a screen, a scanner glass, or a vent, because liquid sprayed at a machine runs down inside and finds the electronics. Wring the cloth almost dry before it goes near a keyboard. And on toner dust, dry cloth only, because water turns loose toner into ink.

**[Section 5: the door]**

Then there is the thing nobody says out loud.

Look at what is in your office that is not office work. The broken lamp. The unopened box from the last move. It is all there for one reason: this is the room with a door that closes, and shutting a door makes a decision disappear from view without ever being made.

That is not a storage problem. All of it walks out today, because anything that stays past today stops being temporary and becomes furniture.

**[Close]**

Take a before photo. Work the six zones. Take the after.

But this room's after does not go on a wall. It goes inside the top drawer, and you match it every evening when you close the laptop, not at the change of season. Reset before you stand up, because by the morning the pile has been there since last night and reads as normal.

Do one zone this week, and make it the desk. Chapter 44. Next: the garage.
