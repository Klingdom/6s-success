# Back Cover Copy: Shine: Cleaning as Inspection

## Back-cover text

You have done a great deal to this room, and you have not once cleaned it on purpose. Sort emptied the space of what did not belong. Straighten gave every keeper a home, placed each by reach, taught each to speak, and gathered them into one shared map. On paper the entryway is finished. But every step so far was about where things go, not the condition of anything, and "the room is cleared and arranged. It has never been inspected." A room that has never been inspected is a room full of small secrets: the umbrella stand quietly holding a little rainwater and rusting its base, the key hook loosening a turn in the wall, none of it visible under the daily blur, all of it cheap to fix now and none of it staying cheap.

Chapter 16 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the first chapter of Part Five, and it opens Shine, the third of the six S's. It does not re-sort or re-arrange a single thing; it changes what cleaning is *for*. "Shine is not a chore you do so the house looks nice for other people. Shine is how the house tells you what it needs, and the only way it can tell you is if you clean it enough to see it." Set out plainly: "Shine means keeping a space clean enough to see clearly, so that the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting, and small problems show themselves early, while they are still small." The goal is not a showroom. "Spotless is not the target and never was. Seen is the target."

The whole method is five words, and it is the rule to keep from this chapter: "Don't just clean it. Read it." Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read, because a wipe does two jobs at once, it cleans the surface and it makes the surface legible, and the second job is the one that protects the house. "A clean surface is an honest one. Dirt hides the small stuff, the drip, the crack, the loose screw, the worn spot, until the small stuff becomes the expensive stuff. Clean it away and the problem shows itself while it is still cheap to fix." A short vocabulary of tells makes it concrete: a water mark means a slow leak, a scratch means something rubbing, gathering crumbs mean a gap, a loose fitting means wear.

Then one first pass proves it. Clean the launch pad properly, perhaps for the first time since you set it up, and look while you do. Lift the umbrella stand and its base is sitting in a shallow ring of rainwater, rusting where it stands. Close a hand on the key hook and it gives, because the screw has worked loose. Both are ten minutes and no cost today, and both were on a straight line to real damage. Nothing was sorted or rearranged; the pass only cleaned and read. The friction meter takes its first Shine step, off the Straighten-complete mark toward calm, and the goal is still ahead, because Shine has only begun and Safety, Standardize, and Sustain still follow. "Clean to see, not to impress." Pick one surface you use every day, clean it properly once, and look at what the cleaning uncovers. You just did your first inspection.
