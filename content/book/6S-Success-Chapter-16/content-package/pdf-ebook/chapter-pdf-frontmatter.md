# Shine: Cleaning as Inspection

**Part Five, Shine**

## How to read this chapter

This is the first chapter of Part Five, Shine, and it picks up exactly where the last one left off, with the room cleared and fully arranged into well-placed, self-explaining homes gathered into a shared map, yet never once cleaned on purpose or looked at. It is not about re-choosing whether a thing gets a home; you made those calls in Chapter 12. It is not about re-placing homes by frequency, or re-marking a home so it speaks; that was Chapters 13 and 14. It is about one job: change what cleaning is for, clean one thing on purpose, and look while you do it, so the cleaning doubles as inspecting.

Read it through once for the idea, then pick up a cloth. Follow one rule as you clean, and it is the entire method in five words: don't just clean it, read it. Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read, because a wipe does two jobs at once, it makes the surface clean and it makes the surface legible, and the second job is the one that protects the house. Learn the short vocabulary of tells so reading a surface becomes concrete: a water mark means a slow leak, a scratch means something rubbing, gathering crumbs mean a gap, a loose fitting means wear. Then do one first pass on the launch pad, the entryway zone you built by the door, and catch what it has been hiding.

Nothing here re-sorts or re-arranges; the keepers are settled and the layout stays. Nothing here lists what to clean across the whole home or divides who does what; that is Chapter 17, and we only point to it. Nothing here teaches cleaning methods, products, or tools; that is Chapter 18. Nothing here builds a repeatable routine or schedule; that is Chapter 19. Nothing re-opens Sort. Today has one job: clean one zone, on purpose, with your eyes open, and let the cleaning catch a small problem while it is still small. This is a shift in what cleaning is for, and the friction meter takes its first Shine step for it.

If you only do one thing: pick one surface you use every day and clean it properly, once. As you do, look at what the cleaning uncovers, a mark, a wobble, a worn spot you had stopped seeing. You just did your first inspection.
