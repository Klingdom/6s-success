# Infographic Spec: The Two Jobs of a Wipe

**Chapter 16, "Shine: Cleaning as Inspection"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a cleared,
arranged home is still only half cared for if no one has ever read its surfaces, so Shine keeps a space
clean enough to see clearly, and the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting, and small
problems show themselves early while they are still small. The concept pairs the hero rule, **Don't
just clean it. Read it.**, with the **two jobs of a wipe** beneath it, one motion that makes a surface
clean and, in the same pass, makes it legible so it can tell you what it needs, so the viewer sees both
the rule that turns cleaning into inspecting and the move it produces, and sees at once that the second
job, the reading, is the one that protects the home.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_16_final.html`. A single small warm
red note marks the tell a wipe uncovers, held back for that one job and used nowhere else on this calm
image; slate carries the rule, the definition, and the tags; wood fill marks the hand and cloth and
the hooks; the honest-surface logic, catch it small and it stays cheap, is the discipline the image
itself should model. "Spotless" appears only as the rejected non-goal, if it appears at all.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest element on the page. Draw it as the one plain move that turns any cleaning
  into an inspection, so the eye grasps the rule before the wipe: you clean in order to see, so the
  surface can tell you what it needs.
- Hold the color discipline: the single small warm-red note marks the tell the wipe uncovers, held
  back for that one job and used nowhere else; slate carries the rule, the definition, and the tags;
  wood fill marks the hand and cloth. This image is calm, so no fill-line red anywhere except the one
  small tell.
- The two jobs are exactly the two the chapter names: the first makes the surface clean, the second
  makes it legible, and the second is the one that protects the home. Keep the mapping one to one and
  plain, and model the chapter's own move by showing a single wipe doing both jobs at once, not two
  separate scrubs.
- This image reframes cleaning as INSPECTION and shows one wipe doing both jobs. Do NOT re-sort or
  re-arrange anything (that was Chapters 12 to 15). Do NOT build the list of what to clean across the
  home or divide the labor (Chapter 17). Do NOT teach a cleaning method, product, or tool (Chapter
  18). Do NOT build a routine or schedule (Chapter 19). Do NOT build standards or maintenance systems
  (the later Standardize and Sustain S's).
- Anti-shame: a home that has gone unread is a completely normal home, not a dirty one, and not
  cleaning on purpose is not a character flaw; the small problems under the daily blur are information
  the room has been trying to give you, not a verdict on the person. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no
  guilt cues, no pressure framing, and no force framing anywhere. "Spotless" appears only as the
  rejected non-goal, if at all; the target is a seen home, not a spotless one.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the wipe drawn as a
  real hand on a real cloth rather than a diagram, the rule set as if jotted rather than typeset.
