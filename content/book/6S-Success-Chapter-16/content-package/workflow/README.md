## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
The room is cleared (Sort) and fully arranged into zones and a shared map (Straighten), but every step so far was about where things go, not the condition of anything, and the surfaces have gone unread under the daily blur. Chapter 16 opens Shine and changes what cleaning is for. Shine means keeping a space clean enough to see clearly, so that the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting, and small problems show themselves early, while they are still small. The hero move is the two jobs of a wipe: every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read, and the reading is the job that protects the home, because dirt hides the small stuff until the small stuff turns expensive. The rule is five words: don't just clean it, read it. A short vocabulary of tells (a water mark, a scratch, gathering crumbs, a loose fitting) makes reading concrete. A first cleaning-and-inspection pass at the launch pad proves it, catching the umbrella-stand base rusting in a shallow ring of rainwater and the loose key hook, both cheap now and both expensive later. The friction meter takes its first Shine step, off the marked STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE mark toward calm, because the room's condition genuinely changed. The goal is still ahead, because Shine has only begun and Safety, Standardize, and Sustain still follow. Hands off to Chapter 17 (Shine Targets and Assignments), which continues Part Five.
