# Facebook Longform Post: The Rust I Walked Past Every Day

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 16 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the chapter that opens Shine, the third S. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes.

---

By my front door I built a little launch pad, and I was quietly proud of it. Keys on their hook, umbrella in its stand, bag in its basket, mail in its tray. Everything had a home, and every home made sense. For weeks I thought that corner was finished.

Then one wet Saturday I finally wiped it down. Not because it looked dirty. It just felt like time. And that ten minutes taught me the whole point of this chapter.

When I lifted the umbrella stand, its base was sitting in a shallow ring of rainwater that had dripped off after every wet day. The metal had started to rust, and a faint orange mark was spreading on the floor underneath. A minute later my hand closed on the key hook out of habit, and it gave, just slightly. The screw had worked loose in the wall. It was holding my keys by less than I thought.

Neither of those was hidden. I had walked past both a dozen times a day for weeks. They were invisible only because nobody had looked. That is the thing about a room you live in: your eye slides right over it, the way it slides over your own face in the mirror.

Here is the line that reframed cleaning for me completely.

"Don't just clean it. Read it."

Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read. A wipe does two jobs at once. The first makes the surface clean. The second makes it legible, and the second job is the one that protects the house. Most of us only ever do the first. We clean on autopilot and learn nothing from it.

The fix cost me nothing extra. I poured out the water, dried the base, set something under it, and turned the loose screw a half turn tight. Ten minutes, no cost. Both of those small things were on a straight line to real damage, a rusted stand and a ruined patch of floor, a hook that lets go with the keys attached, if the cleaning had never looked.

And that is Shine. Not a spotless house. A seen one.

"Clean to see, not to impress."

So pick one surface you use every day and clean it properly, once. Look while you do it. You just did your first inspection.
