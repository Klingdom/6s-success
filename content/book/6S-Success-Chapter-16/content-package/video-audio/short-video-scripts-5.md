## SHORT 1: A room that has never been inspected
**Topic: the starting gap, a cleared, arranged room that no one has looked at. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Your home might be perfectly arranged and still full of small problems you have stopped noticing.

**Script:**
You have done a great deal to this room, and you have not once cleaned it on purpose. Across the last two parts you emptied the space, gave every keeper a home, placed each home by reach, and taught each home to speak. But at no point did the work ask you to wipe a surface or check whether the things you kept are holding up. The room is cleared and arranged. It has never been inspected. And a room that has never been inspected is a room full of small secrets. Not because you are careless, but because you stopped seeing this space clearly a long time ago, the way everyone stops seeing the place they live. The umbrella stand has been quietly holding a little rainwater and rusting its base. The key hook has loosened a turn in the wall. None of it is visible under the daily blur, and all of it is cheap to fix now.

## SHORT 2: Don't just clean it. Read it.
**Topic: the hero rule and the two jobs of a wipe. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** There is one rule that turns any cleaning into an inspection, and it is five words long.

**Script:**
Don't just clean it. Read it. That is the whole method in five words. Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read. A wipe does two jobs at once: the first job makes the surface clean, and the second job makes the surface legible, and the second job is the one that protects the house. Most of us only ever do the first. We clean on autopilot and learn nothing. The move is tiny and costs no extra time: while your hand is already moving, look. Picture it. You give the hall shelf a quick wipe, and this time you notice one end comes away damp when the rest is dry. That damp patch is the shelf telling you there is water getting in from somewhere. Same cloth, same ten seconds, same clean shelf. But now you know about a leak while it is still a damp patch and not yet a ruined shelf.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Don't just clean it. Read it."
- 12s: "Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read."
- 26s: "A wipe does two jobs at once."
- 38s: "You know about a leak while it is still a damp patch."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A single hand drawing a cloth across a surface in one stroke. The wiped stripe comes up clean, and in it a small red problem-spark shows through, a hairline crack or water mark the grime was hiding. The part not yet wiped stays hazed over. Warm light.

## SHORT 3: What a surface tells you
**Topic: the tells, a short inspection vocabulary. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A surface talks in marks, and most of them mean something simple and useful.

**Script:**
Reading a surface sounds mystical, and it is not. It is a short, learnable vocabulary. A water mark or a soft, dark patch means moisture is reaching somewhere it should not, a slow leak or a bad seal. A fresh scratch or a shiny worn line means two things are rubbing that should not be, a drawer catching, a door dragging. Crumbs that keep gathering in one corner usually mean a gap, a crack, or a draft funneling them there. A fitting that gives when you touch it, a hook, a handle, a rail, means a fixing is working loose, and loose fixings only get looser. None of this asks you to fix anything on the spot; reading and repairing are separate acts. A clean surface is an honest one. Dirt hides the small stuff until the small stuff becomes the expensive stuff. Clean it away and the problem shows itself while it is still cheap to fix.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A surface talks in marks."
- 12s: "Water mark = a slow leak. Scratch = something rubbing."
- 26s: "Crumbs in a corner = a gap. Loose fitting = wear."
- 38s: "A clean surface is an honest one."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A calm two-column reference, the mark on the left and its plain meaning on the right: a water mark, a worn line, crumbs gathering, a tilted hook, each paired with its meaning. Warm line-and-flat-color style, legible at a glance.

## SHORT 4: A first pass at the launch pad
**Topic: the worked example, catching two problems early. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Clean one corner properly and look while you do it, and it will start talking.

**Script:**
Pick up a cloth, and do it on the launch pad, the zone by your door. Clean it properly, maybe for the first time since you set it up. Take the tray off the shelf and wipe underneath. Lift the umbrella stand. Run a hand along the hooks. But look while you do it, and two things surface almost at once. When you lift the umbrella stand, its base is sitting in a shallow ring of rainwater and has started to rust, with a faint mark on the floor beneath it. And when your hand closes on the key hook, it gives, just slightly, because the screw has worked loose. Neither was visible yesterday, not because they were hidden but because nobody had looked. Both are ten minutes and no cost now: pour out the water and dry the base, turn the loose screw a half turn tight. And both were on a straight line to real damage. Nothing was sorted or rearranged. The pass only cleaned and read.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Clean one zone. Look while you do it."
- 14s: "Lift the umbrella stand: rust in standing water."
- 28s: "Touch the key hook: the screw has worked loose."
- 38s: "Ten minutes, no cost. Caught while still small."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand cleaning the launch pad, lifting the umbrella stand to reveal a ring of rainwater and the first bloom of rust, then closing on the key hook and feeling it give. Nothing is moved to a new spot. Warm daylight.
