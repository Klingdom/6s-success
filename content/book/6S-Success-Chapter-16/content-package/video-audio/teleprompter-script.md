# Teleprompter Script: Shine: Cleaning as Inspection

**Chapter 16, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** cleaning-as-inspection
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come stand at the door with me again.

That entryway corner is finished.

Every keeper has a home.

Every home sits where your hand reaches.

Every home can explain itself.

It is gathered into one zone. The launch pad.

On paper, that corner is done.

And here is the strange part.

You have done a great deal to this room.

And you have not once cleaned it on purpose.

The room is cleared and arranged.

It has never been inspected.

And a room never inspected is full of small secrets.

Not because you are careless.

But because you stopped seeing this space long ago.

The way everyone stops seeing where they live.

The umbrella stand has been holding a little rainwater.

And rusting its base.

The key hook has loosened a turn in the wall.

None of it is visible under the daily blur.

And all of it is cheap to fix now.

So today, we change what cleaning is for.

The third S is Seiso. It came into English as Shine.

And the most important line about it is plain.

Use cleaning as inspection.

On the factory floor, wiping a machine was checking it.

Catching the loose bolt before it stopped the line.

The cleaning was never the point.

The cleaning was how you got close enough to notice.

Bring that home and nothing changes.

When you clean a surface, you are the only one that close.

Your hand is on it. Your eyes are inches away.

Cleaning is inspecting, if you let it be.

The dirt you remove is also the blindfold you remove.

So here is the definition.

Shine means keeping a space clean enough to see clearly.

So the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting.

And small problems show themselves early.

While they are still small.

And hear this.

Shine is not about a spotless house.

It is not a test of how hard you scrub.

Seen is the target.

Now the rule. The whole method in five words.

Don't just clean it. Read it.

Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read.

A wipe does two jobs at once.

The first job makes the surface clean.

The second job makes the surface legible.

And the second job protects the house.

While your hand is already moving, look.

Reading a surface is a short vocabulary.

A water mark means a slow leak.

A fresh scratch means something rubbing.

Crumbs gathering in a corner mean a gap.

A fitting that gives means a fixing working loose.

A clean surface is an honest one.

Now pick up a cloth.

Do it on the launch pad, the zone by the door.

Take the tray off the shelf and wipe underneath.

Lift the umbrella stand.

Its base is sitting in a ring of rainwater.

And the metal has started to rust.

Close your hand on the key hook.

It gives. The screw has worked loose.

Neither was visible yesterday.

Not hidden. Nobody had looked.

Both are ten minutes and no cost now.

And both were on a line to real damage.

Nothing was sorted. Nothing was rearranged.

The pass only cleaned and read.

Everything in your home is wearing out, all the time.

The only question is whether you meet it early or late.

You were going to clean anyway.

Shine only asks the cleaning to pull double duty.

Clean to see, not to impress.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Because it moves today.

For four chapters it stepped toward calm.

Last chapter it reached Straighten complete.

Today it steps off that mark toward the goal.

Because this time the room genuinely changed.

Not its layout. Its condition.

A blurred surface is now clean and read.

Two small problems caught while still small.

But be honest. This is a real first step.

Not the finish line.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

Because Shine has only begun.

And after Shine come Safety, Standardize, and Sustain.

So here is your job.

Pick one surface you use every day.

Clean it properly, once.

And look at what the cleaning uncovers.

A mark, a wobble, a worn spot you had stopped seeing.

You just did your first inspection.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go and read one surface today.
