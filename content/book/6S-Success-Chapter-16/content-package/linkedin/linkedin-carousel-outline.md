# Chapter 16 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Cleaning as Inspection

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, no force metaphors, permission not exhortation.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You have arranged your whole home and never once looked at it.

**Body:** The room is cleared and arranged. It has never been inspected. Swipe for the third S, where cleaning stops being a chore and becomes the way the house tells you what it needs.

---

## Slide 2: A room full of small secrets

**Headline:** A room that has never been inspected is full of small secrets.

**Body:** The umbrella stand has been rusting in a little rainwater. The key hook has loosened a turn in the wall. None of it is visible under the daily blur, and all of it is cheap to fix now and will not stay cheap.

---

## Slide 3: The reframe

**Headline:** Cleaning is not a separate task from inspecting.

**Body:** When you clean a surface, you are the only person that close to it, that carefully, all day. Your hand is on it and your eyes are inches away. The dirt you are removing is also the blindfold you are removing.

---

## Slide 4: What Shine is

**Headline:** Shine means keeping a space clean enough to see clearly.

**Body:** So that the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting, and small problems show themselves early, while they are still small.

---

## Slide 5: Not a spotless house

**Headline:** Shine is not about a spotless house, and it is not a test of how hard you scrub.

**Body:** It is about keeping a space clear enough that it can tell you what it needs, so a home stays cared for instead of quietly wearing out. Seen is the target.

---

## Slide 6: The rule

**Headline:** Don't just clean it. Read it.

**Body:** The entire method in five words. Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read. A wipe does two jobs at once: it makes the surface clean, and it makes the surface legible.

---

## Slide 7: What a surface tells you

**Headline:** A surface talks in marks, and the vocabulary is short.

**Body:** A water mark means a slow leak. A fresh scratch means two things are rubbing. Crumbs gathering in one corner mean a gap. A fitting that gives when you touch it means a fixing is working loose.

---

## Slide 8: A clean surface is honest

**Headline:** A clean surface is an honest one.

**Body:** Dirt hides the small stuff, the drip, the crack, the loose screw, the worn spot, until the small stuff becomes the expensive stuff. Clean it away and the problem shows itself while it is still cheap to fix.

---

## Slide 9: A first pass at the launch pad

**Headline:** Clean the launch pad once, on purpose, and look.

**Body:** Lift the umbrella stand and its base is sitting in a ring of rainwater, starting to rust. Close your hand on the key hook and it gives; the screw has worked loose. Both caught while they are still small.

---

## Slide 10: The cheapest maintenance

**Headline:** Everything in your home is slowly wearing out, all the time.

**Body:** The only question is whether you meet the wear early, when it is a rust ring and a loose screw, or late, when it is a repair bill and a bad morning. Clean to see, and you meet it early.

---

## Slide 11: You were going to clean anyway

**Headline:** Shine does not ask for more cleaning. It asks the cleaning to pull double duty.

**Body:** You were taught to clean so the place would look good for other people. Shine asks you to clean so the place makes sense to you. Reading it is what actually protects the home.

---

**Headline:** A surface got clean, a small problem got caught early, and Shine has begun.
