# Chapter 16 Calls to Action: Shine: Cleaning as Inspection

This is the first chapter of Part Five, and it opens Shine, the third S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible first move: pick one surface you use every day, clean it properly once, and look at what the cleaning uncovers. The CTAs match the chapter's tone of permission and relief, not exhortation: Shine is not about a spotless house or scrubbing harder, and a home that went unread is a normal home, not a dirty one. The fix costs no extra time, because you were going to clean anyway; it only asks that the cleaning pull double duty.

## Primary CTA
Do your first inspection. "Pick one surface you use every day and clean it properly, once. As you do, look at what the cleaning uncovers, a mark, a wobble, a worn spot you had stopped seeing. You just did your first inspection. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one surface you use every day and clean it properly, once. As you do, look at what the cleaning uncovers, a mark, a wobble, a worn spot you had stopped seeing. You just did your first inspection.
- **Family Challenge:** Give everyone one surface to clean and one question to answer: what did it tell you? Trade findings at dinner. A house where everyone reads while they clean catches its problems years early.
- **6S Tip:** Clean the same spots the same way on a regular rhythm. Inspection works by comparison, so the fresh scratch, the new drip, and the spreading stain only stand out against a surface you already know.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable first move, not a burst of effort: clean one everyday surface on purpose and look while you do it. Lead with "do your first inspection," which is concrete and doable in ten minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of permission and relief, because this opens the third S. Not cleaning enough is not a character flaw, and a home that went unread is a normal home.
- The rule (don't just clean it, read it), the support line (every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read), the "not spotless" reframe, the honest-surface payoff, and "clean to see, not to impress" are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is a shift in what cleaning is for; nothing is attacked, blitzed, or purged. Use "spotless" only inside the frozen "not about a spotless house" reframe.
- Scope note: this reframes and does one first pass; it does not re-sort or re-arrange (Chapters 12 to 15), build the list of what to clean or divide the labor (Chapter 17), teach cleaning methods, products, or tools (Chapter 18), or build a repeatable routine or schedule (Chapter 19). Shine has only begun; the goal is still ahead.
