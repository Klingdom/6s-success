## Hero device: Cleaning as inspection (the two jobs of a wipe)
Shine's move is a shift in what cleaning is FOR. A wipe does two jobs at once: it cleans the surface, and it makes the surface legible. The second job, the reading, is the one that protects the home, because dirt hides the small stuff until the small stuff turns expensive. Source line, verbatim from the 6S materials: "use cleaning as inspection." Its history is Seiso (Shine) in the Toyota Production System, pointing toward Total Productive Maintenance, home-scaled. Referenced lightly, no overclaiming.

## Motifs
- **The unseen room.** Cleared and arranged is not the same as looked-at; condition is a different question from placement.
- **The two jobs of a wipe.** Every surface you clean is a surface you get to read.
- **The tells.** A surface talks in marks: a water mark, a scratch, gathering crumbs, a loose fitting each mean something.
- **The first pass at the launch pad.** The running example gets its first Shine pass and gives up two small problems, caught cheap.
- **The cheapest maintenance.** Catch wear early and it stays small; the same ten minutes protect everything the last two parts built.
- **The first step, honestly.** One pass on one zone is a real step and a small one; the goal is still well ahead.

## Emotional purpose
Permission and relief, not exhortation. Cleaning is a chore many people carry as low guilt or a standard they never meet; this chapter turns it from a performance for others into a private, useful act of noticing. No shame: a home that went unread is a normal home, not a dirty one, and not cleaning enough is not a character flaw. The payoff is a cared-for home that tells you what it needs while the needs are small.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to clean one surface on purpose and look while doing it, to read the common tells, and to make one first cleaning-and-inspection pass on a single zone that catches problems early. Do NOT re-open Sort or re-teach Straighten (done). Do NOT build the list of what to clean or divide the labor (Ch 17). Do NOT teach cleaning methods, products, or technique (Ch 18). Do NOT build a repeatable routine or schedule (Ch 19). Do NOT build standards or maintenance-over-time systems (Standardize, Sustain).
