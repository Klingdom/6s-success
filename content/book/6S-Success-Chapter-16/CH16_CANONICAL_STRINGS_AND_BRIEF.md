## Hero device (frozen): cleaning as inspection (the two jobs of a wipe)
Shine's move is a shift in what cleaning is FOR. Every surface you wipe you also get to read: a wipe does two jobs at once, it cleans the surface and it shows you the surface. The second job, the reading, is the one that protects the home, because dirt hides the small stuff until the small stuff turns expensive.
> **The rule (frozen):** Don't just clean it. Read it.
> **Support line (frozen):** Every surface you wipe is a surface you get to read.

## Definition box (frozen)
> Shine means keeping a space clean enough to see clearly, so that the act of cleaning doubles as the act of inspecting, and small problems show themselves early, while they are still small.

## The "not spotless" reframe (frozen, anti-perfectionism)
> Shine is not about a spotless house, and it is not a test of how hard you scrub. It is about keeping a space clear enough that it can tell you what it needs, so a home stays cared for instead of quietly wearing out.

## The honest-surface payoff (frozen, "abnormal conditions early")
> A clean surface is an honest one. Dirt hides the small stuff, the drip, the crack, the loose screw, the worn spot, until the small stuff becomes the expensive stuff. Clean it away and the problem shows itself while it is still cheap to fix.

## The reframe (frozen, distinct from Ch 14 "you were the label" and Ch 15 "your room to their room")
> You were taught to clean so the place would look good for other people. Shine asks you to clean so the place makes sense to you, because the same ten minutes that make a surface presentable also make it readable, and reading it is what actually protects the home.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> Clean to see, not to impress. When cleaning doubles as inspecting, the house tells you what it needs while problems are still small, and Shine turns a chore you dread into the cheapest maintenance you will ever do.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one surface you use every day and clean it properly, once. As you do, look at what the cleaning uncovers, a mark, a wobble, a worn spot you had stopped seeing. You just did your first inspection.
- **Family Challenge:** Give everyone one surface to clean and one question to answer: what did it tell you? Trade findings at dinner. A house where everyone reads while they clean catches its problems years early.
- **6S Tip:** Clean the same spots the same way on a regular rhythm. Inspection works by comparison, so the fresh scratch, the new drip, and the spreading stain only stand out against a surface you already know.
- **Common Mistake:** Treating Shine as a race to spotless, or cleaning only when guests are coming. If you clean to impress and never to notice, you get a tidy house that is still quietly wearing out where no one is looking.
