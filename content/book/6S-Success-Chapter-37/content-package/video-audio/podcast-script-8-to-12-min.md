# Chapter 37 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Primary Bedroom

Single host, warm and calm.

## Cold open
Here is a thing almost nobody does when they clean a bedroom: wash the pillow. Not the case, the pillow. Today we clean a whole primary bedroom the way it actually needs it, for sleep and for air.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. Today we cross out of the shared rooms and into the private ones, starting with the room whose whole job is to help you stop. The standard here is not a showroom. You spend a third of your life with your face pressed to this bed, breathing what it holds, so you clean it for two things the other rooms never asked of you: for sleep, which means washing the layer that touches your face and cleaning the surfaces the room hides, and for air, because this is the one room you keep the door closed on.

## Part 2: The six zones
The room is six zones: the bed and bedding, your own nightstand, the other sleeper's nightstand, the dresser top, the dresser drawers, and the closet. Start with your own nightstand tonight; it is the smallest and the one you see first and last.

## Part 3: The bed
Strip it right back, and this is the key move: pull the pillow protectors off and send them through the wash with the sheets, put the pillows themselves through when the seasons turn, and air the mattress and the duvet. Then, with the mattress bare, run the vacuum's upholstery tool across the whole face and into the seams, and run the crevice tool down the gap behind the headboard where dust and hair pack against the wall.

## Part 4: Clean where nobody looks
That gap behind the headboard is the theme of the whole room. The surfaces that matter most are the ones it hides: under the bed, behind the drawers, the grey film on the closet rod, and the damp that starts on a closet back wall nobody ever wipes. So you pull the bed out, lift the drawers clear, and clear the closet to bare rod, and you clean the parts of the room you never see.

## Part 5: The shared side
Most bedrooms are shared, so one rule keeps the peace: you clean, they cull. On the other sleeper's side you may wipe the top, dust the lamp, and vacuum the slot beside the bed, and you may not remove one object. Finish your own side to the standard and let the difference do the persuading. The one exception is out-of-date medication, because that is a safety question, not a taste question.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
Two dangers come out at night. Keep the strip of floor between the bed and the door bare, because it is what a bare foot finds at three in the morning, and strap the tall dresser and the closet rod, because both come down on whoever is underneath. And the calls are mostly quiet ones: the third sheet set, the phone charger that calls itself an alarm, the perfume you were given, the clothes that do not fit the body you have this morning.

## Close
A bedroom is not clean because the bed is made. It is clean because you washed the layer that touches your face, aired the mattress, cleaned behind the headboard, and stopped at the middle of a shared bed. Do one zone tonight. Next time: the guest bedroom.
