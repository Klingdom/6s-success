# Facebook Longform Post: Clean for Sleep and Air

A story-driven post from Chapter 37 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the primary bedroom.

---

Here is a thing almost nobody does when they clean a bedroom: wash the pillow.

The primary bedroom is the one room whose whole job is to help you stop, and that changes how you clean it. You do not clean it for tidiness. You clean it for sleep and for air, because you spend a third of your life with your face pressed to this bed, breathing what it holds.

So you wash the layer that actually touches your face. The cases and the sheets get washed, sure, but the pillow inside the case and the protector under it almost never do. Strip the bed right back, send the protectors through the wash too, put the pillows themselves through when the seasons turn, and air the mattress and the duvet so the night's moisture dries out of them rather than sitting in the padding you breathe against.

Then clean where nobody looks. The surfaces that matter most in this room are the ones it hides. Dust and hair pack into the strip behind the headboard, grit gathers under the bed, a grey film builds along the closet rod and transfers straight onto a dark shoulder, and damp starts quietly on a closet back wall nobody ever wipes. Pull the bed out. Lift the drawers clear. Clear the closet to bare rod. Clean the parts of the room you never see.

And if you share the room, one rule keeps the peace: you clean, they cull. You may wipe and vacuum the other person's side, and you may not remove a single object. Finish your own side to the standard and let the difference do the persuading.

Two more, both for the dark. Keep the strip of floor between the bed and the door bare, because it is what a bare foot finds at three in the morning. And strap the tall dresser and the closet rod, because both come down on whoever is underneath.

Do one zone tonight, your own nightstand, and wake to the one surface you see first and last set right.

When did you last wash your pillows?
