# Chapter 37 LinkedIn Posts: The Primary Bedroom

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. Clean for sleep and air
The primary bedroom is the one room whose whole job is to help you stop. So you do not clean it for tidiness. You clean it for sleep and for air.

"You spend a third of your life with your face pressed to this bed, breathing what it holds, so when you strip it you wash the layer that sits against your face and air out what traps the night's moisture."

## 2. Wash the layer that touches your face
Here is the pass almost nobody does. The cases and the sheets get washed; the pillow inside and the protector under it almost never do, though they are the layer against your face all night. So strip the bed right back, send the protectors through the wash too, put the pillows themselves through when the seasons turn, and air the mattress and the duvet. It is the one thing that changes what you breathe all night.

## 3. Clean where nobody looks
"The surfaces that matter most in this room are the ones it hides." Dust and hair pack in behind the headboard, grit gathers under the bed and behind the drawers, a grey film builds along the closet rod, and damp starts on a back wall nobody wipes. Pull the bed out, lift the drawers clear, clear the closet to bare rod, and clean the parts of the room you never see.

## 4. You clean, they cull
Most bedrooms are shared, and every argument about tidiness starts on the other person's side. The rule that settles it: you clean, they cull. You may wipe, dust, and vacuum their side, and you may not remove one object. Finish your own side to the standard and let the difference do the persuading. If they never change a thing, you have still fixed half the room and kept the peace.

## 5. Vacuum the mattress
A vacuum with an upholstery and a crevice tool does most of this room's real work. With the mattress bare, run the upholstery tool across the whole face and into the piped seams where dust and skin flakes settle, then rotate it and do the underside. The same tools clean behind the headboard, the drawer runners, and the closet floor.

## 6. The dark walking strip
The strip of floor between your bed and the door is what a bare foot finds at three in the morning. A storage bin, a shoe, or a charging cable left across it is a fall waiting in the dark. That strip stays bare. It is the smallest safety rule in the house and the one you will be most grateful for at 3am.

## 7. Strap the tall furniture
A tall dresser tips forward the moment a loaded drawer is open and a child leans on it, and a rod crammed end to end tears its brackets out of the wall. While the drawers are empty and the rod is bare for cleaning, do the one thing that matters most: strap the dresser to a stud, anchor the rod, and open one drawer at a time.

## 8. Five things or fewer
Your nightstand holds only what you reach for between getting into bed and falling asleep. Five things or fewer, countable from the doorway: lamp, a lidded drink or a coaster, one book, a small dish for your glasses. When a sixth turns up it is almost always a finished book or an empty glass. Start here tonight; it is the surface you see first and last.

## 9. The phone charger that calls itself an alarm
The phone should not sleep on the nightstand, and you keep it there because it is your alarm. That is one job, and a small clock does it. Move the charger to the dresser across the room and give the phone a landing spot you have to stand up to reach. What you cannot keep doing is calling the current arrangement temporary.

## 10. A bedroom is not clean because the bed is made
"A bedroom is not clean because the bed is made. It is clean because you washed the layer that touches your face, aired the mattress and the duvet, cleaned behind the headboard and under the bed where the dust hides, and stopped at the middle of a bed two people share."
