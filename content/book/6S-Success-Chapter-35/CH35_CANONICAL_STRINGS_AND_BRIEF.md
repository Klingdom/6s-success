## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The six zones (frozen, structural list):** The living room is six small zones, not one big job: the sofa and seating, the coffee table, the media center, the bookshelves and display, the side tables and lighting, and the floor and circulation path. Do them one at a time and the room the house relaxes in comes back to you a zone at a time.

- **The dust-and-touch rule (frozen, living-room Shine principle):** In the living room, dust is not just untidy, it is heat, allergens, and lost light: it mats the vents that keep your electronics cool, it settles deep in the upholstery, and it films the bulb until the lamp barely lights. So you clean where nobody looks, and you match the tool to the surface: a dry cloth on a screen, a soft brush on a vent, the upholstery tool deep in the seams.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished living room is one anyone can walk into, cross in the dark, and sit down in without moving a thing: every seat sittable, the coffee table clear to its tray, the screen dust-free and the vents clear, the bookshelves dusted and strapped to the wall, a lamp that gives its full light, and a clear route from door to door wide enough to pass carrying a basket.

- **The dust-is-heat reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A television or a games console does not usually die of age, it dies of dust. The felt of dust matted across a vent slot traps the heat the fan is trying to move, and a device left to run hot is a device on its way out. So in this room the real cleaning is not the screen, it is the vents and the back panel, where the dust you cannot see is quietly cooking the electronics you paid for.

- **The clean-where-nobody-looks reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Most of what a living room needs cleaning is exactly where you never look: in the seams and the deck under the sofa cushions, on the top of the tallest bookcase, along the top edges of the books, on the outer back of the sofa against the wall, and under the rug. A surface wipe of the open middle leaves all of it. You clean a living room by lifting, pulling forward, and reaching behind.

- **The screen reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A screen is the one surface in the house you never spray and never scrub. A dry microfiber cloth in slow, straight passes is the whole method, because a wet cloth streaks it, a paper towel scratches the coating, and a spray runs down inside the bezel. Gentle and dry, and nothing else.

- **The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure):** This is the living room the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, every seat sittable, the screen and its vents clear, the shelves dusted and strapped, and a clear route from door to door. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a living room this easy to walk into and sit down in is the one the house will actually relax in, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A living room is not clean because the middle is tidy. It is clean because you lifted the cushions and vacuumed the deck, brushed the dust out of the vents, dusted the top of the bookcase and the edges of the books, wiped the bulb, and cleared the route from door to door. Do one zone this weekend, the sofa or the media center, and relax in a room that is clean where it counts.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / dust is heat, so clean the vents / match the tool to the surface. The nine-rule Shine method from Chapter 31 still governs; the living room adds soft furnishings, screens, and the dust that hides where it does the most damage.

## The four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win:** Do the one thing that saves your electronics: brush and vacuum every vent slot and the back panel of the television and the console. If dust has matted solid in a slot, loosen it with a soft detail brush, then lift it with the vacuum. Wipe the screen with a dry cloth only. Twenty minutes, and the devices run cool instead of cooking.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the route from door to door with a full laundry basket, and if you cannot pass without turning sideways, the room holds more furniture than floor. Clear the route to full width, put every remote and controller in one labelled bin, and agree the floor is for furniture legs and the rug, and nothing else.
- **6S Tip:** Match the tool to the surface: a dry microfiber cloth on a screen, never a spray or a paper towel; a soft brush and the vacuum on a vent, because dust there is heat; the upholstery tool deep in the sofa seams; a dry cloth on a cold bulb, because a dusty bulb steals the light. And strap the two tall, top-heavy things in this room, the television and the bookcase, to the wall.
- **Common Mistake:** Wiping the open surfaces and calling the room done, so the dust stays where it does the damage: matted in the vents cooking the electronics, deep in the sofa seams, on the top of the bookcase, and under the rug. And spraying cleaner on the screen, which streaks it and runs inside the bezel. Lift, reach behind, brush the vents, and keep the screen dry.

## The room: source facts (from the Micro Zone Manual, Living Room, 6 zones)
Carry specific nouns. Each zone's full Inputs + numbered Steps + inspect-and-flag are authored in the manuscript from the Manual's shine_detail.

1. **Sofa and seating.** Job: somewhere everyone can sit without first moving something. Done: every seat sittable, throws folded to one size in one basket, one cushion per seat at most, remotes in the arm tray. Shine (work headrest down, lift every cushion): the backrest and headrest tops (hair oil, damp wipe, dry on leather); the arms and front roll (most hand contact); the seat cushions both faces (vacuum, flip and rotate); the deck and seams under the cushions (crevice tool, back to front, the hidden well of crumbs/coins/pet hair); the throw pillows and throws (beat outside, launder even the clean-looking ones, pet-hair concentrator); the remote tray; the outer back and sides against the wall (grey film, months untouched); the base, feet, and skirt. Call: the pillows you move to sit down (keep only the number that can stay in place while a person sits). Hazards: recliner footrests and sofa-bed frames on fingers (fall/cut/crush); a throw hanging to the floor (fall). Inspect: musty/damp deep in the foam (a spilled drink), flexing frame or creaking joint, split seam or crumbling foam, coins/battery surfacing, fabric worn shiny.
2. **Coffee table.** Job: a small shared surface for what is happening now. Done: one tray with four coasters and the remote, at most one book or plant, wood or glass showing. Shine (clear to bare, every ring shows): the tabletop and ring marks (wood damp-then-dry; GLASS two passes, glass cleaner then dry, or every fingerprint shows by lamplight); the underside lip and apron (greasy grip film); the lower shelf/magazine tier; the legs and feet + floor shadow; the tray and coasters (a sticky coaster is old spill). Call: the book you are in the middle of (check the bookmark; not moved in two weeks means you are storing it). Hazards: glass tops and hard corners at a child's head height (fall/cut/crush); a candle among throws and paper (burn/fire). Inspect: a hairline crack or chipped glass edge, a rocking leg, a heat bloom on wood, a bowing lower shelf.
3. **Media center.** Job: keep the TV, sound, and consoles working, cool, and cabled so a fault is findable. Done: every cable labelled at both ends to one labelled strip, nothing plugged in for a device you no longer own, a hand's gap of air at the vents, every controller in one bin. Shine (dust turns warm devices hot, the real work is vents and back panel, screen dry only): the top of the unit; the TV screen (DRY cloth only, slow straight passes, never spray, never a paper towel); the TV back panel and bezel (vacuum with a brush head, the dust that cooks the set); the console and amplifier vent slots (brush then vacuum; loosen matted dust with a detail brush first); the shelf surfaces and front edges (move devices forward); the cable bundle, power strip, and floor behind; the controllers and remotes (the grime you hand person to person). Call: the dead electronics shelf (power each device you actually use; a device off for a year leaves). Hazards: blocked vents and a dust-packed TV back (burn/fire); an unanchored TV on a low unit a child pulls (fall/cut/crush); a drink on the console or by the strip (water/electricity). Inspect: a hot-plastic/scorched smell near the amp, a warm or browning strip/plug/socket, a fan grown louder, dust matted solid in a slot, a cable sheath stiff or tacky with heat.
4. **Bookshelves and display.** Job: the books you will open again and a few objects you want to look at. Done: a hand's width of empty space per shelf, books upright between bookends with nothing stacked flat on top, objects in two or three groups, a strap into the wall. Shine (dust where nobody looks, top of the unit and the top edges of the books): the very top of the tallest unit; the top edges of the books (dry cloth or soft brush, front to back); the shelf front edges (the greasy grip line); the shelf boards behind the books (slide each row forward, wipe the dust band); the frames and objects (lift and dust under and behind, soft brush for carved); the base and skirting + look over the strap. Call: books you keep to be the person who read them (would you open it again or press it on a friend; if not, why keep it). Hazards: a tall unstrapped bookcase a child climbs (fall/cut/crush); reaching the top shelf from a sofa arm (fall). Inspect: a bowed shelf board, a crept-out shelf pin, a slack wall strap or backed-out screw, damp-swollen boards or foxing on pages against an outside wall.
5. **Side tables and lighting.** Job: a light, a drink, and one or two things within reach of the chair. Done: one small tray of no more than four items, a coaster under the glass, a wiped bulb that lights, the lamp cord clipped down the leg so no cable crosses the floor. Shine (a dusty bulb throws less light, start there off and cold): the bulb (off and fully cold, dry cloth, dust steals the light); the shade inside and out (fabric brushed gently); the base and switch (damp, moisture off the socket); the tabletop and ring marks (under the tray); the tray and coasters; the legs and the clipped cord + floor. Call: the medicine beside the chair (the bottle lives in a cupboard, not at knee height where visiting children play). Hazards: loose tablets in an open dish at grab height (poison/choke/strangle); a glass by the lamp base or a phone charging on the same table (water/electricity); a cord across the floor to the chair (fall). Inspect: a flickering/buzzing/browned bulb or a loose switch, a scorched shade near the bulb, a cracked cord sheath at the base, a rocking leg or heat bloom under the tray.
6. **Floor and circulation path.** Job: let anyone cross the room in the dark, carrying something, without touching anything. Done: a continuous clear route door to door, wide enough for two to pass and for the vacuum to run, rug edges flat on a grippy pad, no cord crossing, nothing on the floor but furniture legs, the rug, and baskets against the wall. Shine (clean the whole run, not just the open middle): the clear route (vacuum back to front, finishing toward the door); under the sofa and chair legs (move them out, the hidden pet-hair build-up); the rug both faces (turn it over, grit grinds from below); the gripper pad and rug edges (press to feel if still tacky); the hard floor along the route (damp mop with the floor cleaner, let it dry); the skirting along the route; the baskets against the wall. Call: whether there is simply too much furniture (measure the narrowest gap; if two adults cannot pass, the room holds more furniture than floor). Hazards: a curled rug corner, a cord across the route, a toy underfoot (fall); a route narrowed below full width (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: a gripper pad gone smooth, a crumbling rug backing or a worn walk-line, a lifting or creaking floorboard, a soft/springy patch or swelling skirting under the rug.

## The kit for the living room (product TYPES; no brand names)
- **Clean and shine (the inputs that do the work here):** a compact vacuum with attachments (the upholstery tool, the crevice tool, AND a brush head, the living room's key input, for the sofa seams, the vents, and the floor); a colour-coded microfiber cloth set, with a dedicated dry cloth kept only for screens; a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner; a streak-free glass cleaner (glass tabletops); a floor-material-compatible cleaner (the circulation path); a small detail brush (vent slots, carved objects); a soft dusting brush; an upholstery spot cleaner (a set-in stain).
- **Sort and routing:** a Keep, Relocate, Donate, Recycle, Trash container set.
- **Homes (Storage & Organization):** a large and a small woven basket (throws, and things pushed to the wall); a remote control tray; a controller dock or bin; a magazine file; open-front and clear lidded bins; board-game component bags; a medicine bin set (to get the tablets off the side table and into a cupboard); bookends and a shelf riser; a cable management kit.
- **Standardize (Visual Controls):** a portable label maker; a cord label set (label every cable at both ends); shelf location labels; floor boundary tape (mark the clear route and the no-storage floor); removable and water-resistant labels.
- **Safety:** anti-tip anchors and hardware (the television AND the tall bookcase, the two biggest tip-over risks in the room); a surge protector with overload protection (one labelled strip, nothing chained); cord clips; a drawer safety latch; a stable step stool (a high shelf, never the sofa arm); a moisture absorber or humidity monitor.
