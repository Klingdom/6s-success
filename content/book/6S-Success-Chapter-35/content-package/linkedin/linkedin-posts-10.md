# Chapter 35 LinkedIn Posts: The Living Room

Ten standalone posts. Each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The dirt that matters is the dirt you cannot see

A living room looks fine after a quick tidy of the open middle. Meanwhile the trouble is soft and hidden: dust matted across the vents that keep your electronics cool, grime deep in the sofa seams, a film on the bulb stealing the light.

"In the living room, dust is not just untidy, it is heat, allergens, and lost light: it mats the vents that keep your electronics cool, it settles deep in the upholstery, and it films the bulb until the lamp barely lights. So you clean where nobody looks, and you match the tool to the surface: a dry cloth on a screen, a soft brush on a vent, the upholstery tool deep in the seams."

## 2. Your TV is dying of dust

"A television or a games console does not usually die of age, it dies of dust. The felt of dust matted across a vent slot traps the heat the fan is trying to move, and a device left to run hot is a device on its way out. So in this room the real cleaning is not the screen, it is the vents and the back panel, where the dust you cannot see is quietly cooking the electronics you paid for."

Twenty minutes with a soft brush and a vacuum, and they run cool again.

## 3. Never spray a screen

"A screen is the one surface in the house you never spray and never scrub. A dry microfiber cloth in slow, straight passes is the whole method, because a wet cloth streaks it, a paper towel scratches the coating, and a spray runs down inside the bezel. Gentle and dry, and nothing else."

Match the tool to the surface. The screen wants nothing but a dry cloth.

## 4. Clean where nobody looks

"Most of what a living room needs cleaning is exactly where you never look: in the seams and the deck under the sofa cushions, on the top of the tallest bookcase, along the top edges of the books, on the outer back of the sofa against the wall, and under the rug."

A surface wipe of the open middle leaves all of it. You clean a living room by lifting, pulling forward, and reaching behind.

## 5. Six zones, not one room

"The living room is six small zones, not one big job: the sofa and seating, the coffee table, the media center, the bookshelves and display, the side tables and lighting, and the floor and circulation path."

You never have to face the living room. You face the sofa, or the media center, one at a time. Start with either for the biggest win, because those two hold the dirt you cannot see.

## 6. Lift every cushion

The sofa swallows things into its seams and the deck beneath the cushions: crumbs, coins, a battery, a season of pet hair. Work from the headrest down, lift every seat cushion, vacuum both faces, then run the crevice tool into the deck and the seams, back to front. Beat the throw pillows outside and launder the throws, even the clean-looking ones. That hidden well, not the open middle, is where a sofa is actually cleaned.

## 7. A dusty bulb steals the light

Light is the whole reason a side table has a lamp, and a film of dust on the bulb is quietly stealing it. So clean the bulb first, while it is switched off and fully cold, with a dry cloth. Then the shade, inside and out, and the ring marks hiding under the tray. It is the smallest job in the room and one of the most satisfying: the lamp gives back its full light.

## 8. Strap the two things that tip

Two things in a living room tip forward onto a child who pulls or climbs: the television on its unit, and the tall bookcase. Both are among the biggest tip-over risks in the house. So while the shelves are bare for cleaning and the media unit is pulled out, do the one thing that matters most: strap both to a wall stud, with the weight low. Cleaning is how you find the fault. The safety pass is where you fix it.

## 9. Is it clutter, or too much furniture?

You keep tidying the floor and it keeps failing, and the reason may not be the clutter.

"Walk the route from door to door with a full laundry basket, and if you cannot pass without turning sideways, the room holds more furniture than floor."

Clear the route to full width, put every remote and controller in one labelled bin, and agree the floor is for furniture legs and the rug, and nothing else.

## 10. Clean where it counts

"A living room is not clean because the middle is tidy. It is clean because you lifted the cushions and vacuumed the deck, brushed the dust out of the vents, dusted the top of the bookcase and the edges of the books, wiped the bulb, and cleared the route from door to door."

Do one zone this weekend, the sofa or the media center. Chapter 35 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Next: the family room.
