# Facebook Longform Post: The Room You Clean Where Nobody Looks

A story-driven post from Chapter 35 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the living room.

---

Here is something most people never realize: your television is probably dying of dust.

Not the screen. The vents. A felt of dust matts across the vent slots on the back and underside, and it traps the heat the fan is trying to move. A device left to run hot is a device on its way out, and the dust doing it is dust you cannot see.

That is the living room in one lesson. It looks fine after a quick tidy of the open middle, but in this room the dirt that matters is the dirt that hides: matted in the vents cooking your electronics, worked deep into the sofa seams, filmed on the bulb until the lamp barely lights, packed under the rug and behind the sofa against the wall.

So you clean a living room two ways the other rooms did not ask for. First, you clean where nobody looks: you lift every cushion and vacuum the deck and the seams, you dust the top of the tallest bookcase and the top edges of the books, you reach behind the sofa, and you lift the rug. Second, you match the tool to the surface, because this room holds surfaces that each want a different touch. A dry cloth on a screen, never a spray and never a paper towel, which scratches the coating. A soft brush and the vacuum on a vent, because dust there is heat. The upholstery tool deep in the sofa seams. A dry cloth on a cold bulb, because a dusty bulb steals the light.

Two more things while you are there. Strap the two tall, top-heavy things in the room, the television and the bookcase, to the wall, because both tip forward onto a child who pulls or climbs. And keep a clear route from door to door, wide enough to cross in the dark carrying a basket.

Do one zone this weekend, the sofa or the media center, and relax in a room that is clean where it counts.

When did you last clean the vents on your TV?
