# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 14 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line or image hitting the same follower until it feels canned. This check flags where that can happen.

## High-frequency elements
| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word | Web, LinkedIn, X, FB, newsletter, quote card, graphics | A follower on several platforms sees the rule repeatedly | Calendar staggers it; vary the phrasing per platform |
| The Glance Test | Hero infographic, carousel, deck, LinkedIn, X | Medium | The chapter's hero check; repetition reinforces. Vary the example (cable drawer, keys, pantry). |
| You were the label | LinkedIn, FB, X, quote card | Medium to High | Strongest, most human hook. Use across formats, not stacked on one day. |
| Label the spot, not just the thing | 6S Tip graphic, LinkedIn, video | Low to Medium | Memorable; rotate placement. |
| The three cues (label, outline, boundary) | Toolkit graphic, carousel, video, longform | Low | Practical, meant to repeat; vary which cue you lead with. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings; most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision
The calendar avoids two assets sharing a hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push one back a day so "you were the label" does not land twice in one day.

## Cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline and the slides carousel copy cover the same visual-controls content. Post only one to LinkedIn (use the copy-ready `slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`; keep the outline as planning).
- The LinkedIn newsletter and the email newsletter share structure; do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- Web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 14)
- Chapters 12 to 15 are all Straighten. Keep each distinct: Ch 12 gives each thing a home, Ch 13 places those homes by frequency, Ch 14 makes each home obvious with a visual cue, Ch 15 maps the whole room. Ch 14 assets must not drift into re-assigning homes (Ch 12) or re-placing by frequency (Ch 13).
- Hold Ch 15's "one shared map of the whole room" as a teaser only, so Ch 14 (the individual home's cue) and Ch 15 (the room-wide picture) do not blur.
- Do not let the visual-controls idea slide into the later Standardize S (routines, schedules, household standards for keeping order over time). Ch 14 makes the place visible; keeping it up is a later S.
- The friction-meter "third Straighten step" is specific to this chapter; do not reuse the first- or second-step framing in Ch 14 assets.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter is a draft authored in the book voice, not supplied source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft; re-sync if the chapter is revised.
- Quote cards are verbatim (10 cards plus 5 alternates), grep-verified. Nothing borrowed from outside the book; the chapter avoids the factory term "shadow board" in favor of a plain description.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical.
4. Keep the Part 4 chapters (12 to 15) from blurring, especially Ch 14 versus Ch 15, and keep visual controls distinct from the later Standardize S.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
