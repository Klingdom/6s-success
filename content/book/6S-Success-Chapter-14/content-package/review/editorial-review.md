# Editorial Review: Chapter 14 Content Package

Reviewed against the checklist: repetition, AI-sounding phrasing, unsupported claims, hooks, CTAs, source alignment, overlong social posts, tone, formatting. This review reports only checks actually run; all counts below were measured, and every cited noun/quote was verified present in the manuscript or final HTML before citing.

## Overall verdict
Publish-ready after author sign-off, with one standing caveat: the chapter is a draft (authored in the book voice, no source file supplied). The package holds the warm, anti-shame voice across all channels, the facts trace cleanly to the manuscript and HTML, and the hooks are concrete (the tidy row of blank homes and "where does this go?", the charger-cable drawer only you can read, "you were the label"). Findings below are minor.

## Repetition
- Cross-asset repetition of the spine (the "obvious without a word" rule, the Glance Test, the three cues, "you were the label") is by design.
- The hero vocabulary runs high ("control," "label," "home," "glance," "spot"), because it is the subject, not a tic. When excerpting, vary the surrounding language so a single post does not stack the same words.

## AI-sounding phrasing
- None found. The writing leans on concrete images (the workshop board of painted outlines, the umbrella stand whose form declares its job, the linen closet a guest can load correctly) rather than generic filler.
- Widened dash scan clean (em, en, and spaced-hyphen " - "). No hype words.

## Unsupported claims
- No invented statistics, names, or dates. The origin (visual controls making "a place for everything" visible) is referenced lightly and home-scaled; the chapter deliberately avoids the factory term "shadow board," describing the workshop board plainly instead.
- Testimonials in pdf-ebook/back-cover-copy.md are clearly marked placeholders.

## Hooks
- Strong and specific: the row of correct-but-blank homes with "where does this go?" floating over it, the charger-cable drawer a guest cannot read, and "You were the label."

## CTAs
- Every social post carries a soft CTA to the chapter or the online book. The hero action (give one mystery bin a label or a clear front, then ask someone else to put a thing away there) is concrete and free. Rotate wording before publishing; insert the live URL for "the online book."

## Source alignment
- Strong. The rule, the Glance Test, the three cues (label, outline, boundary) plus seeing-in and color, the entryway pass, and the third-Straighten-step friction move all match the manuscript and HTML.

## Overlong social posts (measured)
- X: a 16-post thread whose header states 16, all posts at or under 280 characters INCLUDING the "N/" number line (longest 277, the "you were the label" reframe); plus 10 short posts (longest 265). Pass.
- LinkedIn: 10 posts, each under 150 words (longest 146). Pass.
- Facebook longform: 433 words in the post body (within the 300 to 450 target). Not a flag.

## Tone
- Correct platform-fit; the light, anti-shame, weight-lifting register holds everywhere.

## Formatting
- Clean Markdown. Both JSON files parse; the publishable HTML JSON-LD validates. CSV has 7 columns and 56 data rows. The final HTML has 4 balanced SVG figures.

2. Insert the real chapter URL for "the online book."
