# Teleprompter Script: Visual Controls for the Home

**Chapter 14, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** visual-controls-for-the-home
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come stand with me at the door one final time.

Every keeper has a home now.

And every home sits where your hand wants it.

The keys, the leash, the bag, the umbrella, the mail.

You could put any of them away in the dark.

Because the whole arrangement lives in your head.

Now watch someone else.

A friend picks up your keys to lock up.

They stand there, looking at a row of hooks.

The hooks all appear the same.

The home is right there, a foot away.

Correct in every respect.

It simply never told anyone.

That gap is the whole of this chapter.

The rule for what belongs there sits in one place.

Your memory.

So the room runs beautifully when you are there.

And quietly comes undone when you are not.

None of this is anyone failing.

It is a design gap.

The knowledge exists.

It has just never been written down.

Not anywhere the thing itself can be seen.

So today we move that rule.

Out of your head, and onto the thing itself.

Think of a workshop shadow board.

Each tool has its own painted shape.

Reach for the hammer, and you know where it goes back.

And if the hammer is gone,

the empty shape tells you so from across the room.

The board holds the layout, so nobody has to.

That is a visual control, shrunk to fit a home.

A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word.

Now here is the test at the center of it.

Picture handing your house to a stranger.

A pet sitter. A new babysitter.

Then ask one question of any spot.

Could someone who has never lived here

put this away correctly, at a glance,

without opening it, reading a list, or asking?

If they can, the home speaks for itself.

If they would have to ask, it is still silent.

Most spots fail this at first.

And they fail it invisibly.

You never notice the ones that only work because of you.

Three simple cues cover almost everything.

The first is the label.

It names what belongs in a spot.

The bin that says batteries. The hook that says leash.

Label the spot, not just the thing.

The second is the outline.

A shape drawn or taped where the item sits.

An empty outline shows at a glance what is missing.

The label says what lives here.

The outline says whether it is home right now.

The third is the boundary.

A tray, a divider, a line.

When the lid will not close, the spot is full.

Add a clear jar to see the level.

Add one color per person.

Then stop there.

One cue per spot is nearly always plenty.

Now step back and notice something.

Every spot that only you understand

has had a label on it the whole while.

The label was you.

A cue on the shelf takes that job off you.

It never leaves, never tires,

and answers everyone the same.

Back to the door.

The keys get a little painted key shape behind the hook.

The leash gets a hook marked leash.

The umbrella needs no label.

Its stand's shape already says what it holds.

The bags get a labeled basket.

The mail gets a tray marked out.

Nothing moved. Each home just learned to speak.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Because it moves once more.

And again, nothing left the house.

The room holds what it held this morning.

The homes just learned to explain themselves.

Every "where does this go" no longer asked

is a small interruption removed.

This is the third step of Straighten.

But be honest about what the meter still shows.

Chapter 13's position is now the faint dashed ghost.

The earlier step sits further back.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

Because one more Straighten chapter remains.

And four more S's follow.

So here is your job.

Pick one bin, drawer, or shelf.

One whose contents are a mystery from the outside.

Give it one label or one clear container.

Then ask someone else to put a thing away there.

If they get it right without asking,

the home just started speaking.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go make one spot speak.
