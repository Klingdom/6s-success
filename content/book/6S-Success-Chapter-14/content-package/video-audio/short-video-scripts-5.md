# Short-Form Scripts: Visual Controls for the Home

**Chapter 14, 6S Success: Home Edition**

**Five scripts for Reels, Shorts, and TikTok, each 30 to 90 seconds.**

## SHORT 1: You were the label
**Topic: the reframe, the room only worked because you were there. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Every spot in your house that only you understand has had a label on it the whole time. The label was you.

**Script:**
Your home runs beautifully when you are there to run it, and quietly comes undone when you are not. A friend picks up your keys to lock up and stands there, looking at a row of hooks that all look the same. The home is a foot away, correct in every respect. It simply never told anyone. Here is why. You were the label. The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it. A visual control moves that knowledge out of your head and onto the shelf, where everyone can read it, including you on a tired day. This is meant to lift a weight off you. You have been the living index of the house. A cue on the shelf takes that job off you.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "The room only works when you are there."
- 14s: "You were the label."
- 28s: "Move the knowledge onto the shelf."
- 38s: "A cue never leaves and never tires."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A neat row of blank hooks and bins by the door; a person hesitating with keys in hand, a floating "where does this go?" Then a small label and a painted key shape appear on the wall, and the next person puts things away without pausing. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 2: The Glance Test
**Topic: the hero check. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** One plain test tells you whether a spot in your home actually explains itself, or just explains itself to you.

**Script:**
Picture handing your house to someone who has never set foot in it, a pet sitter, a new babysitter, a friend watering the plants. Then ask a single question of any spot. Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or asking? If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent. Every clause does work. At a glance means the answer arrives in the time it takes to look. Without opening it rules out the cue you only see once you have already pulled the drawer. And without asking is the heart of it. If they would text you a photo and wait, the home is still keeping its secret, however tidy it looks.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Hand your house to a stranger."
- 12s: "Could they put this away without asking?"
- 26s: "If yes, the home speaks for itself."
- 36s: "If they would have to ask, it is still silent."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A house key handed to a pet sitter at the door. A single spot in soft focus with a question mark hovering, which resolves into a small label. The sitter reads it and puts a thing away in one motion. Calm, warm light.

## SHORT 3: Three simple controls
**Topic: label, outline, boundary, plus seeing-in and color. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You do not need a workshop of techniques. Three cues cover almost everything in a house.

**Script:**
Three simple cues, all read in a look. The first is the label. It names what belongs in a spot: the bin that says batteries, the hook that says leash. The second is the outline, a shape drawn or taped where the item sits. Because it draws a thing's absence as clearly as its presence, an empty outline tells you at a glance that the thing is missing. The label says what lives here; the outline also says whether it is home right now. The third is the boundary, a tray, a divider, a line. Everything inside belongs, and when the lid will not close, the home is telling you it is full. Two lighter cues round it out: a clear jar so you can see the level, and one color per person. Ask what the spot keeps getting wrong, add the one cue that answers it, and stop there.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Three cues cover most of a house."
- 12s: "Label: what belongs here."
- 24s: "Outline: is it home right now?"
- 34s: "Boundary: is the spot full?"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Three calm vignettes in a row. A lidded bin labeled "batteries"; a hook with a drawn key silhouette, the hook empty; a divider tray with a marked fill line. Off to the side, a clear glass jar of pasta with the level visible. Each titled with its cue name.

## SHORT 4: Label the spot, not the thing
**Topic: the 6S Tip and the umbrella lesson. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Most labels go on the wrong thing, and that is why they quietly fail.

**Script:**
Label the spot, not just the thing. The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there. And here is the best trick in the chapter, taught by an umbrella. An umbrella stand needs no label at all, because its shape already tells you what it holds. You would never drop your keys into it. The stand is a cue built into the object's own home. So whenever you can choose a container whose form declares its job, you get the control for free, with nothing to print and nothing to draw. One cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces, is nearly always plenty.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Label the spot, not just the thing."
- 12s: "A label on the home greets the next thing."
- 24s: "An umbrella stand needs no label."
- 32s: "Its shape already says what it holds."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A label sliding off a moving item versus a label staying put on a bin as things come and go. Then an umbrella dropped into a stand whose shape obviously declares its job. Warm, home-scaled.

## SHORT 5: The needle steps again
**Topic: the worked entryway pass and the third Straighten step. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Nothing left the house, and the room still got easier to run.

**Script:**
Watch the door learn to speak. The keys get a little key shape painted behind the hook, so the outline sits empty and obvious when they are out. The leash gets a hook marked leash. The umbrella needs no label, because its stand's shape already says what it holds. The bags get a labeled basket, and the outgoing mail gets a tray marked out. Nothing moved; each home just learned to explain itself. Then look at the friction meter. Nothing left the house, yet the needle steps again toward calm, because the room stopped leaning on one person's memory. Every "where does this go" that no longer gets asked is friction removed. This is the third step of Straighten. The goal is still ahead, because one more Straighten chapter and four more S's remain.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Give each home a way to explain itself."
- 12s: "Keys, leash, umbrella, bags, mail, each speaks."
- 30s: "Nothing left the house. The needle still steps."
- 40s: "The third step of Straighten."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Each entryway home gaining its cue: a painted key shape, a hook marked leash, an umbrella stand, a labeled basket, a tray marked out. Cut to the friction meter: the live needle stepped past Chapter 13's position toward calm, Chapter 13 now a faint dashed ghost, the earlier step and the Sort-complete milestone further back, the GOAL crosshair still ahead on the green side.
