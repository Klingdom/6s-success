# Chapter 14 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Visual Controls for the Home

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, no force metaphors.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** Every home is placed perfectly. So why does everyone still ask where things go?

**Body:** Because a good home can still be silent. Swipe for the third Straighten step, and the one test that tells you whether a spot speaks for itself.

---

## Slide 2: The home that never told anyone

**Headline:** The home is right there, a foot away, correct in every respect. It simply never told anyone.

**Body:** A friend locking up stares at a row of identical hooks. Your child calls out the oldest question in any household: where does this go?

---

## Slide 3: The rule lives in one place

**Headline:** A home can be well placed and still keep its knowledge to itself.

**Body:** The rule for what belongs there sits in exactly one place: your memory. The room runs beautifully when you are there, and quietly comes undone when you are not.

---

## Slide 4: The shadow board

**Headline:** Each tool has its own painted shape on the wall behind it.

**Body:** Reach for the hammer and you know where it goes back. If it is gone, the empty shape tells you so from across the room. The board holds the layout so nobody has to.

---

## Slide 5: The rule

**Headline:** A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word.

**Body:** Any simple cue attached to a spot that makes the answer plain without a person, a conversation, or a list. You are marking storage you already arranged, not buying more.

---

## Slide 6: The Glance Test

**Headline:** Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without asking?

**Body:** If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent. That last clause is the heart of it.

---

## Slide 7: It fails invisibly

**Headline:** Most spots fail the test, and they fail it invisibly.

**Body:** You never notice the ones that only work because of you. Walk your rooms as a polite stranger and the failures show themselves. None of this is anyone failing. It is a design gap.

---

## Slide 8: Three simple cues

**Headline:** The label. The outline. The boundary.

**Body:** The label names what belongs. The outline shows whether it is home right now. The boundary shows when the spot is full. One cue per spot is nearly always plenty.

---

## Slide 9: Label the spot, not just the thing

**Headline:** Label the spot, not just the thing.

**Body:** The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there.

---

## Slide 10: You were the label

**Headline:** You were the label.

**Body:** The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it. A cue on the shelf takes that job off you. It never leaves, never tires, and answers everyone the same.

---

## Slide 11: It scales, and nothing leaves

**Headline:** One afternoon with a marker reaches every room.

**Body:** Labeled pantry bins, one color per child, a divider tray in the junk drawer, a labeled shelf edge in the linen closet. Nothing was added or removed; the homes just learned to explain themselves.

---

**Headline:** A good home does not need you to explain it.
