# Chapter 14 Calls to Action: Visual Controls for the Home

This is the third chapter of Part Four, and it continues Straighten, the second S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible test: give one mystery spot a single label or clear container, then ask someone else to put a thing away there and watch whether they get it right without asking. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: a silent home is a design gap, not a failing, and the fix is an afternoon with a marker, some tape, and containers you likely already own.

## Primary CTA
Make one spot speak. "Pick one bin, drawer, or shelf whose contents are a mystery from the outside, and give it one label or one clear container. Then ask someone else in the house to put a thing away there. If they get it right without asking, the home just started speaking. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Every home is well placed now, but each one is silent, obvious only to the person who built it. Chapter 14 makes each home explain itself with a visual control, judged by one plain test: could a stranger put this away correctly, at a glance, without asking? Read it free online, then Chapter 15 gives the household one shared map of where everything lives, so the whole room can be read at once."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one bin, drawer, or shelf whose contents are a mystery from the outside, and give it one label or one clear container. Then ask someone else in the house to put a thing away there. If they get it right without asking, the home just started speaking.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the house together and find the three spots where people always ask "where does this go?" Add one visual control to each, a label, an outline, or a bin, and let the room answer the question from now on.
- **6S Tip:** Label the spot, not just the thing. The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable test, not a burst of effort: mark one mystery spot and let someone else prove it now speaks. Lead with "make one spot speak," which is concrete and testable in a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame. A silent home is a design gap, not a family's failing; nobody wrote the rule down anywhere the thing itself can be seen.
- The rule (a visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word), the Glance Test, and the reframe (you were the label) are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is giving each already-placed home a way to explain itself.
- Scope note: this makes each home self-explaining; it does not re-assign homes (Chapter 12), re-place them by frequency (Chapter 13), map the whole room (Chapter 15), or build routines for keeping order (the later Standardize S). The homes are settled and placed; Sort is not re-opened.
