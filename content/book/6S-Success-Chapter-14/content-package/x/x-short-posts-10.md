# X Short Posts: Visual Controls for the Home

Chapter 14 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters, counting its number line, and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Chapter 14 of 6S Success: Home Edition continues Straighten. Every keeper has a home now, well placed, but each one is silent, obvious only to the person who built it. This chapter makes each home explain itself with one simple cue. Free online.

(approx. 242 chars)

---

2/
"A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word."

That is the whole idea of this chapter. A cue on the spot, a label, an outline, a clear jar, that answers what belongs here without a person, a conversation, or a list.

(approx. 231 chars)

---

3/
"Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or asking? If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent."

That is the Glance Test. Read it free online.

(approx. 259 chars)

4/
When a visitor guesses wrong or a family member calls out the old question, nobody is failing.

"None of this is anyone failing. It is a design gap. The knowledge exists; it has just never been written down anywhere the thing itself can be seen."

(approx. 244 chars)

---

5/
"You were the label. The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it. A visual control moves that knowledge out of your head and onto the shelf, where everyone can read it, including you on a tired day."

(approx. 236 chars)

---

6/
Three simple cues cover almost everything. The label names what belongs. The outline shows whether it is home right now, so an empty shape reveals a missing thing. The boundary shows when a spot is full. One cue per spot is nearly always plenty.

(approx. 242 chars)

---

7/
"Label the spot, not just the thing. The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there."

(approx. 216 chars)

---

8/
The umbrella stand needs no label: its shape already says what it holds.

"Whenever you can choose a container whose form declares its job, you get the control for free, with nothing to print and nothing to draw."

(approx. 210 chars)

---

9/
Make one spot speak. Pick one bin, drawer, or shelf whose contents are a mystery from the outside, and give it one label or one clear container. Then ask someone else to put a thing away there. If they get it right without asking, the home just started speaking.

(approx. 258 chars)

---

10/
"A good home does not need you to explain it. Put the knowledge where the thing lives, a label, an outline, a clear jar, and the room starts telling everyone what belongs where, so keeping order stops being your job alone."
