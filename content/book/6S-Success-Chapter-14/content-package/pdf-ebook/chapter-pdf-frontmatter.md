# Visual Controls for the Home

**Part Four, Straighten**
Chapter 14 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

## How to read this chapter

This is the third chapter of Part Four, Straighten, and it picks up exactly where the last one left off, with every keeper in a home and every home well placed, but each one silent. It is not about re-deciding whether a thing gets a home; you made those calls in Chapter 12. It is not about re-placing homes by frequency; you did that in Chapter 13. It is about one narrow job: give each spot a way to answer, on its own, the oldest question in any household, where does this go?

Read it through once for the idea, then walk your own rooms as a polite stranger, touching nothing, and let the silent spots show themselves. Judge each one by the Glance Test: could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or asking? Then reach for one of three simple cues and stop there. The label names what belongs in a spot. The outline shows the shape or place a thing goes, so its absence shows at a glance. The boundary shows the edges of a home and when it is overfull. Two lighter cues round out the set, seeing in (a clear jar or bin) and color (one shade per person or kind). Match one cue to the one confusion a spot keeps producing, because one cue per spot is nearly always plenty.

Nothing here re-assigns homes; the keepers are settled and stay. Nothing here re-places homes by frequency; that was Chapter 13. Nothing here maps how the whole room fits together as one shared picture; that is Chapter 15, and we only point to it. Nothing here builds routines, schedules, or standards for keeping order over time; that is a later S. Nothing re-opens Sort. Today has one narrow job: take each home you have already placed and give it a way to explain itself, so the rule inside your head ends up out here, where anyone can read it.

If you only do one thing: pick one bin, drawer, or shelf whose contents are a mystery from the outside, and give it one label or one clear container. Then ask someone else in the house to put a thing away there. If they get it right without asking, the home just started speaking.
