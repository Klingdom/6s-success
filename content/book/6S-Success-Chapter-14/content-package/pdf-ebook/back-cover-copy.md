# Back Cover Copy: Visual Controls for the Home

## Back-cover text

Every keeper has a home now, and every home sits where your hand wants it. Then you watch someone else use the room. A friend picks up your keys to lock up and stands at a row of hooks that all look the same; your child drops the reusable bag wherever and calls out, from the next room, the oldest question in any household: where does this go? "The home is right there, a foot away, correct in every respect. It simply never told anyone."

Chapter 14 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the third chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten, the second of the six S's. Chapter 12 assigned the homes; Chapter 13 placed them by how often you reach; this chapter gives each already-placed home a way to explain itself. Nothing has to be added or removed. The trouble is simple: "A home can be well chosen and well placed and still keep its knowledge to itself, because the rule for what belongs there sits in exactly one place: your memory." A silent home is not a failing; it is a design gap.

The fix is a visual control, and its rule is one line: "A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word." Judge every spot by the Glance Test: "Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or asking? If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent." Three simple cues cover most of a house, the label (what belongs here), the outline (whether it is home right now), and the boundary (when the spot is full), with seeing-in and color as lighter helps. Match one to the confusion a spot keeps raising: "One cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces, is nearly always plenty."

Then the reframe lands, and it is meant to lift a weight off you: "You were the label. The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it. A visual control moves that knowledge out of your head and onto the shelf, where everyone can read it, including you on a tired day." Nothing leaves the house, yet the friction meter takes its third Straighten step toward calm, because the room stopped leaning on one person to remember where everything goes. The step is real, and the goal is still ahead, because one more Straighten chapter and four more S's remain. Pick one mystery spot, give it one label or one clear container, then ask someone else to put a thing away there, and watch the home start speaking.
