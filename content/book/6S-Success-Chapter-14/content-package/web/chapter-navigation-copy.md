## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 14, the third chapter of Part Four, Straighten, and the third step of the second S. Every keeper has a home now, and every home sits where your hand wants it, but each one is silent, obvious only to the person who built it, because the rule for what belongs there lives in one place: your memory. This chapter turns from placing homes well to making each placed home explain itself: it does not re-assign homes (Chapter 12), re-place them by frequency (Chapter 13), map the whole room (Chapter 15), or build routines for keeping order (the later Standardize S), and Sort is not re-opened. Its tool is the visual control, any simple cue that makes the answer plain without a person, a conversation, or a list, judged by the Glance Test: could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without asking? Three simple cues do most of the work, the label, the outline, and the boundary, with seeing-in and color as lighter helps, and the reframe lands that you were the label. The entryway keepers get a voice, keys a labeled hook or painted silhouette, leash a labeled hook, umbrella a stand whose shape says what it holds, bags a labeled basket, mail a tray marked out, then the same cues scale to the pantry, the kids' zone, the junk drawer, and the linen closet. Nothing left the house, yet the friction meter takes its third Straighten step, because the room stopped leaning on one person's memory to run.
