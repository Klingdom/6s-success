# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 14, Visual Controls for the Home

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (the rule)

- og:title: A Visual Control Makes the Right Thing Obvious Without a Word
- og:description: A home can be well chosen and well placed and still keep its knowledge to itself. A visual control moves the rule out of your head and onto the shelf, where anyone can read it.
- twitter:title: A Visual Control Makes the Right Thing Obvious Without a Word
- twitter:description: The label names what belongs, the outline shows whether it is home, the boundary shows when it is full. Three cues that let anyone put a thing back at a glance. From Chapter 14 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A wall-by-the-door pegboard, workshop board shrunk to fit a hallway. Each thing has its own labeled outline drawn behind a hook: the keys hang over a key silhouette marked "keys," the leash over a leash outline marked "leash," and the scissors hook shows only an empty dashed silhouette marked "scissors," so a single glance reads as missing. A small note points to the empty shape: "empty outline = the thing is out." Along the bottom: A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word.

## Variant B (the reframe)

- og:title: You Were the Label
- og:description: If a room only holds together when you personally tend it, this is why. The system lived in your head. A cue on the shelf takes that standing duty off you, and answers everyone the same.
- twitter:title: You Were the Label
- twitter:description: You have been the living index of the house, consulted a dozen times a day, on call even when you are worn out. A control on the shelf never leaves and never tires. From Chapter 14 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: Two halves of the same entryway. On the left, a person stands mid-row holding keys while a floating question, "where does this go?", hangs above blank hooks and unmarked bins. On the right, the same row now labeled and outlined, the person gone, and a friend calmly returning the leash to the hook marked "leash." A curved arrow runs from left to right. Footer: The rule moves off your memory and onto the shelf, where everyone can read it.
