# Facebook Longform Post: The Home That Never Told Anyone

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 14 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the third Straighten step. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes.

---

Every keeper by my door finally had a home, and every home sat right where my hand wanted it. Keys on the first hook, leash beside them, reusable bag a step over, umbrella in its corner, mail in its tray by the door. I was proud of it. It worked, and I could put any of it away in the dark, without looking.

Then a friend came to lock up while we were away. She stood in the entryway holding my keys, looking at a row of hooks that all appeared the same, with no idea which one they belonged on. The home was right there, a foot away, correct in every respect. It simply never told anyone.

That is the whole gap. A home can be chosen well and placed well and still keep its knowledge to itself, because the rule for what goes where sits in exactly one place: my memory. The room ran while I was standing in it, and quietly came undone when I was not. None of this is anyone failing. It is a design gap. The knowledge exists; it has just never been written down anywhere the thing itself can be seen.

The fix has a name from old workshops, where each tool hangs in front of its own painted outline, so a glance shows where everything goes and one empty shape shows what is missing. Shrink that to fit a hallway and you get one sentence.

"A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word."

So I gave each keeper a voice. The keys got a small painted key shape behind the hook, present or plainly gone. The leash got a hook marked leash. The umbrella needed nothing at all, because the stand's shape already says what it holds. The bags got a labeled basket, and the mail got a tray marked out. Whenever a container's form declares its job, you get the control for free, with nothing to print and nothing to draw.

Nothing left the house. I just moved the rules out of my head and onto the shelves, hooks, and bins, where they wait for whoever needs them next, me included, on a day too tired to remember much.

So here is your low-pressure homework for the comments. Pick one spot that only you understand, give it one label or one clear container, then ask someone else to put a thing away there and watch whether they get it right without asking.
