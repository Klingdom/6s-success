# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 14

**"Visual Controls for the Home"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across all
five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single warm
red `#CB4B36` reserved for rare small accents only. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy
depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the settled entryway, and later a pantry shelf, a divider tray, and a
clear jar. Across Chapters 12 and 13, Straighten gave every keeper one clear home and then placed
each home by how often your hand goes to it: the keys, the dog's leash, the reusable shopping bag,
the folding umbrella, and the outgoing mail. Those homes are done, good, and well placed. This
chapter, the third of Part Four, does one narrow thing: it makes each already-placed home
SELF-EXPLAINING with a visual control, so the knowledge of what belongs where lives on the shelf
instead of in one person's head. Nothing is re-decided, re-placed, or removed. No home is
re-assigned (that was Chapter 12), no home is re-placed by frequency (that was Chapter 13), no whole
room is mapped at once as a single shared picture (Chapter 15), and no routine, schedule, or
standard for keeping order over time is built (the later Standardize S). Sort stays closed. Keep the
tone calm and kind: a silent home is a design gap, not a fault in the reader, and the fix is a cue,
not willpower. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no shame cues, and no force or pressure framing.

Separator discipline: any incidental label uses the middot, for example `CHAPTER 14 · STRAIGHTEN`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
self-explaining, easy-reach, see-through, buy-more, once-a-year, house-sitter, Sort-complete,
anti-shame.

---

## 1. The home that does not speak (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of a settled entryway in soft morning daylight. Along the wall by the
door runs a neat row of homes: two wall hooks, a small shelf, a couple of lidded bins, and a
shallow tray. Every home is clearly a good, well-chosen spot, tidy and calm, and every single one
is completely blank, with no label, no drawn outline, no color tag. A person stands in the middle of
the row holding a set of keys, glancing from hook to hook, gently unsure which home the keys belong
to. Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a soft slate blue wall. Editorial
cookbook-style photography, shallow focus along the row of unmarked homes. The room looks settled
and well kept, with one quiet, unspoken question hanging over the row: where does this go?

- Mood: the homes are done, good, and well placed, yet every one of them is mute, so the only
  person who can use the room without guessing is the person who built it, and a newcomer is left
  quietly guessing.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, any clutter or mess
  drama, any sense of re-deciding or moving what is placed, price tags, hurry or stress, and, most
  of all, any label, outline, or color cue on the homes; the opener's whole point is that they are
  blank.

## 2. The home that explains itself (the shadow board)

A warm editorial illustration of a small hook row or pegboard on the wall by a door, borrowing the
classic workshop shadow board and shrinking it to fit a home. Behind each hook is a softly painted
outline of the thing that belongs there. The keys hang over a painted key silhouette; the dog's
leash hangs over a painted leash outline; and one hook shows only an empty painted scissors
silhouette with no scissors on it, so a single glance reads that the scissors are out. Small,
plain, hand-lettered name-plates sit under each station. Warm paper cream background, honey wood and
soft-green painted outlines, ink-brown line work, one small warm-red accent reserved only for a
gentle pointer at the single empty outline. Documentary and unstaged, as if a household had just
traced each thing onto the wall behind its hook.

- Mood: the rule for where each thing belongs has been lifted out of one person's memory and onto
  the wall, where anyone can read it at a glance, and the one empty outline quietly shows what is
  out without anyone taking inventory.
- Avoid: a cold factory or industrial look, a busy wall of dozens of tools, harsh warning colors, a
  warm-red accent anywhere except the single empty outline, floating typeset text, any scoreboard
  or scolding tone about the missing item, any whole-room map. Keep it home-scaled and calm.

## 3. Three cues, three questions (the toolkit)

A warm editorial still-life of three small home-scaled controls in a row, with a fourth to the
side, each answering one plain question. First, a lidded bin with a simple hand-lettered label
reading "batteries" across the front: what belongs here. Second, a wall hook with a softly drawn
key silhouette behind it and no keys on it: whether it is home right now. Third, a shallow divider
tray with three bounded compartments, each holding one kind of small thing, and a light line marked
near the top: how much fits. To the side, a clear glass jar of dry pasta with the level plainly
visible and a small buy-more line drawn partway down: contents and level seen without opening.
Soft daylight, honey wood and paper tones, ink-brown shadows, soft-green on the drawn outline, a
single warm-red reserved only for the two small fill lines. Documentary and unstaged, as if a
household had just marked each spot with the one cue it needed.

- Mood: three ordinary cues cover most of a house, and each answers a slightly different question,
  so the small art is choosing the one cue a spot needs and then stopping there.
- Avoid: piling several cues onto one spot, rows of identical labels, a warm-red accent anywhere
  except the two small fill lines, cold clinical light, clutter or urgency, floating typeset text,
  any sense that more markers is better; one calm cue per spot is the whole lesson.

## 4. You were the label (the reframe)

A calm, warm photograph built as a quiet before-and-after in two small vignettes side by side. On
the left, the silent home: an ordinary blank shelf or bin with no cue at all, and a soft sense that
the only record of what belongs there lives in one tired person's memory, so the spot works only
when they are present. On the right, the same shelf or bin now wearing one small hand-lettered label
and a clear front, with a couple of different people each reading it the same way and putting a
thing away correctly without asking. Both scenes are ordinary and un-staged, and neither is a
success or a failure of character; the only difference is where the rule is written, in a head or on
the shelf. Warm paper cream background, honey light, ink-brown shadows, one soft-green calm note on
the labeled home that speaks. The image reads as two places for the same rule, not two kinds of
person.

- Mood: you have been the living index of the house, consulted a dozen times a day; a cue on the
  shelf takes that standing job off you, because it knows no less than you do, never leaves, and
  never tires, so this is a weight lifted, not a chore added.
- Avoid: a shaming before-and-after, a chaotic or messy left panel, guilt cues, any framing of the
  person as forgetful or at fault, a warm-red danger accent, harsh contrast, glossy retouching,
  floating typeset text. The reframe is a relief; keep it gentle.

## 5. The needle steps again, the third step of Straighten (the friction meter)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge
dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right. The
dark live needle has stepped one clear notch further toward the calm side than last chapter and now
rests a little past the spot the meter held at the end of Chapter 13. Just behind the live needle, a
faint dashed ghost needle marks that Chapter 13 position, showing the step just taken; fainter still,
a lighter dashed needle marks Chapter 12's earlier position further back. A small slate marker for
the Sort-complete milestone sits further back again, ground covered three chapters ago. A small
green marker labels the new live position as the third step of Straighten, the third step of the
second S. A slate crosshair labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag, still sits further out on
the green calm side, clearly ahead of the live needle, because one more Straighten chapter and four
more disciplines remain. Warm paper cream background, ink line work, consistent stroke weight,
friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest third step in the second S, nothing left the house yet the room grew easier to
  keep because the homes now explain themselves, so anyone can find and return a thing with less
  asking and less drifting, and the needle stepped once more, plainly, with plenty of road still
  ahead.
- Avoid: the live needle held in place with no move, the needle swung all the way to calm, the
  needle reaching or passing the goal crosshair, the dashed ghosts drawn solid or bright,
  celebratory confetti, hype, neon color, any sense of Straighten being finished or the journey
  being done. This is only the third step; Chapter 15 and four more S's still remain.
