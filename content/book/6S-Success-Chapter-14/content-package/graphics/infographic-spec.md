# Infographic Spec: The Home That Explains Itself

**Chapter 14, "Visual Controls for the Home"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a home
you chose well and placed well is still only half finished if the sole record of what belongs in it
is you, so a visual control moves that rule onto the thing itself, where anyone can read it. The
concept pairs the hero check, the **Glance Test** (could a stranger put this away correctly, at a
glance, without asking?), with the **three simple cues** beneath it, the label, the outline, and the
boundary, so the viewer sees both the test every spot must pass and the three plain cues that let it
pass, and sees at once that the cue answers what you have been answering by hand.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_14_final.html`. Green marks the cue
that speaks and the "it speaks" side of the check; slate carries the rule, the test, and the tags;
honey and terracotta stay to small accents and cue labels; the single small friction-spark is held
back and used only on a genuine fill line or an empty outline. One cue per spot is the discipline the
image itself should model.

floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
self-explaining, easy-reach, see-through, buy-more, once-a-year, house-sitter, Sort-complete,
anti-shame.

  "A visual control makes / the right thing obvious without a word."

**2. The Glance Test check (the hero, y approx 260 to 620)**
- A slate bordered check-strip carrying the frozen test as a plain question: "Could someone who has
  never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or
  asking?"

**3. The three cues (y approx 700 to 1180)**
- A short three-row stack, each row a cue with its picture, its name, and the one question it

  `what belongs here`.
- Row 2, the outline: a hook with a drawn key silhouette and no key present, a green `THE OUTLINE`

- Row 3, the boundary: a divider tray with a marked fill line, a slate `THE BOUNDARY` tag, and `how

- A small honey side-note under the three: `and a clear jar shows the contents and the level without
  opening` (seeing in), kept lighter than the main trio.
- A slate Newsreader italic line beneath the rows: "One cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion
  that spot produces."

**4. Footer line (y approx 1270)**

## Layout notes

- The Glance Test is the loudest element on the page. Draw it as the one plain question anyone could
  put to any spot, with a clear "it speaks" and "still silent" reading, so the eye grasps the test
  before the toolkit: if a guest would have to ask, the home is still silent, however tidy it looks.
- Hold the color discipline: green means the cue that speaks and the "it speaks" side of the check;
  slate carries the rule, the test, and the boundary tag; terracotta labels the label cue; honey
  stays to the lighter seeing-in note. The friction-spark is held back here; use it only on a fill
  line or an empty outline, never as general decoration, because most of this image is calm.
- The three cues are exactly the label, the outline, and the boundary, each mapped to one question:
  what belongs here, is it home right now, and how much fits. Keep the mapping one to one and plain,
  and model the chapter's own rule by giving each cue a single job.
- This image makes each already-placed home self-explaining and names the plain test it must pass.
  Do NOT re-assign homes or reopen that decision (that was Chapter 12). Do NOT re-place a home by
  frequency of use (that was Chapter 13). Do NOT map or plan a whole room's homes at once as one
  shared picture (Chapter 15). Do NOT build a routine, schedule, or standard for keeping order over
  time (the later Standardize S). Do NOT reopen Sort.
- Anti-shame: a silent home is a design gap, not a verdict on the person, and a spot that only works
  when you are there is information about a missing cue, not a failing. Keep it calm and
  non-coercive, no guilt cues, no pressure framing, and no force framing anywhere.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the rule set as if
  jotted rather than typeset.
