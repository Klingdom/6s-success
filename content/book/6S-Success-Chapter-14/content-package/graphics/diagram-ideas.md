# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 14

**"Visual Controls for the Home"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_14_final.html`:
the tidy but blank entryway where a person mid-row holds a set of keys under a floating question,
the shadow board where each thing has a labeled outline and one empty scissors outline reads as
missing, the three home-scaled controls (a labeled bin, a hook with a drawn key silhouette, a
divided tray with a fill line) plus a clear jar, and the friction meter stepped one notch past
Chapter 13 into the third step of Straighten.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the third of four Straighten chapters. Chapter 12 already gave every
keeper one clear home, and Chapter 13 already placed each home by how often your hand goes to it;
both are done and stay. Chapter 14 does one narrow thing: it makes each already-placed home
SELF-EXPLAINING with a visual control, so the knowledge of what belongs where lives on the shelf
instead of in one person's head. It does NOT re-assign homes (Chapter 12), it does NOT re-place
them by frequency (Chapter 13), it does NOT map how a whole room's homes fit together as one shared
picture (Chapter 15), and it does NOT build routines, schedules, or standards for keeping order over
time (the later Standardize S). It does not reopen Sort. Separator discipline: labels use the
middot, for example `CHAPTER 14 · STRAIGHTEN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between
words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: self-explaining, easy-reach, see-through,
buy-more, once-a-year, house-sitter, Sort-complete, anti-shame. Anti-shame throughout: a silent
home is a design gap, not a family's failing; the room that only works when you are there is
information about a missing cue, not a verdict on anyone. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no guilt
cues, no pressure framing, and no force or violence framing anywhere.

## 1. The Home That Does Not Speak (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's starting point in one image. Every keeper now has a well-placed
home, and that is real progress, but every home is completely unmarked, so the only person who can
use the room without guessing is the person who built it. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The settled entryway from the last two chapters, tidy and well arranged, but
blank. A neat row of homes runs along the wall by the door: two hooks, a small shelf, a couple of
lidded bins, and a tray. Each is clearly a good spot, and each is completely unmarked. A person
stands in the middle of the row holding a set of keys, looking from home to home, unsure. A soft
floating question hangs above their head: where does this go?

**Rough layout:**
- Warm daylight wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient), a low floor line in ink, the wall
  calm and settled.
- A wall rail in soft wood fill `#E7C58B`, rounded corners, holding two blank hooks; a small bare
  shelf below it; two blank lidded bins and a tray on the other side of the frame. Every home is
  drawn clean and correct, and not one carries a label, an outline, or a color.
- A person in soft ink line stands mid-row holding a set of keys, glancing from home to home. A
  small faint sans note beneath the row in muted `#6A625A`: `the person who built it is the only
  one who knows`.
- Above the row, a soft floating question in a rounded panel with a terracotta outline, Fraunces
  italic in `#BC4B2A`: `where does this go?`
- Caption idea: "The homes are all here, well chosen and well placed. But every one of them is
  blank, so the only person who can use the room without guessing is the person who built it. The
  question in the air is the one this chapter answers."

**Note for the illustrator:** the homes here are good, not a mess, so draw the row as tidy and
settled, not as clutter. The quiet tension is that every correct home is mute. Do not move,
re-rank, or re-assign anything; that placement is finished. Do not add any cue yet either, no
label, no outline, no color; the whole point of the opener is that the homes are blank. The one
floating question does all the work. Keep it warm and matter-of-fact, no scolding about the blank
homes, just a quiet noticing.

## 2. The Home That Explains Itself (the hero device, the shadow board)

**Purpose:** Make the visual control concrete by borrowing the classic workshop shadow board,
shrunk to fit a wall by the door. It shows the two things a good cue does at once: it says where a
thing goes, and, when the outline is empty, it says the thing is out. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A pegboard or hook row on the wall where each thing has its own labeled outline
drawn behind a hook. The keys hang over a painted key silhouette, labeled `KEYS`. The leash hangs
over a leash outline, labeled `LEASH`. The scissors hook shows only an empty dashed scissors
silhouette, no scissors on it, labeled `SCISSORS`, so a single glance shows the scissors are out. A
small note points to the empty outline.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label in soft gray: `THE HOME THAT
  EXPLAINS ITSELF`.
- A pegboard in `#F3EEE2` with a light ink border and faint peg dots, rounded corners.
- Keys station: a soft-green painted outline `#6E8B5B` with the ink key drawn present over it, a
  small ink name-plate `KEYS`.
- Leash station: a soft-green painted leash outline with the ink leash present over it (a small
  honey tab for the clip), a name-plate `LEASH`.
- Scissors station: only the dashed soft-green outline, no item present, a green name-plate
  `SCISSORS`. A small friction-spark `#CB4B36` dashed pointer and a Newsreader italic note:
  `empty outline = the thing is out`.
- Along the bottom, a slate Newsreader italic rule: "The rule moves off your memory and onto the
  wall, where anyone can read it."
- Caption idea: "The classic workshop board, shrunk to fit a wall by the door. Each thing has a
  labeled outline, so a look tells you both where it goes and whether it is home. The keys and
  leash are here; the scissors are out, and the empty shape says so without anyone taking
  inventory."

**Note for the illustrator:** the single friction-spark is reserved for the one empty outline, the
one honest snag the board is meant to surface, and used nowhere else. The board is calm and helpful,
never a scoreboard of failure; the missing scissors is information, not blame. Reference the
workshop origin lightly and keep it home-scaled: this is a wall by the door, not a factory. Do not
turn this into a whole-room map; it is one hook row. Keep the stroke weight even and the mood
quietly confident.

## 3. Three Cues, Three Questions (the toolkit)

**Purpose:** Make the three cues concrete and ordinary, and show that each answers a slightly
different question: what belongs here, is it here now, and how much fits. A fourth lighter cue,
seeing in, rounds out the set. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** Three calm, home-scaled vignettes in a row, with a fourth smaller one to the
side. First, a lidded bin with a clear label reading `batteries` across the front (the label).
Second, a hook with a drawn key silhouette behind it, the hook empty, so the outline shows a thing
is out (the outline). Third, a shallow divider tray with three compartments, each holding one kind
of small thing, and a marked fill line near the top (the boundary). Off to the side, a clear glass
jar with the level plainly visible and a small buy-more line drawn partway down (seeing in).

**Rough layout:**
- Panel background, top sans caps label in soft gray: `THREE CUES, THREE QUESTIONS`.
- Vignette 1, the label: a lidded bin in `#EFE7D6` with a wood-fill lid, a cream label plate
  reading `batteries` in ink sans. A terracotta sans caps tag `THE LABEL` and a Newsreader italic
  helper `what belongs here`.
- Vignette 2, the outline: a hook panel in `#F3EEE2` with a soft-green painted key silhouette and
  an empty ink hook peg (no keys present). A green sans caps tag `THE OUTLINE` and a helper `is it
  home right now`.
- Vignette 3, the boundary: a shallow divider tray in `#EFE7D6` with two dividers making three
  bounded slots, each holding one small thing (a honey dot, a slate square, a small green
  triangle), and a friction-spark dashed `full line` near the top. A slate sans caps tag `THE
  BOUNDARY` and a helper `how much fits`.
- Vignette 4, seeing in: a clear see-through jar in cool `#EEF3F6` with a visible fill level in
  wood fill and a friction-spark dashed `buy more` line partway down. A honey-brown sans caps tag
  `SEEING IN` and a helper `contents & level`.
- Faint rule ticks between the vignettes. Along the bottom, a slate Newsreader italic line: "One
  cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces."
- Caption idea: "Three cues cover most of a house. The label names what belongs; the outline shows
  whether it is home; the boundary shows when the spot is full. A clear jar adds a fourth trick: it
  shows the contents and the level without being opened."

**Note for the illustrator:** each vignette answers exactly one question, so keep each one simple
and uncrowded; the chapter's own warning is that piling three cues on one spot just makes it busy.
The friction-spark appears only on the two fill lines (the boundary and the buy-more mark), where it
signals a genuine "full" or "low" reading, and nowhere else. Color stays disciplined: green for the
outline cue, slate for the boundary, honey-brown for seeing in, terracotta for the label tag. Do not
add a room map or a placement rule here; this is the cue toolkit only.

## 4. You Were the Label (the reframe)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's central reframe as a picture: the system that only you understood
was really a label written in your memory, and a visual control simply moves that label out of your
head and onto the shelf, where it never leaves and never tires. This diagram has no direct HTML
twin; it renders the "You Were the Label" section as a paired-panel figure, then the Glance Test as
the check beneath.

**What it shows:** Two facing panels. The left is the silent home: a blank shelf or bin with a
small thought-bubble drawn back to a tired person, the only copy of the rule living inside one
head. The right is the home that speaks: the same shelf or bin, now carrying a small label and a
clear front, with several calm figures reading it the same way. Below them, the Glance Test runs as
a single plain question anyone can put to any spot.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `YOU WERE THE LABEL`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath: "move
  the rule out of your head and onto the shelf".
- Left panel, muted header bar `THE SILENT HOME`: a blank bin with no cue, a dashed thought-line
  running up to a small ink figure with a faint `?` and a Newsreader italic note `the rule lives in
  one head`. Draw it calm and neutral, tired but not sad; a small slate tag at the base `WORKS ONLY
  WHEN YOU ARE THERE`.
- Right panel, green header bar `THE HOME THAT SPEAKS`: the same bin now wearing a small label plate
  and a clear front, with two or three small figures beside it each reading the cue the same way, a
  green calm-dot on the labeled home. Newsreader italic beneath: `the cue never leaves and never
  tires`. A green sans caps line at the base `WORKS FOR EVERYONE, EVEN YOU ON A TIRED DAY`.
- Beneath the two panels, a slate bordered check-strip carrying the Glance Test as a plain
  question: "Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without
  opening it, reading a list, or asking?" with a small green check icon on the "yes, it speaks"
  side and a small muted mark on the "still silent" side.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "You were the label. A visual control moves that knowledge onto
  the shelf, where everyone can read it, including you on a tired day."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a relief, not a reproach, so draw the left panel as an
ordinary design gap, never as a person failing; the reframe lifts a weight off the reader. Keep the
two panels balanced and calm, no friction-spark on the silent home and no scolding cue. The Glance
Test strip is a plain yes-or-no check anyone could run in the time it takes to look, so keep it
uncluttered. Green marks the home that speaks; slate carries the rule and the check. Do not add a
routine, a schedule, or a whole-room map; the reframe stays at the level of the single home's cue.

## 5. The Friction Meter, Third Step of Straighten (the milestone)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest third-Straighten-step move. Nothing left the house
this time either, but making the homes explain themselves cut the room's friction again, so the
live needle steps one more notch past Chapter 13's position toward calm, the third step of the
second S, with the goal still ahead. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle stepped a little further
toward calm than Chapter 13's spot, Chapter 13's position now a faint dashed ghost just behind the
live needle, Chapter 12's earlier position fainter still, the Sort-complete milestone sitting
further back as a passed marker, and the goal crosshair still further ahead on the calm side.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark (right),
  on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- The `SORT COMPLETE` milestone in slate, marked on the band with a small slate chip, now sitting
  further back as ground covered three chapters ago.
- The live needle in ink, stepped one clear notch past Chapter 13's position toward calm, its new
  resting spot flagged by a green marker `STRAIGHTEN · STEP 3` with a small green dot.
- Behind the live needle, a faint dashed gray needle at Chapter 13's position, tagged in small sans
  `Ch 13`, at about 55 percent opacity so it reads clearly as the ground just covered, not the live
  reading.
- Fainter still, a lighter dashed needle at Chapter 12's earlier position, tagged `Ch 12` at about
  40 percent opacity, the earlier step further back.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still clearly
  ahead of the live needle, because one more Straighten chapter (15, The Room Map) and then Shine,
  Safety, Standardize, and Sustain all still follow.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The needle steps again, deeper into Straighten. The goal
  is still ahead."
- Caption (frozen): "The needle steps again, the third step of Straighten. Nothing was added or
  removed; the homes just learned to explain themselves, so the room stopped leaning on one person
  to remember where everything goes. The goal is still ahead, because one more Straighten chapter
  and four more S's remain."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is the THIRD step of Straighten, not the second, not a
hold, and not an arrival. The live needle must sit one clear notch past Chapter 13's position, with
Chapter 13's spot drawn as the faint dashed ghost just behind it, Chapter 12's earlier spot fainter
still, and the Sort-complete milestone sitting further back again. The needle must NOT reach the
goal crosshair; the goal stays clearly ahead on the green side, because Chapter 15 still completes
Straighten and then four more S's (Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain) remain. Do not draw a held
needle with no move, and do not swing it all the way to calm. The honest message is exact: nothing
left the house, yet the homes learning to explain themselves made the room easier again, so the
needle stepped once more, plainly, and stopped well short of the end. No confetti, no completion
glow.
