**The recurring motif.** The hero device of the chapter is the visual control: the home that speaks. A home can be well chosen (Chapter 12) and well placed (Chapter 13) and still be silent, obvious only to the person who built it. A visual control is any simple cue attached to a spot that moves the rule for what belongs there out of one person's memory and onto the thing itself, so the home works even when you are not there to run it. The three cues carry fixed colours across the set: the label is terracotta #BC4B2A, the outline is green #6E8B5B, the boundary is slate #3C5A6B, and the lighter seeing-in cue is honey #DDA63A. Green also marks the home that speaks and the calm side of the meter; soft #6A625A carries the silent, blank, unmarked state and the captions; friction spark #CB4B36 is reserved for the friction side of the meter; terracotta doubles as the chapter kicker; slate carries the chapter labels, the GOAL crosshair, and the milestone ticks. The ghost needle #8A8073 is Chapter 13's position on the meter, now the dashed ghost, with Chapter 12's fainter still.

**Two fences to hold visually.** First, this is making each already-placed home self-explaining, not re-assigning it (Chapter 12) or re-placing it by frequency (Chapter 13): every keeper already has a well-placed home, so never redraw the giving-a-home pass, never re-sort the homes by reach, and never reopen Sort; this chapter only gives each home a cue so it can answer for itself. Second, do not draw the single map that shows where everything lives at once (that is Chapter 15, a teaser only), and do not build routines, schedules, or household standards for keeping order over time (that is the later Standardize S, a teaser only). Keep the focus on the individual home's cue.

## Beat 4 · What a visual control is

The chapter's core idea, carried by the shadow-board image. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Composition: the shadow board. A hook row or pegboard where each thing has its own labeled outline in ink #2B2622 on paper ground, so a glance shows where everything goes. One outline is empty, drawn as a green #6E8B5B gap that reads at once as a thing that is out.
- Palette: ink outlines on paper, green #6E8B5B on the one empty outline, slate #3C5A6B on the caption. Calm and ordinary, not a factory.
- Caption, verbatim from the figure: "The rule moves off your memory and onto the wall, where anyone can read it."
- Point: the board took the rule for where each tool belongs and lifted it out of one person's memory and onto the wall. That is the whole idea of a visual control, shrunk to fit a home: any simple cue attached to a spot that makes the answer plain without a person, a conversation, or a list. You are marking the storage you already arranged, not buying more.
- The frozen rule sits beneath as a Fraunces pull-quote on panel #FBF7EF with slate #3C5A6B rules: "A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word." The frozen definition box may accompany it: "A visual control is any simple cue, a label, an outline, a color, a boundary, a clear container, that makes it obvious at a glance what belongs in a spot and whether it is there. It moves the rule out of your memory and onto the thing itself, so the home works even when you are not there to run it."

## Beat 5 · The Glance Test

The hero check of the chapter, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the Glance Test set large in Fraunces medium #2B2622. Beside it, a small vignette: a plain drawer with a strip of tape reading "cables" in ink #2B2622, and a hand dropping a stray cable in.
- Palette: slate on the rules, ink on the tape, a single green #6E8B5B calm-dot marking that the guest got it right without asking. Paper ground. No spark.
- A soft #6A625A tag: "the guest reads it in a second."
- Caption, verbatim (the frozen hero check): "Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without opening it, reading a list, or asking? If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent."
- Point: every clause does work. At a glance, without opening it, without reading a list, and, at the heart of it, without asking. Run the test on your own house and most spots fail it invisibly, because you never notice the ones that only work because of you.

## Beat 6 · Three simple controls

The three cues, made concrete and ordinary. Rebuild from the HTML SVG.

- Composition: three home-scaled vignettes in a row, with a fourth to the side. First, a lidded bin with a clear label reading "batteries" across the front, titled in terracotta #BC4B2A "THE LABEL." Second, a hook with a drawn key silhouette behind it and no keys on it, titled in green #6E8B5B "THE OUTLINE." Third, a shallow divider tray, three compartments each holding one kind of small thing, a marked fill line near the top, titled in slate #3C5A6B "THE BOUNDARY." To the side, a clear glass jar of pasta with the level visible and a small buy-more line, titled in honey #DDA63A "SEEING IN."
- Palette: terracotta on the label, green on the outline, slate on the boundary, honey on the seeing-in jar. Paper ground, ink outlines. Calm, home-scaled, no clutter.
- Caption, verbatim: "Three cues cover most of a house. The label names what belongs; the outline shows whether it is home; the boundary shows when the spot is full. A clear jar adds a fourth trick: it shows the contents and the level without being opened."
- Point: each cue answers a slightly different question, what belongs here, is it here now, and how much fits. The empty outline is the quiet star: it draws a thing's absence as clearly as its presence.

## Beat 7 · Match the cue to the question

The small art of the chapter: one cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces.

- Composition: a matching diagram on paper ground. Three questions on the left in soft #6A625A, "what belongs here?", "is it here now?", "how full is it?", each joined by a single slate #3C5A6B line to its one cue on the right: the label in terracotta #BC4B2A, the outline in green #6E8B5B, the boundary in slate #3C5A6B. To the side, one drawer drawn busy with three cues piled on, greyed and set aside.
- Palette: soft on the questions, the three cue colours on the right, a soft #6A625A note on the busy drawer: "one confusion, one cue." No spark on the diagram itself.
- Beneath, the 6S Tip in a slate #3C5A6B bordered callout, verbatim: "Label the spot, not just the thing. The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there." And the Common Mistake in a friction spark #CB4B36 bordered callout, verbatim: "Making the cue for you instead of for a stranger. Codes, abbreviations, and \"I will remember\" markers keep the system in your head. If a guest could not read it and act on it, it is decoration, not a control."
- The frozen line may run along the base in Newsreader italic: "One cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces, is nearly always plenty."

## Beat 8 · You were the label

The reframe, the anti-shame center of the chapter, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the reframe set large in Fraunces medium #2B2622. Beside it, a gentle before-and-after: a person drawn as a paper label tied to every hook by soft #6A625A threads, then the same threads let go, the labels now resting on the hooks themselves in green #6E8B5B, the person stepping back, lighter.
- Palette: soft #6A625A on the threads and the person carrying every rule, green #6E8B5B on the cues once they rest on the shelf. No verdict colours; this beat lifts a weight, it does not add one.
- A soft #6A625A tag: "the shelf holds the answer now."
- Caption, verbatim (the frozen reframe): "You were the label. The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it. A visual control moves that knowledge out of your head and onto the shelf, where everyone can read it, including you on a tired day."
- Point: you have been the living index of the house, consulted a dozen times a day, on call even when you are worn out. A cue on the shelf takes that job off you. It knows no less than you do; it just never leaves, never tires, and answers everyone the same.
