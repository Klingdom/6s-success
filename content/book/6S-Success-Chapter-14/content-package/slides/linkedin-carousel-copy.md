## Visual Controls for the Home

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 14. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36, ghost needle #8A8073. ### Slide 1 (hook)

**Every home is placed perfectly. So why does everyone still ask where things go?**

Because a good home can still be silent.

Swipe for the third Straighten step, and the one test that tells you whether a spot speaks for itself.

[VISUAL] The finished entryway: a neat row of homes by the door, hooks, a shelf, lidded bins, a tray, every one well placed and completely blank. A person stands in the middle holding keys, looking from hook to hook. A soft floating question above: "where does this go?" Warm paper, slate chapter label. No cue colours yet; the point is their absence.

---

### Slide 2

**The home is right there, a foot away, correct in every respect. It simply never told anyone.**

A friend locking up stares at a row of identical hooks. Your child calls out the oldest question in any household: where does this go?

The room runs beautifully when you are there, and quietly comes undone when you are not.

[VISUAL] A split moment: left, the builder landing the keys on the first hook without looking, a soft green practiced path; right, a friend in the same spot holding the keys, a soft "where does this go?" above the identical hooks. No spark; nobody here is failing.

---

### Slide 3

**A home can be well placed and still keep its knowledge to itself.**

The rule for what belongs there sits in exactly one place: your memory.

None of this is anyone failing. It is a design gap. The knowledge exists; it has just never been written down anywhere the thing itself can be seen.

[VISUAL] The tidy row of blank homes, tagged in soft "well chosen · well placed · silent." Above, a thought-bubble holding the whole layout as a tiny sketch, chained to one head, labelled "the only copy of the answer." A slate footnote: "a design gap, not a failing."

---

### Slide 4

**Each tool has its own painted shape on the wall behind it.**

Reach for the hammer and you know where it goes back. If it is gone, the empty shape tells you so from across the room.

The board holds the layout so nobody has to. That is the whole idea, shrunk to fit a home.

[VISUAL] The shadow board: a hook row where each thing has its own labeled outline in ink on paper, so a glance shows where everything goes. One outline is an empty green gap, a thing that is out. Slate caption: "the rule moves off your memory and onto the wall."

---

### Slide 5

**A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word.**

Any simple cue attached to a spot that makes the answer plain without a person, a conversation, or a list.

You are marking storage you already arranged, not buying more.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the frozen rule set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A single green calm-dot beside a small tag: "mark the storage you already have." No sparks.

---

### Slide 6

**Could someone who has never lived here put this away correctly, at a glance, without asking?**

If they can, the home speaks for itself. If they would have to ask, it is still silent.

That last clause is the heart of it.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the Glance Test set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Beside it, a plain drawer with a strip of tape reading "cables" and a hand dropping a stray cable in, a green calm-dot: got it right without asking.

---

### Slide 7

**Most spots fail the test, and they fail it invisibly.**

You never notice the ones that only work because of you. Walk your rooms as a polite stranger and the failures show themselves.

None of this is anyone failing. It is a design gap.

[VISUAL] A room drawn in soft outline with several spots quietly flagged in soft, each carrying a small "would have to ask" tag. A person walks through as a polite stranger, touching nothing. Patient, never scolding; no verdict colours.

---

### Slide 8

**The label. The outline. The boundary.**

The label names what belongs. The outline shows whether it is home right now. The boundary shows when the spot is full.

One cue per spot, aimed at the one confusion that spot produces, is nearly always plenty.

[VISUAL] Three home-scaled vignettes: a lidded bin labeled "batteries" in terracotta "THE LABEL"; a hook with a drawn key silhouette, empty, in green "THE OUTLINE"; a divider tray with a marked fill line in slate "THE BOUNDARY." To the side, a clear jar of pasta with a honey buy-more line, "SEEING IN."

---

### Slide 9

**Label the spot, not just the thing.**

The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks.

A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there.

[VISUAL] A labeled bin drawn twice: once with the label stuck on a battery that then leaves the bin, the label gone with it; once with the label on the bin's front, staying to greet the next battery. A green calm-dot on the labeled home. The 6S Tip on a slate-bordered callout beneath.

---

### Slide 10

**You were the label.**

The system lived in your head, so the room only worked when you were there to run it.

A cue on the shelf takes that job off you. It never leaves, never tires, and answers everyone the same.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the frozen reframe set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Beside it, a person drawn as a paper label tied to every hook by soft threads, then the threads let go, the labels resting on the hooks in green, the person stepping back, lighter.

---

### Slide 11

**One afternoon with a marker reaches every room.**

Labeled pantry bins, one color per child, a divider tray in the junk drawer, a labeled shelf edge in the linen closet.

Nothing was added or removed. The homes just learned to explain themselves.

[VISUAL] A four-room strip: the pantry with a clear jar and a honey buy-more line; the kids' zone with a green and a blue cubby; the junk drawer with slate divider slots; the linen closet with a terracotta "sheets here · towels there" shelf edge. Soft footer: "a marker, tape, and containers you already own."
