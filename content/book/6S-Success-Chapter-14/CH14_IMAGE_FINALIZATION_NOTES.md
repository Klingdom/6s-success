## MUST FIX BEFORE PRINT · the LABEL tip on `ch14-image05` is inverted

`ch14-image05` is the chapter's anchor plate. It is the only plate that teaches all three controls
correctly, in the frozen order, with the frozen definitions, correctly demoting SEEING IN to a
bonus, and it carries the outline's signature power ("When the outline is empty, you know
something's missing").

**But its LABEL tip reads:**

> "Label what it is, not where it goes."

**The chapter's frozen 6S Tip reads:**

> "Label the spot, not just the thing."

These are exact opposites. The frozen tip exists for a stated reason: *a label on the item travels
away with the item; a label on the home stays.* The plate tells the reader to do the opposite, and
it will sit within a page of the callout that says so.

Verified directly, not via audit. The plate was wired in because the chapter's toolkit section
cannot stand unillustrated and everything else on it is correct, but **this single line cannot go
to print.** It is a one-line fix on an otherwise excellent plate.

## Other defects on the wired-in set

### Canon and vocabulary

- **The frozen rule never appears anywhere.** "A visual control makes the right thing obvious
  without a word" is not printed on any of the 14 plates.
- **The Glance Test is paraphrased.** `image03` prints "when a stranger can put something away
  correctly without asking a single question", dropping the frozen "who has never lived here", "at
  a glance", and "without opening it, reading a list".
- **The reframe is half-printed.** `image04` gives the title "You Were the Label" but renders the
  second frozen sentence as "The system depends on your memory" rather than "The system lived in
  your head."
- **`image02` renames the controls.** Its callouts read HOME / OUTLINE / MISSING ITEM / VISUAL CUE.
  Only OUTLINE is canon; "HOME" re-states Chapter 12. The plate was kept because it matches the
  existing shadow-board figure exactly and teaches the empty-outline power well, but the callout
  names should be reconciled to LABEL / OUTLINE / BOUNDARY.

### The umbrella is wrong on every plate that shows it

The frozen worked example makes a specific teaching point: the **umbrella stand is already a visual
control by its shape and needs no label**, which demonstrates that the best control is sometimes
the object itself.

On `image06` the umbrella hangs on a hook while an unlabelled stand sits nearby, uncalled-out. The
same is true of every held plate that shows it. Only `image01`, the pre-controls opener, shows
umbrellas in the stand. **The single best teaching moment in the worked example is never taken.**

`image06` also carries two smaller faults: the umbrella callout says "easy to reach", which is
Chapter 13 language, and callouts 3 and 5 appear mis-routed, with the reusable-bags leader line
terminating at the umbrella stand.

### Rights and copy

- **`ch14-image08` shows legible Bounty paper-towel packaging** in both the before and after
  baskets. This is the chapter's best hero plate, so it is worth clearing.
- **`ch14-image04` has two pantry jar labels that render as unreadable glyph strings.** Minor
  anywhere else; in a labels chapter it undercuts the argument.
- **`ch14-image07` prints "BROWN SUGAR" on two adjacent containers.**
- **Contractions** on `image02`, `image04`, `image05` and `image08`. House voice uses none.
- **No em dashes anywhere in the set.** Clean on that count.
- **No six-S sequences anywhere**, so no Safety or "Set in Order" violations.

### QR codes

`image07` and `image08` carry QR codes among the wired-in set; four more appear on held plates.
All promise near-identical things ("scan to see more real homes"). They were **not** machine-tested

consider consolidating seven near-identical promises down to one.

### The outline is the chapter's weakest link

The outline is one of the three frozen controls, and its signature power, that an **empty** outline
shows what is missing, appears on only **two** of fourteen plates (`image02` and `image05`). It is
absent by name from six plates. For a control with a distinctive teaching point, it is badly
under-represented, and any regeneration should lean into it.

A reader moving through the full set would meet three different schemes: the canon LABEL / OUTLINE
/ BOUNDARY (on 05, 07, 08), a four-item invention on `image02`, and a six-item invention on
`image09` and `image14`. Holding the last group resolves this for now.

# SALVAGE PASS · 2026-07-21

A second pass over the six held images, run the same way as Chapter 12's.

**Result: none recovered. The chapter is unchanged.**

Chapter 14's held set fails differently from Chapter 13's, and the difference is worth recording
because it points at a cheaper fix.

## The photography is fine. The copy is what fails.

Unlike Chapter 13's held plates, which are about the wrong subject, **every held Chapter 14 plate
actually depicts visual controls**: labelled bins, an OUTGOING MAIL basket, taped boundaries, a
before-and-after of a silent room becoming a speaking one. The images are on-subject. What
disqualifies them is printed text sitting on top of correct photographs.

`ch14-image10` "The Power of Visual Controls" is the clearest case, and I examined it directly. It
is a genuine silent-versus-speaking before and after of the canonical entryway, with labelled
baskets and the outgoing-mail slot. It fails on three counts, none of them photographic:

1. A visible typo in the tip box: **"the system is hard to yse and easy to break."**
2. The centre roundel is clipped by the arrow graphic and reads **"SAME SPAC."**
3. Its right-hand column names the controls CLEAR PURPOSE / EASY TO SEE / SET BOUNDARIES / LESS
   FRICTION. Only one of those is canon, and neither LABEL nor OUTLINE is named.

It is also substantially redundant with the wired-in `image08`, which does the same silent-versus-
speaking contrast in a closet **and** names all three controls correctly.

## The rule that blocks caption-rescue here

**A caption may reframe an image's scope. It must not contradict the image's own printed labels.**

Chapter 12's rescue worked by *adding* context a plate did not deny: "these two panels are the next
two chapters." Chapter 14's held plates would need a caption that *argues with* what the reader can
plainly see printed on the artwork, telling them that the six callout names on `image09` and
`image14` are not really the controls. That is worse than leaving the plate out.

This is why the renamed-control plates cannot be captioned back into scope no matter how good the
photograph underneath them is.

## Two further reasons not to force these in

The chapter already carries eight figures, and the manuscript runs **4,487 words against a frozen
target of 3,600 to 3,900**, roughly 600 to 900 words long, in a brief that says "Keep it TIGHT."

## What would actually improve this chapter

Not more plates. The chapter has three real content gaps that no supplied image fills, and they are
worth commissioning over rescuing anything above:

1. **The frozen rule**, "A visual control makes the right thing obvious without a word", appears
   nowhere in the set.
2. **The umbrella teaching moment.** The canonical point is that an umbrella stand is already a
   visual control by its shape and needs no label. Every plate that shows an umbrella puts it on a
   hook instead.
3. **The empty outline.** Its signature power, that an empty outline shows what is missing, appears
   on only two of fourteen plates.
