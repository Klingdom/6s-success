## Hero device: The visual control (the home that speaks)
A home can be well chosen (Ch 12) and well placed (Ch 13) and still be silent: only the person who built it knows what belongs there. A visual control is any simple cue, a label, an outline, a color, a boundary, a clear container, that makes the home explain itself. Its history is the Straighten (Seiton) idea of making "a place for everything" visible, the shadow board being the classic example. Referenced lightly, home-scaled, no overclaiming.

## Callouts (inventory; frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one bin, drawer, or shelf whose contents are a mystery from the outside, and give it one label or one clear container. Then ask someone else in the house to put a thing away there. If they get it right without asking, the home just started speaking.
- **Family Challenge:** Walk the house together and find the three spots where people always ask "where does this go?" Add one visual control to each, a label, an outline, or a bin, and let the room answer the question from now on.
- **6S Tip:** Label the spot, not just the thing. The bin, the shelf edge, the hook, that is where a guest looks. A label on the item travels away with the item; a label on the home stays to greet the next thing that belongs there.
- **Common Mistake:** Making the cue for you instead of for a stranger. Codes, abbreviations, and "I will remember" markers keep the system in your head. If a guest could not read it and act on it, it is decoration, not a control.
- **Definition box:** "A visual control."
- **One Idea to Keep** (frozen box).
