## Identity strings (frozen; copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)
- **Title:** Daily 6S Times
- **Subtitle:** Everything you have built to keep the home still depends on someone remembering to do it, and remembering is exactly what fails on a tired day. The fix is to stop relying on memory and hang the small acts of keeping on moments that already happen in the day, so a couple of short, shared upkeep times run on the rhythm of the day itself, and keeping the home stops being a decision and becomes simply part of how the day goes.
- **Masthead:** "Part Eight · Sustain" / "Chapter Twenty-Nine"
- **The rule (frozen):** Keeping fails when it depends on remembering. Give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory.
- **The three qualities (frozen):** What makes a daily time work: keep it short, a few minutes with a hard stop; anchor it to a moment that already happens, so no one has to remember; and do it together, so it is quick and shared rather than one person's chore.
- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A daily 6S time is a short, shared moment of keeping, anchored to something that already happens in the day, so the small upkeep of the home runs on the day's rhythm rather than on anyone's memory. A couple of them, a few minutes each, are enough to keep a home that is already good.
- **The memory-versus-rhythm reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The reason your good intentions keep failing is not that you are forgetful. It is that you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing. A keeping-habit tied to a fixed time in the day happens on the day you would have forgotten, which is the only day it ever really mattered.
- **The anchor reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail. You do not need to remember to reset the kitchen after dinner if the reset simply is part of after dinner, the way clearing the plates already is.
- **The daily-hum reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The audit catches drift and problem-solving removes what keeps returning, but the daily times are the quiet hum underneath both: the small, constant upkeep that means the home never drifts far enough to need catching or solving in the first place. This is the rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** anchor it to the day, not to your memory / short, anchored, together / a rhythm, not a schedule. Chapters 26 to 28 gave the mindset and the tools; Chapter 29 gives the daily rhythm that carries them.
