# Back Cover Copy: Daily 6S Times

## Back-cover text

You have the mindset, the friendly audit, and a shared way to solve what keeps coming back. Chapter 26 gave the mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, held good over the years by small, shared habits. Chapter 27 gave the friendly audit that catches drift while it is still small. Chapter 28 gave the shared way to solve the recurring problems, at the cause and once. Held together they are most of what keeping takes, but they share one quiet weakness that shows up on exactly the wrong days: every one of them still depends on somebody remembering to do it. And remembering, it turns out, is the least reliable tool in the whole box. It works on the calm, well-slept days a home barely needs keeping, and it fails, predictably, on the tired, overwhelmed days a home most wants to slide. Here is the fix that ends that, and it is the whole of this chapter: "Keeping fails when it depends on remembering. Give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory." You stop hanging keeping on your memory and start hanging it on the day.

Chapter 29 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the fourth chapter of Part Eight, and it hands the household the rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic. Its whole work is that one move, and it names the trouble with memory first, before offering the way out: "The reason your good intentions keep failing is not that you are forgetful. It is that you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing. A keeping-habit tied to a fixed time in the day happens on the day you would have forgotten, which is the only day it ever really mattered." The way out is to anchor: "The easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail. You do not need to remember to reset the kitchen after dinner if the reset simply is part of after dinner, the way clearing the plates already is." Your day is already full of things that happen without fail, the kettle boiling, the school run leaving, the plates being cleared, the lights going off, and you bolt a small keeping-act onto one of them, so the reliable old habit drags the small new one along behind it. The keeping is no longer a separate thing you might forget; it is a passenger on a trip the day was already taking. It fits the hero in a plain box: "A daily 6S time is a short, shared moment of keeping, anchored to something that already happens in the day, so the small upkeep of the home runs on the day's rhythm rather than on anyone's memory. A couple of them, a few minutes each, are enough to keep a home that is already good." Three qualities decide whether a daily time survives contact with real life: "What makes a daily time work: keep it short, a few minutes with a hard stop; anchor it to a moment that already happens, so no one has to remember; and do it together, so it is quick and shared rather than one person's chore." And these small times are quietly the engine of the whole of Part Eight: "The audit catches drift and problem-solving removes what keeps returning, but the daily times are the quiet hum underneath both: the small, constant upkeep that means the home never drifts far enough to need catching or solving in the first place. This is the rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic." The chapter walks one ordinary weekday with a couple of keeping-times folded in, a two-minute morning tidy bolted to leaving and a short after-dinner reset bolted to clearing the plates, and nobody has to remember either one, because the day remembers them: "That is a home keeping itself, and it looks like almost nothing, which is exactly how you know it will last." Start your own daily rhythm today with **The Daily Times card**, and map the moments that already happen in your day with **The Anchor Your Habits sheet**. Then look at the friction meter, which moves again, a little further along the dashed last-gap path: "The needle takes its third Sustain step, and it is nearly at the goal now. A home whose keeping runs on the rhythm of the day, rather than on anyone remembering, is a home that mostly keeps itself, which is very nearly the whole of what this book has been walking toward. Only a sliver of the gap remains, and the final chapter, the return to the start of the loop, closes it." The move is small in distance and large in meaning, and it comes to rest very close to the goal, though not at it. Next, Chapter 29 hands off to Chapter 30 · Your Next 6S Event, the periodic renewal that carries the household around the full six-S loop again, completes Sustain, and, on the friction meter, finally reaches the goal and closes the book.
