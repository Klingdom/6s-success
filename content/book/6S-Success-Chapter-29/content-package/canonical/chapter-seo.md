## Search intent
Keeping a good home good, after the reader has built it, taken on the keeping mindset from Chapter 26, started the friendly audit from Chapter 27, and learned to solve recurring problems from Chapter 28. Readers arriving here have a home that is cleared, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized, and three real ways to keep it, but they have bumped into the weakness all three share: every one still depends on somebody remembering to do it, and remembering fails on the tired, busy days a home most wants to slide. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: stop hanging keeping on your memory and hang it on the day, and keeping fails when it depends on remembering, so give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory. It names the trouble with remembering (your good intentions fail not because you are forgetful but because you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing); it delivers the hero, anchor it to the day (the easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail), with the definition (a daily 6S time is a short, shared moment of keeping, anchored to something that already happens in the day); it delivers the three qualities that make a daily time survive, short, anchored, and together, and names the daily times as the quiet hum underneath the audit and problem-solving, the rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic; and it walks a worked example of one ordinary day with a couple of keeping-times folded in. It reads the friction meter as a small THIRD Sustain step, not a milestone: a home whose upkeep rides on the rhythm of the day is a home that mostly keeps itself, which is very nearly the whole of what the goal represents. Five of six S's are done; the goal is reached only at Chapter 30.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Names the real reason good intentions fail: not forgetfulness or lack of discipline, but asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing, so a keeping-plan that runs on memory works only when you least need it and collapses precisely when you do
- Reframes the fix away from character (which you cannot change much) and toward method (which you can change completely), removing the shame most tidiness advice quietly loads onto the reader
- Makes the mechanism concrete: you do not invent a new slot to remember, you bolt a small keeping-act onto a moment that already happens without fail, so the reliable old habit drags the new one along behind it, and the keeping becomes a passenger on a trip the day was already taking
- Gives the three qualities that decide whether a daily time survives real life, short (length breeds avoidance), anchored (so it needs no memory), and together (faster, lighter, and shared), rather than an ambitious routine that lasts a week
- Insists the plan is genuinely modest, a couple of short moments hooked to things that were going to happen anyway, not a schedule, a rota, or a wall planner, because a home that is already good needs only a tiny, regular correction
- Distinguishes the daily times from the audit and the problem-solving: the daily hum runs every day in the background and stops most drift before it ever becomes something to catch or solve, making it the engine that finally makes keeping automatic
- Walks a full worked example of one ordinary day, a two-minute morning launch-pad tidy anchored to leaving and a short after-dinner reset anchored to clearing the plates, so the reader sees how little it disturbs a day
- Reads the friction meter honestly as a small third Sustain step, not a milestone: the needle comes very close to the goal because the daily rhythm is the piece that makes keeping automatic, leaving only a sliver for the final chapter to close
- Keeps the register warm, light, and freeing, a rhythm and never a rigid chore schedule, a habit and not willpower, with no scolding and no discipline-as-punishment, because forgetting is normal and the whole method exists precisely because memory is unreliable
