## Short summary (about 100 words)

You have the mindset, the friendly audit, and the shared way to solve recurring problems; but all three still depend on someone remembering to do them, and remembering is what fails on a tired day. This chapter removes the remembering. Instead of relying on your memory, you hang the small acts of keeping on fixed moments that already happen every day without fail, so the upkeep rides on the rhythm of the day itself. ## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 29 is the fourth chapter of Part Eight and is the piece that finally makes all the rest run on its own. You now have three real ways to keep a home: the mindset that a home is kept and not finished, the friendly audit that catches drift while it is small, and the shared way to solve the problems that keep coming back. Held together they are most of what keeping takes, but they share one big weakness: every one of them still depends on somebody remembering to do it. And remembering is the least reliable tool in the whole box; it works on the calm, well-slept days a home barely needs keeping, and it fails, predictably, on the tired, overwhelmed days a home most wants to slide.

The fix is to stop hanging keeping on your memory and start hanging it on the day. Your day is already full of things that happen without fail, the kettle boiling, the school run leaving, the plates being cleared, the lights going off. You bolt a small keeping-act onto one of these, so the reliable old habit drags the small new one along behind it, and the keeping becomes a passenger on a trip the day was already taking. A couple of such times, kept short, anchored, and together, are the quiet hum that finally makes keeping automatic. The needle takes its honest third Sustain step and comes to rest nearly at the goal, with only a sliver left for the final chapter to close.
