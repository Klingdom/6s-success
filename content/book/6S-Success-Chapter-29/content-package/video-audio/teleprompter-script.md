# Teleprompter Script: Daily 6S Times

**Chapter 29, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** daily-6s-times
**Length:** about 780 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the fourth chapter of Sustain.

You have the keeping mindset.

The friendly audit that catches drift while it is small.

And the shared way to solve recurring problems.

Held together, that is most of what keeping takes.

But they all share one weakness.

Every one of them depends on someone remembering to do it.

And remembering is the least reliable tool in the box.

It works on the calm, well-slept days.

The days a home barely needs keeping.

And it fails on the tired, overwhelmed days.

The days a home most wants to slide.

So this chapter removes the remembering.

You stop hanging keeping on your memory.

And start hanging it on the day.

First, be honest about why remembering fails.

When the home slides, most people blame themselves.

They decide they are disorganized. Undisciplined.

That is wrong, and it is expensive.

It aims the fix at your character.

Which you cannot change much.

Instead of your method, which you can change completely.

You have not been failing to keep your home.

You have been running a plan with one weak point.

Your memory.

And asking it to hold up on its most overloaded days.

The reason your good intentions keep failing.

Is not that you are forgetful.

It is that you are asking your memory.

To do a job the day should be doing.

The answer is not to remember harder.

It is to build a plan that needs no remembering.

So, how do you do that.

It rests on one small trick.

Your day is already full of things that happen without fail.

The kettle boils. The school run leaves.

The dishes get cleared. The lights go off.

Each is a moment that arrives on its own.

And each can serve as an anchor.

You take a small act of keeping.

And bolt it onto one of these moments.

So the reliable old habit drags the new one along.

You do not remember to wipe the counter.

Wiping the counter is now just the last thing.

That happens while the coffee brews.

The keeping is no longer a separate thing you might forget.

It is a passenger on a trip the day was already taking.

The easiest new habit is one you never have to start.

Because it is bolted to an old one that already runs.

And notice how modest this is.

Not a schedule. Not a rota. Not a wall planner.

Just a couple of short moments.

Hooked to things that were going to happen anyway.

A daily 6S time is short, and shared.

Anchored to something that already happens.

So the upkeep runs on the day, not on memory.

Now, three qualities decide if a time survives.

Short. Anchored. Together.

Drop any one, and the plan quietly dies.

Short, because length breeds avoidance.

A two-minute reset gets done on a bad night.

A twenty-minute one gets postponed forever.

Anchored, so it needs no memory.

And together, because a shared time is lighter.

Four people for three minutes is a full reset.

And it belongs to the whole household.

Not one weary pair of hands.

And these daily times are the most important practice.

The audit and the huddle happen occasionally.

The daily times run every single day.

So gently you barely notice them.

They stop most drift before it becomes a problem.

They are the quiet hum underneath everything.

The rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic.

Now see it in an ordinary day.

In the morning, before the door shuts.

Two minutes: everyone clears the launch pad.

And the breakfast surface. Then they go.

Then the day runs, untouched.

In the evening, while the plates are cleared.

A short shared reset of the common rooms.

Everyone to a zone for a few minutes.

Then it stops, and the evening is free.

Two small times. A handful of minutes.

Each riding a moment that happened anyway.

Nobody had to remember either one.

Because the day remembered them.

That is a home keeping itself.

And it looks like almost nothing.

Which is exactly how you know it will last.

Now go and look at the friction meter.

It moves again, and comes to rest almost at the goal.

The audit took the first Sustain step.

Solving problems took the second.

This chapter takes the third.

Because you added the thing that makes keeping automatic.

A home whose upkeep rides on the day.

Is a home that mostly keeps itself.

The step is small in distance.

And large in meaning.

Only a sliver of the gap remains.

So here is the rule that holds it all.

Keeping fails when it depends on remembering.

Give it a fixed time in the day instead.

Anchored to a moment that already happens.

And it runs on the rhythm of the day.

Rather than on your memory.

So here is your job. One of the smallest in the book.

Pick one thing that already happens without fail.

The kettle. The school run. The lights at night.

And bolt one two-minute keeping-act to it, today.

You will never have to remember it again.

Because the day will remember it for you.

Do not ask your memory to keep the home.

Hand the job to the day.
