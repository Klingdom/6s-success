# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the reason your tidy-ups keep failing is not that you are forgetful, it is that you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing, and the fix is to bolt a small keeping-act to a moment that already happens without fail) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. The whole thing is warm and freeing: forgetting is normal, this is a rhythm and not a chore schedule, and nobody needs more willpower. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small shift that finally made the daily tidy stick after years of it not sticking, and then I want to hear from you all.

We had the whole 6S thing going, a weekly check-in, all of it. But the everyday upkeep kept sliding, because every version of it depended on me remembering to do it. And on the good days I did not need it, and on the tired days I did not remember it.

Here is the idea that flipped it for me.

"The reason your good intentions keep failing is not that you are forgetful. It is that you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing. A keeping-habit tied to a fixed time in the day happens on the day you would have forgotten, which is the only day it ever really mattered."

That took all the guilt out of it. I had been treating it as a willpower problem, me not trying hard enough. It was never that.

"The easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail. You do not need to remember to reset the kitchen after dinner if the reset simply is part of after dinner, the way clearing the plates already is."

So I stopped trying to remember to reset the kitchen and just hung it on something that already happens every single night: clearing the plates. Now the reset simply is part of after dinner. Nobody decides to do it. The rule underneath it is simple.

"Keeping fails when it depends on remembering. Give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory."

Two minutes, everyone at once, then it stops and the evening is free. The day remembers it for us now.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is one thing that already happens in your day without fail, the kettle boiling, the school run leaving, the lights going off at night? And if you bolted one tiny two-minute tidy to it, so you never had to remember it again, what would you hang there?

I will go first in the comments. 😄
