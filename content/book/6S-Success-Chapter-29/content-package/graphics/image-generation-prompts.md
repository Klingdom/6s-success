# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 29

**"Daily 6S Times"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain*

Four prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across
all four: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the
warm accent, the day's-rhythm side, the Sustain-step tag), honey `#DDA63A` (the single accent, the bolted-on
keeping-acts, the keeping-blocks), slate blue `#3C5A6B` (data, cues, the day-timeline, milestones, the
catch), soft green `#6E8B5B` (the small approving marks, the last-gap path, the goal), a single warm red
`#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only, the no-backslide bar, used barely if at all on these calm
images. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the finished home the reader has cleared, arranged, cleaned, made safe, and
standardized. This chapter, the FOURTH of Part Eight, CONTINUES Sustain, the sixth and final S, and adds the
piece of keeping-machinery that finally makes all the rest run on its own. Chapter 26 gave the keeping
mindset, Chapter 27 the friendly audit, and Chapter 28 the shared way to solve the problems that keep coming
back; all three are real, but all three still depend on somebody remembering to do them, and remembering is
exactly what fails on the tired, busy days when a home most wants to slide. This chapter removes the
remembering. Its whole work is a reframe: keeping fails when it depends on memory, so you stop hanging keeping
on your memory and start hanging it on the day, bolting the small acts of upkeep onto fixed moments that
already happen every day without fail, so the upkeep rides along on the rhythm of the day whether or not
anyone is thinking about it. It draws the memory-versus-rhythm reframe (your good intentions fail not because
you are forgetful but because you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing) and delivers the
hero, anchor it to the day (the easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an
old one that already runs without fail; you do not need to remember to reset the kitchen after dinner if the
reset simply is part of after dinner). It delivers the three qualities that make a daily time survive (short,
a few minutes with a hard stop; anchored, so no one has to remember; together, so it is quick and shared) and
the daily-hum reframe (the daily times are the quiet hum underneath the audit and problem-solving), and walks
an ordinary day with just two short keeping-times folded in (a two-minute launch-pad tidy anchored to the
morning leaving, and a short after-dinner reset anchored to clearing the plates). Nothing of the first five
S's is re-taught, the 15-minute reset of Chapter 19 is referenced in one light line only, the individual
schedule of Chapter 24 is not re-taught, the mindset, audit, and problem-solving of Chapters 26 to 28 are
used but not re-explained, and the recurring 6S event of Chapter 30 is named only as still to come. Keep the
tone calm, warm, and freeing: forgetting is normal and expected, the whole method exists precisely because
memory is unreliable, and a skipped time means the time is wrong, too long or badly anchored, never that the
reader lacks discipline. Do not ask your memory to keep the home; hand the job to the day. Crucially,
describe only the calm, warm illustrated house style: no corporate or boardroom look, no force, control, or
discipline-as-punishment framing (no no-excuses, no stay-disciplined, no make-yourself, no hold-the-line), no
hype (no unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, optimize-your-day), no photoreal polish; the harried figure
carrying keeping on memory is weary and human, never incompetent, and the day's-rhythm side is calm,
ordinary, light, and quietly settled.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in
the scene, a hand-lettered card, a handwritten line on a real sticky note, a hand-drawn mark on a real
timeline, or the reading on a real gauge, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental
hand-lettered label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 29 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating hyphen or a
floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: daily-times, memory-versus-rhythm,
keeping-habit, keeping-act, keeping-time, keeping-block, day's-rhythm, habit-anchoring, launch-pad,
after-dinner, two-minute, three-minute, fifteen-minute, well-slept, three-things-at-once, last-gap,
three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, Shine-complete,
Safety-complete, Standardize-complete.

## 1. On memory, on the day's rhythm (the opener)

A warm, wide editorial illustration of the same small daily upkeep, drawn twice side by side in soft
daylight, to show that the identical keeping can be carried two completely different ways. On the left side,
labelled on memory, a single harried figure stands in the middle of a home papered with hand-lettered
sticky-note reminders reading reset kitchen, tidy hall, wipe counters, clutching a long handwritten to-do
list; several notes have fallen to the floor unread, the figure looks overwhelmed and behind, and the home
around them is quietly slipping because the reminders keep being missed. On the right side, labelled on the
day's rhythm, the same home is shown as a calm horizontal ribbon of a single day, from a small sunrise on the
left to a moon on the right, with just two small keeping-acts drawn as neat little tags clipped onto moments
that already happen along the ribbon: a two-minute tidy clipped to the morning leaving-time, and a short
reset clipped to the after-dinner clearing-up. The figure on this side is relaxed, because the day is
carrying the upkeep for them. The two sides are unmistakably the same small tasks, so the only difference the
eye reads is what carries the keeping, an exhausted memory or the reliable rhythm of the day. Warm paper
cream, ink-brown line work, cool slate for the on-memory chaos, warm terracotta and soft green for the
day's-rhythm side, one honey accent on the two bolted-on keeping-acts, warm-red held back. Editorial
storybook illustration. The same upkeep, two ways to carry it.

- Mood: one carried by a tired memory, the other carried by the day; on the left, sticky notes, a long list,
  and the quiet failure of trying to remember it all; on the right, a couple of small keeping-acts clipped to
  moments that already happen, carried by the day itself; the register is warm and human, the harried figure
  weary but never incompetent, the day's-rhythm scene calm, ordinary, and quietly settled.
- Avoid: a foolish, lazy, or shamed figure on the memory side, anyone being scolded, a corporate or boardroom
  look, floating typeset text overlays, a menacing or nagging tone, warm-red anywhere except the smallest
  marks, brand names or recognizable products; keep the two sides plainly the same small tasks, the left a
  harried figure lost in sticky notes and a long list, the right a calm horizontal day-ribbon from sunrise to
  moon with just two small keeping-acts clipped onto moments that already happen and the figure relaxed.

## 2. Anchor it to the day (the habit-anchoring mechanic)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-made infographic titled anchor it to the day, on a soft cream panel,
in soft daylight, showing the habit-anchoring mechanic as three neat pairings stacked top to bottom. Each
pairing shows an existing daily moment on the left, hand-drawn solid and settled as a reliable cue, joined by
a small hand-drawn bolt or link to a little keeping-act on the right, drawn as the passenger it drags along.
Pair one: coffee brews bolted to plus wipe the counter. Pair two: dinner cleared bolted to plus the
after-dinner reset. Pair three: lights off bolted to plus clear the launch pad. Across the foot, a single
hand-lettered line reads hang the new habit on one that already runs. The existing cues are hand-drawn in
calm slate, the bolted-on keeping-acts in a warm honey, and the link between them emphasised so the eye reads
the attaching. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, solid slate for the existing cues, one honey
accent held to the bolted-on keeping-acts, the link emphasised, soft green available for a small approving
mark, no warm-red on this calm image. A picture of one plain idea: you never invent a new slot to remember,
you clip a small keeping-act onto a moment that already happens, so the old reliable habit carries the new
one.

- Mood: the easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already
  runs without fail; you do not have to remember to wipe the counter, because it is now simply the last thing
  that happens while the coffee brews; the keeping is no longer a separate thing you might forget, it is a
  passenger on a trip the day was already taking; the register is calm, friendly, and reassuring.
- Avoid: a cold flow chart, a chore rota, a wall planner covered in coloured blocks, gridlines everywhere,
  neon color, a nagging tone, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names or named habit systems; keep
  the three pairs clean and equal in weight with the existing cues drawn solid and the keeping-acts small and
  light, the link between them the emphasised thing, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but not childish.

## 3. A day with its times (the worked day)

A warm, homely editorial illustration of a hand-made infographic titled a day with its times, on a soft cream
panel, in soft daylight, showing a single ordinary day drawn as a calm horizontal timeline from a small
sunrise on the far left to a moon on the far right, with the ordinary events of a day lightly indicated along
it. Marked on the timeline are just two short keeping-times, each hand-drawn as a small warm honey block with
a tiny clock showing only a few minutes: the first, near the morning end, hand-lettered morning, two-minute

clearing up. The vast majority of the timeline is left open and unmarked, so the eye reads at once that this
is two small moments and not a packed schedule. A hand-lettered line beneath reads two short times, riding

two honey keeping-blocks with tiny clocks, soft green available for a small tick, no warm-red anywhere. A
picture of one plain idea: a daily rhythm is only a couple of short moments folded into the natural shape of a

- Mood: how little a daily rhythm actually is, just a couple of short times folded into the natural shape of
  a day, most of which is left alone, so it reads as light and doable rather than as another full timetable;
  two short shared times, each riding on a moment that happened anyway, holding the whole home steady day
  after day; the register is calm, homely, and freeing.
- Avoid: a packed timetable, a wall planner full of coloured blocks, a busy grid, gridlines everywhere, neon
  color, a rigid chore-schedule look, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names; keep the timeline
  mostly empty with just two small keeping-blocks and a lot of open day, the sunrise and moon plain, the two
  blocks small with tiny clocks showing only a few minutes each, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but
  not childish.
