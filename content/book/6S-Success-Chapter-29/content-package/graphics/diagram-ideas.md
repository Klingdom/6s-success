Scope fence: this chapter is the FOURTH chapter of Part Eight, and it CONTINUES Sustain, the sixth and FINAL
of the six S's, with the piece of keeping-machinery that finally makes all the rest run on its own. Chapter
26 gave the keeping mindset, Chapter 27 the friendly audit, and Chapter 28 the shared way to solve the
problems that keep coming back; all three are real, but all three still depend on somebody remembering to do
them, and remembering is exactly what fails on the tired, busy days when a home most wants to slide. This
chapter removes the remembering. Its whole work is a reframe: keeping fails when it depends on memory, so you
stop hanging keeping on your memory and start hanging it on the day, bolting the small acts of upkeep onto
fixed moments that already happen every day without fail, so the upkeep rides along on the rhythm of the day
whether or not anyone is thinking about it. It draws the memory-versus-rhythm reframe (your good intentions
fail not because you are forgetful but because you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be
doing); it delivers the hero, anchor it to the day (the easiest new habit is one you never have to start,
because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail) with the anchor reframe and the definition
box (a daily 6S time is a short, shared moment of keeping, anchored to something that already happens); it
delivers the three qualities that make a daily time survive (short, anchored, together) and the daily-hum
reframe (the daily times are the quiet hum underneath the audit and problem-solving, the constant upkeep that
means the home never drifts far enough to need catching or solving); and it walks an ordinary day with just
two short keeping-times folded in (a two-minute launch-pad tidy anchored to the morning leaving, and a short
after-dinner reset anchored to clearing the plates). It USES the mindset Chapter 26 built, the audit Chapter
27 built, and the problem-solving Chapter 28 built without re-teaching any of them, and does NOT build the
recurring 6S event (Chapter 30), which is named only as still to come. CRITICAL fence: this is the DAILY
keeping-rhythm, a couple of short, shared times anchored to the day, NOT the friendly drift-catching audit of
Chapter 27, NOT the shared problem-solving of Chapter 28, NOT the 15-minute reset of Chapter 19 (referenced
in one light line as one example of a daily time, not re-taught), NOT the individual-standard schedule of
Chapter 24, and NOT the recurring, periodic renewal event of Chapter 30. Because a home whose keeping runs on
the rhythm of the day mostly keeps itself, the friction needle takes its THIRD Sustain step, a small MOVE
further along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from Chapter 28's position toward the goal, and
comes to rest very close to it; this is NOT a milestone, and honestly small in distance, because you have only
bolted a couple of tiny times to the day, but it is large in meaning, because those times are the engine that
lets the whole of keeping run without effort. Five of the six S's were complete going in; the goal is reached
only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example
`CHAPTER 29 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their
internal hyphen: daily-times, memory-versus-rhythm, keeping-machinery, keeping-habit, keeping-act,
keeping-time, keeping-block, day's-rhythm, habit-anchoring, launch-pad, after-dinner, two-minute,
three-minute, fifteen-minute, well-slept, three-things-at-once, single-point-of-failure, last-gap,
three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, Shine-complete,
Safety-complete, Standardize-complete. Anti-shame throughout: forgetting is normal and expected, and the
whole method exists precisely because memory is unreliable; the home does not slide because someone is lazy
or undisciplined, it slides because a keeping-plan built on memory has a single point of failure that fails
on exactly the days it is most overloaded; a skipped time means the time is wrong (too long or badly
anchored), never that the reader lacks discipline. No force, control, or discipline-as-punishment framing
anywhere (no no-excuses, no stay-disciplined, no make-yourself, no hold-the-line), no hype (no unlock,
leverage, elevate, supercharge, optimize-your-day), no corporate or boardroom register, no brand names or
named habit systems. The register is calm, warm, homely, and freeing, the relief of no longer having to
remember or nag: do not ask your memory to keep the home, hand the job to the day.

## 1. On Memory, On the Day's Rhythm (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole argument in one image. The identical small daily upkeep, drawn twice,
so the only variable the eye can read is what carries it: an exhausted memory, or the reliable rhythm of the
day. On the LEFT, on memory: a single harried figure in the middle of a home papered with sticky-note
reminders and a long to-do list, several notes fallen to the floor unread, the figure overwhelmed and
behind, the home quietly slipping because the reminders keep being missed. On the RIGHT, on the day's rhythm:
the same home drawn as a calm horizontal ribbon of a single day, from a sunrise on the left to a moon on the
right, with just two small keeping-acts clipped onto moments that already happen along the ribbon, and the
figure relaxed because the day is carrying the upkeep for them. Same tasks, two carriers. Mirrors Figure 1 in
the HTML.

**What it shows:** The same daily upkeep, drawn two ways. The LEFT scene, `ON MEMORY`, shows a single
harried figure surrounded by sticky notes reading `reset kitchen!`, `tidy hall!`, `wipe counters!`,
clutching a long to-do list, some notes fallen and forgotten on the floor, the figure trying and failing to
remember it all, the home around them quietly slipping. The RIGHT scene, `ON THE DAY'S RHYTHM`, shows the
same home as a calm horizontal day-ribbon from sunrise to moon, with two neat little keeping-tags clipped
onto moments that already happen: a two-minute tidy clipped to the morning leaving, and a short reset clipped
to the after-dinner clearing-up, each running by itself, the figure relaxed.

**Rough layout:**

  muted `#6A625A`: `the same upkeep, two ways to carry it`.
- LEFT scene eyebrow, cool slate sans caps: `ON MEMORY`. The harried figure amid the fallen sticky notes and
  the long list, an overwhelmed but sympathetic expression, the home slipping behind them, with a Newsreader
  italic line beneath in slate: `sticky notes, a long list, and the quiet failure of trying to remember it
  all`.
- RIGHT scene eyebrow, warm terracotta sans caps: `ON THE DAY'S RHYTHM`. The calm day-ribbon from sunrise to
  moon with two small keeping-acts clipped to moments that already happen, the figure at ease, with a
  Newsreader italic line beneath in green: `a couple of small keeping-acts clipped to moments that already
  happen, carried by the day itself`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "The same upkeep, two ways to carry it. On the left, on memory: sticky
  notes, a long list, and the quiet failure of trying to remember it all. On the right, on the day's rhythm:

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is that the identical upkeep can be carried two completely different
ways, so draw the two scenes unmistakably the same small tasks, only the carrier different, an exhausted

foolish: they are a sympathetic human trying to remember too much, not a failure; the sticky-note memory is
the villain, not the person. Keep the day's-rhythm scene calm and quietly settled, the day-ribbon plain and
horizontal, the two keeping-acts small and ordinary, clipped lightly onto moments that already happen. Hold
the color discipline: cool slate for the on-memory chaos, warm terracotta and green for the day's-rhythm
side, one honey accent held to the bolted-on keeping-acts; spark-red barely appears if at all. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, the register of a before-and-after that turns entirely on whether the upkeep hangs
on someone's memory or rides the rhythm of the day.

## 2. Anchor It to the Day (the habit-anchoring mechanic, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's hero as one picture: you do not invent a new slot to remember, you clip a
small keeping-act onto a moment that already happens without fail, so the old reliable habit carries the new
one. The value is in the three neat pairs and the single line that names the mechanism. Mirrors Figure 2 in
the HTML.

**What it shows:** Three neat pairings, read top to bottom. Each pairing shows an existing daily moment on
the left, drawn solid and settled as a reliable cue, joined by a small link or bolt to a little keeping-act
on the right, drawn as the passenger it drags along. Pair one: `Coffee brews` bolted to `+ wipe the
counter`. Pair two: `Dinner cleared` bolted to `+ the after-dinner reset`. Pair three: `Lights off` bolted
to `+ clear the launch pad`. Across the foot, a single line reads `hang the new habit on one that already
runs`. The existing cues are drawn in calm slate, the bolted-on keeping-acts in a warm accent, and the link
between them emphasised so the eye reads the attaching.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`, warm and homely. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft:
  `ANCHOR IT TO THE DAY`.
- Three pairs stacked, each an existing cue in a solid slate `#3C5A6B` block on the left, a small emphasised
  bolt or link, and a warm honey `#DDA63A` keeping-act tag on the right:
  - `Coffee brews` + `wipe the counter`
  - `Dinner cleared` + `the after-dinner reset`
  - `Lights off` + `clear the launch pad`
- The link between each pair drawn as a small bolt or clip, emphasised so the eye reads the new act being
  attached to the old cue, the reliable old habit dragging the small new one along behind it.
- A line across the foot in Fraunces slate: `hang the new habit on one that already runs`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "Anchoring, in three pairs. On the left, moments that already
  happen every day without anyone deciding; on the right, a small keeping-act bolted to each, carried along
  for free. You never have to remember to wipe the counter, because it is now just the last thing that
  happens while the coffee brews."

**Note for the illustrator:** the message is that you never have to start a habit bolted to one that already
runs, so draw the existing cues solid and settled and the keeping-acts small and light, the link between them
the emphasised thing, so the eye reads the attaching. Keep the three pairs clean and equal in weight, plain
and readable, never a flow chart or a chore rota. Hold the color discipline: solid slate for the existing
cues, one honey accent held to the bolted-on keeping-acts, the link emphasised, green available for a small
approving mark; spark-red does not appear. Warm line-and-flat-color style, the register of a small, almost
sneaky trick that makes a new habit need no remembering at all.

## 3. A Day With Its Times (the worked day, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's worked example as one picture: a whole ordinary day with just two short
keeping-times folded in, most of the day left completely open, so the reader sees the rhythm as light and
doable rather than as another full timetable. The value is in how little of the day is marked. Mirrors Figure
3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A single ordinary day drawn as a calm horizontal timeline, from a small sunrise on the far
left to a moon on the far right, with the ordinary events of a day lightly indicated along it. Marked on the
timeline are just two short keeping-times, each drawn as a small warm honey block with a tiny clock showing
only a few minutes: the first, near the morning end, `morning: 2-min tidy, anchored to leaving`; the second,
in the evening, `after dinner: short reset, anchored to clearing up`. The vast majority of the timeline is
left open and unmarked, to make the point visually that this is two small moments and not a packed schedule.
A line beneath reads `two short times, riding the day`.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`, warm and homely. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft: `A
  DAY WITH ITS TIMES`.
- A single slate `#3C5A6B` horizontal timeline from a small sunrise on the left to a moon on the right, the
  ordinary events of a day lightly indicated, most of the line left open and unmarked.
- Two short honey `#DDA63A` keeping-blocks marked on the line, each with a tiny clock showing only a few
  minutes:
  - near the morning end: `morning: 2-min tidy, anchored to leaving`
  - in the evening: `after dinner: short reset, anchored to clearing up`
- A line beneath in Fraunces slate: `two short times, riding the day`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "A whole day of keeping, folded in. Two short shared times, one

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is how little a daily rhythm actually is, so keep the timeline

few minutes each, both clearly clipped to moments that were going to happen anyway. Hold the color
discipline: slate for the timeline, two honey keeping-blocks, green available for a small tick; spark-red
does not appear. This is not a schedule: it is a calm ordinary day with a couple of tiny moments folded in,
drawn so the reader feels how little it disturbs the day. Warm line-and-flat-color style, friendly but not
childish, the register of a home quietly keeping itself.
