# Infographic Spec: Daily 6S Times

**Chapter 29, "Daily 6S Times"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: keeping fails
when it depends on remembering, so you stop hanging keeping on your memory and start hanging it on the day,
bolting a couple of small upkeep-acts onto fixed moments that already happen, so the home runs on the rhythm
of the day rather than on anyone's memory. The concept pairs the hero rule, **Keeping fails when it depends
on remembering. Give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it
runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory.**, with the picture at its heart: **the same
upkeep, two ways to carry it**, the same small daily keeping drawn twice, on the left a harried figure lost
in sticky-note reminders and a long to-do list, trying and failing to remember it all, on the right the same
home drawn as a calm horizontal day-ribbon from sunrise to moon with just two small keeping-acts clipped onto
moments that already happen, the figure relaxed. So the viewer sees the whole move at once, that keeping hung
on a tired memory is a losing battle of sticky notes and guilt, and the same keeping hung on the fixed
moments of the day simply runs.

It stays consistent with the four inline figures in `chapter_29_final.html`. The two sides are unmistakably
the same small tasks; cool slate carries the on-memory chaos, warm terracotta and soft green carry the
day's-rhythm side, and one honey accent is held to the two bolted-on keeping-acts so the eye reads the two
carriers as the same upkeep; the single small warm red is held back for the smallest marks only, because this
is a gift of a rhythm, not a hazard. "Discipline" is not the point, and neither is "memory"; a home whose
keeping runs on the day's rhythm is lighter and freer, a couple of small acts folded into an ordinary day
rather than a chore schedule to consult and remember. Anti-shame throughout: forgetting is normal and
expected, the whole method exists precisely because memory is unreliable, and a skipped time means the time
is wrong, too long or badly anchored, never that the reader lacks discipline. The register is warm and
freeing, never corporate, never nagging; do not ask your memory to keep the home, hand the job to the day.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: keeping fails when it
  depends on remembering, so give it a fixed time anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on
  the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory. The eye should read the rule, then see the same upkeep,
  two ways to carry it make it real, the left side a harried figure carrying keeping on memory, the right side
  the same keeping riding the fixed moments of the day.
- The same upkeep, two ways to carry it is the hero, so make the two sides unmistakably the same small tasks:
  the same daily keeping, only the carrier different, an exhausted memory on the left and the reliable day on
  the right. Keep the harried figure weary but never incompetent or foolish, a sympathetic human trying to
  remember too much, the sticky-note memory the villain and not the person, and the day's-rhythm side calm and
  quietly settled, a plain horizontal day-ribbon with two small keeping-acts clipped on. Set the rule, the
  definition, and the three qualities byte-for-byte as they read in the chapter; they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: cool slate carries the on-memory chaos, warm terracotta and green carry the
  day's-rhythm side, one honey accent is held to the two bolted-on keeping-acts so the two carriers read as
  one upkeep; the single small warm red is reserved for the smallest marks and is barely used, because this
  image is a gift of a rhythm and not a live hazard.
- This image makes the concept, the reframe that opens daily 6S times: keeping fails on memory and runs on the
  day's rhythm. Do NOT re-teach the first five S's, do NOT re-teach the 15-minute reset of Chapter 19 (named
  in one light line only as one example of a daily time), do NOT re-teach the individual schedule of Chapter
  24, do NOT re-teach the mindset, audit, or problem-solving of Chapters 26 to 28 (used but not re-explained),
  and do NOT build the recurring 6S event (Chapter 30), which is named only as still to come. And name no
  brand and no habit system and draw no specific product; the coffee, the dinner, the lights, the launch pad,
  and the keeping-acts are plain and generic, teaching the idea, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: forgetting is normal and expected, the whole method exists precisely because memory is
  unreliable, and a skipped time means the time is wrong, too long or badly anchored, never that the reader
  lacks discipline. What you are building is not a stricter routine to remember, it is a couple of small acts
  bolted to moments that already happen. Keep the register warm and freeing, a rhythm folded into an ordinary
  day and not a timetable to consult; no force, control, or discipline-as-punishment language (no no-excuses,
  no stay-disciplined, no make-yourself, no hold-the-line), no scolding presented as virtue, no hype. Use
  memory ONLY as the unreliable carrier the day replaces.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the two sides drawn by hand as
  the same small home scene rather than clinical thumbnails, the harried figure weary but human and the
  day's-rhythm home calm and settled, the two bolted-on keeping-acts a small honey accent, the whole image the
  calm, warm, freeing register of no longer having to remember or nag once the upkeep rides the rhythm of the
  day.
