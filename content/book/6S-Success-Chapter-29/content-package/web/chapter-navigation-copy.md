## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 29, the fourth chapter of Part Eight, Sustain, which continues the sixth and final S. Chapter 26 gave the keeping mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, on small shared habits, until keeping becomes second nature. Chapter 27 gave the friendly check-in that catches drift while it is small. Chapter 28 gave the shared way to solve the recurring problems the check-in keeps finding, at the cause, once. This chapter adds the piece that finally makes all the rest run on its own, because they share one quiet weakness: every one of them still depends on somebody remembering to do it, and remembering is the least reliable tool in the whole box, working beautifully on the calm days a home barely needs keeping and failing, predictably, on the tired days a home most wants to slide. This chapter removes the remembering. The reason your good intentions keep failing is not that you are forgetful; it is that you are asking your memory to do a job the day should be doing. So you stop hanging keeping on your memory and start hanging it on the day: your day is already full of things that happen reliably without anyone deciding, the kettle boiling, the school run leaving, the plates being cleared, the lights going off, and you bolt a small act of keeping onto one of these moments, so the reliable old habit drags the small new one along behind it. The easiest new habit is one you never have to start, because it is bolted to an old one that already runs without fail; the keeping is no longer a separate thing you might forget, it is a passenger on a trip the day was already taking. Three qualities decide whether a daily time survives real life, short, anchored, and together: short because length breeds avoidance, anchored so it needs no memory, together because a shared time is faster and lighter and belongs to the whole household rather than one weary pair of hands. And the daily times are quietly the most important of the three keeping-practices, the quiet hum underneath the audit and the problem-solving, the small, constant upkeep that means the home never drifts far enough to need catching or solving in the first place, the rhythm that finally makes keeping automatic. The chapter walks a whole ordinary weekday with a couple of keeping-times folded in, a two-minute morning launch-pad tidy anchored to the leaving and a short after-dinner reset anchored to clearing the plates, until the home holds itself steady day after day and nobody had to remember either one, because the day remembered them. The friction meter takes its third Sustain step. The needle moves a little further along the dashed last-gap path that appeared in Chapter 26, because a home whose daily upkeep rides on the rhythm of the day is a home that mostly keeps itself, with almost no attention at all, which is very nearly the whole of what the goal has always represented. The step is small in distance and large in meaning, so this is a small move and not a milestone; only a sliver of the gap remains, and the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. The rule to carry: keeping fails when it depends on remembering, so give it a fixed time in the day instead, anchored to a moment that already happens, and it runs on the rhythm of the day rather than on your memory.
