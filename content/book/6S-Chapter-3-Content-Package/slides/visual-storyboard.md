# Visual Storyboard
## The Six Steps That Transform Any Space
### Chapter 3, 6S Success: Home Edition

This storyboard ties the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system. It describes the recommended image or diagram for each key beat, the palette cues, and how the green calm-dot and red friction-spark motif recurs from the messy open to the calm close.

---

## The design system in one place

**Palette**
- Warm paper #F7F2E9, the default background for every slide
- Panel #FBF7EF, for cards, callouts, and figure frames
- Ink #2B2622, body and headline text
- Terracotta #BC4B2A, primary accent, eyebrows and section kicks
- Honey #DDA63A, secondary accent, notes, timers, "you are here" markers, art notes (the diamond glyph)
- Slate #3C5A6B, reserved for data and for Safety, the same role it played in Chapter 2's growth diagram
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B, the "this is resolved, this is at rest" signal
- Red friction-spark #CB4B36, the "this is a snag, this needs attention" signal

**Type**
- Fraunces for display, titles, loop node labels, pull quotes
- Newsreader for body and captions
- Inter-style sans for small labels, eyebrows, and checklist headers

**Line and figure style**
- Warm line-and-flat-color illustration, roughly 3px stroke, rounded corners, carried over from Chapters 1 and 2
- Figures sit in a panel-colored frame with a thin rule and a soft single-line shadow

**The two-color storytelling rule**
The whole chapter is told in two dots. Red friction-spark marks every unresolved snag at the start. Green calm-dot marks every snag that has been handed off and resolved. The story is watching red become green, never by scrubbing it away, but by walking the loop. Caption to carry the idea: "The sparks did not get cleaned away. They got handed, step to step, until there was nothing left to spark about."

## Beat 1: The feral cabinet (opener)

**Image** Full-bleed warm photo, or the line-art version, of the under-sink cabinet, doors open, seen straight on. Half-used spray bottles, a crusted sponge, three brands of trash bags, a forgotten bucket, a faint dark stain at the back. Honest clutter, not staged chaos. Warm light.

**Palette cues** Paper and ink dominate. Title set in Fraunces in the lower third. Terracotta eyebrow reading "Part One, Discovering 6S."

**Motif** This is the all-red frame. Place red friction-sparks on every snag: the leak at the back corner, the drain cleaner down low, the rag tangle, the crusty sponge. No green anywhere yet. Establish the spark vocabulary before any of it resolves.

**Used on** Teaching slides 1 and 2, carousel slides 1 and 2.

---

## Beat 2: The 6S Loop (hero motif)

**Diagram** Six nodes in a ring, connected clockwise by handoff arrows: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Each arrow carries a short verb. The arrow from Sustain returns to Sort, closing the loop. Center label: "Walk it once. Then it goes around again."

**Handoff verbs on the arrows** Sort makes room, Straighten gives it a home, Shine reveals the truth, Safety protects the people, Standardize makes the good way the easy way, Sustain keeps it alive.

**Palette cues** Node rings and labels in ink on paper. Five nodes use terracotta and honey accents. Safety is rendered in slate, deliberately set apart, the same slate used for data, so the eye reads it as the structural, protective step at the middle of the loop. Arrows in honey so the handoffs, not the nodes, carry the visual weight.

**Motif** This is the map, so it stays neutral, no sparks or calm-dots on the loop itself. Build it so a single node can be highlighted later, since the Part openers reuse this loop and light up one node at a time.

**Used on** Teaching slides 4 and 5 (slide 5 zooms one arrow and lights it honey, with a small parcel icon riding it to show a handoff being passed), carousel slide 3.

---

## Beat 3: Sort and Straighten (two-panel)

**Diagram** Two small panels side by side.
- Panel 1, Sort: everything pulled out onto a towel in daylight, grouped into keep, belongs-elsewhere, and gone.
- Panel 2, Straighten: the cabinet with a caddy up front, frequency zones labeled (daily at the front, monthly toward the back, rare up or out), and a deliberately clear back corner.

**Palette cues** Panel background, warm line art. Zone labels in slate, since they are data. The caddy and kept items in honey and terracotta.

**Motif** The handoff is the story between the two panels. In Panel 1 the discard pile still carries red friction-sparks. In Panel 2 the kept items begin to earn green calm-dots, the first red-to-green movement of the chapter. Leave one red spark on the clear back corner of Panel 2, because that is where Shine is about to find the leak. The spark is a promise the next beat pays off.

**Used on** Teaching slides 6 and 7, carousel slide 4.

## Beat 4: The Shine leak close-up

**Image** Tight close-up of a hand with a cloth wiping the floor of the now-empty cabinet. Where the cloth has passed, a dark water ring and a soft, swollen patch of board are revealed.

**Palette cues** Muted paper and ink for the clean cabinet, with the stain rendered in a darker, desaturated tone so it reads as damage. One red friction-spark sits on the stain. A honey pin note beside it: "Note the leak. Repair before it rots through."

**Motif** This is the single most important beat, so it isolates one red spark against an otherwise clean, calm surface. The contrast makes the point that cleaning is inspection: the space is nearly resolved, yet the slow wipe surfaces the one thing that was hiding. Caption to carry it: "The leak was there the whole time. Clutter hid it, speed would have missed it, and a slow wipe found it for free."

**Used on** Teaching slides 8 and 9, carousel slides 5 and 6.

---

## Beat 5: The Safety swap

**Diagram** A before and after pair.
- Left: a drain-cleaner bottle low on the cabinet floor at the front, a red friction-spark, and a small reaching child's hand.
- Right: the same bottle moved to a high shelf with a green calm-dot, and a simple child latch on the cabinet door.

**Palette cues** Slate is the lead accent here, on the word Safety and on the latch, tying this beat to the loop's slate Safety node. The reaching hand is drawn small and low to make the child's-height read land.

**Motif** The cleanest single red-to-green swap in the chapter. One red spark on the left becomes one green calm-dot on the right, with nothing else changed except the bottle's height. It visualizes the line "a moved bottle is a real safety improvement." Carry the crouch idea in the caption: "You are looking at the cabinet from roughly the height of a two-year-old."

**Used on** Teaching slide 10, carousel slide 7.

---

## Beat 6: Standardize and Sustain

**Diagram** The open cabinet door with a small printed photo and label taped inside, matched to the real caddy below it. A tiny circular "ten-second reset" arrow loops over the caddy. A longer dashed arrow curves away from the scene back toward the Sort node, showing Sustain handing back to Sort.

**Palette cues** Photo label framed in panel color with a slate caption. Reset arrow in honey. Green calm-dots on the caddy and on the matched items, showing the standard holding.

**Motif** This beat is mostly green now. The photo-on-the-door is "make normal visible, so abnormal becomes obvious," shrunk to one cabinet door. The dashed return arrow is the loop closing: Sustain leads back to Sort, not to a tidy ending.

**Used on** Teaching slides 11 and 12, carousel slide 8.

---

## Beat 7: The before/after split

**Diagram** The same cabinet, twice.
- Left: the original, covered in red friction-sparks, the leak, the low chemical, the tangle, the crusty sponge.
- Right: after the full loop, sorted and nearly bare, a caddy at the front, the leak dried and marked for repair, the chemical moved to a high shelf, a small photo label inside the door, every snag now a green calm-dot.

**Palette cues** Identical framing, lighting, and composition on both sides so the only change the eye reads is red becoming green. Keep the cabinet, the caddy, and the moved bottle consistent with every earlier figure.

**Motif** This is the payoff frame, the mirror of the all-red opener. Every red spark from Beat 1 now has a matching green calm-dot. Caption: "Same cabinet, one full lap of the loop. The sparks did not get cleaned away. They got handed, step to step, until there was nothing left to spark about."

**Used on** Teaching slide 13, carousel slide 9.

---

## Beat 8: The friction meter (close)

**Diagram** The recurring friction meter, a simple gauge with a needle, taking its first small step from rest toward the calm (green) side, just off where it sat at the end of Chapter 2. A fifteen-minute timer icon in honey sits beside the one-small-space checklist.

**Palette cues** Green at the calm end of the dial, red friction at the far end, slate for the gauge frame and tick marks. The needle barely moves, by design.

**Motif** The honest, modest move. This is where the chapter's two-color story resolves into a single instrument: the green calm-dot vocabulary becomes the green end of the dial. Caption: "One small cabinet, one small move. The bigger moves start when you choose a real target in Chapter 4."

**Used on** Teaching slide 14, carousel slide 10.

## How the red-to-green motif tracks across the deck

| Beat | Friction-sparks (red) | Calm-dots (green) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feral cabinet | All snags | None |
| 6S Loop | Neutral map, none | None |
| Sort and Straighten | Discard pile, and the back corner | First kept items |
| Shine leak | One, isolated on the stain | Surrounding surface calm |
| Safety swap | One, bottle low | One, bottle high |
| Standardize and Sustain | None | Most of the scene |
| Before/after split | Left side only | Right side, every snag matched |
| Friction meter | Far end of the dial | Needle moves a notch toward it |

The arc is deliberate. Red is never erased, it is handed forward and resolved, one step to the next, until the close shows a meter that has earned a small, honest move toward calm.
