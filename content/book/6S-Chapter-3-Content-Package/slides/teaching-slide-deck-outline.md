# Teaching Slide Deck Outline
## The Six Steps That Transform Any Space
### Chapter 3, 6S Success: Home Edition

A 14-slide teaching deck that walks the whole 6S loop once, through the cabinet under a kitchen sink. Demonstrate the method, do not just list it. Warm voice, concrete over motivational.

Design system: warm paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B (data and Safety), green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36. Fraunces for display, Newsreader for body. Hero motif: the 6S Loop.

### Slide 2: The Cabinet That Went Feral
- It did not become a disaster on purpose. One item at a time, each one reasonable, none of them ever leaving
- It is not dirty, and it is not even that full
- The problem is that nothing in there has a job, a home, or a reason

[VISUAL] Hand-drawn line art of the same cabinet, before. Red friction-sparks on the snag points: the leak at the back, the chemical bottle down low, the tangle of rags, the crusty sponge.

---

### Slide 3: A Lap Before the Real Training
- You already know what 6S is. Now we use it
- Before we slow down on each step, we walk all six at once, in order, on one small space
- Think of it as a slow lap around the track, so your hands learn where the corners are

[VISUAL] A simple racetrack oval drawn in slate, with six soft markers spaced around it. Honey "you are here" dot at the start line.

---

### Slide 4: Six Steps, One Loop
- The six steps are not separate chores that start with the same letter. They are a chain
- Each step sets up the next. Do them in order and the work compounds
- Sort makes room. Straighten gives things a home. Shine reveals the truth. Safety protects the people. Standardize makes the good way the easy way. Sustain keeps it alive

[VISUAL] The hero infographic, the 6S Loop. Six nodes in a ring connected by handoff arrows, clockwise: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety (in slate), Standardize, Sustain. Short verb on each arrow. Sustain loops back to Sort. Center label: "Walk it once. Then it goes around again."

---

### Slide 5: The Real Secret Is the Handoffs
- The method is not really about the six words, which you already know
- It is about the quiet moment one step passes the next something it could not have done alone
- Sort passes Straighten an empty space. Shine hands Safety a clear view of the danger

[VISUAL] Zoom-in on two adjacent loop nodes with the arrow between them lit in honey. A small parcel icon riding the arrow to suggest a handoff being passed.

---

### Slide 6: Sort, Make Room
- Take everything out onto a towel, in daylight, where every item has to answer one question: do I actually use this?
- Three piles only: keep, relocate, release. You are not organizing yet, you are deciding
- The handoff: Sort just gave Straighten an empty cabinet to work in

[VISUAL] Left panel of the two-panel illustration: the cabinet emptied onto a towel, items grouped into keep, belongs-elsewhere, and gone. Friction-sparks still on the discard pile.

---

### Slide 7: Straighten, Give It a Home
- Give each survivor a home by frequency of use. Daily things to the front where your hand lands, rare things back or up
- Tidy means everything looks lined up. Easy means the thing you need most is the thing you reach first
- Buy the caddy now, not before, because now you know what it has to hold. Leave the back corner clear

[VISUAL] Right panel of the two-panel illustration: the cabinet with a caddy up front, frequency zones labeled, and a deliberately clear back corner where the next step will find something.

---

### Slide 8: Shine, Clean Until It Talks
- Wipe the nearly empty cabinet slowly. Shine is not about clean, it is about inspection
- Cleaning slowly, with your hands already there, is when a space tells you the truth it was hiding
- The handoff: a clean, fully visible space, so Safety can finally see every danger

[VISUAL] Close-up of a hand and cloth wiping the cabinet floor. A dark water ring and a soft, swollen patch of board appear where the cloth has passed. One small red friction-spark on the stain.

---

### Slide 9: The Leak Was There the Whole Time
- As the cloth reaches the back corner, the wood is darker, a little soft, and it gives when you press it
- A slow drip the clutter had hidden for months. You found it by cleaning, not by hunting for leaks
- A plumber would have charged you to discover this. Your sponge found it for free

[VISUAL] Tight macro of the warped board and water ring, friction-spark pulsing on the stain. A small honey note pinned beside it: "Note the leak. Repair before it rots through."

---

### Slide 10: Safety, Now Look Low
- Stay crouched. You are looking at the cabinet from roughly the height of a two-year-old
- The drain cleaner is not in storage down here. It is on display, at grabbing height, behind a door that does not lock
- Move anything that can burn, poison, or cut up high, behind a dollar latch. Safety rides on the back of the first three steps, which is why it sits in the middle

[VISUAL] The Safety swap. Left: drain-cleaner bottle low, red friction-spark, a small reaching hand. Right: the bottle moved to a high shelf with a green calm-dot, and a child latch on the door.

---

### Slide 11: Standardize, Make the Good Way the Easy Way
- The cabinet will not stay nice on its own, and willpower is not the answer
- Make the good arrangement so obvious that doing it right is easier than doing it wrong
- Take a photo of the finished cabinet and tape it inside the door. The standard is now a picture anyone can match, not a memory

[VISUAL] The open cabinet door with a small printed photo and label taped inside. A green calm-dot beside the caddy showing the match between photo and reality.

---

### Slide 12: Sustain, Keep It Alive
- Sustain is tiny, not a weekly overhaul. A ten-second reset attached to something you already do
- Add one slow-rhythm check: once a month, glance at the back corner where the leak was. Dry? Good
- Sustain does not lead to a tidy ending. It leads back to Sort, because a home is alive and its contents keep changing

[VISUAL] A small ten-second reset arrow looping over the caddy, then a longer dashed arrow curving back to the Sort node of the loop. Green calm-dots along the path.

---

### Slide 13: How the Steps Hand Off
- The magic was never in any single step. It was in the chain
- Sort cleared the cabinet for Straighten. Straighten opened surfaces for Shine. Shine revealed the leak and the view for Safety. Safety made it sound. Standardize made it the default. Sustain kept it alive
- Fix a space that will not stay fixed by finding the step you skipped. A drawer that will not stay neat was usually never truly sorted

[VISUAL] The before/after split. Left: the original cabinet covered in red friction-sparks. Right: the same cabinet sorted, caddy in front, leak dried and marked, chemical up high, photo on the door, green calm-dots throughout.

---

### Slide 14: Your First Lap (soft CTA close)
- Stop watching. Take one lap today on a space small enough that you cannot lose
- Not the garage. One drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink. Fifteen minutes, all six steps
- One full, imperfect lap teaches your hands the whole method. The goal is not perfect, it is finished. Read the chapter free in the online book
