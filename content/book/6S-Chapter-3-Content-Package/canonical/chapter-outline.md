# Chapter 3 Outline: The Six Steps That Transform Any Space

A section-by-section map of the chapter as written in the final HTML and manuscript. The whole chapter rides one space: the cabinet under a kitchen sink.

## Opener: The under-sink cabinet

## 1. Chapter Opening: The Cabinet That Went Feral
The under-sink cabinet did not become a disaster on purpose. It happened one reasonable item at a time. It is not dirty or even that full. The problem is that nothing in there has a job, a home, or a reason. The cabinet stopped being storage and became a place where things land. We are going to fix it together, walking all six steps in order, before slowing down to master each one. A lap around the track before the real training.

## 2. What You Will Learn (objectives)
- Name the six steps in order, and explain why the order matters
- Watch each step hand the next one something it could not do alone
- Recognize the most common place each step goes wrong
- See why 6S is a loop, not a to-do list
- Walk one small space of your own through all six steps in fifteen minutes

## 3. One Loop, Walked Once
New people treat the six S's like a menu and skip the ones that sound like homework. It never works, because the steps are a chain, not six separate chores. You cannot Straighten before you Sort, cannot Shine around a pile, will miss hazards hidden by clutter. The real subject of the chapter is the handoffs between steps. Do them in order and the work compounds; the space almost finishes itself.
- Hero infographic: "The 6S Loop" (six nodes, handoff verbs, Safety in slate, Sustain loops to Sort)
- Definition box (The loop in one line): Sort makes room. Straighten gives things a home. Shine reveals the truth. Safety protects the people. Standardize makes the good way the easy way. Sustain keeps it alive. Then it goes around again.

## 4. Sort: Make Room
Take everything out onto a towel where you can see it in daylight. Alone, each crusty bottle must answer one question: do I actually use this? Sort into three piles only: keep, relocate, release. You are not organizing yet, you are deciding. Sort makes every later step smaller, and hands Straighten an empty cabinet.
- Callout (Common Mistake): Buying bins before you sort. The container aisle is step two's reward, not step one's shortcut.

## 5. Straighten: Give It a Home
Give each survivor a home by frequency of use. Daily things go to the front where your hand lands; rare things move back or up. Tidy means lined up; easy means the thing you need most is the thing you reach first. A caddy earns its place now, because now you know what it must hold. Leave the back corner clear, because that is where the next step finds something. The handoff: clear surfaces and a clear view for Shine.
- Illustration: Sort and Straighten two-panel (emptied onto a towel, then caddy plus frequency zones)

## 6. Shine: Clean Until It Talks
Wipe the nearly empty cabinet slowly. Shine is not about clean, it is about inspection. As the cloth reaches the clear back corner, the wood is darker and soft: a slow drip the clutter had hidden for months. You found it not by looking for leaks but by cleaning slowly with your hands already there. The loose hinge, the rust, the crack: a space hands all of this to whoever cleans it carefully. The handoff: a clean, fully visible space so Safety can see every danger.
- Illustration: Shine close-up (cloth revealing the water stain and warped board)
- Callout (Common Mistake): Cleaning fast to get it over with. Clean slowly enough to notice.

## 7. Safety: Now Look Low
Stay crouched, at a two-year-old's eye level, and see the drain cleaner sitting on the cabinet floor at grabbing height. You could not have seen it clearly until now; Safety rides on the back of the first three steps, which is why it sits in the middle of the loop. Move anything that can burn, poison, or cut up high, behind a dollar latch. Put the leak on a short repair list. At home, the inspector is you.
- Illustration: Safety swap (chemical low with spark, then high with calm-dot and a door latch)
- Callout (Quick Win): Open one low cabinet, crouch to a child's height, move one dangerous bottle up. A real Safety step in under a minute.

## 8. Standardize: Make the Good Way the Easy Way
The cabinet will not stay nice on its own, and willpower is not the answer. Standardize makes the good arrangement so obvious that doing it right is easier than doing it wrong. Take a photo of the finished cabinet and tape it inside the door. The standard is no longer a memory, it is a picture anyone can match. This is the visual workplace idea from Chapter 2, shrunk to one cabinet door. The handoff: a stable default for Sustain to protect.

## 9. Sustain: Keep It Alive
Sustain keeps the gain, and it is tiny, not a big recurring effort. A ten-second reset attached to something you already do, plus a monthly glance at the back corner where the leak was. Sustain does not lead to a tidy ending, it leads back to Sort, because a home is alive and its contents keep changing. That is why 6S is a loop.
- Callout (Family Challenge): Everyone walks one small space through all six steps this week, then reports the single thing Shine revealed. Best discovery wins.

## 10. How Each Step Hands Off the Next
The magic was never in any single step, it was in the chain. Sort cleared the cabinet for Straighten; Straighten opened surfaces for Shine; Shine revealed the leak and the view for Safety; Safety made the arrangement sound; Standardize made it the default; Sustain kept it alive until it hands you back to Sort. This is also how you fix a space that will not stay fixed: find the step you skipped.
- Illustration: Before / after split (sparks cabinet vs calm-dots cabinet)
- Callout (6S Tip): If a space keeps sliding back, look for the step you skipped.

## 11. Your First Lap
Stop watching and take one lap today on a space small enough that you cannot lose. Not the garage. One drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink. Fifteen minutes. The goal is not a transformed home, it is to feel all six handoffs once in your own hands. An imperfect full lap beats a perfect Sort and nothing else.
- Checklist (One small space, six steps): Empty it (Sort). Put back only what stays, easy things in front (Straighten). Wipe it out and notice what the mess was hiding (Shine). Crouch low and move anything dangerous up (Safety). Make the good arrangement obvious (Standardize). Pick one tiny habit (Sustain).
- Callout (Quick Win): Set a fifteen-minute timer and point it at one small space. Aim for one full lap, not perfect.

## 12. Chapter Close
You leave having watched six words do real work, in order, until a feral cabinet became calm, clean, safe, and easy to keep. The room-by-room playbooks later are not new methods, they are this same loop. Go take your first lap. The friction meter moves for the first time now, a small move for a small space. The bigger moves start in Chapter 4.
