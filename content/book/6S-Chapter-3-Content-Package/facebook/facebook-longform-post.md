# Facebook Longform Post: The Leak the Cabinet Was Hiding

A single longer post (about 300 to 450 words) telling the under-sink-cabinet story and the hidden-leak discovery, gently making the loop-not-a-menu point, ending with the fifteen-minute first-lap CTA. Written like talking to a friend.

---

Let me tell you about a cabinet under a kitchen sink, because there is a small lesson hiding in it that I think about all the time now.

You know the cabinet. Half-used spray bottles, a crusted sponge, three brands of trash bags, a forgotten bucket. It did not get that way on purpose. One item at a time, each one perfectly reasonable, none of them ever leaving. It quietly stopped being storage and became a place where things land.

So picture actually fixing it, the right way, in about fifteen minutes.

First you pull everything out onto a towel on the floor, where each thing has to answer one honest question: do I actually use this? Most of it cannot pass. You sort into three piles only, keep, relocate, release, and suddenly the cabinet is empty. Then you put back just the survivors, easy things in front where your hand lands without looking.

Now the good part. You wipe out the bare cabinet, slowly. And as the cloth reaches the back corner, you notice the wood there is darker. A little soft. There is a faint ring, and when you press it, it gives. A slow drip had been warping that board for months, completely hidden by all the clutter on top of it.

You did not go hunting for a leak. You found it because you were cleaning slowly, with your hands already there. That is the whole secret of the Shine step: cleaning done slowly is really inspection. A plumber would have charged you to discover that drip. Your sponge found it for free.

And here is the thing that ties it all together. You only found that leak because Sort emptied the cabinet first, and you only crouch down next and spot the drain cleaner sitting at a toddler's grabbing height because the space is finally clear enough to see it. Each step handed the next one something it could not have done alone.

That is why 6S is a loop, not a menu. You do not get to pick your three favorite steps and skip the rest. You walk the order, and the order does the work.

If your own under-sink cabinet just came to mind, that is your sign. Set a fifteen-minute timer this week and walk one small space through all six steps. Do not aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap.
