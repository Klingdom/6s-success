# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 3

**The Six Steps That Transform Any Space**
**6S Success: Home Edition**

Five diagrams, each drawn in the book's warm line-and-flat-color style (roughly 3px stroke, rounded corners) using only the chapter palette: paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark `#CB4B36`. Several of these already exist as SVGs in `chapter_03_final.html`; the notes below treat those as the reference so any standalone version stays consistent.

---

## 1. The 6S Loop with handoffs (hero)

**Purpose:** Give the reader the entire method as one shape and make the handoffs visible, which is the chapter's real subject.

**What it shows:** Six nodes in a ring, clockwise from the top: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Five nodes are white with a terracotta ring; Safety is a filled slate node, sitting at the bottom of the ring, set apart on purpose. Each clockwise arrow carries one italic handoff verb: makes room, gives a home, reveals truth, protects, sets default, keeps alive. The Sustain to Sort arrow closes the loop and is drawn a touch heavier so the eye reads the return. Center label, Newsreader italic: "Walk it once. Then it goes around again."

**Rough layout:** Square or 4:5. Faint guide ring in rule color. Numerals 1 to 6 in small honey or terracotta caps inside each node. This is the recurring book motif, so build it so a single node can later be lit while the rest dim.

## 2. Sort plus Straighten, two panel

**Purpose:** Show the first handoff. Sort makes the room that Straighten needs.

**What it shows:** Left panel labeled SORT: EVERYTHING OUT. A towel on the floor with three labeled piles, KEEP (green), RELOCATE (slate), RELEASE (spark), holding small line-icons of bottles, a stray screwdriver, a cracked bucket. A terracotta arrow leads to the right panel labeled STRAIGHTEN: ONLY KEEPERS BACK. The cabinet now holds just a caddy of daily things at the front and a dashed green CLEAR CORNER at the back, with a small note that the clear corner is where the next step will find something.

**Rough layout:** Wide 16:9 or 3:2, split down the middle with a dashed rule. Caption in Newsreader italic: decide first, then assign homes.

---

## 3. Shine, the leak reveal close-up

**Purpose:** Carry the chapter's most memorable idea, that cleaning is inspection and the leak was always there, just hidden.

**What it shows:** A close-up of the empty cabinet floor. A hand holds a cloth mid-wipe. To the left of the cloth the board is clean and pale; to the right a dark water ring and a soft, swollen patch are revealed where the cloth has passed. One red spark sits on the stain. Small motion marks trail the cloth. Caption, italic: the leak the clutter was hiding.

**Rough layout:** Wide, low banner (about 3:1). Eyebrow at top in sans caps: CLEANING IS INSPECTION. Keep the reveal on the right so the eye travels clean-to-truth, left to right.

---

## 4. The Safety swap, before and after

**Purpose:** Show that the open, clean space is what finally makes the hazard visible, and that the fix is small and real.

**What it shows:** Left cabinet labeled AT A CHILD'S HEIGHT in spark color: a drain-cleaner bottle low on the floor with a red warning band and a red spark, a small reaching hand at the edge. Right cabinet labeled UP, AND LATCHED in green: the same bottle moved to a high shelf with a green calm-dot, the low zone now a dashed-green empty rectangle, and a slate child-latch on the door edge. Caption, italic: poison up high, low zone clear.

**Rough layout:** Two cabinets side by side, dashed divider. Use height literally; the bottle's vertical move from floor to high shelf is the whole story, so keep the shelf lines clear.

---

## 5. The skipped-step diagnostic

**Purpose:** Give the reader a repair tool for a space that will not stay fixed, straight from the "How Each Step Hands Off the Next" section. (Note: this is the one diagram below that is not already drawn in the chapter HTML; it is an inference-friendly extension of the chapter's own logic, built only from lines the chapter states.)

**What it shows:** The six-node loop drawn faintly, with one node greyed out to represent a skipped step. Two worked examples branch off as small callouts, each in the book's voice:
- "A drawer that will not stay neat" points to a greyed **Sort** node, with the line: usually never truly sorted.
- "A standard nobody follows" points to a greyed **Standardize** node, with the line: skipped the part where you made the right way the easy way.
A short header reads: If a space keeps sliding back, look for the step you skipped.

**Rough layout:** The loop on the left, two diagnostic callouts stacked on the right with thin terracotta connector lines to the relevant node. Greyed node uses soft/rule tones so it clearly reads as missing. Keep it diagnostic and calm, not alarmist.
