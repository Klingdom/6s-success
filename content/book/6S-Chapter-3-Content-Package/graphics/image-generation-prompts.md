# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 3

**The Six Steps That Transform Any Space**
**6S Success: Home Edition**

Five prompts tuned to the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. They lean on the chapter palette (paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark `#CB4B36`) and on real materials and real light. Each prompt names composition, lighting, materials, palette, and mood, and ends with a short "avoid" note. None of these prompts or notes use the word that means joining without a visible seam.

---

## Prompt 1, the hero "before" cabinet (photographic)

A straight-on documentary photograph of the cabinet under a domestic kitchen sink, both doors open, shot at the cabinet's own height so the viewer is kneeling in front of it. Inside: a few half-used spray bottles leaning against each other, a crusted green sponge, two or three mismatched trash-bag boxes, a cracked plastic bucket, a bottle of drain cleaner sitting low at the front, and a faint dark water stain spreading across the particle-board floor at the back. Honest, lived-in clutter, not theatrical mess; nothing staged to look filthy. Warm late-afternoon kitchen light coming from the left, soft shadows, the cream and oat tones of the book's paper palette with small hits of terracotta and slate on the bottle labels. Mood: familiar, a little embarrassing, quietly hopeful. Editorial cookbook-photography quality, shallow but honest depth of field, natural color.
**Avoid:** glossy advertising sheen, neon cleaning-product colors, perfect symmetry, fake grime, lens flare, text or logos on the bottles.

---

## Prompt 2, the same cabinet after one lap (photographic pair to Prompt 1)

The identical under-sink cabinet, same camera height and framing as the "before," now walked through all six steps. Inside: a small woven or matte caddy at the front holding one dish soap and one all-purpose cleaner, trash bags standing where a hand can pull one, the back corner wiped clean and dry, the drain cleaner gone from the floor and visible on a higher shelf, a small printed photo taped inside one door showing where things go. Calm and uncrowded, not sterile or showroom-empty; it still looks like a real home. Same warm left light and paper-toned palette, with green and honey accents reading as calm. Mood: relief, order that looks easy to keep. Matched-pair editorial styling so it can sit beside Prompt 1.
**Avoid:** staged perfection, color-coded label-maker overload, plastic showroom emptiness, cold blue light, the word that means joining without a visible seam.

---

## Prompt 3, the Shine reveal (illustration, hand-drawn)

A warm hand-drawn close-up illustration in the book's line-and-flat-color style: a roughly 3px ink outline, flat fills, gently rounded forms. A hand holding a cloth wipes the floor of an emptied cabinet. Where the cloth has already passed, the board is clean and pale paper-cream; ahead of the cloth, a dark honey-brown water ring and a soft swollen patch of board are appearing. A single small four-point friction-spark in `#CB4B36` marks the stain. A few light terracotta motion strokes trail the cloth. Background flat panel cream. Mood: a quiet small discovery, the moment a space tells the truth. Editorial spot-illustration feel, like a thoughtful instructional book, not a cartoon.
**Avoid:** photorealism, heavy shading or gradients, comic-style action lines, glossy 3D rendering, harsh outlines, busy texture.

---

## Prompt 4, the Safety swap (illustration, two-panel hand-drawn)

A warm two-panel hand-drawn illustration in the same line-and-flat-color book style, divided by a thin dashed rule. Left panel: a simple cabinet with a drain-cleaner bottle low on the floor, a red warning band and a small red friction-spark on it, and a small child's hand reaching in from the side; label feeling of "at a child's height." Right panel: the same cabinet with the bottle moved up to a clearly higher shelf marked with a green calm-dot, the low zone now empty and outlined in a dashed green line, and a small slate child-latch on the door edge. Flat fills in paper, terracotta, slate, green, with `#CB4B36` reserved only for the hazard. Mood: practical, reassuring, a small real fix rather than alarm. Clean instructional clarity, generous negative space.
**Avoid:** scary or graphic imagery, skull-and-crossbones realism, photoreal hands, cluttered backgrounds, drop shadows, gradient lighting.

---

## Prompt 5, the 6S Loop as a printable fridge card (illustration, graphic)

A warm editorial graphic of the 6S Loop designed to live on a fridge or a phone lock screen. Six labeled nodes arranged in a clean ring, clockwise from the top: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Five nodes are cream circles with a terracotta ring and an ink serif label in a Fraunces-like display face; the Safety node is a filled slate circle with a cream label, sitting at the bottom and set apart. Slim terracotta arrows curve between the nodes, each carrying one small italic handoff word (makes room, gives a home, reveals truth, protects, sets default, keeps alive), and the final arrow loops from Sustain back to Sort. Centered italic line: "Walk it once. Then it goes around again." Flat paper-cream background, a faint guide ring, honey numerals 1 to 6. Mood: calm, tidy, hand-made but precise, like a well-set page from a warm reference book. Generous margins, print-ready balance.
**Avoid:** corporate flat-vector slickness, gradients, glow effects, drop shadows, dense clip-art icons, more than the six palette colors, stocky infographic clutter.
