Hi friend,

There is a cabinet under your kitchen sink, and I think we both know how it is doing in there.

It did not become a disaster on purpose. Nothing in it is dirty, exactly, and it is not even that full. It just filled up the way these things always do. One item at a time, each one perfectly reasonable, none of them ever leaving. The dish soap, a dead flashlight, three brands of trash bags, a sponge that has clearly given up on life. The cabinet stopped being storage and quietly became a place where things land. I have started calling spaces like this feral. Running themselves, badly, while you are busy living your life.

This week's chapter does something I have been waiting to do since we started: it stops explaining 6S and actually walks it, start to finish, through one small space. That cabinet. All six steps, in order, before the book slows down to master each one. Think of it as a slow lap around the track before the real training begins.

Here is the part most people miss, and it is the whole point. The six steps are not a menu. You do not get to like the sound of Sort, enjoy a good Shine, and quietly skip the ones that sound like homework. They are a chain. Each step hands the next one something it could not have done alone. Sort empties the cabinet onto a towel and hands Straighten an open space to work in. Straighten gives every survivor a home by how often you use it, and hands Shine clear surfaces to clean. And Shine is where it gets good.

Because Shine is not really about clean. It is about inspection. You wipe the empty cabinet slowly, with your hands already there, and the wood in the back corner is darker than the rest. A little soft. You press it and it gives. That is a slow drip that has been warping the board for months, the one the clutter was politely hiding the whole time. You did not go looking for it. You found it because you were cleaning slowly. A plumber would have charged you to find that. Your sponge found it for free.

Then comes Safety, and here is why it sits in the middle of the loop instead of tacked on the end. You are still crouched in front of the cabinet, which means you are looking at it from the height of a two-year-old. From down there the drain cleaner is not in storage. It is on display, at grabbing height, behind a door that does not lock. You only saw it because the first three steps cleared everything out of the way. So you move it up high, click a dollar latch shut, and that is a real safety improvement done in under a minute.

Standardize locks it in by taping a small photo inside the door, so the right arrangement is something anyone can match instead of something only you remember. Sustain keeps it alive with a ten-second reset and a monthly glance at that back corner. And Sustain does not end the story. It hands you back to Sort, because a home is alive and its contents keep changing. That is why 6S is a loop, not a line.

**One thing to try this week:** take your first lap. Not the garage. Not the closet that haunts you. Pick one drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink. Set a fifteen-minute timer and walk all six steps once. Empty it, decide what stays, put the daily things up front, wipe it slowly and notice what the mess was hiding, crouch low and move anything dangerous up, make the good arrangement obvious, then pick one tiny habit to protect it. Do not aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap. A perfect Sort and nothing else is just a very tidy pile of unfinished work. One imperfect lap teaches your hands the whole method, and the next space goes faster because of it.

If you want to watch the whole thing work, leak and all, the full chapter walks through that cabinet step by step. Read it free in the online book whenever you have a few minutes. Then go find your fifteen minutes and one small space.

Talk soon, and good luck with that back corner.

Warmly,
The 6S Success team
