# B-Roll and Visual Notes

**Chapter 3, 6S Success: Home Edition, The Six Steps That Transform Any Space**
A shot list and visual reference for the YouTube, short-form, and teleprompter scripts. One throughline space: the cabinet under a kitchen sink. Keep the cabinet, the leak, the caddy, and the moved bottle consistent across every shot so the before and after read as the same real space.

---

## Look and feel

- Warm kitchen light, real home, not a staged set. Honest clutter, not theatrical chaos.
- Palette echoes the book: warm paper tones, terracotta and honey accents, slate for the Safety and data moments.
- Motif vocabulary carried from the chapter: green calm-dots for things that are now right, red friction-sparks for snags and hazards.
- Hero motion graphic: the 6S Loop. Six nodes in a ring, Safety rendered in slate. Reuse it whenever you need a reset or a chapter beat.
- Closing motif: the friction meter, taking its first small move from rest toward calm.

---

## Shot list (in story order)

### 1. The feral cabinet, before (hero opener)
- [B-ROLL] Straight-on, both doors open. Half-used spray bottles, a crusted sponge, three brands of trash bags, a forgotten bucket, a faint dark water stain at the back. Hold a beat too long so it gets slightly uncomfortable.
- [B-ROLL] Insert: a hand reaches in, a bottle tips, something rolls to the back into the dark.
- Optional overlay: red friction-sparks animate onto each snag point (the leak stain, the low chemical bottle, the tangle).
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Six steps. One small cabinet.
- Lower third: "The cabinet that went feral."

### 2. Everything out on the towel (Sort)
- [B-ROLL] A towel on the floor. Items come out of the cabinet one at a time and land in daylight. Top-down angle works well here.
- [B-ROLL] Three forming piles. A crusty bottle held alone, turned over, considered.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Keep. Relocate. Release.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] You are not organizing yet. You are deciding.
- Lower third callout card: "Common mistake: buying bins before you sort."

### 3. The caddy and frequency zones (Straighten)
- [B-ROLL] The small keep pile, surprisingly little. A few survivors going back into the cabinet.
- [B-ROLL] A small caddy placed at the front. Daily soap and cleaner set into it. Trash bags stood where one can be pulled without excavating.
- [B-ROLL] Insert: the back corner deliberately left clear and empty. Linger here, it sets up the leak.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Daily things to the front. Where your hand lands without looking.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Tidy means lined up. Easy means you reach it first.

### 4. The slow wipe and the leak (Shine, the key beat)
- [B-ROLL] Extreme close-up. A cloth wiping the floor of the nearly empty cabinet, slowly. Match the chapter illustration.
- [B-ROLL] The cloth reaches the clear back corner. The wood there is visibly darker. A faint ring.
- [B-ROLL] A thumb presses the spot and it gives, soft and swollen. One small red friction-spark marks the stain.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Cleaning is inspection.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] A plumber would have charged you. Your sponge found it for free.
- Lower third callout card: "Common mistake: cleaning fast to get it over with."

### 5. Crouch to a child's height (Safety)
- [B-ROLL] Camera drops to floor level, the cabinet seen from a toddler's eye line. Important: shoot this low angle deliberately, it is the point.
- [B-ROLL] The drain cleaner bottle, warnings visible, at the front of the cabinet floor. A small hand could reach it. Red friction-spark on the bottle.
- [B-ROLL] The bottle moves up to a high shelf. A green calm-dot animates over its new home.
- [B-ROLL] Insert: a one-dollar latch clicks shut on the cabinet door.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Crouch to a child's height. Look low.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Burn, poison, or cut? It goes up high.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] About a dollar. About a minute.
- Lower third: "At home, the inspector is you."

### 6. The photo inside the door (Standardize)
- [B-ROLL] A phone snaps a photo of the finished cabinet.
- [B-ROLL] The photo, printed small, gets taped inside the cabinet door. Close-up of the door swinging to show the label in place.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Make the right way the easy way.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Not a memory. A picture anyone can match.

### 7. The ten-second reset (Sustain)
- [B-ROLL] A hand pulls the spray bottle, quickly nudges a stray item back, closes the door. Real-time, ten seconds.
- [B-ROLL] Insert: a monthly glance at the back corner where the leak was, a thumb checking it is dry.
- [VISUAL] Small looping arrow graphic over the caddy, labeled "10-second reset."
- [VISUAL] The 6S Loop graphic returns, the Sustain node lights, the arrow traces back around to Sort.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Tiny habit. Monthly glance. Then back to Sort.

### 8. Before and after split (payoff)
- [B-ROLL or composite] Split screen of the same cabinet. Left, the feral version covered in red friction-sparks. Right, the calm version: sorted, caddy at the front, leak dried and marked for repair, chemical up high, photo label inside the door, green calm-dots throughout.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Same cabinet. One full lap of the loop.

### 9. The fifteen-minute lap (CTA)
- [B-ROLL] A phone timer set to 15:00. A single small drawer or shelf, nothing huge.
- [B-ROLL] Quick montage, one beat each: empty onto towel, a few things back, a slow wipe, a low crouch, a phone photo taped inside a door, a door closing.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] One small space. Fifteen minutes. All six steps.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Don't aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap.

### 10. The friction meter, first move (close)
- [VISUAL] The friction meter takes its first small step from rest toward calm. Honest and modest, just a notch.
- [ON SCREEN TEXT] Small space. Small move. The bigger moves start when you choose a real target.

## The 6S Loop graphic (reusable motif)

- Six nodes in a ring, clockwise: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain.
- Safety rendered in slate to stand apart from the others.
- Short handoff verb on each arrow: Sort makes room, Straighten gives a home, Shine reveals the truth, Safety protects the people, Standardize makes the good way the easy way, Sustain keeps it alive.
- The arrow from Sustain curves back to Sort to close the loop.
- Center label option: "Walk it once. Then it goes around again."
- Built so a single node can be highlighted on its own for later chapter beats.

## Audio and pacing notes

- Let the leak reveal breathe. Cut the music or drop it to a hush as the thumb presses the soft wood. It is the emotional center of the piece.
- The Safety crouch wants a low, quiet, slate-colored tone, a small gut-punch, not alarm.
- Keep the close warm and encouraging. The chapter rewards starting, not perfection. Soft CTA only: one small space, fifteen minutes, one full lap.
