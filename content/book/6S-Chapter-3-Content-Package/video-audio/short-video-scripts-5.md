## SHORT 1: The Cabinet That Went Feral
**Target length: about 35 seconds**

**Hook line:** The cabinet under your sink did not become a disaster on purpose.

[VISUAL: Straight-on shot of a messy under-sink cabinet, doors swung open. Hold a beat too long.]

[SPOKEN]
The cabinet under your kitchen sink did not become a disaster on purpose. Nobody decided to store the dish soap next to a dead flashlight and a bottle of something blue from a previous decade. It happened one item at a time. Each one perfectly reasonable. None of them ever leaving.

[VISUAL: A hand reaches in, a bottle tips, something rolls to the back.]

[SPOKEN]
And here is the thing. It is not even dirty. It is not even that full. The problem is that nothing in there has a job, a home, or a reason. It stopped being storage and became a place where things land. That is not messy. That is feral.

[ON SCREEN TEXT, in beats]
- Not dirty.
- Not full.
- Just feral.
- Nothing has a job, a home, or a reason.

## SHORT 2: Cleaning Is Inspection (The Leak)
**Target length: about 45 seconds**

**Hook line:** A plumber would have charged you to find this. Your sponge found it for free.

[VISUAL: Close-up, a hand wiping the floor of an empty cabinet, slowly.]

[SPOKEN]
Most of us clean fast. Wipe it, make it look okay, move on. But cleaning fast tells you nothing. Watch this.

[VISUAL: The cloth reaches the back corner. Darker wood. A faint ring. A thumb presses it and it gives, soft.]

[SPOKEN]
As the cloth reaches the back corner, the wood is darker. A little soft. Press it and it gives. That is a slow leak that has been quietly warping the board for months. And you did not find it by looking for leaks. You found it because you were cleaning slowly, with your hands already there.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- Cleaning fast tells you nothing.
- Clean slowly enough to notice.
- Shine is really inspection.

[SPOKEN]
A plumber would have charged you to discover this. Your sponge found it for free. That is what cleaning slowly actually buys you. The leak, the loose hinge, the rust. The space finally tells you the truth.

## SHORT 3: Crouch to a Toddler's Height
**Target length: about 40 seconds**

**Hook line:** Crouch down in front of your low cabinet. Now look at it like a two-year-old.

[VISUAL: Camera drops to floor level, looking into an open low cabinet from a toddler's eye line.]

[SPOKEN]
Crouch down in front of a low cabinet. Right down to the floor. Because now you are looking at it from the height of a two-year-old, and you will see something you have walked past for years.

[VISUAL: A drain cleaner bottle, warnings all over it, sitting at the front of the cabinet floor. A small hand could reach it.]

[SPOKEN]
That drain cleaner, covered in warnings, sitting on the cabinet floor. Standing up, it is unremarkable. From down here, it is not in storage. It is on display, at grabbing height, behind a door that does not lock.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- Anything that can burn, poison, or cut...
- ...belongs up high.
- A latch costs about a dollar.

[VISUAL: The bottle moves to a high shelf, a green dot appears, a one-dollar latch clicks shut.]

[SPOKEN]
So move it up. Anything that can burn, poison, or cut goes high, behind a latch that costs about a dollar. A moved bottle is a real safety step, done in under a minute. At home, the inspector is you.

## SHORT 4: It's a Loop, Not a Menu
**Target length: about 50 seconds**

**Hook line:** Your organizing keeps falling apart because you treated it like a menu.

[VISUAL: The 6S Loop graphic, six nodes in a ring: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Sustain arrow curves back to Sort.]

[SPOKEN]
If your organizing never lasts, here is the likely reason. You treated the steps like a menu. You did the ones you liked and skipped the ones that sounded like homework. But these six steps are not separate chores that happen to start with S. They are a chain. Each one sets up the next.

[VISUAL: Each node lights in turn as it is named.]

[SPOKEN]
You cannot give something a home until you know it is staying. You cannot clean around a pile. You will miss the hazards hidden behind the clutter. Skip a step and you feel like you are working hard while the space fights you the whole way.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- A drawer that won't stay neat? It was never truly sorted.
- A standard nobody follows? You skipped making the right way the easy way.

[SPOKEN]
So when a space keeps sliding back, you did not fail at willpower. You skipped a step. Find the skipped step, and the space stops fighting you.

## SHORT 5: The Fifteen-Minute Lap
**Target length: about 40 seconds**

**Hook line:** You do not need a free weekend. You need fifteen minutes and one small space.

[VISUAL: A phone timer set to 15:00. A single drawer or shelf, nothing huge.]

[SPOKEN]
You do not need a free weekend to start this. You need fifteen minutes and one small space. Not the garage. Not the closet that haunts you. One drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your sink.

[VISUAL: Quick montage, one beat each: empty onto a towel, a few things go back, a slow wipe, a low crouch, a phone photo taped inside a door, a door closing.]

[SPOKEN]
Empty it. Sort into keep, relocate, release. Put back only what stays, easy things in front. Wipe it slowly and notice what the mess was hiding. Crouch low and move anything dangerous up. Make the good arrangement obvious. Pick one tiny habit to protect it. Then close it.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- Don't aim for perfect.
- Aim for one full lap.
- A perfect Sort and nothing else is just a tidy pile of unfinished work.

[SPOKEN]
It will not be perfect, and it does not need to be. One full, imperfect lap teaches your hands the whole method. That is the first lap.

[CTA, one line]
Chapter 3 of 6S Success, Home Edition walks you through it. Read it free in the online book, then go set your timer.
