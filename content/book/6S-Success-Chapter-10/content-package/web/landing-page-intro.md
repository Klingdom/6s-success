# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 10, Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

You are almost done with the entryway, and the last two things on your hands are the two you could not answer. The console is clear and holding its five working things. The overflow basket sits empty. Off to the side, an out box waits with the unnecessary things you decided you do not use. And still in your hands are one or two objects that refused to answer the question. You do not use them, exactly, but you cannot quite say to let them go. They are not clutter and they are not keepers. They are honest maybes.

This is the chapter that gives them a plan. Chapter 9 made the clear calls and set the unnecessary things aside. What it could not resolve were the genuine maybes, and the quieter thing that circles after any release: the small voice that asks, a day later, whether you should have. So this chapter is short and specific. It builds a calm system for the maybes so a thing you cannot call does not stall the whole pass, and it handles the second thoughts, both the fear before and the rare regret after. Neither needs willpower. Both need a little structure.

The mistake to head off first is the tidy, motivated one: forcing a verdict onto a thing you cannot read. Force a keep and it goes back on the shelf undecided in all but name. Force a release and you send to the out box a thing you were not ready to let go, which is precisely how remorse is manufactured: not from letting go, but from letting go before you had an answer. A genuine maybe does not have an answer yet. That is what makes it a maybe. A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove.

The system is the red tag, borrowed and scaled down from the factory floor. A red tag carries three lines, and only three: what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date. The tagged thing goes to a holding area, one known spot out of the daily flow, and time makes the call. Reach for it before the date and it earned its keep, so you untag it. Let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no, and it joins the out box. The deadline is the whole point, because a holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile.

Then the chapter meets the fear head on. Sorter's remorse comes in two kinds. The first is the anticipated what-if, felt before anything is gone, and the holding area answers it: while a thing waits under a red tag it is not gone, it is one shelf away, so the fear has nothing to grip. The second is the rare real miss, and the honest odds answer that. You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss.

Read Chapter 10 free in the online book, then red-tag one honest maybe. On a slip of tape, write what it is and a decide-by date, stick it on, and set the thing in one spot out of the way. If you reach for it before the date, it stays. If not, the decision already made itself.
