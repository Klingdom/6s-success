# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 10, Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (the red tag hook)

- og:title: For the Thing You Cannot Decide
- og:description: Do not force a guess. Red-tag it with what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date, set it in a holding area out of the daily flow, and let time make the call.
- twitter:title: For the Thing You Cannot Decide
- twitter:description: A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove. Tag it, give it a decide-by date, and let time answer. From Chapter 10 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: The red tag, close up. A single red tag tied to an umbrella handle shows three labeled lines, "what it is", "date tagged", and "decide-by". Two paths branch from it: one green to a panel reading "reached for it in time, it earned its keep, untag it," the other slate to a panel reading "date passed, untouched, the out box." Caption line: Three lines on the tag, two ways it can end.

## Variant B (sorter's remorse)

- og:title: You Almost Never Regret What You Let Go
- og:description: You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss. While a thing waits under a red tag it is not gone, it is one shelf away, so the fear has nothing to grip.
- twitter:title: You Almost Never Regret What You Let Go
- twitter:description: Sorter's remorse comes in two kinds: the fear before, answered by the holding area, and the rare regret after, answered by the honest odds. From Chapter 10 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: The reassurance card. A clean card carries the line "You almost never regret what you let go," and below it a small tagged item waits on a shelf one step out of the daily path, dated and retrievable, showing that a tagged thing is not gone. Footer: Let time make the call.
