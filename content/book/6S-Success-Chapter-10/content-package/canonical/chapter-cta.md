# Chapter 10 Calls to Action: Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

This is the third Sort chapter. Its strongest CTA is red-tagging a single honest maybe: something you cannot call, given a decide-by date and set aside to let time answer. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: not knowing is normal, and the system decides kindly, so you never have to force a guess.

## Primary CTA
Red-tag one maybe. "On a slip of tape write what it is and a decide-by date, stick it on, and set the thing in one spot out of the way. If you reach for it before the date, it stays. If not, the decision already made itself. You do not have to be sure today. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Not every thing answers on the spot. Chapter 10 gives the honest maybes a red tag, a holding area, and a decide-by date, and lets time make the call, then quiets the second thoughts before they run the pass. Read it free online, then Chapter 11 routes the out box to where each thing should truly go."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Red-tag one honest maybe right now. On a slip of tape write what it is and a decide-by date, stick it on, and set the thing in one spot out of the way. If you reach for it before the date, it stays. If not, the decision already made itself.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone red-tags one "I am not sure" thing with the same decide-by date, and you all agree not to argue about anyone else's tags until the date comes. Whatever no one reached for by then goes, no debate.

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the brief)
- **The Red Tag:** printable tags with the three lines, what it is / date tagged / decide-by date, several to a sheet, so a maybe gets a proper tag in seconds.
- **The Holding Area card:** a printable on how to set up a time-boxed holding area, pick a decide-by date by item type (about a month for everyday, a season for seasonal), and honor it.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is red-tagging one maybe, however small. Lead with "write what it is and a decide-by date," which is concrete and can be done in a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and low-stakes. The holding area is a temporary, time-boxed limbo, not storage; expired maybes join the out box, and where the out box goes is Chapter 11.
- "A maybe is not a failure of nerve" and "you almost never regret what you let go" are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is postponing a decision on purpose and letting time, not willpower, make the call.
