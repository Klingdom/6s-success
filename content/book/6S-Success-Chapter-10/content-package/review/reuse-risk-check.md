# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 10 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it. Every element named below was grep-verified in the manuscript, and every asset placement was read in the file named, before being listed.

## Same-day collision check
The calendar is built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, do not let the "holding area without a deadline" line and the "you almost never regret what you let go" reframe both land the same day, since they both carry the chapter's core anti-fear beat.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same red-tag content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version (`linkedin/linkedin-newsletter-version.md`) and the email newsletter version (`newsletter/newsletter-version.md`) share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, and the like) to point back with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Part 3, Sort, Chapters 8 to 11)
- **Keep Chapter 10 "the maybes and the remorse" distinct from Chapter 8 and Chapter 9.** Chapter 8 sorted by belonging, Chapter 9 sorted by use, and Chapter 10 handles only what those passes could not resolve. The manuscript draws the line, verbatim: "It is not about the easy yeses or the easy noes; you made those already. It is about the two things Chapter 9 could not resolve." Do not let a Chapter 10 asset re-open the belonging or use question; its job is the honest remainder, not the whole sort.
- **Hold the seam to Chapter 11.** Chapter 11 is named in the handoff, verbatim: "Next: Chapter 11 · What to Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, or Throw Away." Where the out box goes is Chapter 11's work, not this one's. The manuscript is explicit that "That box is not this chapter's business" and defers routing, so keep Chapter 10's out box as a settled, waiting corner and do not let a Chapter 10 post promise or instruct donate/sell/store/recycle/discard. That is Chapter 11's routing, and mixing it in would blur both chapters.
- **The out box is a shared object across the seam.** It was filled in Chapter 9, it receives the expired maybes in Chapter 10, and it is routed in Chapter 11. When Chapters 9, 10, and 11 assets are live together, make sure a Chapter 10 asset does not show the out box being emptied or routed early, which is Chapter 11's payoff.
- **The friction meter holds this chapter, and that is the distinguishing beat.** Chapter 8 stepped the needle, Chapter 9 stepped it again but shorter, and Chapter 10 holds it because nothing new left the room. The manuscript says so plainly: "The meter only moves when the room does." A new faint dashed forward arrow marks the projected next step for when the decide-by dates pass (present in both HTML files, verified). Make sure no Chapter 10 asset shows the needle advancing past where Chapter 9 left it, and preserve the dashed projection so the hold reads as honest discipline, not a stall.
- **The entryway example keeps running.** The console, the basket, and now the holding-area shelf are the same entryway carried since Part 2. When Part 2 and Part 3 assets are live together, vary which step each post leads with so the same entryway does not feel overused.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter itself is a draft authored in the book voice, not supplied source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft. If the author revises the chapter, refresh the assets so they do not drift from the published version.
- Nothing is borrowed from outside the book without saying so. The one external idea, the red tag, is lightly attributed in the chapter itself to its Lean origin ("the idea is borrowed from the factory floor", verified), so no asset should present it as the book's invention.
- The ten quote cards are verbatim from the manuscript (each string grep-verified as byte-identical; the file marks Cards 1 through 10 as **verbatim** and labels its four alternates **verbatim** as well). That labeling is accurate.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate the six CTA phrasings across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep Chapter 10 "the maybes and the remorse" clearly separate from Chapter 8 and Chapter 9, and hold the Chapter 11 routing as Chapter 11's, not this chapter's.
5. Preserve the "needle holds, steps when the dates pass" framing and the dashed forward projection in every asset that shows the gauge.
6. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
