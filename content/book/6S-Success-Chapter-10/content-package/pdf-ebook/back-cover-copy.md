# Back Cover Copy: Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

## Back-cover text

The easy calls are made. What is left in your hands is the handful of things that would not answer: not clutter, not keepers, just honest maybes. And circling somewhere behind them is the late voice that asks, a day after you release something, whether you should have.

Chapter 10 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the third Sort chapter, and it handles exactly those two things. You cannot force a maybe; pushing a verdict onto an uncertain thing only reopens the question or breeds regret. So you tag it instead. A red tag carries three lines: what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date. The tagged thing goes to a holding area out of the daily flow, and time makes the call. Reach for it before the date and it earned its keep. Let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no. The deadline is the whole point, because "a holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile."

Then it meets the fear that guards clutter. Anticipated remorse, felt before anything is gone, is answered by the holding area, since a tagged thing is not gone, only waiting one shelf away. The rare real regret is answered by the honest odds: "You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss."

"A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove." Tag the maybes, give them a date, and let the one thing you cannot argue with, time, make the call for you.
