# 6S Success: Home Edition (Ebook Description)

Most decluttering advice breaks down on the same objects: the handful of things you honestly cannot call. Not clutter, not keepers, just genuine maybes that stall the whole pass while you stand there trying to feel certain on a schedule. Chapter 10 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is where you learn to stop forcing them.

"Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse" is the third Sort chapter, and it clears the two loose ends the last chapter named. The first, an out box of unnecessary things in the corner, simply waits; where it goes is a later chapter, not this one. The second, the honest maybes still in your hands, is the whole job. A maybe cannot be forced. Push a verdict onto an uncertain thing and you either reopen the same question next month or release something you were not ready to lose. As the chapter puts it, "A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove."

The tool is the red tag, borrowed and scaled down from the factory floor. A red tag carries three lines: what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date. The tagged thing goes to a holding area, one known spot out of the daily flow, and time makes the call. Reach for it before the date and it earned its keep; let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no. The deadline is the whole point, because "a holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile." Match it to the thing: about a month for an everyday object, a season for a seasonal one. A short worked pass tags the two entryway maybes, the gift umbrella and the folding poncho, with no forced guess.

The chapter also meets the fear that guards clutter: sorter's remorse, in its two kinds. Anticipated remorse, the what-if felt before anything is even gone, is answered by the holding area itself, since a tagged thing is not gone, only waiting. Rare real regret is answered by the honest odds: "You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss." Neither kind is a reason to keep everything.

Nothing new left the room today, so the friction meter holds exactly where the last pass left it, with a faint dashed arrow ahead marking where it steps next, when the decide-by dates pass. You are allowed to not know. Tag the maybes, give them a date, and let the one thing you cannot argue with, time, make the call for you.
