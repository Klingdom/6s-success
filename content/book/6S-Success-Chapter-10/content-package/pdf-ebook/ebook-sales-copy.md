# 6S Success: Home Edition (Sales Page Copy)

## The easy calls are made. Two things will not answer.

You are almost done with the entryway. The console is clear and holding its five working things. The overflow basket is emptied. Off to the side, an out box holds the unnecessary things, waiting together for somewhere to go. And still in your hands are one or two objects that refused the question. You do not use them, exactly, but you cannot quite say to let them go. They are not clutter and they are not keepers. They are honest maybes.

There is also a quieter thing circling. A day after you release something, a small voice sometimes asks whether you should have. That voice has a name in this work, and left alone it will creep back into every pass you ever run and talk you out of finishing.

## You cannot force a maybe

The tidy, motivated move at exactly this point is to just decide, be firm, make the call and move on. But a genuine maybe does not have an answer yet. That is what makes it a maybe. Pushing harder does not produce one; it only produces a guess wearing the costume of a decision. Force a keep and the thing goes back on the shelf undecided in everything but name. Force a release and you may send off something you were not ready to lose, which is exactly how remorse is manufactured: "not from letting go, but from letting go before you had an answer."

So this chapter stops forcing and builds a system instead. "A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove."

## What you get in this chapter

- The red tag, the gentlest tool in the book, carrying three lines and only three: what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date
- The holding area, one known spot out of the daily flow where tagged maybes wait out their deadlines together, because "a holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile"
- How to match a decide-by date to the thing: about a month for an everyday object, a season for a seasonal one, so real life has a fair chance to test it
- A short worked pass on the two entryway maybes, the gift umbrella and the folding poncho, tagged and dated with no forced guess
- The two shapes of sorter's remorse, the anticipated fear before anything is gone and the rare real regret after, and the calm answer to each

## Nobody is asked to be sure

You will not throw a single thing while you decide. A tagged maybe is not gone; it is right there, dated, one shelf away, until time answers for you. Reach for it before its date and it earned its keep. Let the date pass untouched and the maybe quietly became a no. You set up the one judge that cannot be argued with, which is time, and you handed both questions to it.

Nothing new leaves the room today, so the friction meter holds exactly where the last pass left it, with a faint dashed arrow ahead marking where it steps next, when the decide-by dates pass. That is the honest reading, and this book would rather be accurate than flattering.

Read the chapter, pick one honest maybe, and give it a tag and a date. You do not have to be sure today.
