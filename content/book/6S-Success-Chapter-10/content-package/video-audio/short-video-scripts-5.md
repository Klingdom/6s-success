## SHORT 1: The tag for the thing you cannot decide
**Topic: the hero device, the red tag. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** For the thing you cannot decide, you do not need to be sure. You need a tag.

**Script:**
For the thing you honestly cannot decide, you do not have to force a guess. You tag it. A red tag carries three things, and only three: what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date. A scrap of paper or a strip of tape will do. Then you set it in a holding area, out of the daily flow, and time answers for you. Reach for it before the decide-by date, and it earned its keep, so untag it. Let the date pass without ever reaching for it, and the maybe just became a no. You are no longer choosing between keeping forever and letting go right now. You are choosing to find out, and time does the finding.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Cannot decide? Do not guess. Tag it."
- 12s: "what it is · date tagged · decide-by date"
- 26s: "Reach for it in time = keep"
- 36s: "Date passes untouched = it goes"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand loops a paper tag onto an umbrella handle and writes a date. Two soft paths branch off the tag: green up to a "keep" panel, slate down to an "out box" panel. Warm morning light.

## SHORT 2: A maybe is not a failure of nerve
**Topic: the core reframe, anti-shame. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You do not have to feel certain on a schedule.

**Script:**
You get to the thing you cannot call, and you think: I will just be firm, I will decide. But a genuine maybe does not have an answer yet. That is what makes it a maybe. Pushing harder does not produce one; it only produces a guess wearing the costume of a decision. And a forced guess is exactly how remorse is made: not from letting go, but from letting go before you had an answer. So stop trying to feel certain on a schedule. You are allowed to not know. A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Trying to just 'be firm'?"
- 10s: "A genuine maybe has no answer yet"
- 22s: "You are allowed to not know"
- 30s: "A maybe is not a failure of nerve"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand hovers over an uncertain object, tenses as if to force a call, then relaxes and loops a tag on it instead. Calm, unhurried.

## SHORT 3: A holding area without a deadline
**Topic: the 6S Tip, the deadline is the point. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile.

**Script:**
A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile. A box of undated maybes is not a decision and it is not progress. It is the same indecision you started with, moved into a tidier container and given a calmer name. The deadline is the whole game, because it is the thing that eventually forces an answer. So match the date to the thing. About a month for an everyday object, a season for a seasonal one, because a snow shovel cannot prove itself in July. And put the date where you will see it, because a deadline you do not honor is not a deadline; it is a wish.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A holding area without a deadline..."
- 8s: "...is just a nicer junk pile"
- 20s: "A month for everyday · a season for seasonal"
- 30s: "A deadline you do not honor is a wish"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A labeled bin of tagged items on a shelf. One tag has a clear decide-by date circled; one has a blank date line. The blank one dims. A calendar reminder pings.

## SHORT 4: The two entryway maybes
**Topic: the worked example, kept short. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Two things you could not call. Here is how time calls them for you.

**Script:**
Two things left on your hands that would not answer. First, a good umbrella that was a gift. Better than the one you actually grab, and you never grab it, and letting it go feels a little rude. That flicker is exactly why you cannot call it. So do not. Tag it. Decide-by: one season, because an umbrella needs weather to prove itself. Second, a folding poncho you used exactly once. Might be handy, might not. Tag it. Decide-by: a month, or the next trip. Both go to the holding area. Reach for either and it stays; let the date pass untouched and it joins the out box. Two tags, two dates, not a single forced guess.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Two maybes you could not call"
- 10s: "Gift umbrella: decide-by one season"
- 24s: "Folding poncho: decide-by a month"
- 34s: "Time makes the call, not willpower"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand tags the gift umbrella, then the folded poncho, and sets both into a labeled bin on a low shelf. The cleared console and empty basket soft in the background.

## SHORT 5: You almost never regret what you let go
**Topic: sorter's remorse, the reframe. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The fear that you will regret it is louder than the regret ever is.

**Script:**
A day after you let something go, a small voice asks whether you should have. Here is the thing about that fear. You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss. Think back over everything you have ever cleared from a home, then count the things you genuinely missed afterward. For most people the honest number is close to zero, and the few real ones were cheap to replace. Keeping everything to guard against a small, rare regret guarantees the opposite: the daily, certain weight of living among things you do not use. And while a thing waits under a red tag, it is not even gone. It is one shelf away. So you get to act while unsure, which is the only way anyone ever acts at all.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Afraid you will regret letting it go?"
- 12s: "You almost never regret what you let go"
- 24s: "The fear is louder than the loss"
- 34s: "Act while unsure. The tag keeps it near."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand rests on a tagged item in a holding-area bin, then closes the lid calmly. Optional insert: a nearly empty tally sheet under the words "things I actually missed." Warm, unhurried.
