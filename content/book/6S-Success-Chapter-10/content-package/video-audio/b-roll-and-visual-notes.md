# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

**Chapter 10, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** red-tags-holding-areas-and-sorters-remorse
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero device is the red tag: a paper tag, a strip of masking tape, or an index card looped on with string, showing three labeled lines, "what it is", "date tagged", and "decide-by", the third emphasized.
- A red tag branches two ways: green up to "reached for it in time, keep, untag" and slate down to "date passed untouched, the out box". Time is the switch between them.
- The holding area is one known spot out of the daily flow: a labeled box, a bin, a reserved drawer. Everything in it wears a date.
- Green calm-dots still glow on things that get kept. Slate marks the path to the out box. The tags themselves are a quiet terracotta red, the color of a decision postponed on purpose, not avoided.
- The out box from Chapter 9 sits full and settled in a corner. Nothing is routed, donated, sold, or thrown away on camera this chapter; where the out box goes is Chapter 11.
- The friction meter HOLDS this chapter. The live needle stays exactly where Chapter 9 left it, a little past the honey middle toward calm. A NEW faint dashed forward arrow points a short way further toward calm, marking the projected next step for when the decide-by dates pass. The GOAL crosshair stays ahead on the green side.
- Warm morning light, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.
- Nothing new leaves the room this chapter. The only actions are tagging maybes and setting them in a holding area to wait.

### Shot 3: You cannot force a maybe
- Close on a hand that picks up an uncertain object and tenses, as if to force a verdict, then relaxes and loops a tag on it instead.
- Optional split: on the left, a forced keep goes back on the shelf undecided; on the right, a forced release drops a thing the hand was not ready to lose. Both dim. The tagged path stays warm and lit.
- The point: a genuine maybe has no answer yet, and forcing one only makes a guess wearing the costume of a decision.

### Shot 4: The holding area on a timeline

- Sell the rule: the holding area is a waiting room, not a destination; the deadline is the trigger that empties it.

- **Lower third:** The Holding Area: where tagged maybes wait for a date

### Shot 5: Matching the date to the thing
- A calendar or phone reminder view. An everyday object gets "about a month." A seasonal object, a snow shovel or a beach umbrella, gets "a season."
- Optional beat: a date written on the lid of the holding-area box itself, then a reminder pinging when it comes up, and a hand opening the box to settle what it governs.
- Sell the rule: the window is long enough for real life to test the thing, short enough that the maybe cannot become permanent by default. A deadline you do not honor is a wish.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A month for everyday. A season for seasonal." then "Keep the appointment."
- **Lower third:** Match the date to the thing, then honor it

## EDITING NOTES

- This is the quiet, structural chapter between two action passes. Nothing new leaves the room; the win is a system for the maybes and a calm answer to the fear. Let the cut breathe and stay unhurried.
- Keep the tone anti-shame and non-prescriptive. Not knowing is normal, and the system decides kindly. Never frame a maybe as a failure of nerve or willpower.
- Do show tagging and setting things in a holding area. Do NOT show anything donated, sold, recycled, or thrown away; where the out box goes is Chapter 11. The out box only sits and waits this chapter.
- Never show a maybe being forced into a yes or no. Show the hand relaxing and reaching for a tag instead.
- Avoid any force or violence framing in visuals: no purge montage, no aggressive dumping. Tags are looped on calmly, things are set gently in a bin to wait.
- The friction-meter shot in Shot 9 must read as a deliberate HOLD, not a move and not a backslide. Keep the live needle exactly where Chapter 9 left it; the only new element is the faint dashed forward arrow. The GOAL crosshair stays ahead on the green side.
- Color and warmth should match Chapters 1 through 9: warm paper tones, terracotta for the tags, honey accents, slate for data overlays and the out-box path, green for the calm-dots and the keep path.
