# Teleprompter Script: Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

**Chapter 10, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** red-tags-holding-areas-and-sorters-remorse
**Length:** about 690 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come back to the entryway one more time.

You are almost done here.

And the last two things on your hands are the two you could not answer.

You do not use them, exactly.

But you cannot quite say to let them go.

They are not clutter and they are not keepers.

They are honest maybes.

There is also a quieter thing waiting.

A day after you let something go,

a small voice sometimes asks whether you should have.

It is the fear that one of them mattered.

So here is the mistake people make right here.

You think: I will just decide. I will be firm.

But a genuine maybe does not have an answer yet.

That is what makes it a maybe.

Pushing harder does not produce one.

It only produces a guess wearing the costume of a decision.

And a forced guess is how remorse is made.

Not from letting go.

But from letting go before you had an answer.

So stop trying to feel certain on a schedule.

You are allowed to not know.

A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove.

So here is the tool.

It is a tag, borrowed from the factory floor.

A red tag carries three things, and only three.

What it is.

The date you tagged it.

And a decide-by date.

A scrap of paper or a strip of tape will do.

Then you set the thing aside, out of the daily flow.

And it can only end two ways.

Reach for it before the decide-by date,

and it just proved it was necessary. Keep it. Untag it.

Let the date pass, and never reach for it,

and the maybe just became a no.

You are no longer choosing between forever and right now.

You are choosing to find out.

And time does the finding.

Now, the tagged thing needs somewhere to wait.

That somewhere is a holding area.

One known spot, out of the daily flow.

A labeled box, a bin, a reserved drawer.

And here is the whole game.

A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile.

A box of undated maybes decides nothing.

The deadline is the thing that forces an answer.

So match the date to the thing.

About a month for an everyday object.

A season for a seasonal one.

A snow shovel cannot prove itself in July.

And put the date where you will see it.

Because a deadline you do not honor is not a deadline.

It is a wish.

Let us tag the two maybes.

First, a good umbrella that was a gift.

You never grab it, but letting it go feels rude.

That flicker is why you cannot call it. So do not.

Tag it. Decide-by, one season.

Second, a folding poncho you used exactly once.

Might be handy. Might not.

Tag it. Decide-by, a month, or the next trip.

Two tags, two dates, not a single forced guess.

Now, the second thoughts.

They come in two kinds.

The first arrives before anything is gone.

What if I need it. What if the week I release it, it pours.

But notice: all of that is imagined.

And while a thing waits under a red tag, it is not gone.

It is one shelf away.

So the fear has nothing to grip.

The system lets you act while unsure.

The second kind is rarer, and real.

Sometimes the date passes and weeks later you miss it.

But look honestly at how often.

You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss.

Count the things you truly missed after clearing a home.

For most people the number is close to zero.

Now go look at the friction meter.

And this time, watch it not move.

Nothing new left the room today.

You gave the maybes a system. You quieted the fear.

That is real work. But it is not a clearing.

So the needle holds where Chapter 9 left it.

The meter only moves when the room does.

But look just ahead of the needle.

A faint dashed arrow, pointing a short way further toward calm.

That is the projection.

When the decide-by dates pass and the untouched tags convert,

the room gets lighter, and the needle takes that step.

Still inside the first S. Still short of the goal.

So here is your job.

Red-tag one honest maybe right now.

Write what it is and a decide-by date.

Stick it on, and set the thing out of the way.

Reach for it before the date, it stays.

If not, the decision already made itself.

You do not have to be sure about everything today.

Go tag one maybe.
