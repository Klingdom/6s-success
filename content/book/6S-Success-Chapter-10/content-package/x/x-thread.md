3/
The mistake here feels like discipline: just decide, be firm. But a genuine maybe has no answer yet. Force a keep and it quietly reopens next month. Force a release and remorse is made "not from letting go, but from letting go before you had an answer."

(approx. 251 chars)

---

4/
So stop trying to feel certain on a schedule.

"A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove."

You are allowed to not know. Uncertainty about one umbrella is accurate information, not a crack in your resolve.

(approx. 250 chars)

5/
The tool is borrowed from the factory floor. When something could not be judged on the spot, nobody guessed. They hung a red tag on it, set it in a marked area for a stretch of time, and let the days decide. It scales down to a home almost perfectly.

(approx. 249 chars)

---

6/
A red tag carries three lines, and only three: "what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date." A scrap of paper, a strip of tape. Neatness does not matter. Those three lines do.

(approx. 189 chars)

---

7/
Do not skip the middle line. The date you tagged it tells you the truth at a glance weeks later: this has waited a long time on a question I keep dodging, or this only went in last week. That small stamp of when keeps the whole area honest.

(approx. 237 chars)

---

8/
Then the tag decides for you, and it ends only two ways. Reach for the thing before its decide-by date and it proved it was necessary; untag it and keep it. Let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no. It joins the out box.

(approx. 234 chars)

---

9/
Notice what this does to the fear. You are no longer choosing between keeping forever and releasing right now, the impossible choice that froze you. "You are choosing to find out, and time does the finding." That is the whole trick.

(approx. 231 chars)

---

10/
A tagged thing needs somewhere to wait: a holding area. One known spot, out of the path you actually walk, where the red-tagged maybes sit out their deadlines together. A labeled box, a bin, a single drawer. Everything in it is wearing a date.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

11/
That last part is the whole game.

"A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile."

Undated maybes are not progress. They are the same indecision, moved into a tidier box and given a calmer name. The date is what forces an answer.

(approx. 244 chars)

---

12/
So match the date to the thing. Give an everyday object about a month, long enough for ordinary use to come around. Give a seasonal thing a season; a snow shovel cannot prove itself in July. Long enough to test it, short enough that it cannot become permanent.

(approx. 259 chars)

---

13/
One habit makes it work: keep the appointment. Put the dates where you will see them, and when one comes up, open the holding area and settle it. Reached for it? Keep it. Never touched it? Out box. Because "a deadline you do not honor is not a deadline; it is a wish."

(approx. 265 chars)

---

14/
Run it once. A good umbrella that was a gift: you never grab it, but releasing a gift feels rude. Do not cut that knot. Tag it. Decide-by: one season, because an umbrella needs weather to prove itself. Reach for it and it stays. If not, the gift moves on without guilt.

(approx. 266 chars)

---

15/
The second maybe: a folding poncho you used once. Might be handy, might not. You genuinely cannot say, so you do not. Tag it. Decide-by: a month, or the next trip. Two objects, two dates, not a single forced guess. Then you close the box and go make coffee.

(approx. 253 chars)

16/
Now the second thoughts. The first kind arrives before anything is gone: what if I need it? But under a red tag nothing has left. It is right there, dated, one shelf away. The fear has nothing to grip. You act while unsure, which is the only way anyone acts at all.

(approx. 261 chars)

17/
The second kind is rarer and real: the date passes and weeks later you miss it. It happens. But run the arithmetic honestly.

"You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss."

(approx. 220 chars)
