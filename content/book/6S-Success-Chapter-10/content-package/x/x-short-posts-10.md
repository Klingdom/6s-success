# X Short Posts: Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

Chapter 10 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Some things will not answer the keep-or-let-go question, and forcing a guess is how remorse gets made. So you do not force it. You tag it and let time answer. Chapter 10 of 6S Success: Home Edition builds a calm system for the honest maybes. Free online.

(approx. 251 chars)

---

2/
For the thing you cannot decide, do not reach for willpower.

"A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove."

You are allowed to not know. The system decides kindly, so you never have to feel sure on a schedule.

(approx. 253 chars)

---

3/
The tool is the red tag, borrowed from the factory floor. It carries three lines: "what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date." Set the tagged thing aside, out of the daily flow, and let time make the call for you.

(approx. 226 chars)

---

4/
Reach for a tagged thing before its decide-by date and it earned its keep; untag it. Let the date pass and you never touched it, and the maybe just became a no. Either way you never had to be sure today. Your own behavior answered the question.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

5/
The tagged maybes wait in a holding area: one spot, out of the way, everything in it wearing a date.

"A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile."

The date is the whole point. Without it, the maybe waits forever and nothing is decided.

(approx. 252 chars)

---

6/
How long? Match the date to the thing. About a month for an everyday object, long enough for ordinary use to come around. A season for a seasonal one, because a snow shovel cannot prove itself in July. Long enough to test it, short enough that it cannot stall.

(approx. 258 chars)

---

7/
The fear that arrives before anything is gone (what if I need it?) has a quiet cure. Under a red tag, nothing has actually left. It is right there, dated, one shelf away. You get to act while unsure, which is the only way anyone ever acts at all.

(approx. 243 chars)

---

8/
For the rarer fear, that you will truly miss a released thing, run the arithmetic honestly.

"You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss."

Most released things are cheap to replace if the rare need comes.

(approx. 257 chars)

---

9/
Keeping everything to guard against a small, rare regret guarantees the opposite cost: the daily weight of living among things you do not use, paid in space and attention every single day. The loud fear should not get to outvote the quiet math.

(approx. 241 chars)

---

10/
Try it: red-tag one honest maybe right now. On a slip of tape write what it is and a decide-by date, stick it on, and set the thing out of the way. Reach for it before the date and it stays. If not, the decision already made itself. Read the chapter free online.

(approx. 259 chars)
