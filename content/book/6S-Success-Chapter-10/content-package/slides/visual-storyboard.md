# Visual Storyboard

## Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 10. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

---

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_10_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper #F7F2E9: slide and card backgrounds
- Panel #FBF7EF: card fill, definition and idea boxes
- Ink #2B2622: primary text, object outlines
- Soft #6A625A: captions, secondary axis labels
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: the red tag itself, the chapter kicker
- Honey #DDA63A: secondary accent, the middle of the meter, the honest maybe, the anticipated what-if, the calendar mark
- Slate #3C5A6B: chapter labels, the out box, the unnecessary side, the GOAL flag
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: the keep outcome, the things a real morning proved necessary, the dashed forward projection on the meter
- Friction spark #CB4B36: the friction side of the meter only
- Ghost needle #B9AC96: the faint tick marks behind the held needle
- Rules and hairlines #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8: dividers, dashed lines
- Display font Fraunces: titles, pull quotes, the gate lines, the column headers
- Body font Newsreader: worked pass, captions, running text
- Label font Inter: uppercase, wide letter-spacing, for kickers, tags, axis labels

**The recurring motif.** A terracotta #BC4B2A red tag is the hero object of the chapter: three lines on a scrap of paper, tied to a thing that could not be called. Green calm-dot #6E8B5B marks the keep outcome, the maybe a real morning proved necessary. Slate #3C5A6B marks the out box, where an untouched tag sends the thing when its date passes. Honey #DDA63A stays warm and neutral, never a verdict: it carries the middle of the meter, the one honest maybe, the anticipated what-if, and the calendar mark. The red spark #CB4B36 belongs to the friction side of the meter only.

**Two fences to hold visually.** First, the out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination: never draw a trash can, a donation truck, or a sale table on it, because where it goes is Chapter 11. Second, the holding area is a time-boxed waiting room, not a storage scheme: it is a spot where dated maybes run out their clocks, not a place you teach where keepers should live.

## Beat 1 · The holding-area bin, tagged and dated

The chapter and both decks open the same way: a small holding-area bin on a low shelf, out of the daily path, holding the two red-tagged maybes.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm morning light. A small labeled bin on a low shelf holds two red-tagged items, a good umbrella and a folded rain poncho, each wearing a visible paper tag with a written decide-by date. Behind them, softened, the cleared console holds its five working things and the overflow basket sits empty.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, ink #2B2622 outlines, terracotta #BC4B2A on the two tags, a single green calm-dot #6E8B5B near the console. The tags are the quiet terracotta red of a decision postponed on purpose, not avoided.
- Motif: the clear calls are made; these are the honest maybes, given a tag, a date, and a home of their own to wait in.
- Caption line, from the book: "Two things you could not call, given a tag and a date instead of a guess. They wait here, out of the way, until time answers for you."

---

## Beat 2 · You cannot force a maybe

The mistake to head off before the tool arrives, given the book's pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove."
- Beneath it, a small Inter tag: "You are allowed to not know."
- Palette: no dots, no sparks. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the sentence you write on the fridge.
- Point: a genuine maybe does not have an answer yet, so forcing one only produces a guess wearing the costume of a decision. Honesty gets a system, not a shove.

## Beat 3 · The Red Tag gate

The chapter's hero device and the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: a single terracotta #BC4B2A red tag tied by string to an item (an umbrella handle). The tag shows three labeled lines in Inter: "what it is", "date tagged", and "decide-by", the third emphasized. From the tag, two paths branch.
- KEEP branch: a green #6E8B5B path curves up to a panel "reached for it before the date · keep it · untag," carrying a green calm-dot.
- OUT BOX branch: a slate #3C5A6B path curves down to a panel "date passed, never reached for it · the out box."
- Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "Three lines on the tag, two ways it can end."
- This is the clearest single frame of the red-tag logic. Everything else echoes it. Do not paraphrase the three lines "what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date," nor the outcome words "keep," "the out box."

## Beat 4 · The date you tagged it

The middle line, the one people skip, given its own quiet beat.

- Composition: a close crop of the red tag with the middle line, "date tagged," lit and emphasized, while "what it is" and "decide-by" sit softer above and below.
- Palette: terracotta #BC4B2A tag, ink #2B2622 lettering, a soft #6A625A gloss on the two flanking lines so the middle one carries the eye. Paper ground.
- Motif: the tagged date keeps the whole area honest. Weeks later it tells you at a glance whether a thing has waited a long time on a question you keep not answering, or only went in last week.
- Small Inter caption: "the line people skip, and the one that keeps the area honest."

## Beat 5 · The Holding Area on a timeline

Where tagged maybes wait, and the deadline as the trigger. Rebuild from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: a horizontal timeline. On the left, two terracotta-tagged items enter a labeled "holding area" box. The line runs right to a clear vertical marker, "decide-by date," the visual pivot of the whole figure.
- At the marker the path splits into two exits. The upper exit, green #6E8B5B, loops back with a label "reached for it · keep." The lower exit, slate #3C5A6B, continues to a small box labeled "the out box · on to Chapter 11."
- Palette: terracotta on the entering tags, small date labels in soft #6A625A under each, green on the keep exit, slate on the out-box exit. Paper ground.
- Caption idea, from the book: "The holding area is a waiting room, not a destination. The decide-by date is the trigger."
- Fence: the out-box exit is a plain set-aside box, never a bin or a donation truck.

## Beat 6 · A holding area without a deadline

The anti-junk-drawer line, given the pull-quote treatment. A pause beat, restrained.

- Composition: the book's pull-quote treatment. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile."
- Beneath, a small Inter tag: "6S TIP: the date is the whole point."
- Palette: no sparks, no dots. Slate does the work.
- Tone note: the deadline is what separates a holding area from a junk drawer, because it eventually forces an answer. Keep the frame plain, not scolding.

## Beat 7 · The red-tag pass on the entryway

The worked example, two tags, kept short. Use it as a copyable template.

- Layout: two terracotta #BC4B2A tag cards on paper ground, each showing three filled lines in Newsreader. Tag one: "the gift umbrella · date tagged: today · decide-by: one season." Tag two: "the folding poncho · date tagged: today · decide-by: a month or the next trip."
- Both tags point with soft slate arrows to a shared holding-area bin set out of the flow.
- Palette: terracotta on the tags, ink lettering, soft #6A625A on the dates. Paper ground, no verdict colors, because both are still waiting.
- Footer, Newsreader italic slate #3C5A6B: "Two objects, two tags, two dates, and not a single forced guess."
- Note: keep it two items only. The pass is short on purpose; the great majority of any real sort is easy yeses and easy noes.

## Beat 8 · Sorter's remorse, in two kinds

The second loose end, split into its two shapes so the fix for each is legible.

- Layout: one canvas split by a vertical dashed divider in #D9CDB8. Two panels, clearly labeled and clearly not the same problem.
- Left, "ANTICIPATED · before anything is gone": a honey #DDA63A thought-cloud of the what-if hovering over a red-tagged item still on the shelf, answered by a small line "the holding area: nothing has actually left."
- Right, "REAL · rarer than it feels": a slate #3C5A6B item genuinely gone, answered by "look honestly at how often."
- Palette: honey for the imagined fear on the left, slate for the real but rare case on the right, paper ground. Honey because the anticipated what-if is a waypoint, not a verdict.
- Motif: treating the two kinds the same is why the fear wins. The first is answered by the holding area, the second by an honest look at the odds.

## Beat 9 · You almost never regret what you let go

The anti-shame center of the remorse section, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the book's pull-quote treatment. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the reframe in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss."
- Beneath, a small Inter tag: "a large certain cost, traded for a small unlikely one."
- Palette: no sparks, no dots. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the room going quiet.
- Tone note: the point is permission, not shame. Keeping everything to guard against a small, rare, replaceable regret guarantees the daily, certain weight of things you do not use. Keep the frame light.

---

## Beat 10 · The needle holds

The landmark payoff, and the close of both decks. Rebuild from the HTML SVG, and make the HOLD unmistakable: the needle does not move, and a new dashed arrow projects the next step.

- Composition: the semicircular friction meter. The arc runs green #6E8B5B (CALM) on the left, honey #DDA63A in the middle, red spark #CB4B36 (FRICTION) on the right.
- The hold: the live needle sits exactly where Chapter 9 left it, a little past the honey middle toward calm, labeled HELD in ink #2B2622. It has not moved and does not budge; nothing new left the room, so a swing here would be flattery.
- The new element: just ahead of the live needle, a faint dashed green #6E8B5B arrow points a short distance further toward calm, marking the projected next step for when the decide-by dates pass and the untouched tags convert to the out box. It is dashed, not solid, because it has not happened yet. Faint ghost #B9AC96 tick marks sit behind the needle.
- The GOAL marker: a slate #3C5A6B crosshair on the calm side, still ahead of both the needle and the projection, carrying its slate PURPOSE flag.
- Footer caption, verbatim: "The needle holds. Nothing new left the room today; you gave the maybes a system and quieted the second thoughts, which is real work but not a clearing. The dashed arrow shows where it steps next, when the decide-by dates pass and the untouched tags convert. The meter only moves when the room does."
- Motif close: a deliberate HOLD with a forward projection, not a move and not a backslide. Honest, not triumphant. Still within the first S, still short of the goal.

## Carrying it across the set

- Open every sequence with the tagged holding-area bin; close every sequence with the friction meter holding, the dashed arrow projecting the next step. The two bookend the third Sort chapter.
- Use Fraunces only for the load-bearing lines: titles, the tag lines, the pull quotes, the column headers. Keep running copy in Newsreader so the slides breathe.
- Terracotta is the red tag and only the red tag; do not spend it elsewhere. Green is the keep outcome and the forward projection; slate is the out box and the unnecessary side; honey carries only neutral waypoints, the middle of the meter, the honest maybe, the anticipated what-if, and the calendar mark. The red spark is the friction side of the meter only.
- The frozen lines "what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date," "A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove," "A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile," and "You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss" appear on the tag, the holding-area figure, and the pull-quote cards. Copy them exactly.
- Keep generous paper margins and a single idea per frame. The chapter's voice is calm, concrete, and anti-shame; the visuals should feel the same, decided but never loud, with no force or hype imagery. Footer strip for the set: TAG IT · DATE IT · DECIDE BY.
