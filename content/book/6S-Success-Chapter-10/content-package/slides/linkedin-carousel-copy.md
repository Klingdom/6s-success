# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 10. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader. Footer strip: TAG IT · DATE IT · DECIDE BY.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**For the thing you honestly cannot decide, do not force it.**

You do not use it, exactly, but you cannot quite say to let it go. It is not clutter and it is not a keeper. It is an honest maybe.

Swipe for the gentlest tool in the book.

[VISUAL] A small labeled bin on a low shelf holding two red-tagged items, a good umbrella and a folded rain poncho, each wearing a terracotta tag with a written decide-by date. The cleared console behind, warm paper, slate chapter label.

---

### Slide 2

**A maybe is a decision you keep postponing.**

You set it apart rather than force a call you did not have. Good. That was the right move.

But apart is not a plan. The maybe needs a system.

[VISUAL] Two honey maybes held apart in a hand beside a plain slate out box that waits for a later chapter. A thin slate rule between them: "not the same problem, not the same tool."

---

### Slide 3

**You cannot force a maybe.**

Force a keep and it goes back on the shelf undecided in all but name. Force a release and you send off a thing you were not ready to let go.

That is how remorse is manufactured: not from letting go, but from letting go before you had an answer.

[VISUAL] One umbrella at a fork, both slate paths drawn strained and wince-marked: one loops back to a shelf, the other pushes to the out box. Honey caption: "a guess is not a decision."

---

### Slide 4

**A maybe is not a failure of nerve.**

It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove.

You were never lacking nerve. You were trying to supply, out of willpower, an answer only time can give. You are allowed to not know.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Small tag: "You are allowed to not know."

---

### Slide 5

**The red tag carries three lines, and only three.**

What it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date.

A scrap of paper or a strip of tape will do. Neatness does not matter. Those three lines do.

[VISUAL] A single terracotta red tag tied to an umbrella handle, three labeled lines "what it is", "date tagged", "decide-by" (the third emphasized), branching up to a green "keep" panel and down to a slate "the out box" panel.

---

### Slide 6

**Then time makes the call.**

Reach for it before the decide-by date and it proved necessary. Pull the tag and keep it, no second-guessing.

Let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no. It joins the out box.

[VISUAL] The two endings paired on paper. Fraunces line "You are choosing to find out, and time does the finding." A green calm-dot beside "reached for it: keep," a slate marker beside "date passed: the out box."

---

### Slide 7

**A tagged thing needs somewhere to wait.**

A holding area: one known spot out of the daily flow, a labeled box, a bin, a reserved drawer, where the tagged maybes sit out their deadlines together.

One spot, off the path you walk, everything in it wearing a date.

[VISUAL] The holding area on a timeline. Two terracotta-tagged items enter a "holding area" box, run to a "decide-by date" marker, then split: green "keep" up, slate "the out box · on to Chapter 11" down.

---

### Slide 8

**A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile.**

The date is what separates it from a junk drawer, because the date is the thing that eventually forces an answer.

Without it, the maybe waits forever and you have decided nothing.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Small tag: "6S TIP: the date is the whole point." No sparks or dots; slate does the work.

---

### Slide 9

**Match the date to the thing.**

About a month for an everyday object. A season for a seasonal one, because a snow shovel cannot prove itself in July.

Long enough for real life to test it. Short enough that it cannot become permanent by default.

[VISUAL] Two dated tag cards side by side, one "decide-by: about a month," one "decide-by: a season," with a small honey calendar mark between them: "keep the appointment."

---

### Slide 10

**The fear shows up before anything is even gone.**

What if I need it? What if the week I release it is the week it pours?

It is all imagined. And while a thing waits under a red tag, it is not gone. It is dated, retrievable, one shelf away.

[VISUAL] A honey what-if thought-cloud over a red-tagged item still on the shelf, answered by a small line: "the holding area: nothing has actually left."

---

### Slide 11

**You almost never regret what you let go.**

You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss.

Count the things you truly missed after clearing a home. The honest number is close to zero, and the few were cheap to replace.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The reframe set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. No sparks or dots; slate does the work. Small tag: "a large certain cost, traded for a small unlikely one."

---

### Slide 12 (soft CTA)

**You do not have to be sure today.**

Tag the maybes, give them a decide-by date, and let the one thing you cannot argue with, time, make the call for you.
