# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 10

**"Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across
all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single
warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for tension. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth,
real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the entryway drop zone. Chapter 8 cleared the console of what
did not belong; Chapter 9 opened the overflow basket and moved the unused to a set-aside out
box. This chapter handles only what Chapter 9 could not call: the honest maybes, given a red
tag and a decide-by date and set in a holding area to wait, and the second thoughts that
circle after letting go. Nothing is bought, cleaned, or routed anywhere; the out box simply
waits in its corner for a later chapter. Keep the tone calm and kind: a maybe is not a
failure of nerve, and postponing a decision on purpose is honest, not weak. Keep it calm and
non-coercive, no shame cues.

Separator discipline: any incidental label uses the middot, for example `CHAPTER 10 · SORT`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal
hyphen: red-tag, decide-by, out-box.

---

## 1. The holding-area shelf (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of a small labeled bin on a low shelf, set out of the entryway's
daily path, in early morning light. The bin holds exactly two red-tagged items standing
together: a good folding umbrella and a folded rain poncho. Each wears a visible small paper
tag, tied on with string, with a handwritten decide-by date showing, one reading a season,
one reading a month. Behind them, softened and a little out of focus, the cleared entryway
console holds its five working things in calm order, and the emptied overflow basket sits
open and bare on the floor. Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a
gentle slate blue wall behind, a single soft-green calm note near the console. Editorial
cookbook-style photography, shallow focus on the two tagged items and their dates. The mood
is a decision parked on purpose, kindly, to let time answer.

- Mood: calm postponement, honest maybes given a home and a deadline.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, a garbage bag
  or donation box or truck in frame, any sense of throwing away, dramatic mess, price tags.

## 2. The red tag, close up (the hero device)

A close still-life photograph of a single terracotta-red paper tag tied by string to an
umbrella handle, resting on a warm wood surface. The tag is hand-lettered with three short
lines, what it is, the date it was tagged, and a decide-by date, the last line clearly the
firmest. Faint honest wear on the handle. Warm side light, honey light on the wood, paper
cream background, ink-brown shadows, shallow focus on the tag's three lines. The image should
read as a simple, gentle tool, the gentlest in the book, the one that lets you not know yet.

- Mood: quiet clarity, a small paper promise that hands the question to time.
- Avoid: a factory or industrial look, harsh red, warning-label styling, clutter behind it,
  a brand-new catalog umbrella, harsh flash, showroom gloss, floating typeset text.

## 3. The two maybes in the holding area (the worked pass)

A warm editorial still-life of the two entryway maybes waiting side by side in a reserved bin
or single drawer, out of the daily flow: the gift umbrella and the folding poncho, each
wearing its red tag with a written decide-by date. Soft daylight, honey and paper tones,
ink-brown shadows, shallow depth on the two tags. Documentary and unstaged, as if the bin were
just eased open to check a date. One tag reads a season, the other a month. Nothing else in
the bin; the point is two honest maybes, dated and waiting, not a pile.

- Mood: a small, trustworthy system running quietly, no drama, no scolding.
- Avoid: an overfull bin or junk drawer, urgency, clutter drama, cold clinical light, any bin
  or giveaway pile in frame, a calendar of anxiety, shame cues.

## 4. The second thought, met gently (sorter's remorse)

A close, calm photograph of a hand resting on the lid of a labeled holding-area box, pausing
rather than pulling anything out, in soft warm light. The gesture is reassurance, not regret:
the thing under the tag is still right there, one shelf away, so there is nothing yet to
grieve. Warm paper cream background, honey light on the fingers, ink-brown shadows, a single
faint warm-red note only if a touch of tension is wanted, kept small. Editorial, tactile,
shallow focus shared between the hand and the box lid. The image should read as a fear
answered by an actual moment, not by an argument in the head.

- Mood: quiet reassurance, the fear before, met kindly and set down.
- Avoid: a frowning or anxious face, shame cues, a bin or donation box as the answer, harsh
  contrast, glossy retouching, a thing being thrown out, floating typeset text.

## 5. The needle holds (the friction meter, held with a forward projection)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge
dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the
right. The dark live needle sits a little past the honey middle toward calm, held in exactly
the position it reached last chapter, plainly not moved. Just ahead of it, a new faint dashed
arrow points a short distance further toward the calm side, marking the projected next step,
for when the decide-by dates pass and the untouched tags convert to the out box. A slate
crosshair labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag, sits fully on the green calm side,
still clearly ahead of both the live needle and the dashed projection. Warm paper cream
background, ink line work, consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest hold, real work done today but nothing new cleared, a step promised for
  when time does its part.
- Avoid: the live needle moved further this chapter, the needle swung all the way to calm, the
  needle sitting flat in the dead middle, the dashed arrow drawn solid or bright, anything
  reaching the goal crosshair, celebratory confetti, hype, neon color, any completion feel.
  Sort is not done, and five S's still remain.
