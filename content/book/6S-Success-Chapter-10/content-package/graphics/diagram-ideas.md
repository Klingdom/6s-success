# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 10

**"Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are
working specs, not finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in
`chapter_10_final.html`: the holding-area shelf opener, the red-tag close-up with two
outcomes, the holding area on a timeline, and the friction meter held with a dashed forward
projection.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`,
ink `#2B2622`, soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`,
calm green `#6E8B5B`, friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill
`#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter handles only the two leftovers from Chapter 9, the genuine maybes
and the second thoughts. It does not re-run the Use Test or re-litigate necessary vs.
unnecessary (Chapter 9), it does not route the out box or expired-tag items to a destination,
donate, sell, store, recycle, or throw away (Chapter 11), and it does not store keepers by
frequency of use (Chapter 13). The out box is always a set-aside spot that waits, never a
destination. The holding area is a temporary, time-boxed limbo, never a storage scheme.
Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 10 · SORT`, never a
floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Anti-shame throughout: not knowing is
normal, and the system decides kindly.

## 1. The Holding-Area Shelf (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's move in one image. The clear calls are made; these are the
honest maybes, given a tag, a date, and a home of their own to wait in. Mirrors Figure 1 in
the HTML.

**What it shows:** A low shelf, out of the daily path, holding a labeled bin with two
red-tagged items. The cleared console and the empty basket sit softened behind.

**Rough layout:**
- Warm dawn wash background, a floor line low in the frame.
- Center foreground: a wood-toned shelf holding a bin, a small panel label on the bin in
  terracotta sans caps: `HOLDING AREA`.
- In the bin, two objects standing together: the good gift umbrella and the folded rain
  poncho (a soft slate-blue rectangle). Each wears a small terracotta paper tag tied on with
  string, the tag reading `DECIDE-BY` over a written window, "one season" on the umbrella,
  "one month" on the poncho.
- Under each item a Newsreader italic label in soft gray: "the gift umbrella", "the folding
  poncho".
- Background, softened to about 40 percent: the cleared console holding its five working
  things, a small sans note "the console: still clear". Lower left, the empty overflow
  basket, note "the basket: empty now".
- Caption idea: "Two things you could not call, given a tag and a date instead of a guess.
  They wait here, out of the way, until time answers for you."

**Note for the illustrator:** the tags are the quiet terracotta red of a decision postponed
on purpose, not avoided. No bin, truck, or donation pile anywhere; the out box is not this
image's subject. Keep the light warm and the mood calm, a decision parked kindly.

## 2. The Red Tag, Close Up (the hero device)

**Purpose:** Make the hero device legible at a glance: three lines on the tag, two ways it
can end, time as the switch between them. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A single red tag tied by string to an item (an umbrella handle), showing
its three labeled lines, and two paths branching to the only two outcomes.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE RED TAG`.
- Left: a plain umbrella-handle glyph in ink line, labeled beneath in sans, soft gray: "one
  thing you could not call". A string runs up to the tag.
- Center-left: the terracotta tag itself, a notched card, carrying three stacked lines in a
  cream sans: "what it is", "date tagged", and "decide-by" (the third emphasized, largest and
  brightest). Thin divider rules between the lines.
- Middle: a Newsreader italic switch line in soft gray: "then time decides".
- Up-right branch, green: a green flow line and arrow to a green-bordered panel. Green sans
  caps tab `REACHED FOR IT IN TIME`, Fraunces ink "it earned its keep", Newsreader italic
  "untag it, it was necessary after all". A green calm-dot marks the tab.
- Down-right branch, slate: a slate flow line and arrow to a slate-bordered panel. Slate sans
  caps tab `DATE PASSED, UNTOUCHED`, Fraunces ink "the maybe became a no", Newsreader italic
  "it joins the out box".
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "No guess required. Time makes the call."

**Note for the illustrator:** exactly two exits, no third. The keep path is green and warm;
the out-box path is calm slate, never red or alarming. The out box is where an expired maybe
waits with the other unnecessary things; do not draw where it goes next.

## 3. The Holding Area on a Timeline (the deadline as trigger)

**Purpose:** Show the deadline as the trigger. Tagged things wait, the decide-by date
arrives, and it sends each thing to one of exactly two places. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A horizontal timeline from a holding-area box on the left to a decide-by
marker that is the visual pivot, then two exits.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE HOLDING AREA`.
- Left: a wood-toned holding-area box with a `HOLDING AREA` panel label in terracotta, two
  small red tag glyphs sitting inside it.
- A slate timeline runs right, with small tick marks and a Newsreader italic note above: "the
  maybes wait".
- Center: the pivot, a terracotta dashed vertical marker crowned with a solid terracotta chip
  reading `DECIDE-BY DATE`. This is the loudest structural element, the trigger.
- Upper exit, green: a curved line looping up to a green panel, sans tab `REACHED FOR IT`,
  Fraunces "back to keep", a green calm-dot.
- Lower exit, slate: a curved line down to a plain slate carton labeled `THE OUT BOX`, with a
  small Newsreader italic teaser beneath, soft gray: "on to Chapter 11". One line only, no
  disposal choices drawn.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "The date is the trigger. It sends each thing one of two
  ways."

**Note for the illustrator:** the decide-by marker is the pivot of the whole figure; give it
weight. Keep the two exits balanced and calm. The out box is a plain carton that waits, not a
truck, bin, or giveaway pile. The Chapter 11 teaser is a single label, not a routing diagram.

## 4. Two Kinds of Second Thought (the remorse reframe)

**Purpose:** Make the chapter's emotional move visible: sorter's remorse comes in two kinds,
so different that treating them the same is why the fear wins. Each kind gets its own answer.
This diagram has no direct HTML twin; it renders the "Sorter's Remorse" section as a picture.

**What it shows:** Two facing cards, one for the fear before and one for the regret after,
each paired with the exact tool that answers it.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `SORTER'S REMORSE`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath: "two
  kinds, two answers".
- Left card, honey header bar `ANTICIPATED, BEFORE`: a small thought-cloud glyph over an
  object, sans helper "the what-if, felt before anything is gone". Newsreader italic beneath:
  "nothing has actually left". The answer chip below, green-bordered: `THE HOLDING AREA`,
  small line "while it waits under a tag, it is still right there, one shelf away".
- Right card, slate header bar `REAL, AFTER`: a calendar-passed glyph, sans helper "the rare
  case you truly miss a released thing". Newsreader italic beneath: "count the ones you
  genuinely missed". The answer chip below, slate-bordered: `THE QUIET MATH`, small line "the
  honest number is close to zero, and the few were cheap to replace".
- Between the two, a slate rule and the Fraunces line, ink: "Neither is a reason to keep
  everything."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you
  will, and the fear is louder than the loss."

**Note for the illustrator:** keep it kind and matter-of-fact, never a scoreboard of failure.
No shame cues, no frowning faces. The footer line is verbatim from the manuscript; set it as
a quote. Do not overstate the "after" case; the whole point is that it is rare.

## 5. The Friction Meter, Held (the honest hold with a forward projection)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest reading. Nothing new left the room today, so
the live needle holds exactly where Chapter 9 left it; a new faint dashed arrow shows where it
will step when the decide-by dates pass and the untouched tags convert. Mirrors Figure 4 in
the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle held in place, plus a
dashed forward projection ahead of it toward calm, and the goal crosshair still further ahead.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark
  (right), on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- The live needle in ink, sitting a little past the honey middle toward calm, exactly where
  Chapter 9 left it, tagged in small sans caps `HELD`.
- A new faint dashed green arrow starting from the same hub, pointing a short distance
  further toward the calm side, tagged in small sans "next step, when dates pass". Muted, set
  to about 70 percent opacity so it clearly reads as projection, not the live reading.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still
  clearly ahead of both the live needle and the dashed projection.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The needle holds; the dashed arrow shows what is
  next."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a deliberate HOLD. The live needle must sit at
exactly the Chapter 9 position, not one step further and not flat in the middle. The only new
element versus Chapter 9 is the faint dashed forward arrow, which is a promise the tags have
already made, not a move that has happened, so it stays dashed and muted. Do not swing the
live needle to calm, do not move it further this chapter, and do not let anything reach the
goal crosshair. Still inside the first S, with the out box yet to route (Chapter 11) and the
other five S's still ahead.
