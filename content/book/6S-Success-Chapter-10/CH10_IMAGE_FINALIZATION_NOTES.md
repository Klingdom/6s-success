### The frozen spec is honoured exactly by the wired-in set

- **Hero device.** `4a` renders the red tag with precisely the three frozen lines: *what it is,
  the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date* (Item: gift umbrella / Tagged: Oct 4 / Decide by:
  Jan 4). The brief's wording and the plate's wording agree.
- **Worked example.** The frozen example is a **gift umbrella** (decide-by one season) and a
  **folding rain poncho** (decide-by a month). Plates 1a, 2a, 4a and 5a all use exactly those two
  items, with Oct 4 → Jan 4 for the umbrella and Oct 4 → Nov 4 for the poncho. One season and one
  month, correct in both cases.
- **Outcome vocabulary.** *red tag*, *holding area*, *decide-by date* and *out box* all appear
  verbatim in 2a, 5a, 6 and 7. The out box is correctly shown as "route next / route later",
  respecting the fence that routing belongs to Chapter 11.
- **Frozen framings.** `7` is titled *Holding Area or Nicer Junk Pile?*, which is the frozen line
  "a holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile" turned into a figure. `5a`
  renders the frozen two-outcome branch exactly: reached for it before the date → untag and keep;
  date passed untouched → the maybe becomes a no and joins the out box.
- **Deadline guidance.** `8` matches the frozen instruction to match the deadline to the item's
  natural cycle, about a month for everyday things and a season for seasonal ones.

## HELD BACK · 13 images

### A rival five-step system (7 plates)

`ch10-image12` through `ch10-image18` are a run of near-identical before/after plates, each
carrying a footer branding a **five-step system**: *Tag It · Date It · Review It · Decide It ·
Repeat It*, variously labelled "THE DAILY 5-STEP FLOW", "THE SYSTEM IN EVERY ROOM", "THE ONE
DRAWER FLOW", "THE 5-STEP HABIT FLOW" and "THE 5-STEP TRANSFORMATION".

**This is a canon problem, not merely a scope one.** The book's system is the six S's. These
plates present a competing five-step framework as *the* system, apply it to every room in the
house, and never mention the six S's at all. Repeated across seven figures it would read as the
book's actual method. Red-tagging is one tool inside Sort, not a rival methodology.

### Misapplied hero device

`ch10-image12` red-tags **every item in a working drawer** — pens, sticky notes, flashlight, lip
balm, a power bank — each with a decide-by date. Red tags are for the maybes you genuinely cannot
call, which then go to a holding area *out of the daily flow*. Tagging things you are actively
keeping and using inverts the tool and would be absurd in practice.

### Competing name for the frozen term

`ch10-image11` labels the bin **"DEADLINE DECISION ZONE"** with the strapline "TAG. DATE. REVIEW.
DECIDE." The frozen vocabulary is **holding area**, and this is the chapter whose job is to
establish that term. Introducing a second name for the same object here is exactly what the
frozen vocabulary list exists to prevent.

### Out of scope

`ch10-image13` (labelling systems) is Straighten and Standardize territory. `ch10-image15` and
`ch10-image18` are whole-closet and whole-home transformations. The frozen brief's scope
discipline section explicitly says not to pre-empt or re-run neighbouring chapters.

### Superseded originals (5 plates)

`ch10-image1` through `ch10-image5` (Jul 13 to 14) are superseded by the `a` variants, but not
uniformly, and two points are worth recording:

- **`ch10-image1`** labels the bin **"MAYBE"** rather than *holding area*, and uses generic
  contents (books, camera, scarf, helmet, racket) instead of the canonical umbrella and poncho.
  `1a` matches the frozen opener spec exactly, so `1a` was used.
- **`ch10-image2`** is in some respects the *better* rendering of the tag: its red tags carry
  explicit field labels **WHAT / DATE TAGGED / DECIDE-BY**, which mirrors the frozen wording more
  literally than `2a` does (2a's tags show only the item and the decide-by date, omitting the
  tagged date). However `ch10-image2` uses a camera and a camping lantern rather than the
  canonical items, and **prominently renders the Canon and Coleman brand logos**. The canonical
  worked example won. If `2a` is ever regenerated, adding the "Tagged:" line would make it
  perfect.
