## Hero device (frozen): The Red Tag
For an item you honestly cannot call, you do not force a guess. You tag it. A red tag carries three things:
> **what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date.**
The tagged item goes to a holding area, out of the daily flow. Then time answers for you:
- You reach for it before the decide-by date → it earned its keep. Untag it; it was necessary after all.
- The decide-by date passes and you never reached for it → the maybe just became a no. It joins the out box.
Frozen line: **A maybe is not a failure of nerve. It is honest, and honesty gets a system, not a shove.**

## The Holding Area (frozen framing)
The holding area is one known spot, out of the daily flow (a labeled box, a bin, a shelf), where red-tagged maybes wait for their deadline. The deadline is the whole point.
> Frozen: **A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile.**
Match the deadline to the item's natural cycle: about a month for everyday things, a season for seasonal ones. Without a date, the holding area is not a decision; it is indecision in a tidier box.

## Sorter's Remorse (frozen framing)
Two kinds of second thought:
1. **Anticipated remorse** ("what if I need it?"), felt before anything is even gone. The holding area is its cure: while a thing waits under a red tag, nothing is actually gone, so there is nothing yet to regret. The system lets you act without the fear having to be settled first.
2. **Real remorse**, the rare case where you truly miss a released thing. Frozen reframe: **You almost never regret what you let go. You fear you will, and the fear is louder than the loss.** Most released things are cheap to replace if the rare need comes, and keeping everything to avoid a small, rare regret guarantees the larger, daily cost of the clutter.

## Worked example (the entryway maybes)
Chapter 9 set aside "one or two truly undecidable things." Use two honest maybes from the entryway:
- **A good umbrella that was a gift** (you never grab it, but releasing a gift feels rude, so you cannot call it). Red-tag it, decide-by one season. If you reach for it when it rains, it earned its keep. If the season passes and you never do, the gift can move on without guilt.
- **A second folding rain poncho you used once** (might be handy, might not). Red-tag it, decide-by a month or the next trip. Reach for it and it stays; let the date pass and it joins the out box.
Both go to the holding area with their dates written on the tag. You made no forced guess; you set up the one judge that cannot be argued with, which is time.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> You do not have to be sure about everything today. Tag the maybes, give them a decide-by date, and let the one thing you cannot argue with, time, make the call for you.

## Definition box (frozen)
> Red-tagging is how you decide the things you cannot decide: label an uncertain item with a decide-by date, set it in a holding area out of the daily flow, and let the deadline answer. Reach for it in time and it earned its keep. Let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Red-tag one honest maybe right now. On a slip of tape write what it is and a decide-by date, stick it on, and set the thing in one spot out of the way. If you reach for it before the date, it stays. If not, the decision already made itself.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone red-tags one "I am not sure" thing with the same decide-by date, and you all agree not to argue about anyone else's tags until the date comes. Whatever no one reached for by then goes, no debate.
- **6S Tip:** A holding area without a deadline is just a nicer junk pile. The date is the whole point; without it, the maybe waits forever and you have decided nothing.
- **Common Mistake:** Letting the holding area become permanent. If you never set the decide-by date or never honor it, you have not made a decision, you have only moved the indecision into a tidier box.
