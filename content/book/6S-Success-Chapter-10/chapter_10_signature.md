## Hero device: The Red Tag
For an item you honestly cannot call, you do not force a guess. You tag it. A red tag carries what it is, the date you tagged it, and a decide-by date. The tagged item goes to a holding area, out of the daily flow, and time answers: reach for it before the decide-by date and it earned its keep (untag it, it was necessary after all); let the date pass untouched and the maybe just became a no (it joins the out box). This is a real, home-scaled version of the Lean 5S/6S red-tag practice: a tag on a questionable item, moved to a red-tag area for a set time. Reference lightly, do not overclaim.

## Motifs
- **You cannot force a maybe.** Forcing a guess on a genuinely uncertain thing is how a pass stalls or breeds regret. Let time decide instead.
- **The deadline is the whole point.** A holding area without a decide-by date is not a decision; it is indecision in a tidier box.
- **Nothing is gone yet.** While a thing waits under a red tag, nothing has actually left, so anticipated remorse has nothing to grip.
- **The needle holds.** The maybes were set aside last chapter; today you gave them a system. Real work, but nothing new left the room, so the meter is honest and holds.

## Emotional purpose
Permission not to be sure. Uncertainty is normal, not weakness. The reader is protected from the two failure modes of a maybe: forcing a false call, and letting the maybe stall the whole pass. The system decides kindly, and the fear that guards clutter is named and gently deflated.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to red-tag an honest maybe (what it is, date tagged, decide-by date), set it in a time-boxed holding area, match the deadline to the item's natural cycle (about a month for everyday things, a season for seasonal ones), and honor the date. Handle anticipated remorse with the holding area and real remorse with the reframe. Do NOT route the out box, do NOT teach storage by frequency, do NOT re-teach the Use Test.
