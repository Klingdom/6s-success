# Chapter 49 Signature: The Stair Landing

The Part 9 recurring close. The friction meter is retired (it reached GOAL at Chapter 30); each room playbook ends instead on a matched before-and-after room signature: the before kept per Chapter 6, the after held as the ideal-state standard per Chapter 25.

## The before-and-after room signature
Stand at the bottom and shoot up the flight. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, with whatever is on the treads left exactly where it is, because a box on step four is the most honest thing in the picture. Then work the three zones and take the after from the same spot.

## The variation: a check you stand in rather than a photograph
This is **the only Part 9 close whose extra element is a position rather than an image.** After the pair is taken, you run a check: stand at the bottom in the dark and look up at the top step, then stand at the top and look down at the bottom.

The reason is specific to this space. Every other room's standard is something you can look at. This one's is something you have to stand in, because the two positions that matter on a staircase are the two nobody occupies on purpose. From anywhere else the flight looks fine, which is exactly the problem: the fall does not happen from anywhere else.

It also catches the one fault that photographs will not. A dead bulb over the top step is invisible from the landing, because you are standing under it, and invisible from halfway up, because you are looking at your feet. The before-and-after pair, shot from the bottom in ordinary light, will not show it either. Only the check does.

Compare with the variations used so far: Ch 39 the child takes the after photo; Ch 40 the after is the nightly safe state; Ch 43 the after contains one detail that expires; Ch 44 the after is taped inside a drawer and matched nightly; Ch 45 the after keeps working as a diagnostic floor; Ch 46 the after is judged on time-to-first-cut; Ch 47 the after is judged on the floor strip; Ch 48 a third photograph is added. **This one is the only close that is not a photograph at all.**

## The before-and-after caption (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> This is the stair landing the whole loop was for: the same flight you started with, every tread bare from nose to riser including the bottom three, the runner flat and fixed, the rail clear from newel to newel, both ends lit, and a console holding three things and a basket that sleeps empty. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard. Then do what this room asks: stand at the bottom in the dark and look up at the top step, and stand at the top and look down at the bottom, because that is the view the fall would have.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> A staircase is not safe because it looks tidy. It is safe because nothing obstructs the passage, the hand that needs the rail, or the light that shows the step. And it stays safe because the reasonable sentence, I will take it up next time I go, was given a basket that empties every night instead of being argued with. Do one zone this week, and make it the flight itself, because bare treads and working light at both ends set the limit on everything else this landing is allowed to hold.
