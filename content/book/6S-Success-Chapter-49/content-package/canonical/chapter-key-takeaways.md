# Chapter 49 Key Takeaways: The Stair Landing

- A stair landing is not a room, it is a route, and the only space in the book nobody ever stops in.
- So it is judged by whether a person can pass through it at night with their arms full, not by what it holds.
- The correct amount of storage on a staircase is very nearly none.
- A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it. A stair hazard waits in the dark for somebody thinking about something else.
- Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task. It is the thing you do on the way to the task.
- Start with the flight even though it looks least cluttered, because bare treads and working light set the limit on everything else.
- The trap is not laziness, it is a reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go. It is true in principle and false in practice.
- You do not defeat that sentence, you give it a container. One basket, emptied nightly whether it is full or not.
- If an item rides that basket three nights running, it has no home on the destination floor.
- The handrail is one of the most touched and least washed surfaces in the house. Wipe it end to end weekly, underside included.
- Vacuum the crevice where each tread meets its riser, because that trapped grit is what polishes a runner slick.
- A dead bulb over the top step is the most dangerous thing this room can hold, and it is only visible from the bottom of the flight looking up in the dark.
- A runner that has lifted once has already told you what it will do again. Refix it properly or take it up, but do not press it down and wait.
