# Chapter 49 Summary: The Stair Landing

The nineteenth room playbook of Part Nine, the shortest chapter in the run, and the only space in the book that is not a room. A stair landing is a route. Nobody sits down here, nobody works here, and nobody keeps anything here on purpose. It exists to be passed through, several times a day, by every person in the house, which means it is judged by an entirely different question: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it. The honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

That would make it the simplest chapter in the book except for one thing. This is also the place where a small oversight stops being untidiness and becomes a real injury, and the chapter is precise about why that differs from the workshop. A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it: you are holding the tool, your care is switched on. A stair hazard waits in the dark for somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over. Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task.

The room breaks into three zones, the fewest in Part Nine, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the stair and floor path, the landing surface or console, and the wall and display zone. The flight comes first even though it looks least cluttered, because bare treads and working light set the limit on what the other two may hold.

On top of the nine rules of Shine it adds four: this is a route rather than a room; the hazard here arrives unattended; the trap is a reasonable sentence, "I will take it up next time I go", which needs a container rather than an argument; and the handrail is the most touched and least washed surface in the house.
