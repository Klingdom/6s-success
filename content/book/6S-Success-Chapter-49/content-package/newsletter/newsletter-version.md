# Chapter 49 Newsletter: The Stair Landing

**Subject:** The only space in the book that is not a room
**Preview:** A route, not a room, and judged by passage rather than contents.

---

This is the second-to-last chapter, and it is about the only space in the book that is not a room.

A stair landing is a route. Nobody sits down here, nobody works here, nobody keeps anything here on purpose. It exists to be passed through, several times a day, by every person in the house. Which means it is judged by an entirely different question from every other space you have worked: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it.

And the honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

That would make this the simplest chapter in the book, except for one thing. This is also the place where a small oversight stops being untidiness and becomes a real injury.

**The workshop was dangerous too, and the difference is worth being precise about.** A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it. You are holding the tool. You are looking at the blade. Your care is switched on. A stair hazard does the exact opposite. It waits, in the dark, for somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over, at the end of a long day.

Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task. It is the thing you do on the way to the task.

**And the thing that fills this space is not laziness.** It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go. Every person in your house says it, several times a day, and each time it is true in principle and false in practice. That single sentence is how a landing console fills, and how the bottom three steps quietly become a shelf.

So do not try to defeat it. Give it a container instead: one basket, on the floor at the end nearest the top step, emptied every night whether it is full or not.

**One thing to do tonight.** Stand at the bottom of the flight in the dark and look up at the top step. A dead bulb there is the single most dangerous thing this room can hold, and it is only visible from that one position.

Do one zone this week, and make it the flight itself.
