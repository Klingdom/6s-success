# Chapter 49 Facebook Longform Post: The Stair Landing

---

This one is about the only space in your house that is not a room.

A stair landing is a route. Nobody sits down there. Nobody works there. Nobody keeps anything there on purpose. It exists to be passed through, several times a day, by everyone in the house.

Which means it gets judged by a completely different question: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it. And the honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

That would make it simple, except for one thing. It is also the place where a small oversight stops being untidiness and becomes a real injury.

Here is the difference that matters, and it is worth sitting with. A workshop is dangerous, but a workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it. You are holding the tool. You are looking at the blade. Your care is switched on.

A stair hazard does the opposite. It waits. In the dark. For somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over, at the end of a long day.

Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task. It is the thing you do on the way to the task.

Now, the thing that actually fills this space. It is not laziness. It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go.

Everyone in your house says it, several times a day, and every single time it is true in principle and false in practice. That one sentence is how a landing console fills, and it is how the bottom three steps quietly become a shelf.

So do not try to defeat it, because you will not. Give it a container instead. One basket, on the floor at the end nearest the top step, emptied every night whether it is full or not. That is the only version of the promise that survives a busy week.

And one thing to go and do tonight. Stand at the bottom of your stairs in the dark and look up at the top step. A dead bulb up there is the most dangerous thing a staircase can hold, and you cannot see it from anywhere else, because from the landing you are standing under it and from halfway up you are looking at your feet.
