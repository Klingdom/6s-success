# Chapter 49 LinkedIn Posts: The Stair Landing

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. Not a room, a route
A stair landing is the only space in this book that is not a room. Nobody sits down there, nobody works there, nobody keeps anything there on purpose.

Which means it is judged by a completely different question: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it. And the honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

## 2. Attended and unattended hazards
A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it. You are holding the tool, you are looking at the blade, your care is switched on.

A stair hazard does the opposite. It waits, in the dark, for somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over, at the end of a long day.

## 3. A staircase is not a task
Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task. It is the thing you do on the way to the task. That is exactly why the small oversights there matter so much more than they would anywhere else in your house.

## 4. The trap is a reasonable sentence
It is not laziness. It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go.

Everyone in your house says it, several times a day, and every time it is true in principle and false in practice. That single sentence is how a landing console fills and how the bottom three steps become a shelf.

## 5. Give it a basket, not an argument
You will not defeat that sentence, so do not try. Give it a container instead. One basket, on the floor at the end nearest the top step, emptied every night whether it is full or not. It is the only version of that promise that survives a busy week.

## 6. The three-nights test
And here is the rule that settles the rest. If an item rides that basket three nights running without landing anywhere, it is not waiting for a trip. It has no home on the destination floor. Give it one tonight, or route it out of the house.

## 7. The most touched, least washed surface
The handrail is one of the most touched and least washed surfaces in the whole house. Every hand that has ever used those stairs has left something on it, and almost nobody wipes it, because it does not look dirty the way a counter looks dirty. End to end, every week, underside included.

## 8. The bulb you cannot see
A dead bulb over the top step is the most dangerous thing a staircase can hold, and it is nearly invisible. Not from the landing, because you are standing under it. Not from halfway up, because you are looking at your feet. Only from the bottom, looking up, in the dark, which is a position nobody stands in deliberately.

## 9. The runner that lifted once
If a corner has lifted and you pressed it back down and carried on, that is the wrong shape of decision. A runner that has lifted once has already told you what it will do again. Refix it along its whole length, or take it up entirely. Bare wood you know is slippery beats a runner you have quietly stopped trusting.

## 10. The view the fall would have
"Stand at the bottom in the dark and look up at the top step, and stand at the top and look down at the bottom, because that is the view the fall would have."
