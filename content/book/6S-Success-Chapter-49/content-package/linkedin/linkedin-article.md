# The Stair Landing: A Route, Not a Room

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 49 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

This is the second-to-last chapter of the book, and it is about the only space in it that is not a room.

> The stair landing is not a room. It is a route, and it is the only space in this book that nobody ever stops in. Every other space you have worked is judged by what it holds; this one is judged solely by whether a person can pass through it at night, with their arms full, without anything going wrong. Which means the correct amount of storage here is very nearly none.

## Why the stakes differ from the workshop

> A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it: you are holding the tool, you are looking at the blade, you know what you are doing is sharp. A stair hazard does the opposite. It waits, in the dark, for somebody who is thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over, at the end of a long day.

## The trap is a sentence, not a habit

> The trap in this room is not laziness. It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go. Every person in your house says it, several times a day, and each time it is true in principle and false in practice.

So you do not argue with it. You give it a container: one basket, emptied every night whether it is full or not.

## And the surface nobody washes

> The handrail is one of the most touched and least washed surfaces in the whole house.
