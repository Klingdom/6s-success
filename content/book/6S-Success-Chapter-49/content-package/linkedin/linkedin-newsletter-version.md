# Chapter 49 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Stair Landing

**Subject:** The only space in the book that is not a room

A stair landing is a route. Nobody sits down there, nobody works there, nobody keeps anything there on purpose. It exists to be passed through, several times a day, by every person in the house.

Which means it is judged by a completely different question from every other space: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it. And the honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

That would make this the simplest chapter in the book, except for one thing. It is also the place where a small oversight stops being untidiness and becomes a real injury.

**The workshop was dangerous too, and the difference matters.** A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it: you are holding the tool, your care is switched on. A stair hazard waits, in the dark, for somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over. Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task.

**And the trap here is not laziness.** It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go. Everyone says it, several times a day, and every time it is true in principle and false in practice. So you do not argue with it. You give it a container: one basket, emptied every night whether it is full or not.

Do one zone this week, and make it the flight itself, because bare treads and working light at both ends set the limit on everything else.
