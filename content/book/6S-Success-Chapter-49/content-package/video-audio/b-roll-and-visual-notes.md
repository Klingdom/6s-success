# Chapter 49 B-Roll and Visual Notes: The Stair Landing

## Must-get shots
- The flight shot from the bottom looking up, with things on the treads. Hold on it.
- The same shot after clearing. The two frames are the chapter.
- A hand wiping the underside of the handrail. This is the shot most viewers will not have seen before.
- The vacuum crevice nozzle going into the join where a tread meets its riser.
- A coat being lifted off a newel post, with the usable length of rail visibly extending as it goes.
- The bottom three steps being cleared, one item at a time.
- A basket on the landing floor, then the same basket being carried up as a hand reaches for the light switch.
- The same basket returned, empty.
- A frame being press-tested: a hand pushing up and out on it.
- The flight lit at both ends, shot in genuine low light so the difference reads.

## Do not shoot
- **Anyone standing on a tread to reach a frame, a bulb, or a light fitting.** If height is needed, the ladder is footed on the flat landing and that must be visible in frame.
- Anyone carrying a load they cannot see over on a staircase, even to illustrate the hazard.
- A fall, a stumble, or a near-miss of any kind, staged or otherwise.
- Any readable brand name, logo, or label text.
- Any repair in progress: no runner being fixed, no bracket being tightened, no bulb being wired.

## Tone
Calm, and quieter than the other room films. This is the shortest chapter with the highest stakes, so the footage should be unhurried and the low-light sequence should be genuinely dim rather than stylised.
