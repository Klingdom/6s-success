# Chapter 49 YouTube Script: The Stair Landing

*Six to eight minutes.*

---

**[Cold open, shot up a flight from the bottom, things on the treads]**

This is the only space in your house that is not a room. And it is the one where a small oversight becomes a real injury.

**[Title]**

**[Section 1: a route]**

A stair landing is a route. Nobody sits down here, nobody works here, nobody keeps anything here on purpose. It exists to be passed through, several times a day, by everyone in the house.

So it gets judged by a completely different question: not what does it hold, but can a person get through it. And the honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

**[Section 2: attended and unattended]**

We did the workshop three chapters ago, and that room was genuinely dangerous. But the difference matters.

A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it. You are holding the tool. You are looking at the blade. Your care is switched on.

A stair hazard waits. In the dark. For somebody thinking about something else entirely, carrying a basket they cannot see over, at the end of a long day.

Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task. It is the thing you do on the way to the task.

**[Section 3: the reasonable sentence]**

Now, what actually fills a landing. It is not laziness.

It is a completely reasonable sentence: I will take it up next time I go.

Everyone in your house says it, several times a day, and every time it is true in principle and false in practice. Nobody is lying. The trip was going to happen. It just did not happen with that thing in hand.

That one sentence is how a console fills and how the bottom three steps become a shelf.

So do not try to defeat it. Give it a container. One basket, at the end nearest the top step, emptied every night whether it is full or not. And if something rides that basket three nights running, it has no home on the destination floor.

**[Section 4: two cleaning things]**

Two things almost nobody does. The handrail is one of the most touched and least washed surfaces in the house, so wipe it end to end every week including the underside, which is the part carrying the oil.

And vacuum right into the crevice where each tread meets its riser. That trapped grit is what polishes a runner smooth and slick under a stocking foot.

**[Section 5: the bulb]**

And the one to go and do tonight. Stand at the bottom of your flight in the dark and look up at the top step.

A dead bulb there is the most dangerous thing a staircase can hold, and it is nearly invisible. Not from the landing, you are under it. Not from halfway up, you are looking at your feet. Only from the bottom looking up, in the dark, which is a position nobody stands in deliberately.

**[Close]**

That is how this chapter ends, and it is the only close in the book that is a check rather than a photograph. Stand at the bottom in the dark and look up, then stand at the top and look down, because that is the view the fall would have.

Do one zone this week, and make it the flight. Chapter 49. Next: the patio, and the last chapter of the book.
