# Chapter 49 Final Package Summary: The Stair Landing

**Chapter 49, The Stair Landing.** Part Nine, the nineteenth of twenty room playbooks. Three zones, 4,985 manuscript words, 56 package files plus four source files.

## What this chapter is

The only space in the book that is not a room. A stair landing is a route: nobody sits there, nobody works there, nobody keeps anything there on purpose, so it is judged by whether a person can get through it rather than by what it holds. The honest answer to how much storage belongs on a staircase is very nearly none.

That would make it the simplest chapter in the book, except that it is also where a small oversight stops being untidiness and becomes a real injury.

## The layer, four rules added to the nine rules of Shine

1. **A route, not a room.** Judged by passage, not by contents.
2. **The hazard waits, unattended.** A workshop hazard hurts you while you are paying attention to it; a stair hazard waits in the dark for somebody thinking about something else. Nobody approaches a staircase carefully, because a staircase is not a task.
3. **The trap is a reasonable sentence.** "I will take it up next time I go" is true in principle and false in practice, every time. Give it a basket, not an argument, and apply the three-nights test to anything that will not land.
4. **Most touched, least washed.** The handrail, end to end, weekly, underside included.

## The zones
1. Stair and floor path (first, though it looks least cluttered, because bare treads and light set the limit on the other two)
2. Landing surface or console
3. Wall and display zone

- **The only Part Nine close that is not a photograph**: a check you stand in, from the bottom in the dark looking up and from the top looking down.

- **Zone order departs from the Manual's listing**, following the Manual's own where-to-start tip, and the departure is stated in the chapter text.

## The process lesson from this chapter
A refinement to the Part Nine length model was proposed at the brief stage, fitted to Chapters 45 to 48, and adopted. It predicted 4,650 words; the chapter landed at 4,985, an error of +7.2%. The original model, `3,600 + 460 per zone`, predicted 4,980, an error of +0.1%, and is now five-for-five within -0.6% to +3.4%.

The refinement had been fitted to the very sample that produced it. **Do not re-fit a model on its own sample, and do not adopt a refinement until an out-of-sample point has tested it.** Recorded in the brief, the editorial review, and the Part Nine superprompt.

A second finding: per-zone words rose to 587 even though step count was correctly set from the Manual's surface count. The steps themselves are longer, 59 words each against Chapter 48's 51. **For Chapter 50, budget words per step, not just steps per zone.**
