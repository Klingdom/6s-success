# Chapter 50 Summary: The Patio or Deck

The twentieth and final room playbook of Part Nine, and the last chapter of the book.

The patio is the only room in the book with no roof, and that single fact inverts everything. Every other space is somewhere you keep the weather out of; this is the only one where the weather is the reason you are there. You cannot solve it by excluding conditions, only by deciding what can survive them.

It is also the only room with a clock running. Indoors, a postponed decision costs you convenience and the thing waits for you. Out here it does not wait: sun makes plastic brittle, damp gets into fabric and end grain, and a season of putting things off arrives in spring as a replacement cost.

One rule settles the whole space, and it comes straight out of the Micro Zone Manual unchanged: if it cannot take a night of rain, it belongs behind a door.

The room breaks into six zones and 46 shine surfaces, the highest surface count of any six-zone room in the book. The surface, rail, and safety zone comes first even though the Manual lists it last, because the Manual's own where-to-start tip says so and because a wobbling rail post does not get safer once the cushions are clean.

This is also where the oldest idea in the book arrives at its most serious use. Chapter 16 introduced Shine as cleaning that inspects. Out here the thing you are cleaning is the thing holding you up, and you cannot see a soft board from a chair; you feel it through a deck brush.

It is the only room where all five safety questions answer at full strength, which is not a coincidence: a space with no roof has no boundary doing quiet work on its behalf.

And it closes the book. The chapter's after photograph goes beside the very first photograph the reader took, back in Chapter 6, and it is the only standard in the book that visibly ages. Weather will undo part of it, which is not a failure of the standard but the reason there is one.
