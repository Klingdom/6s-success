# Chapter 50 Reuse Risk Check: The Patio or Deck

What could go wrong when this chapter's material is cut up and republished, and what stops it.

## Highest risk in this chapter: the repair boundary

This is the chapter with the most things a reader could hurt themselves attempting, and it names every one of them without teaching any of them. The fence has four parts, stated in the README, the frontmatter, the b-roll notes, the image prompts, and the repurposing map:

1. **No structural or carpentry instruction.** The chapter tests a board with a screwdriver, reaches a verdict, and stops. "Replacing one board is a morning's work" is a sizing statement, not a method, and no asset may extend it into one.
2. **No gas-fitting instruction, ever.** The soapy-water leak test is a standard check and stays. **The verdict on bubbles is always replace the hose, never tighten it.** The Manual's own phrasing, "not tighten it and hope", is carried verbatim for a reason and must not be softened in any asset.
3. **No electrical instruction.** Outdoor-rated cord on a socket with a residual current device is a specification, not a wiring guide.
4. **No pressure washing, sealing, staining, or refinishing.** The chapter does not mention them at all. That silence is deliberate, because pressure washing destroys soft wood and the reader coming from "scrub the boards" is one search away from it. **B-roll must not show a pressure washer**, since footage reads as endorsement even with no words attached.

**The pressure to breach fence one is the strongest in the book.** A reader just told their board is rotten will want to know how to fix it. The chapter's answer, and the answer of every derived asset, is the verdict plus the professional boundary: soft in one spot, replace it; soft in two adjacent spots or at the wall, stop putting people on it and get someone underneath it with a torch.

## Second risk: the Chapter 6 callback out of context

The close asks the reader to put this photograph beside the first one they took fifty chapters ago. **That only works for someone who has read the book.** In a standalone social asset it is either meaningless or, worse, reads as an instruction to a reader who never took a first photo.

Every standalone asset that carries the callback presents it as an invitation rather than an instruction, and the discussion questions gate it with "if you have read the whole book". Keep that gating.

And keep the qualifier: **the difference between the two images is "not tidiness, it is that you can now read a room."** Without it the callback is nostalgia and the chapter's argument disappears.

## Third risk: the standard-that-ages line read as permission

"Weather will undo part of it, and that is not a failure of the standard" is the chapter's last idea and its most quotable. Detached, it can be heard as permission to let the patio go, which is the opposite of the point.

It is always paired with the instruction that follows it in the same frozen string: do one zone this week, and make it the boards and the rail. **Keep the pairing in every asset.**

## Fourth risk: the grate scraper misread as an addition

The scraper is the only input in the book that exists to **replace** something rather than add to it. An asset that lists it alongside a wire brush, or that shows a wire brush in use, actively creates the hazard the type was catalogued to remove. **No wire grill brush appears in any asset, in copy or in image.**

## Fifth risk: chemicals

Garden concentrates are the most dangerous items in the chapter and the handling is narrow: they stay sealed in their original containers, they are never decanted, they are never mixed, an unreadable label means the container is finished, and disposal is always household hazardous waste collection and **never a drain**. Nothing in the package explains what any specific concentrate does, which is deliberate.

The pellets-look-like-food-to-a-dog hazard is stated. **No veterinary advice follows it and none may be added.** Likewise the swallowed bristle is called a genuine emergency and nothing further; **no medical content anywhere.**

## Sixth risk: depiction

- **Never depict anyone under a raised structure**, at any time, for any reason.
- Never depict a repair in progress: no board being replaced, no rail refixed, no hose or regulator worked on.
- Never depict a lit grill being cleaned, or any flame in a cleaning sequence.
- Never depict a child near the deck box hinge, even to illustrate the crush hazard.
- Chemical containers are shot from behind or out of focus, so no label is readable.

## Standing fences, held

- Product **types** only, never brand names. Thirty-two types, four new.
- No invented prices, no fall or injury statistics.
- No horticultural advice. Pots are containers here, not a growing guide.
- The kit stays mostly brushes; padding it with chemistry contradicts the chapter.

## One thing unique to this chapter

**It hands off to nothing.** Any asset that ends on a next-chapter tease is wrong, because there is no next chapter. The series close points backward to Chapter 6 and outward to the reader's own next room.

## Verdict
Cleared for reuse subject to the four-part repair boundary, the Chapter 6 gating, the standard-that-ages pairing, and the no-wire-brush rule, all of which are written into the package rather than held here.
