# Chapter 50 Newsletter: The Patio or Deck

**Subject:** The last room, and it has no roof
**Preview:** The only room you are not defending against the weather but visiting it in.

---

There is one room left, and it has no roof.

That sounds like a small difference. It is not. Every other space in this book is somewhere you keep the weather out of. You have spent forty-nine chapters working on the inside of a boundary, and the whole time that boundary was doing quiet work on your behalf. It meant that a thing you put down and forgot about was, six months later, still more or less the thing you put down.

Out here none of that is true.

**The patio is the only room where the weather is not something you keep out.** It is the reason you are there. Nobody sits outside in spite of the conditions; the whole point is that it puts you in them. So the strategy that worked everywhere else, control the environment and then arrange things inside it, does not work here at all. You cannot make it stop raining on the deck box. What you can do is decide, with your eyes open, what is allowed to live in those conditions.

**And it is the only room with a clock running.** Indoors, the cable you never ruled on is still a cable: it annoyed you, and it will be exactly as good on the day you finally decide as it was on the day you did not. The cost of postponing was irritation. Out here, the cushion you meant to bring in is not the same cushion in April. Sun makes plastic brittle, damp gets into fabric and end grain, and a season of putting things off arrives in spring as a bill.

**So one question settles the whole space.** Not is this useful, not do I love it, not have I used it this year. Can it take a night of rain? A cushion cannot. A cardboard box inside the deck box cannot. Each is fine for a week, and that is precisely the trap.

**Start with the boards**, on a dry morning, before you touch a single cushion. If the structure is not sound then everything you arrange on top of it is decoration.

**One thing to do today.** Push the top rail hard at every post, outward and then down. Ninety seconds. Any movement, any softness, any proud screw head is a stop and fix today.

Chapter 50, and the last chapter of 6S Success: Home Edition.
