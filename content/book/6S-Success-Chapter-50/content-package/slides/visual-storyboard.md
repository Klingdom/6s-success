# Chapter 50 Visual Storyboard: The Patio or Deck

Four figures, matching the chapter's SVGs. Warm paper, terracotta, honey, slate, green. Types, not brands.

## Figure 1: the opener, two states
A full-width pair shot from the back door. Left, the patio as it really is: a folded lounger across the route to the steps, cushions under a towel that is visibly darker underneath, saucers standing full of water, a deck box with its lid half open in the rain, green film on the shaded treads. Right, the same patio: boards bare with daylight showing through the gaps, table clear but for the umbrella and one weighted tray, chairs tucked under, deck box closed flat and latched.

## Figure 2: the six zones
A warm plan view with the house wall along one edge. **Zone one is drawn differently from the other five:** it is the whole floor plane and the rail line, tinted, with the other five zones sitting as panels on top of it. That single drawing carries the chapter's argument about order. Footer line: the boards come first, because everything else is arranged on top of them.

## Figure 3: the method and the kit
Two panels. Left, the four rules of the patio layer with a gloss under each. Right, the kit, which is mostly brushes and should read that way at a glance: a long-handled deck brush, a stiff scrubbing brush, a detail brush, a bristle-free grate scraper, a hose, gloves, and only two liquids. Footer line: outdoors is agitation and rinsing, not chemistry.

## Figure 4: the before-and-after signature, and the first photograph
A matched pair shot from the back door with the camera icon between them. **Below the pair, connected by a thin honey line, a smaller and slightly faded panel labelled "and the first photograph you ever took", with the subline "fifty chapters ago, of a room you could not yet read."** The after caption reads "the after, and the standard that ages". This is the only figure in the book that references a figure from another chapter, and the only close that reaches back to Chapter 6.
