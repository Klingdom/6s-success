# The Patio: A Room With No Roof, and a Clock

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 50 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the final chapter.*

There is one room left, and it has no roof.

That sounds like a small difference. It is not.

> The patio is the only room in this book where the weather is not something you keep out. It is the reason you are there. So you do not win this room by excluding conditions, you win it by deciding what can survive them, and everything that cannot take a night of rain belongs behind a door.

## What a boundary was doing for you

Every other space is one you work on the inside of a boundary, and the boundary meant a thing you put down and forgot about was, six months later, still more or less the thing you put down.

> **The clock:** the rate at which outdoor conditions turn a postponed decision into a replacement cost. Indoors a delayed decision costs you convenience, and the thing waits for you. Out here it does not wait.

## Start with the structure

> Start with the boards and the railing on a dry morning, before you touch a single cushion, because if the structure is not sound then everything you arrange on top of it is decoration.

## And the oldest idea in the book, arriving at its most serious use

> You will never find a popped fastener, a soft board end, or a spider web in a burner tube while sitting in a chair. You find them with a brush in your hand and your face close to the thing. This is the last chapter of the book, and it is the one where the oldest idea in it finally carries your whole weight: cleaning is not tidying. It is how you read what you are standing on.
