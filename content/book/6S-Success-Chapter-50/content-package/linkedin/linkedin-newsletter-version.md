# Chapter 50 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Patio or Deck

**Subject:** The last room, and it has no roof

Every other space in this book is somewhere you keep the weather out of. You spend forty-nine chapters working on the inside of a boundary, and the whole time that boundary is doing quiet work on your behalf: a thing you put down and forgot about is, six months later, still more or less the thing you put down.

Out here none of that is true.

**The patio is the only room where the weather is not something you keep out. It is the reason you are there.** Nobody sits outside in spite of the conditions. So the strategy that worked everywhere else, control the environment and then arrange things inside it, does not work at all. You cannot make it stop raining on the deck box.

**And it is the only room with a clock running.** Indoors, a decision you postpone costs you convenience and the thing waits for you. Out here it does not wait: sun makes plastic brittle, damp gets into fabric and end grain, and a season of putting things off arrives in spring as a replacement cost.

**One question settles the whole space.** Not is this useful, not do I love it. Can it take a night of rain? A cushion cannot. A cardboard box inside the deck box cannot. Each is fine for a week, and that is exactly the trap.

**Start with the boards**, on a dry morning, before you touch a single cushion. If the structure is not sound then everything you arrange on top of it is decoration.

And this is where the oldest idea in the book gets serious. Chapter 16 said cleaning is inspection. Out here the thing you are cleaning is the thing holding you up, and you cannot see a soft board from a chair.

Chapter 50, and the last chapter of 6S Success: Home Edition.
