# Chapter 50 YouTube Script: The Patio or Deck

*Six to eight minutes. The last film in the series.*

---

**[Cold open, shot from the back door across a cluttered deck]**

There is one room left. And it has no roof.

**[Title]**

**[Section 1: what a boundary was doing]**

Every other space in this book is somewhere you keep the weather out of. And the whole time, that boundary was doing quiet work for you. It meant a thing you put down and forgot about was, six months later, still more or less the thing you put down.

Out here, none of that is true. The patio is the only room where the weather is not something you keep out. It is the reason you are there.

Which means the approach that worked everywhere else, control the environment and then arrange things inside it, does not work at all. You cannot make it stop raining on the deck box.

**[Section 2: the clock]**

Indoors, the cable you never ruled on is still a cable. It annoyed you, and it will be exactly as good on the day you finally decide as it was on the day you did not. The cost of postponing was irritation.

Out here, the cushion you meant to bring in is not the same cushion in April. The resin chair that has stood through six summers of sun is not the chair you bought. It looks like it, which is the dangerous part.

Sun makes plastic brittle. Damp gets into fabric and end grain. A season of putting things off arrives in spring as a bill.

**[Section 3: the question]**

So one question settles the whole space. Not is this useful, not do I love it. Can it take a night of rain?

A cushion cannot. A cardboard box inside the deck box cannot. A bag of charcoal on the ground cannot.

And the reason that question works with other people, when almost nothing else does, is that the answers are not arguable.

**[Section 4: start with the boards]**

Not the cushions. The boards and the railing, on a dry morning.

Push the top rail hard at every post, outward and then down. Ninety seconds. Any movement, any softness, any proud screw head is a stop and fix today.

Then rake the gaps between the boards clear until you can see daylight through them. A deck almost never rots from the top down; it rots from the end grain outward, in a gap you cannot see into.

And scrub the algae off the shaded treads, which is the slipperiest surface on the property after rain.

Because if the structure is not sound, everything you arrange on top of it is decoration.

**[Section 5: the oldest idea]**

Chapter 16 said cleaning is inspection. It was true in every room since.

But out here, the thing you are cleaning is the thing holding you up. You will never find a popped fastener or a soft board end while sitting in a chair. You find them with a brush in your hand and your face close to the thing.

You cannot see a soft board from a chair. You can feel it through a deck brush.

**[Close]**

And one last thing, which only this room could teach you.

This is the only room whose standard visibly ages. By August the algae is back and the gaps have packed again. If you thought done was a destination, that feels like failure.

It is not. It is the clock, which was always running in every other room too, just quietly enough to ignore. You never finished any of them. You renewed them.

So take the after photograph. Then find the very first photograph you took, back in Chapter 6, of a room you had not yet learned how to read, and put them side by side.

The difference between them is not tidiness.

Fifty chapters. Twenty rooms. Go and push the rail.
