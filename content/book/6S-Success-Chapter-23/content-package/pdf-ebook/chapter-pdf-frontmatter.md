# Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

**Part Seven, Standardize**
Chapter 23 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

Subtitle · Everything you have built so far lives in your head and your hands, which means it lasts exactly as long as your memory and your energy do. Standardize is how the best way stops depending on you: you write it down. A standard is not a rule carved in stone, it is the best way you know today, held in place only until you find a better one.

---

## Chapter descriptor

The first chapter of Part Seven, and Standardize opens here, the fifth of the six S's. Four S's have made the home sorted, arranged, kept clean, and safe, and this chapter names the quiet problem underneath all of it: almost all of that lives in exactly one place, the person who runs the home, so it lasts only as long as their memory and energy and quietly stops the day they are sick, away, or tired. That is the gap the fifth S closes, and this chapter delivers the concept that answers it and the first written standard. You meet the drift reframe, that everything you have built lives in your head and your hands and a standard is how a good way of doing something stops depending on you, and you learn why gains drift back, not from carelessness but because a way that lives in one head is invisible, so everyone does it their own way and the best way is a secret, which is the blunt job the people on the factory floors of Toyota gave this S: prevent reversion, by making the best way visible and shared. Then the chapter takes the cold out of the word. A standard is not bureaucracy but a recipe, the best way you know to make something turn out right, written down so it turns out right for anyone, even on a tired day, a gift that takes skill out of one person's hands and gives it to anyone who can read; and the definition asks not for length or formality but only that the way be the best you currently know, written plainly, and followable by anyone. Then comes the most important word in the title, today, the living-standard heart: a standard is not the last word but the current best word, a starting point not a finish line, rewritten without ceremony the moment a better way is found, so it is the thing that makes improvement stick rather than a cage. Then the chapter puts it in your hands: pick one small recurring task, watch yourself do it once and write the best way in a few plain steps, put it where the work happens, and use the shortcut of asking the person who does it best to teach it. The friction meter holds and does not move, because nothing in the rooms changed, but for the first time a catch appears behind the needle, a ratchet that keeps a gain from sliding back, before the hand-off to Chapter 24 and the tools that carry standards.

## How to read this chapter

This is the first chapter of Part Seven, Standardize, and it opens the fifth S. It picks up from a home that four S's have already made sorted (Sort), arranged (Straighten), kept clean and read (Shine), and safe (Safety, Chapters 20 to 22), and it uses all of that without re-teaching any of it. It opens no new way to tidy and runs no new hunt. It is about the one move none of the earlier S's made: taking the good ways you have already found and writing them down, so they stop living only in your head and stop drifting back the moment your attention moves elsewhere. Read it through once for the idea, then do the one small, concrete thing it asks: write a single plain standard for a recurring task, and put it where the work happens.

The chapter rests on a quiet, honest observation. The good you have built lives in one place, and that place is you, so it lasts exactly as long as your memory and your energy do and quietly stops the day you are sick, away, or out of patience. Things drift back not because anyone is careless but because a way that lives only in one head is invisible to everyone else, so everyone else does it their own way, and their own way is rarely the way you worked out; multiply that by every task and every person, and the drift is not a moral failing, it is just what happens when the best way is a secret. A standard is how you make the best way visible, so following it becomes the easy, obvious thing. And the word to keep in mind throughout is warm: a standard is not a rule anyone is enforcing, it is a recipe, a gift, the best way you know written down so it turns out right for anyone, even on a tired day, and measured not by how thorough it looks but by whether the next person actually follows it and gets the result you would have gotten. The most important word in the title is today: a standard is the current best word, not the last word, rewritten without ceremony the moment someone finds a better way, a starting point and not a cage.

A word on the scope fence, so the chapter stays the thing that opens Standardize rather than drifting into the rest of the part or the S that follows. It writes one plain standard as an example, and it deliberately does not build the tools that carry standards, checklists so nothing is forgotten, schedules so recurring work has a time, and responsibilities so every standard has a name, which are Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities. It does not build the visual and pictorial standards, the pictures of the ideal state that show a result rather than describe it, which are Chapter 25. It does not build the long-term adherence, audit, and habit-as-a-system work that keeps everyone doing all of it over years, which is Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30; it may note that a standard becomes habit through repetition, but it does not build that system. And it does not re-open Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety; a one-line callback is fine, but the earlier S's already cleared, arranged, cleaned, and made safe the home this chapter works on. Because writing down the best way changes nothing in the rooms, the friction meter holds exactly where Safety left it and does not move, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. But for the first time the dial shows something new, behind the needle rather than ahead of it: a catch, a ratchet, holding it against the pull back toward friction. Every gain until now was quietly reversible, a sorted room re-clutters, a clean surface re-soils, a good habit lapses; a written standard is the first thing that blocks the backslide. The needle did not advance today; it got something arguably more valuable, it stopped being able to retreat. The goal is still ahead on the calm side, because Standardize has only begun and Sustain still follows. The register throughout is warm, human, and freeing, never corporate: a standard is a gift to the household, not a compliance demand, and there is no shame in a home whose good ways have always lived in one head, because that is the normal case.

If you only do one thing: pick one recurring task that mostly works only when you do it, loading the dishwasher, the bedtime routine, taking out the recycling, watch yourself do it once, write the best way in a few plain steps, and stick it where the task happens. That one card, taped inside the cupboard door where the next person cannot avoid seeing it, is the smallest possible proof of the whole chapter, and it is the move that makes everything you have built capable of lasting. A standard is the best way you know today, written down, and it is how the gains last.
