## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
Four S's have made the home sorted, arranged, clean, and safe, and yet almost all of that lives in exactly one place: you. You know the best way to load the dishwasher, the order of the bedtime routine, the trick that keeps mornings calm, and none of it was ever written down, so the home works beautifully right up until the day you are sick, away, or tired, and then it quietly stops. That is the gap the fifth S closes. Things drift back not because anyone is careless but because a way that lives only in one head is invisible to everyone else, so everyone does it their own way and the best way stays a secret (the drift reframe); the people who first named these steps, on the factory floors of Toyota, gave this S a blunt job, prevent reversion back to the old way, done not through willpower or nagging but by making the good way visible and shared. The chapter's hero is that a standard is a recipe: not bureaucracy, but the best way you know to make something turn out right, written down so it turns out right for anyone, even on a tired day (the recipe reframe), a gift that takes skill out of one person's hands and makes it available to anyone who can read. The word doing the quiet work is today: a standard is not the last word, it is the current best word, rewritten without ceremony the moment a better way is found, a starting point not a finish line, the thing that makes improvement stick rather than a cage (the today reframe and the living-standard line). Then the chapter walks the reader through writing one plain standard for a recurring task, capture the way you already use, watch yourself do it once and write the steps in plain words (most tasks are three to six steps; step eleven means you are describing personal quirks), and put the standard where the task happens, not in a folder, with the shortcut of asking the person who already does it best to teach it. The friction meter HOLDS and does not move, because writing down the best way changes nothing in the rooms, but for the first time the dial shows something new behind the needle, a catch, a ratchet, holding it against the pull back toward friction; every gain until now was quietly reversible (a sorted room re-clutters, a clean surface re-soils, a habit lapses), and a written standard is the first thing that holds a gain in place, so the needle did not advance, it stopped being able to retreat. The goal stays ahead on the calm side, because Standardize has only begun (Chapters 24 and 25 remain) and Sustain still follows. Hands off to Chapter 24 · Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities, which turns standards into their tools.
