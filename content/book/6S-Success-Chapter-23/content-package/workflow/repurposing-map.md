# Chapter 23 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- Four S's have made the home sorted, arranged, clean, and safe, and yet almost all of that lives in exactly one place: you; so the home works beautifully right up until the day you are sick, away, or tired, and then it quietly stops. Standardize closes that gap by writing the best way down
- The drift reframe: everything you have built so far lives in your head and your hands, which means it lasts exactly as long as your memory and your energy do; a standard is how a good way of doing something stops depending on you
- Things drift back not from carelessness but because a way that lives only in one head is invisible to everyone else, so everyone does it their own way and the best way is a secret; the source's blunt job for this S, prevent reversion back to the old way, done by making the good way visible and shared, not through willpower or nagging
- The recipe reframe (the hero): a standard is not bureaucracy, it is a recipe, the best way you know to make something turn out right, written down so it turns out right every time, for anyone, even on a tired day; a recipe is a gift that takes skill out of one person's hands and makes it available to anyone who can read
- The definition, frozen: a standard is the current best way to do a recurring task, written down plainly enough that anyone in the home can follow it and get the same result, and kept open enough that it is rewritten the moment a better way is found; measured by use, not thoroughness, usually a few plain lines about one task, and if nobody reads it, it has failed
- The today reframe (the living-standard heart): the word doing the quiet work is today; a standard is not the last word, it is the current best word, and the moment someone finds a better way, the standard is not broken, it is simply what you improve; a starting point, not a finish line, and the thing that makes improvement stick rather than a cage; the living-standard line, frozen: a standard is not a rule carved in stone, it is the best way you have found so far, held in place only until you find a better one, and then rewritten without ceremony
- Write your first standard: pick a small recurring task that mostly works only when you do it, watch yourself do it once and write the steps in plain words (most tasks are three to six steps; step eleven means you are describing personal quirks), and put it where the task happens, not in a folder, so the next person meets it at the moment they need it; the shortcut, ask the person who does it best to teach it, because the best way usually already exists, fully formed, in somebody's hands, and a standard is often just the act of making one person's good habit into everyone's
- The rule, frozen (hero): a standard is the best way you know today, written down; the One Idea to Keep, it takes the good you have built out of your head and your hands and puts it somewhere it can hold without you, be followed by anyone, and be improved by everyone, and it is how the gains last
- The friction meter HOLDS and does NOT move, because writing down the best way changes nothing in the rooms; but for the first time the dial shows something new behind the needle, a catch, a ratchet, holding it against the pull back toward friction; every gain until now was quietly reversible (a sorted room re-clutters, a clean surface re-soils, a habit lapses), and a written standard is the first thing that holds a gain in place; the needle did not advance, it stopped being able to retreat; the goal stays ahead because Standardize has only begun and Sustain still follows

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the first chapter of Part Seven and it OPENS Standardize, the fifth S, with the concept of a standard and one written standard. It USES the finished home the first four S's built (sorted, arranged, clean, safe) but does not re-open any of them. Do not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety (Chapters 16 to 22); Standardize is a new layer over the same home, the act of writing the best way down so it holds without you.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities): the standard TOOLS belong to the next chapter, checklists so nothing is forgotten, schedules so recurring work has a time, and responsibilities so every standard has a name (assign accountability, integrate into daily work). Chapter 23 writes ONE plain standard as an example; do NOT build the checklist, schedule, or responsibility system here.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 25, Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards): the visual and pictorial standards (pictures of the ideal state) belong to Chapter 25. Chapter 23 keeps the standard a plain written card; do NOT build visual standards here.
- CRITICAL fence (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30, later S): the long-term adherence, audits, and habit-as-a-system belong to Sustain. Chapter 23 may note in a line that a standard becomes habit through repetition, but does NOT build the adherence or audit system. Standardize opens the fifth S; Sustain is the sixth.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home whose good ways live only in one person's head is the normal case, not a failing; things drift back not from carelessness but because a way that lives in one head is invisible, so the best way stays a secret. Keep it warm, relief and a gift, not lecture; the weight of being the only one who knows how the home works, finally set down.
- A standard is a gift, not a compliance demand: the danger word is corporate, so keep every asset human, warm, and freeing, a recipe and a shared gift, never rigid or controlling. No force or control metaphors (no enforce, comply, discipline the household, lock down), no hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "a dishwasher", "a note inside the cupboard door", or "a cupboard", not a brand); teach the concept, the definition, and the write-one-standard steps, not a shopping list.
- The source's Principles of the Standardize Step (prevent reversion back to the old way, remember that it takes repetitive actions for something to become habit) and the first of the Steps to Complete Standardize (document standards for Sort, Straighten and Shine activities) are drawn from the 6S source, home-scaled into the drift reframe, the recipe, and writing one standard, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to standard work and Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed. The remaining Standardize steps map onto Chapters 24 and 25.
- The friction-meter reading is specific to Ch 23: it HOLDS and does NOT move, because writing down the best way changes nothing in the rooms, so the needle stays exactly where Safety left it (four of six S's done, past the SAFETY COMPLETE milestone). For the first time the dial shows something new BEHIND the needle, a catch, a ratchet, holding it against the pull back toward friction, with a faint barred arrow marking the backslide the standard now blocks. Every gain until now was quietly reversible (a sorted room re-clutters, a clean surface re-soils, a habit lapses); a written standard is the first thing that holds a gain in place, so the needle did not advance, it stopped being able to retreat, which is arguably more valuable than a step. This is a HOLD with a NEW anti-backslide motif, NOT a move and NOT a milestone; no new marker on the dial, and the goal stays ahead on the green calm side, because Standardize has only begun and Sustain still follows.
- Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical; point syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium) back to it.
- Keep the framing plain: "write the best way down", "a standard is a recipe", "the best way you know today", "prevent reversion", "put it where the work happens", "rewritten without ceremony the moment a better way is found". A standard is the best way you know today, written down, and it is how the gains last.
