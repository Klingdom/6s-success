# Chapter 23 Calls to Action: Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

This is the first chapter of Part Seven, and it opens the fifth S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea: pick one recurring task that only really works when you do it, write down the best way in a few plain steps, and stick it where the task happens. The CTAs match the chapter's tone, warm and freeing, not corporate: a home whose good ways live only in one person's head is the normal case, and a standard is a gift to the household, not a compliance demand or a rule to be defended. The move costs about five minutes now, because the value is lifting the good you have built out of one head and putting it where anyone can reach it.

Write one standard today. ## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick one recurring thing that only really works when you do it, loading the dishwasher, the bedtime routine, taking out the recycling, and write down the best way in five plain steps. Stick it where the task happens. One standard, today.
- **Family Challenge:** Ask the person who does a task best to teach it to everyone else, and write down what they do. The best way usually already lives in someone's hands; a standard just makes it everyone's.
- **6S Tip:** Write the standard where the work happens, not in a binder. A note taped inside the cupboard door beats a perfect document nobody opens. The best standard is the one you cannot avoid seeing.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: pick one recurring task, write the best way in a few plain steps, and stick it where the task happens. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in about five minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of warmth and relief. A home whose good ways live only in one person's head is the normal case; a standard is a gift to the household, not a compliance demand.
- The rule (a standard is the best way you know today, written down), the recipe reframe (a standard is not bureaucracy, it is a recipe), and the living-standard line (a standard is not a rule carved in stone) are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid corporate framing, hype, and any force or control language (no enforce, comply, lock down). A standard is warm and freeing, a recipe and a shared gift, never rigid or controlling. Avoid brand names.
- Scope note: this opens Standardize with the concept and one written standard; it does not build the checklist, schedule, and responsibility system (Chapter 24), the visual and pictorial standards (Chapter 25), or the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30), and it does not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety. Four of six S's are done, and the goal is still ahead because Standardize has only begun and Sustain follows.
