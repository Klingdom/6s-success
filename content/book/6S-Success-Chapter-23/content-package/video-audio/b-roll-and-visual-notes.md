# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

**Chapter 23, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** standardize-the-best-way-we-know-today
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero is not a room being changed but knowledge changing where it lives. Where the last part's chapters changed the state of the rooms, this one changes nothing in the rooms and instead moves the best way out of one head and onto the walls. Show the whole warm home split gently by an idea rather than a hard line. On one side, a single person stands under a large, full thought-bubble crammed with how everything is done, which drawer holds what, how the dishwasher gets loaded, the order of the bedtime routine, while two family members stand nearby looking slightly unsure, hands a little helpless, because all of it is in that one head. On the other side, the same home, but now small slate cards have appeared at the points where the work happens: a card taped inside a cupboard door, a card by the dishwasher, a card at the launch pad by the door, each holding a few plain steps, and now everyone is at ease and doing things. Nothing changed about the rooms. What changed is that the knowledge came out of one head and onto the walls, where it belongs to everyone. The register is warm, human, and freeing: a standard is a gift and a relief, not bureaucracy.
- The rule carries the whole chapter, frozen, render verbatim: "A standard is the best way you know today, written down." The definition box is also frozen: "A standard is the current best way to do a recurring task, written down plainly enough that anyone in the home can follow it and get the same result, and kept open enough that it is rewritten the moment a better way is found." The living-standard line is frozen: "A standard is not a rule carved in stone. It is the best way you have found so far, held in place only until you find a better one, and then rewritten without ceremony."
- The three reframes are the spine of the chapter, each frozen. DRIFT, frozen, render verbatim: "Everything you have built so far lives in your head and your hands, which means it lasts exactly as long as your memory and your energy do. A standard is how a good way of doing something stops depending on you." RECIPE, frozen, render verbatim: "A standard is not bureaucracy. It is a recipe: the best way you know to make something turn out right, written down so it turns out right every time, for anyone, even on a tired day." TODAY, frozen, render verbatim: "The word in the title doing the quiet work is today. A standard is not the last word, it is the current best word, and the moment someone finds a better way, the standard is not broken. It is simply what you improve."
- The starting point is the home from the end of Part Six: cleared, arranged, kept clean on a light rhythm, and made safe. Four of the six S's, done. It is a genuinely good home. This chapter does NOT re-clean it, re-tidy it, or re-run any earlier S; it does NOT build the checklist, schedule, and responsibility system (Chapter 24), the visual and pictorial standards (Chapter 25), or the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain). Show the good home, and then the one uncomfortable fact that means the work is not done: almost all of it lives in exactly one place, and that place is you, so it works beautifully right up until the day you are sick, away, or tired, and then it quietly stops.
- The reason things drift back, drawn not as carelessness. A way of doing something that lives only in one person's head is invisible to everyone else, so everyone else does it their own way, and their own way is rarely the way you worked out. Show the mixing bowls put in the low cupboard for a good reason, then someone who does not know the reason putting them back on the counter, undoing the system while genuinely trying to help. Multiply that by every task and every person. It is not a moral failing; it is just what happens when the best way is a secret. Grounded in source, referenced lightly: the people who first named these steps, on the factory floors of Toyota, gave this S a blunt job, prevent reversion back to the old way, by making the good way visible and shared, not through willpower or nagging.
- The hero is that a standard is a recipe. Deflate the cold word "standard", which sounds like rules, clipboards, and someone checking whether you complied, and replace it with an actual recipe card in a kitchen: not a rule anyone is enforcing but a gift, someone worked out how to make the thing turn out well and wrote it down, so it comes out right whether you are sharp or half asleep. A recipe takes skill that lived in one person's hands and makes it available to anyone who can read, and that is exactly what a home standard does for the recurring tasks of a home. A standard is measured by use, not thoroughness: a few plain lines about one task, and the test is whether the next person actually follows it and gets the result you would have gotten.
- The living-standard heart is the word today. Show that today keeps a standard from becoming the very cage people fear. A standard is a starting point, not a finish line: it captures the best way you know as of today so you are not forever reinventing it, and it stays fully open to being replaced the moment someone works out something better. When the teenager finds a faster way to fold the laundry, the better way wins and you spend thirty seconds updating the card. Draw the living-standard LOOP: a gentle circle of arrows, not a rigid flowchart, with stops "The best way you know today", "Write it down (the standard)", "Everyone follows it", "Someone finds a better way", "Rewrite the card", curving back to a fresh "The best way you know today", a small note in the center reading "a standard is meant to be improved". The word today is what keeps the loop turning, so a standard captures improvement instead of freezing it out.
- Writing your first standard is small and human. Pick a single recurring task that mostly works only when you do it, not the hardest thing in the home but the opposite, something small and ordinary you have quietly become the keeper of, loading the dishwasher, the morning launch-pad routine, taking out the recycling. Capture the way you already use, watch yourself do it once and write the steps in plain words, as few as will do the job, three to six steps, and if you reach step eleven you are describing your personal quirks, not the task. Then put the standard where the task happens, not in a folder, taped inside the cabinet above the dishwasher, so the next person meets it at the moment they need it. And the shortcut: if someone already does a task best, ask them to show you and write down what they do. The best way usually already exists, fully formed, in somebody's hands.
- The sample standard card is the portable tool. A single warm card drawn like a recipe card or a note you would tape up: a plain title (for example "Loading the Dishwasher"), four or five short numbered steps in plain language, and a small line at the bottom reading "Lives: inside the cabinet above the dishwasher", with a little curled piece of tape at one corner. Warm paper card, slate text, one honey accent on the title. This is not a document; it is a recipe card for a recurring task, and it took about five minutes to make.
- The friction meter HOLDS, and for the first time cannot slide back. The live needle sits exactly where Chapter 22's SAFETY COMPLETE milestone left it, unmoved, resting at the fourth marked tick; it has not advanced, because nothing in the rooms changed and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. What is new is a small slate catch or ratchet drawn behind the needle, near the pivot, and a faint dashed arrow pointing back toward the FRICTION side barred with a small block, showing the backslide the standard now prevents. Every gain until now was quietly reversible; a written standard is the first thing that blocks the backslide. The Safety-complete, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, and Sort-complete milestones sit as small passed markers. The GOAL crosshair, tagged "purpose", stays ahead on the green calm side, because Standardize has only begun and Sustain still follows. This is NOT a move and NOT a milestone: it is a HOLD with a new catch, the first thing that keeps a gain from sliding back.
- Warm daylight, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look. Slate cards at the points of work, a soft honey accent on card titles, one terracotta accent on "Someone finds a better way" in the loop. The tone is warm and freeing: a standard is a gift to the household and a relief, the weight of being the only one who knows how the home works, set down.
- Keep the whole thing warm, human, and anti-shame, in a register of relief. A home whose good ways live only in one person's head is the normal case, not a failing. No corporate or compliance framing, no force or control words (no enforce, comply, lock down), no hype, no brand names (say "a fire extinguisher", "a dishwasher", not a brand). A standard is a recipe and a shared gift, never rigid or controlling. Four of the six S's are done, and the goal is still ahead because Standardize has only begun.
