# Teleprompter Script: Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

**Chapter 23, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** standardize-the-best-way-we-know-today
**Length:** about 740 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the first chapter of Standardize.

The fifth S. A turn in the book.

For the last twenty-two chapters you changed the state of your rooms.

Today, you change nothing in the rooms at all.

Stand back and look at what you built.

Cleared. Arranged. Kept clean. Made safe.

Four of the six S's, done. A genuinely good home.

Now notice something uncomfortable.

Almost all of it lives in exactly one place. And that place is you.

You know which drawer things go back into, and why.

You know the best way to load the dishwasher.

The order of the bedtime routine. The trick with the launch pad.

Nobody wrote it down. You did not need to. You simply know it.

So the home works beautifully.

Right up until the day you are sick, or away, or tired.

And then it quietly stops.

Because the person it was all running on is not available.

That is the gap the fifth S closes.

And here is the good news.

Standardize is not about adding rules.

It is about writing the good ways down, so they stop depending on you.

It is more of a relief than it sounds.

You get to set down the weight of being the only one who knows.

Start with a worry that runs under the whole book.

Will it last?

You sort a drawer, and know it could be a mess again in three months.

That worry is accurate. And it is what this S answers.

Things drift back for a reason that is not laziness.

A way that lives only in one head is invisible to everyone else.

So everyone else does it their own way.

You put the bowls low for a good reason.

Someone who does not know the reason puts them back on the counter.

Undoing your system, while genuinely trying to help.

Multiply that by every task, and every person.

It is just what happens when the best way is a secret.

So let me say plainly what a standard is.

The word sounds cold. Rules, clipboards, someone checking.

Put all of that out of your mind.

A standard is not bureaucracy. It is a recipe.

The best way you know to make something turn out right.

Written down, so it turns out right for anyone, even on a tired day.

An actual recipe is not a rule anyone is enforcing. It is a gift.

Someone worked it out and wrote it down.

So it comes out right whether you are sharp, or half asleep.

It does not need to be long.

A good home standard is a few plain lines about one task.

The test is never how thorough it looks.

It is whether the next person follows it and gets your result.

Now the most important word in the title.

Today.

A standard is not the last word. It is the current best word.

The moment someone finds a better way, the standard is not broken.

It is simply what you improve.

A standard is a starting point, not a finish line.

When your teenager finds a faster way to fold the laundry,

the better way wins. You spend thirty seconds updating the card.

A standard is not a rule carved in stone.

It is the best way you have found so far.

Held in place only until you find a better one.

And then rewritten without ceremony.

So make one. It is smaller than it sounds.

Pick a recurring task that mostly works only when you do it.

Watch yourself do it once. Write the steps in plain words.

As few as will do the job. Three to six steps.

If you reach step eleven, you are describing your quirks, not the task.

Then put it where the task happens.

Taped inside the cabinet above the dishwasher. Not in a binder.

The next person meets it at the moment they need it.

And a shortcut. If someone already does a task best,

ask them to show you, and write down what they do.

The best way usually already lives in somebody's hands.

Now go look at the friction meter.

And notice what it does not do. It does not move.

Nothing in the rooms changed. So the needle holds.

But for the first time, something appears behind it.

A small catch. A ratchet, holding it against the pull back.

Every gain until now was quietly reversible.

Standardize is the first S that keeps a gain from sliding back.

The needle did not advance today.

It got something more valuable. It stopped being able to retreat.

So here is your job.

Write one standard today. The best way you know, in five plain steps.

Stick it where the task happens.

You are not writing rules.

You are writing down the best way you know today.

So the good you built can be followed by anyone, and improved by everyone.

That is all a standard is. And it is how the gains last.
