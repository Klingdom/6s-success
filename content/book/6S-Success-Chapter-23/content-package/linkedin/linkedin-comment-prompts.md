# Chapter 23 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Standardize, The Best Way We Know Today

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, human, freeing voice, reassurance and permission not compliance: a home whose good ways live only in one person's head is the normal case, and a standard is a gift and a relief, not a rule to be enforced or defended. No corporate framing, no force or control language, no brand names.

---

1. Almost every good home runs on one person's head. You know which drawer things go into and why, the best way to load the dishwasher, the order of the bedtime routine, and none of it was ever written down, because you simply know it. Which means it all works right up until the day you are sick, away, or out of patience. Be honest with me: what is the one thing in your home that only really works when you do it, the thing that quietly falls apart the moment you step away?

2. Things drift back not because anyone is careless, but because a way that lives only in one person's head is invisible to everyone else, so everyone does it their own way. Someone puts the mixing bowls back on the counter, undoing your system while genuinely trying to help, because they never knew the reason for the low cupboard. When has this happened in your home, and looking back, was it ever really carelessness, or just a best way that had stayed a secret?

3. The word people fear in "standardize" is the coldness of it, the clipboards and the compliance. But a home standard is closer to a recipe: not a rule anyone is enforcing, but a gift someone worked out and wrote down so it turns out right for anyone, even on a tired day. Does thinking of it as a recipe rather than a rule change how it lands for you? What is one recurring task in your home you would actually want a recipe card for?

4. Here is the smallest possible version of the whole idea: pick one recurring thing that only really works when you do it, watch yourself do it once, and write the best way in a few plain steps. Then stick it where the task happens, not in a binder. It costs about five minutes. If you were going to write exactly one standard this week, which task would you pick, and where in the home would the card actually live?

5. The word doing the quiet work is today. A standard is not a rule carved in stone; it is the best way you have found so far, held in place only until someone finds a better one, and then rewritten without ceremony. That is what keeps it from becoming a cage. Where in your home has a "better way" already turned up, a faster fold, a smarter order, that you would happily let win over the way you have always done it?
