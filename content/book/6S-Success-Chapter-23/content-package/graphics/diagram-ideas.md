# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 23

**"Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Seven · Standardize*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_23_final.html`: the
head-to-walls opener, one home shown twice, on the left a single keeper with a large full
thought-bubble and two unsure onlookers while all of it lives in one head, on the right the same home
with small slate standard cards at the points of work (taped inside a cupboard door, by the
dishwasher, at the launch pad by the door) and everyone at ease; the living-standard loop, a friendly
open circle of arrows with five stops (best way today, write it down, everyone follows it, someone
finds a better way in a terracotta accent, rewrite the card, curving back to a fresh best way today,
a centre note reading a standard is meant to be improved); the sample standard card, a taped-up
recipe card titled Loading the Dishwasher with five short numbered steps, a Lives line, and a curl of
tape; and the friction meter, which this chapter HOLDS, the live needle unmoved at the Safety-complete
position with a new slate catch behind it and a barred backslide arrow, the goal still ahead.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B` (standard cards, data, the
catch), calm green `#6E8B5B` (ease and check-marks), friction spark `#CB4B36` (marks only), rule
`#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`. Incidental fills used in the figures: card-slate `#EEF3F6`, cream `#FCF8F0`,
wood-warm `#EFE7D6`, dawn gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`, terracotta card fill `#FDEDE4`, gauge
track `#EDE3CE`, tape `#E8DFCB` with edge `#C9BEA8`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the FIRST chapter of Part Seven, and it OPENS Standardize, the fifth of
the six S's. It does one turn: it takes the home that four S's made sorted, arranged, clean, and safe,
names the quiet problem underneath it (almost all of it lives in one person's head and hands, so it
lasts only as long as their memory and energy and quietly drifts back the day they are tired or away),
and delivers the concept that answers it, what a standard is and why it prevents reversion. It teaches
the drift reframe (a way that lives in one head is invisible, so everyone does it their own way and the
best way is a secret), delivers the hero (a standard is a recipe, the best way you know today, written
down plainly so it turns out right for anyone, not bureaucracy but a gift, carried by the recipe
reframe and the definition box), delivers the living-standard heart (the word today, the current best
word not the last word, rewritten without ceremony, a starting point not a cage, carried by the today
reframe and the living-standard line), walks the reader through writing one plain standard and putting
it where the work happens (with the shortcut of asking the person who already does it best to teach
it), and reads the friction meter as a HOLD with a new catch behind the needle. It USES the cleared,
arranged, cleaned, and safe home from the first four S's and does NOT re-teach any of them. CRITICAL
fence: this is the OPENING of Standardize, the concept of a standard and one written standard, NOT the
checklist, schedule, and responsibility system (Chapter 24), NOT the visual and pictorial standards
(Chapter 25), and NOT the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain, later S, named only as still
to come). Because writing down a way you were already doing changes nothing in the rooms, the friction
needle does NOT move this chapter; it HOLDS exactly where Safety left it, and for the first time a
catch appears behind it that keeps a gain from sliding back, so the meter is a HOLD with a new
anti-backslide catch, NOT a move and NOT a milestone. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for
example `CHAPTER 23 · STANDARDIZE`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound
terms keep their internal hyphen: living-standard, standard-card, launch-pad, best-way, thought-bubble,
head-to-walls, cupboard-door, recipe-card, anti-backslide, Safety-complete, Shine-complete,
Straighten-complete, Sort-complete, bedtime-routine, five-minute. Anti-shame throughout: a home whose
good ways live in one person's head is the normal case, not a failing; things drift back not because
anyone is careless but because a way that lives in one head is invisible, so everyone does it their own
way. A standard is warm and freeing, a recipe and a shared gift, never a cage. No force or control
framing anywhere (no enforce, comply, lock down, no clipboards presented as virtue); no corporate or
office register; no hype; no brand names. The register is calm, warm, human, and illustrated, the
relief of setting down the weight of being the only person who knows how the home works.

## 1. Knowledge Moves From One Head to the Walls (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole idea in one image. The home the reader has built runs on one
person's memory; Standardize takes the good ways out of that one head and puts them where the work
happens, so they belong to everyone. Nothing about the rooms changes; what changes is where the
knowledge lives. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** One warm home drawn twice, split gently by an idea rather than a hard line. On the
left, a single person stands with a large, full thought-bubble crammed with little pictures of how
everything is done (a drawer, a plate, a clock) while two family members stand nearby looking slightly
unsure, each with a small terracotta question mark, because all of it is in that one head. A broad
terracotta arrow labeled written down leads to the right, where the same home now carries small slate
standard cards at the points where the work happens: a card taped inside a cupboard door, a card by
the dishwasher, and a card at the launch pad by the door. Now the people are at ease and doing things,
each with a small green check.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm daylight wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint
  Newsreader italic line across the top, muted: `the good ways, lifted out of one head and onto the walls`.
- A single ink floor line across the base, the home read twice as two warm cutaways at ease,
  lived-in and cared-for.
- LEFT panel, `IN ONE HEAD` in soft gray sans caps at the foot: a keeper figure in honey with a large
  full thought-bubble of crammed glyphs, and two smaller onlooker figures in card-slate, each with a
  small terracotta `?`, because the best way is a secret held in one head.
- A broad terracotta arrow across the middle with a muted Newsreader italic tag, `written down`, the
  act that moves the knowledge.
- RIGHT panel, `ON THE WALLS` in terracotta sans caps at the foot: the same home with three small
  slate standard cards at the points of work (inside a cupboard door, above the dishwasher, at the
  launch pad), a keeper figure in green and an onlooker now at ease, each with a small green check.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "On the left, a home that runs on one person's memory: they know
  the best way to do everything, and so nothing quite works when they are tired or away. On the right,
  the same home after the best ways have been written down and put where the work happens. Nothing
  changed about the rooms. What changed is that the knowledge came out of one head and onto the walls,
  where it belongs to everyone."

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is the shift from knowledge trapped in one head to knowledge
placed where anyone can reach it, so draw the left home warm and capable but quietly fragile (the
onlookers unsure, not scolded), and the right home the same rooms, unchanged, only now with small
slate cards at the points of work and everyone at ease. Hold the color discipline: honey for the
keeper, slate for the cards, soft green for the checks of ease, one terracotta for the written-down
arrow and the question marks; spark-red does not appear. The onlookers are unsure, never blamed; a
home that runs on one person is the normal case, not a failing. Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate
cards, the register of relief, the weight of being the only keeper set down.

## 2. The Living-Standard Loop (the best way we know today)

**Purpose:** Render the living-standard heart as a picture: a standard is a stop on a loop, not the
end of the road. The word today is what keeps the loop turning, so a standard captures improvement
instead of freezing it out. This is the single strongest image of the chapter and the subject of the
infographic spec. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A friendly, calm loop of arrows drawn as a gentle open circle rather than a rigid
flowchart, five stops turning clockwise. Starting at the top: the best way you know today, then write
it down (the standard), then everyone follows it, then someone finds a better way (drawn in a
terracotta accent), then rewrite the card, and the arrow curves back to a fresh best way today. A
small note in the centre reads that a standard is meant to be improved.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `THE BEST
  WAY WE KNOW TODAY`.
- Five rounded slate-bordered stop cards on an oval, joined by slate arrows curving clockwise, with
  one arrow and one card in a terracotta accent:
  - Top, `The best way you know today` (cream card, slate border).
  - Right, `Write it down` with a small Newsreader italic under-line `the standard` (cream card).
  - Lower right, `Everyone follows it` (cream card).
  - Lower left, `Someone finds a better way` (terracotta card fill `#FDEDE4`, terracotta border and
    text, the one warm accent that marks where the loop stays alive).
  - Left, `Rewrite the card` (cream card), the arrow from it curving back up to the top.
- Centre, two lines of Newsreader italic in warm gray: `a standard is` / `meant to be improved`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "A standard is a stop on a loop, not the end of the road. You
  write down the best way you know today, everyone follows it, someone finds a better way, and you
  rewrite the card. The word "today" is what keeps the loop turning, so a standard captures improvement
  instead of freezing it out."

**Note for the illustrator:** the message is that the loop is open and turning, never closed and
rigid, so draw the arrows as a gentle friendly circle, not a boxed flowchart, and keep clear air
between the stops. Set the five stop labels and the centre note exactly as they read. Hold the color
discipline: slate for four stops and their arrows, one terracotta accent held only on `Someone finds a
better way` and the arrow leaving it, because that is the stop that keeps the standard living; no
spark-red anywhere. Draw no lock, no cage, no clipboard; this is a warm, turning loop, not a
procedure. Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate arrows, the register of a good idea that stays open
to a better one.

## 3. A Sample Standard Card (the whole thing, once)

**Purpose:** Show a whole standard at a realistic size, start to finish, so the reader sees how small
and light a standard actually is: a plain title, a handful of steps anyone can follow, and one line
saying where it lives. Not a document, a recipe card. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A single warm standard card drawn like a taped-up recipe card or a note you would
stick to a cupboard door. A plain title at the top in a honey accent, four or five short numbered
steps in plain slate words, a small line at the bottom naming where the card lives, and a little curl
of tape at one corner.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `A SAMPLE
  STANDARD CARD`.
- The card as a cream `#FCF8F0` panel with a slate border, a small curl of tape at the top-left corner
  in `#E8DFCB` with a `#C9BEA8` edge.
- Title, Fraunces in honey: `Loading the Dishwasher`, a hairline rule beneath.
- Five short numbered steps, each a small slate number-dot and a plain Newsreader line:
  - `1` : `Scrape, do not rinse.`
  - `2` : `Big items along the sides and back.`
  - `3` : `Bowls and cups angled down on the top rack.`
  - `4` : `Knives point down.`
  - `5` : `Run it when it is full, not before.`
- A dashed hairline, then a bottom line in Newsreader italic slate: `Lives: inside the cabinet above
  the dishwasher`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "A whole standard, start to finish. A plain title, a handful of
  steps anyone can follow, and one line saying where it lives. This is not a document; it is a recipe
  card for a recurring task, and it took about five minutes to make. That is the entire craft of
  standardizing, done once."

**Note for the illustrator:** the card must read as small, warm, and doable, a recipe you would tape
up, never a form or a policy, so keep the steps few, plain, and hand-made, and let the curl of tape
say it lives on a real surface. Set the title, the five steps, and the Lives line exactly as they
read; they are frozen wording and must not be trimmed or reworded. Hold the color discipline: slate
for the step numbers and text, one honey accent on the title, soft rule hairlines; no spark-red, no
clipboard, no binder. Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate text, honey title, the register of a
five-minute gift stuck in the right spot.

## 4. A Recipe, Not a Rulebook (the reframe, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's central turn as a picture: the word standard sounds cold, like
rules and clipboards and someone checking whether you complied, but a home standard is the warm
opposite, a recipe, a gift written down so the thing turns out right for anyone. This diagram has no
direct HTML figure twin; it renders the recipe reframe as a calm contrast, so the reader feels the
difference between bureaucracy and a shared gift.

**What it shows:** Two calm facing panels, read left to right as the two things the word standard can
mean. Panel one, what people fear: a cold, formal binder or clipboard, thick and unread, drawn dim and
low-contrast, the register of a rule nobody opens. Panel two, what a home standard actually is: a warm
recipe card in a kitchen, a few plain steps in a friendly hand, a gift someone worked out and wrote
down so you do not have to work it out from scratch, drawn in full warm color.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `A RECIPE, NOT A RULEBOOK`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath
  carrying the section's spine: "A standard is not a rule anyone is enforcing; it is a gift someone
  worked out and wrote down, so the thing turns out right whether you are sharp or half asleep."
- Two warm cream `#FCF8F0` panels with ink outlines, left to right, each headed with a small slate tab.
  Panel `WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE`: a dim, formal binder or clipboard, thick and closed, a muted Newsreader
  italic note, `rules, clipboards, someone checking that you complied`, drawn low-contrast and cool.
  Panel `WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS`: a warm recipe card on a kitchen surface with a few plain steps and a
  curl of tape, a muted italic note, `a gift, written down so it turns out right for anyone`, drawn in
  full warm color.
- Beneath, a small slate-bordered strip carrying the recipe reframe in ink: `A recipe takes skill that
  lived in one person's hands and makes it available to anyone who can read.`
- To the right, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `CHECKLISTS · SCHEDULES · PICTURES ·
  NEXT` in muted honey sans caps, a muted italic note beneath, `Standards get their tools in the
  chapters ahead`, with a fainter panel outline behind it for the S still to come. Hold the right group
  quiet and low-contrast, clearly ahead, not yet reached.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "Put the cold picture out of your mind. A home standard is warmer
  and simpler: someone worked out the best way and wrote it down for everyone."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the chapter's central turn, so keep it calm and plain, never
scolding; the point is not that rules are evil but that a home standard is not a rule at all, it is a
recipe and a gift. Draw the feared binder dim and closed, a little tired, and make the recipe card
visibly warm and open and used. Hold the color discipline: slate for the tabs and the sub-strip, one
honey for the dim next-chapters panel; no spark-red, no cage, no lock, no one looking over a shoulder.
Keep the next panel dim, unlit, and clearly ahead; you may name the tools as next, but teach nothing of
them, only that standards get their tools in the chapters that follow. The register is warmth and
relief, not correction: a gift, not a compliance demand.

## 5. The Needle Holds, and Stops Sliding Back (the friction meter)

**Purpose:** Deliver the honest HOLD beat and close the chapter. The needle does NOT move this
chapter, because nothing new was cleared; you wrote down a way you were already doing. But for the
first time the dial shows something new behind the needle rather than ahead of it: a small slate
catch, a ratchet, holding the needle against the pull back toward friction, and a faint dashed
backslide arrow barred with a small block. Every gain until now was quietly reversible; a written
standard is the first thing that blocks the backslide. It must read as a true HOLD with a new catch,
NOT as a move and NOT as a new milestone, and the goal is still ahead. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML,
and drives the live data-driven gauge.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle unmoved at the Safety-complete
position it reached last chapter, resting on the fourth marked tick, which is now itself a passed
marker. Behind the needle, near the pivot, a small slate ratchet or catch holds it against the pull
back. A faint dashed arrow points back toward the friction side and is barred with a small spark-red
block, tagged NO BACKSLIDE. The Safety-complete, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, and
Sort-complete milestones sit as small passed slate markers further back. The GOAL crosshair, tagged
purpose, still sits ahead on the green calm side, because Standardize has only begun and Sustain still
follows.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark (right), on
  a pale `#EDE3CE` track. The gauge is a live, data-driven SVG, consistent with prior chapters. Pivot
  at (210,198); the LIVE needle HOLDS at the Safety-complete position (67.6,154.9), unmoved from last
  chapter, its tip marked by a small green calm-dot.
- The `SORT COMPLETE`, `STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE`, and `SHINE COMPLETE` milestones in slate at reduced
  opacity, small passed markers furthest back as ground covered many chapters ago.
- The `SAFETY COMPLETE` milestone (Chapter 22) in slate, now itself a passed marker the needle rests
  on, faded, its chip and leader reading as the position the needle holds at, not a new gain.
- A NEW slate catch or ratchet drawn behind the needle near the pivot, a small stepped shape in
  card-slate `#EEF3F6` with a slate outline, with a Newsreader italic tag `a catch holds it`, so it
  reads plainly as the first thing that keeps the needle from sliding back.
- A faint dashed backslide arrow pointing back toward the friction side, barred with a small spark-red
  cross-block and tagged `NO BACKSLIDE` in spark sans caps, showing the slide the standard now blocks.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still clearly
  ahead of the live needle, with visible room between the two, because the needle reaches the goal only
  at the very end and Standardize and Sustain remain.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The needle holds, and for the first time cannot slide back."
- Caption (frozen): "The needle does not move this chapter, because nothing new was cleared. But for
  the first time, something appears behind it: a catch that keeps it from sliding back. Everything you
  built until now could quietly reverse; a written standard is the first thing that holds a gain in

  needle, having come this far, stays."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a HOLD, not a move, and that is the whole point of the
chapter showing up honestly on the dial. The needle does NOT travel this chapter, because nothing in
the rooms changed; you only wrote down a way you were already doing. Keep the live needle exactly at
the Safety-complete position, unmoved, and do NOT advance it. What is new is behind the needle, not
ahead of it: a small slate catch, a ratchet, holding it against the pull back, and a barred backslide
arrow showing the slide the standard now blocks. Draw the earlier milestones as passed ground further
back, with Safety-complete now the position the needle rests on rather than a fresh gain. The GOAL
crosshair stays ahead on the green calm side, with clear room between it and the live needle, room that
the rest of Standardize and Sustain will take. No completion glow, no confetti, no forward step; the
gain this chapter is that a gain can no longer quietly reverse, and that is worth as much as a move.
The needle did not advance; it stopped being able to retreat.
