# Infographic Spec: Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

**Chapter 23, "Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Seven · Standardize**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a standard
is not the end of the road but a stop on a loop, and the word today is what keeps the loop turning, so
a standard captures improvement instead of freezing it out. The concept pairs the hero rule, **A
standard is the best way you know today, written down.**, with the picture at its heart: **the
living-standard loop**, a friendly open circle of five stops, the best way you know today, write it
down, everyone follows it, someone finds a better way, rewrite the card, curving back to a fresh best
way today. So the viewer sees the whole move at once, that a standard is written in pencil, meant to be
improved, a starting point and not a cage.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_23_final.html`. Slate carries the
stops, the arrows, the definition, the headings, and the labels; one terracotta accent is held only on
the stop that keeps the standard living, someone finds a better way; the single small warm red is held
back for marks only and is barely used, if at all, on this calm image, because a standard is a recipe
and a gift, not a live hazard. "Rules" is not the point, and neither is "bureaucracy"; a home standard
is warmer and simpler, skill that lived in one person's hands, written down so it turns out right for
anyone who can read. Anti-shame throughout: a home whose good ways live in one head is the normal case,
not a failing, and things drift back not because anyone is careless but because a way that lives in one
head is invisible. The register is warm and freeing, never corporate, never a cage; a standard is a
gift to the household.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: the best way you
  know today, written down. The eye should read the rule, then see the loop make it living, the open
  circle that keeps a standard from freezing into a cage.
- The living-standard loop is the hero, so make it clear and open: a friendly circle of five stops,
  gentle arrows, clear air between the stops, never a boxed or closed flowchart. Set the five stop
  labels and the centre note byte-for-byte as they read in the chapter; they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: slate carries the stops, the arrows, the definition, and the labels; one
  terracotta accent is held only on `Someone finds a better way`, because that is the stop that keeps
  the standard living; the single small warm red is reserved for marks and is barely used, if at all,
  because this image is a warm recipe and a gift, not a live hazard. This image is calm, so no fill-line
  red anywhere.
- This image makes the concept, the living standard and its rule and its loop. Do NOT re-teach the
  earlier four S's, and do NOT build the checklist, schedule, and responsibility system (Chapter 24),
  the visual and pictorial standards (Chapter 25), or the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain,
  a later S); this is the opening of Standardize, the concept of a standard and one written standard.
  And name no brand and draw no specific product; the standard is a plain, generic recipe card, teaching
  the idea, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: a home whose good ways live in one person's head is the normal case, not a failing, and
  things drift back not because anyone is careless but because a way that lives in one head is invisible.
  What you are building is not rules, it is a gift to the household. Keep the register warm and freeing,
  a standard is a recipe, never a cage; no force or control language (no enforce, comply, lock down), no
  clipboards or binders presented as virtue, no cage or lock, no hype.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the loop and the stops
  drawn by hand rather than as a clinical chart, the arrows and glyphs sketched rather than typeset, the
  whole image the calm, warm register of a good idea that stays open to a better one rather than a
  procedure.
