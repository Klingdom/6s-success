# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (after you get a home sorted, arranged, clean, and safe, almost all of it still lives in one person's head, so it drifts back the day they are away, and the fix is a standard: a recipe, the best way you know today written down, kept living by the word today) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. A standard is framed as a gift and a relief, never corporate or a rule. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small idea that genuinely changed how I think about keeping my house running, and then I want to hear from you all.

A while back I got the whole place sorted, arranged, clean, and honestly safe. It felt solid. Then I was down with a cold for a few days, and the house quietly stopped working. Not because anyone was careless, but because nobody else knew the best way to load the dishwasher, or the order of the bedtime routine, or the trick that keeps mornings calm. All of it lived in exactly one place, which was my head.

Here is the line that stopped me.

"Everything you have built so far lives in your head and your hands, which means it lasts exactly as long as your memory and your energy do. A standard is how a good way of doing something stops depending on you."

That word, standard, made me flinch at first. It sounds like rules and clipboards. But the book reframes it in a way I have not been able to shake.

"A standard is not bureaucracy. It is a recipe: the best way you know to make something turn out right, written down so it turns out right every time, for anyone, even on a tired day."

A recipe. Not a rule anyone is enforcing, just the best way written down so it stops being a secret. And the part I loved most is the one word that keeps it from becoming a cage.

"A standard is not a rule carved in stone. It is the best way you have found so far, held in place only until you find a better one, and then rewritten without ceremony."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the one task in your home that only really works when you do it? The thing nobody else quite gets right, that you have never written down because you just know it. And has anyone here actually written one down, taped a few plain steps inside a cupboard door, and had it stick?

I will go first in the comments. 😄

(If anyone wants it written out properly, this is from Chapter 23 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the one idea that took the whole house out of my head and put it somewhere everyone can reach it.)
