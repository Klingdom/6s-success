# Read Next Recommendations: Chapter 23, Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today

1. Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities
   The next step, and where Standardize gets its tools. Chapter 23 opened Part Seven and the fifth S with the concept of a standard, the best way you know today written down, and by writing one plain standard as proof. But one card on one cupboard door is a proof of concept, not a system. Chapter 24 turns standards into the things that carry them: checklists so nothing is forgotten, schedules so recurring work has a time, and responsibilities so every standard has a name. Chapter 23 gives the idea; Chapter 24 gives it a body.

2. Chapter 22, Improve Overall Safety
   The setup, and the chapter just behind this one. Chapter 22 completed Safety, the fourth S, by preventing new hazards from taking hold and preparing for the harm you cannot prevent, and with it four of the six S's were done. But everything those four S's built lives largely in your head and your hands, and works because you keep it going. Read how Safety was finished, then see how Standardize writes the best way down so the whole home stops depending on you alone.

3. Write one standard today (the chapter action)
   Pick one recurring thing that only really works when you do it, loading the dishwasher, the bedtime routine, taking out the recycling, and write down the best way in five plain steps. Stick it where the task happens. One standard, today. It costs about five minutes, and it is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea.

4. The Standard Card template (free resource)
   A printable and phone wallpaper, a simple card to capture one standard, a plain title, a few steps, and one line saying where it lives, so a household can write the best way for any recurring task and stick it where the work happens.

5. The Best Way Today worksheet (free resource)
   A printable to capture a recurring task's current best way and leave a spot to note improvements, so the standard stays living, the best way you know today, rewritten without ceremony the moment someone finds a better one.
