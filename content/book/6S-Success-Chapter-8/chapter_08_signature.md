## Signature Overview

**Length:** 16 pages (8 spreads)
**Part:** Part Three · Sort (the OPENING chapter of Part 3; it follows Chapter 7's defined purpose and hands off to Chapter 9 · Necessary vs. Unnecessary)
**Reading time:** 9 to 12 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 58% text, 42% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Have the reader make one real Sort pass on their chosen space: separate what belongs here from what does not, judged only by the space's purpose, and physically carry the "does not belong" items to a better home. This is the first action chapter. It is also where the friction meter moves for the first time in the book, because objects finally leave the space. Sort is the first of six S's, so the needle takes a real step toward calm but stops partway, still aimed at the goal, not arrived.

**Throughline space:** the entryway drop zone, carried through Chapters 4 (chosen), 5 (audited at 14 out of 30, weakest on Standardize and Sustain), 6 (photographed for the before shot), and 7 (given its purpose). The number, the photo, and the purpose all pay off here as the drop zone gets its first real pass.

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapters 1 to 7. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data. Green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns: items that belong here take a calm-dot, items that do not throw a spark and leave by an arrow. The friction meter appears at the close, moved.

## Identity strings (frozen; copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)
- **Title:** Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong
- **Subtitle:** The first S, and the first time you move a thing. Take out what does not belong here, and half the mess leaves before you buy a single bin.
- **Masthead:** "Part Three · Sort" / "Chapter Eight"
- **The purpose that judges (frozen, from Ch 7):** This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic.
- **Hero question (frozen):** Does this help the space do its job?
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** items that pass "belong here"; items that fail "belong somewhere else" and go to "a better home."

## Hero device: The One Question
Chapter 7 handed the reader a purpose. Chapter 8 turns that purpose into a working tool: a single gate every object passes through. **Does this help the space do its job?** Yes, it belongs here and stays, marked with a calm-dot. No, it belongs somewhere else and leaves for a better home. The purpose is the standard; the One Question is how you apply it, object by object, without re-arguing your whole life each time.

## Motifs
- **Two piles, not a verdict.** Sort separates "belongs here" from "belongs somewhere else." It does not decide worth, need, or fate. Those are later questions.
- **The space gets lighter before you buy anything.** Removal, not storage, does the first and biggest share of the work.
- **The needle steps.** Objects moved, so the meter moves, for the first time in the book.

## Emotional purpose
Relief and momentum. After four chapters of preparing, the reader finally does something, and it works fast. The chapter protects the reader from the shame that stalls most decluttering by insisting that removing an item from a space is not a judgment on the item or on them. It is only an address change.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to run one honest Sort pass on one space, using the purpose sentence as the judge and the One Question as the tool, and to carry the non-belonging items out to where they actually live, leaving only the door-job things behind.

## Friction meter call
**Moves for the first time.** Held on the friction side through Chapters 4 to 7, the needle now steps toward calm, landing in the honey middle zone, clearly off the red. It does NOT reach the GOAL crosshair, which stays fully aimed on the green calm side (named "purpose" since Chapter 7). A faint ghost of the old needle position remains on the friction side so the move is unmistakable. This is the honest reading: one S done, five to come. Frozen caption governs the figure.

## Scope fences (do not pre-empt later chapters)
- **Ch 9 (Necessary vs. Unnecessary):** Ch 8 sorts by "does it belong in THIS space," not "do you need to own it at all." An item can belong somewhere else and still be necessary. Do not open the own-it-or-not question.
- **Ch 10 (Red Tags, Holding Areas, Sorter's Remorse):** no red-tag system, no formal holding area. One short forward teaser is allowed ("the maybes get their own system in a couple of chapters"); do not build it.
- **Ch 11 (Donate/Sell/Store/Recycle/Throw):** Ch 8 removes items from the space; it does not decide their disposal. "A better home" means where the item actually lives now, not the giveaway pile.

## Section-by-section outline
1. **Chapter Opening (drop-cap):** Back in the drop zone, purpose in hand, number and photo on the fridge. Today, for the first time, we move something.
2. **What Sort Actually Is:** The first S. Separate, then remove. Sort is not tidying and not cleaning. Light nod to Seiri and the Toyota factory origin, no overclaim.
3. **The One Question:** The hero device. Purpose becomes a single repeatable gate. Belongs here vs belongs somewhere else. Definition box.
4. **A Pass Through the Drop Zone:** The worked example. Belongs-here list and belongs-somewhere-else list, judged against the frozen purpose. The surface gets lighter.
5. **Where "Belongs Somewhere Else" Goes:** A better home means the place it actually lives. Keep it moving; do not stall on the hard ones. One-line teaser that the maybes get their own system soon.
6. **It Is Not About Throwing Away:** Anti-shame. The frozen reassurance line. Removal is an address change, not a sentence.
7. **The Needle Moves:** The friction-meter payoff. Nothing bought, nothing cleaned, and the gauge still moved, because objects left.
8. **Chapter Close and What's Next:** One pass done. Hand off to Chapter 9's harder question about what is left.

## Production notes / review flags
- First action chapter: the reader physically moves objects. Keep the instruction gentle and non-prescriptive; one object is a complete, legal Sort.
- Meter moves for the first time. Confirm the needle reads as clearly stepped off red into the honey middle, and clearly short of the GOAL crosshair. The ghost needle sells the move.
- Hold the scope fences hard: no necessary/unnecessary, no red tags, no donate/discard. "A better home" is an address, not a fate.
- Cardinal dash rule: no em dash, en dash, or spaced hyphen. Label separators use the middot.
