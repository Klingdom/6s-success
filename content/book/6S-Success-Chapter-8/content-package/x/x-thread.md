# X Thread: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

Chapter 8 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. One thread, 15 posts. Each post is at or under 280 characters and stands on its own. Plain "1/" numbering on its own line.

1/
Four chapters of preparing, and not one object moved. Chapter 8 of 6S Success: Home Edition is where that changes. This is Sort, the first S, and the first time you pick something up and it leaves the space. Sleeves rolled up.

(approx. 221 chars)

---

2/
Sort has one aim: separate what belongs in this space from what does not, then carry the noes somewhere else. That is the whole move. You buy nothing. You wipe nothing. You just decide, object by object, what earns its place here.

(approx. 226 chars)

---

3/
Sort is not tidying; a tidied pile is still a pile. Sort is not cleaning either, that is Shine, later. Sort is the blunt step before both. It asks one thing of every object: does this live here, yes or no? The yeses stay. The noes leave.

(approx. 231 chars)

---

4/
The clutter is not a filing problem. It is an occupancy problem, and step one is simply to notice the squatters and show them the way out. Most of what makes a space chaotic is the presence of things that were never supposed to be there.

(approx. 233 chars)

---

5/
Sort has two halves, in order. First separate: split everything into belongs here and belongs somewhere else. Then remove: actually carry the second group out. People love the separating and skip the removing, so rooms end up merely rearranged.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

6/
Here is the hero device, one gate every object passes through. Does this help the space do its job? Yes, it belongs here, and it stays. No, it belongs somewhere else, and today its job is to leave and go to a better home.

(approx. 217 chars)

---

7/
This is easier than most decluttering, which interrogates each object's whole relationship to your life. The One Question refuses that. A charger is genuinely useful and the answer is still no. You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address.

(approx. 256 chars)

---

8/
A pass through the entryway drop zone, judged by its job: getting out the door fast, landing softly on the way in. Belongs here: keys, outgoing mail, sunglasses, the dog's leash, a folded reusable bag, the umbrella, a small tray for keys and wallet.

(approx. 244 chars)

---

9/
Belongs somewhere else: already-read magazines, junk mail, a charger (better at a desk), finished homework and school papers, a stray screwdriver, a shed sweater (the closet), the decorative bowl that just collects coins. The console gets lighter.

(approx. 243 chars)

---

10/
Where do the noes go? Usually somewhere obvious. The charger to the desk, the screwdriver to the toolbox, the sweater to the closet. A better home means the place the object actually lives. You are not inventing homes. You are returning things to the ones they wandered from.

(approx. 271 chars)

---

11/
A few will stump you. Do not solve them now, or the pass grinds to a halt. Set the honest maybes aside in one spot and keep moving. They get a system of their own in a couple of chapters. For today, a maybe is not a failure and not a decision.

(approx. 239 chars)

---

12/
The thing that stalls a Sort is not the objects. It is guilt. A simple address change starts to feel like a verdict, the hand hesitates, and the pass dies. So keep this close: You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here.

(approx. 254 chars)

13/
Now the payoff. Through all of Part 2 the friction meter held perfectly still, because nothing had physically moved. Today objects moved. The console is measurably emptier, so the needle finally steps off the friction side and travels toward calm.

(approx. 244 chars)

14/
Read the gauge honestly, though. It stops in the middle, pointed at the goal but not arrived. Sort is only the first of six S's. Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain are still ahead. Far enough to feel real, short of a finish not yet earned.
