# Facebook Longform Post: The First Time You Actually Move Something

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 8 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA to run a one-object Sort pass. No em dashes.

---

For years I thought the mess by my front door was a tidying problem. Turns out it was more of an occupancy problem. Too many things were living somewhere they were never meant to live.

Here is the fix, and it is smaller than you would guess. You do not need a system, a bin, or a free Saturday. You need one question.

Stand in front of your messiest little space and, for every object on it, ask: Does this help the space do its job? That is the whole test. If yes, it belongs here and it stays. If no, it belongs somewhere else, and today its job is to go back to where it actually lives.

Take my entryway console. Its job is getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in. So I walked it, object by object.

The keys stay, obviously. The dog's leash stays, because half our departures involve the dog. Sunglasses, the umbrella, a folded bag by the door, tomorrow's mail, a little tray to catch keys and a wallet. All yes. Every one of them helps somebody get out the door.

Then the other pile. The magazines I already read do nothing for the morning rush, so they go. The junk mail, gone. The phone charger lives a better life at a desk. The school papers belong wherever we actually deal with paper. The stray screwdriver goes back to the toolbox. The sweater goes to the closet. And that decorative bowl that just quietly collects coins? It goes too.

I bought nothing. I wiped nothing. I just carried a handful of things back to where they already live, and the console that used to drown suddenly looked like it could breathe.

Here is the part that keeps people stuck, so let me say it plainly. Moving a thing out is not throwing it away. You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here. The charger can be valuable and needed and still belong at the desk. It is an address change, not a verdict.

So here is your low pressure homework for the comments, if you want it. Pick your busiest little space, ask one question of the first thing your eye lands on, and if it does not help the space do its job, carry it to where it belongs. One object. That is a legal, complete Sort.
