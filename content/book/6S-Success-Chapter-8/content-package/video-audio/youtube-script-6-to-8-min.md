## SECTION 1: WHAT SORT ACTUALLY IS

[HOST]
Let us be precise, because the word does quiet work. Sort is not tidying. Tidying makes a pile look neater and leaves all of it exactly where it is, and you already know a tidied pile is still a pile. Sort is not cleaning either. Cleaning is Shine, three chapters from now. Sort is the step before both. It asks one blunt question about every object. Does this thing live here, yes or no?

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Sort is not tidying. Sort is not cleaning.]

[HOST, voice over]
The idea has a name. Sort is the English word for Seiri, the first of the five S's that grew up on factory floors, where a cluttered bench was slow and unsafe. The insight was almost embarrassingly simple. Before you organize anything, remove what does not belong in the area at all. Because most of what makes a space chaotic is not disorder. It is the sheer presence of things that were never supposed to be there.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The clutter is not a filing problem. It is an occupancy problem.]

[HOST, on camera]
So Sort has two halves, and you do them in order. First you separate. You split everything into what belongs here and what belongs somewhere else. Then you remove. You carry the second group out to where it lives. People love the first half and skip the second, which is why so many organizing afternoons end with the room just as full, only rearranged. Separating is thinking. Removing is the part that changes the room.

## SECTION 2: THE ONE QUESTION

[HOST]
Here is where the purpose you wrote earns its keep. A purpose that sits on an index card is a nice thought. A purpose you can point at an object is a tool.

[VISUAL: The hero infographic, "The One Question." Small objects flow toward a central gate. Above the gate, the question. They split into a green branch and an arrow leading out of frame.]

[HOST, voice over]
Turn the sentence into one gate that every item passes through. It is the same question for the keys and for the coupons and for the stray screwdriver. Does this help the space do its job? That is the whole test. If the answer is yes, it belongs here, and it stays, and it earns a small green dot. If the answer is no, it belongs somewhere else, and its job today is to leave and go to a better home.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Does this help the space do its job?]

[HOST, on camera]
Notice how much easier this is than the way most of us declutter. The usual method picks up each object and interrogates its whole relationship to your life. Do I still like this. Will I use it someday. Who gave it to me. That is exhausting, and it is why so many of us stall out with three half-sorted piles and a headache. The One Question refuses all of that. A phone charger is genuinely useful, and the answer is still no, because charging phones is not what the entryway is for. You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address.

## SECTION 3: A PASS THROUGH THE DROP ZONE

[HOST]
Enough theory. Let us walk the console, object by object, with the purpose held up like a ruler. The job is getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in.

[VISUAL: Two-column infographic, "The First Pass." Left, "Belongs here" in green with calm-dots. Right, "Belongs somewhere else" in slate with out-arrows and each item's real home in parentheses.]

[HOST, voice over]
Start with the easy yeses, because it is good for morale. The keys belong here. The outgoing mail that rides out the door tomorrow belongs here. Sunglasses, yes. The dog's leash, yes. A folded reusable bag, the umbrella, a small tray to catch keys and a wallet, all yes. Every one of them earns its dot the same way. It helps you get out the door or land softly coming back.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Belongs here: keys, mail out, sunglasses, leash, bag, umbrella, a small tray.]

[HOST]
Now the other group, and here the surface starts to change. The stack of already-read magazines does nothing for getting out the door, so it belongs somewhere else. The junk mail, same answer. The charger lives a better life at a desk. The finished homework and school papers go wherever paper gets dealt with. The stray screwdriver belongs with the tools. The shed sweater belongs in the closet. And the decorative bowl, the one that quietly collects coins and receipts, belongs somewhere else entirely.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Belongs somewhere else: magazines, junk mail, charger, homework, screwdriver, sweater, the coin bowl.]

[HOST, on camera]
Watch what happens as those things leave. You added nothing. You bought no baskets, installed no hooks, wiped no wood. You simply carried the noes to their real addresses, and the console that was drowning now holds a tray, a leash, an umbrella, a bag, and tomorrow's mail. It is doing its one job and nothing else. The surface is calmer, and so, a little, are you.

## SECTION 4: IT IS NOT ABOUT THROWING AWAY

[HOST]
I want to slow down on one thing, because it is the reason most people never finish a Sort. And it has nothing to do with the objects.

[B-ROLL: A hand lifts the decorative bowl, pauses, hesitates, almost sets it back down.]

[HOST, voice over]
Somewhere in the pass you will pick up a thing that does not belong here and feel a small tug of guilt. The bowl was a gift. The magazines cost money. Suddenly a simple address change feels like a verdict, and verdicts are heavy, so the hand hesitates and the object goes back down, and the pass dies right there, on the guilt. That is not a character flaw. It is the single most common way decluttering stalls.

[HOST, on camera]
So here is the sentence to keep close. You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here. The bowl can be lovely and still not belong on the launch pad. Moving a thing out of a space says nothing about whether it is good, or whether you will regret it. It says one modest thing. This is not where it goes. And to be clear, sorting an item out is not throwing it away. The charger went to the desk. It was not donated, sold, or discarded. It moved. Where things go to be let go is a later chapter. Today you are only changing addresses inside your own home.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here.]

Read the gauge honestly, though. It stops in the middle, pointed at the goal but not arrived, and there is a good reason for the restraint. Sort is only the first of six S's. You removed what does not belong, the biggest single step, but the things that stay are not yet arranged, the surface is not yet clean. Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain are all still ahead. So it stops partway, which is exactly where an honest first step should stop. Far enough to feel real, short of a finish you have not yet earned.

## CLOSE AND SOFT CTA

[HOST]
Look at what you did today with no money and no cleaning supplies. You took a space that was failing because it held too much, asked one question of everything on it, and carried out every no. What is left is a launch pad that actually launches. That is a full and legal Sort. It proves that most of what overwhelms us was never disorder to be organized, only surplus to be removed.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: You do not organize a space by adding storage. You organize it by removing what was never supposed to be there.]

[HOST, on camera]
So here is your job. Stand in your space. Say its one job out loud. Pick up the first thing your eye lands on and ask the One Question. If it does not help the space do its job, carry it to where it actually belongs. One object is a legal, complete Sort. And do not buy bins first. Storage bought first just organizes the clutter and locks it in place. Remove what does not belong, then buy for what is left, if you even still need to.
