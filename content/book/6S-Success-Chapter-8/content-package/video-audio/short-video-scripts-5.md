## SHORT 1: One question sorts any space
**Topic: the hero device, the One Question. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** One question sorts any space, and it is not "do I love this?"

**Script:**
One question sorts any space, and it is not "do I love this." Most decluttering fails because you pick up each object and interrogate your whole life. Do I still like it. Will I use it someday. Who gave it to me. That is exhausting, and it stalls you out with three half-sorted piles and a headache. So stop asking about the object. Ask about the space. Does this help the space do its job? Yes, it belongs here. No, it belongs somewhere else. A phone charger is genuinely useful, and the answer is still no, because charging phones is not what the entryway is for. You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address.

- 22s: "Does this help the space do its job?"
- 34s: "You're not judging it. You're checking its address."

## SHORT 2: It is not throwing away
**Topic: the anti-shame reframe. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The reason you never finish decluttering is not the stuff. It is the guilt.

**Script:**
The reason you never finish decluttering is not the stuff. It is the guilt. You pick up the bowl that was a gift, and suddenly moving it feels like a verdict. Verdicts are heavy, so your hand hesitates, the bowl goes back down, and the whole thing dies right there. So here is the sentence to keep close. You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here. The bowl can be lovely and still not belong by the front door. Moving it out of the space is not throwing it away. It has not been donated or discarded. It just moved. You are only changing an address inside your own home.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "It's not the stuff. It's the guilt."
- 12s: "A simple move starts to feel like a verdict"
- 24s: "You are not deciding what it is worth"
- 32s: "You are only deciding it does not live here"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand lifts a decorative bowl, pauses, almost sets it back down. On the reframe line, the hand steadies and carries the bowl calmly out of frame. Warm light, unhurried.

## SHORT 3: The one-object Sort
**Topic: the Quick Win, lowest-stakes start. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You can finish a real Sort in the next sixty seconds.

**Script:**
You can finish a real Sort in the next sixty seconds. You do not need a free weekend or a single bin. Here is the whole move. Stand in your space. Say its one job out loud. Then pick up the first thing your eye lands on and ask one question. Does this help the space do its job? If it does not, carry it to where it actually belongs. That is it. One object, walked to its real home, is a legal, complete Sort. Momentum is the whole game here, and it starts with one thing leaving.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A real Sort in 60 seconds"
- 8s: "Say the space's one job out loud"
- 18s: "Pick up ONE thing. Ask the question."
- 28s: "One object is a legal, complete Sort"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Real-time, single take feel. Person stands at a cluttered drop zone, says the job, picks up one stray item, walks it to another room, sets it down. Simple and calm.

## SHORT 4: Do not buy the bins first
**Topic: the Common Mistake. Runtime ~38 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Do not buy a single bin until you have done this.

**Script:**
Do not buy a single bin until you have done this. The instinct is always to shop first. New baskets, cute containers, a label maker. But storage bought first just organizes the clutter and locks it in place. You end up with a neatly contained pile that is still a pile. Here is the order that actually works. Remove what does not belong first. Ask one question of everything in the space. Does this help the space do its job? Carry out every no. Then, and only then, buy for what is left, if you even still need to. Most of the time, you will not.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Don't buy the bins first"
- 10s: "Storage bought first just locks in the clutter"
- 22s: "Remove what does not belong FIRST"
- 32s: "Then buy for what's left, if you still need to"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Open on a shopping cart of empty bins, then a slow shake-of-the-head cut. Switch to a hand simply carrying stray objects out of a space, leaving it lighter and empty-handed of any new product.

## SHORT 5: The needle finally moves
**Topic: the friction-meter payoff. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** For four chapters, this meter did not move. Today it does.

**Script:**
For four chapters, this meter did not move, and that was on purpose. We chose the space, scored it, photographed it, and gave it a job. Real work, but not a single object moved, so the needle stayed put. A meter that swings when nothing has changed is just flattery. But today, objects moved. You carried the magazines and the charger and the bowl out to their real homes, and the space is measurably lighter. So the needle finally steps off the friction side and travels toward calm. Read it honestly, though. It stops in the middle, pointed at the goal but not arrived. Sort is only the first of six S's. Five more to come.
