# Teleprompter Script: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

**Chapter 8, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** sort-remove-what-does-not-belong
**Length:** about 650 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Here we are at last.

Standing in front of the drop zone.

Sleeves rolled up.

For four chapters, we prepared.

You chose this space.

You gave it an honest score.

You took its picture.

You wrote it a job.

And through all of it, not one object moved.

Today the patience pays off.

Today you pick something up.

And it leaves.

This is Sort.

The first of the six S's.

The simplest one to describe.

And the most satisfying one to feel.

It has one aim.

Separate what belongs in a space from what does not.

Then carry the ones that do not somewhere else.

You will buy nothing.

You will clean nothing.

You will just decide what lives here.

Let me be precise.

Sort is not tidying.

Tidying makes a pile look neater and leaves it right where it is.

A tidied pile is still a pile.

Sort is not cleaning either.

Cleaning is Shine, three chapters from now.

Sort is the step before both.

It asks one blunt question about every object.

Does this thing live here, yes or no?

The yeses stay.

The noes leave.

The clutter is not a filing problem.

It is an occupancy problem.

Most of what makes a space chaotic

is the sheer presence of things

that were never supposed to be there.

So here is the tool.

The purpose you wrote becomes one question.

The same question for the keys and the coupons and the stray screwdriver.

Does this help the space do its job?

If yes, it belongs here, and it stays.

If no, it belongs somewhere else.

And it goes to a better home.

A phone charger is genuinely useful.

And the answer is still no.

Because charging phones is not what the entryway is for.

You are not judging the charger.

You are just checking its address.

So let us walk the console.

The keys belong here. The leaving place needs them.

The outgoing mail belongs here. It rides out the door tomorrow.

Sunglasses, the leash, a folded bag, the umbrella, a small tray. All yes.

Now the other group.

The already-read magazines do nothing for getting out the door.

The junk mail, the same.

The charger lives better at a desk.

The homework goes where paper gets dealt with.

The screwdriver goes with the tools.

The sweater goes to the closet.

The bowl that just collects coins goes somewhere else entirely.

You added nothing.

You bought no baskets. You wiped no wood.

You carried the noes to their real addresses.

And the console that was drowning

now holds only its door-job things.

Now, the part that stalls most people.

It is not the objects. It is the guilt.

You pick up the bowl that was a gift.

And moving it starts to feel like a verdict.

So here is the sentence to keep close.

You are not deciding what it is worth.

You are only deciding it does not live here.

Moving a thing out is not throwing it away.

The charger went to the desk.

It was not donated, or sold, or discarded.

It moved.

You are only changing an address inside your own home.

When removing stops feeling like throwing away,

you stop flinching.

And when you stop flinching, you finish.

Now go look at the friction meter.

For four chapters it held perfectly still.

Because nothing had physically moved.

But today, objects moved.

The space is measurably lighter than it was this morning.

So the needle finally steps off the friction side.

And travels toward calm.

Read it honestly, though.

It stops in the middle.

Pointed at the goal, but not arrived.

Sort is only the first of six S's.

Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain are still ahead.

So it stops partway.

Far enough to feel real.

Short of a finish you have not yet earned.

Here is your job.

Stand in your space.

Say its one job out loud.

Pick up the first thing your eye lands on.

Ask the One Question.

If it does not help the space do its job, carry it to where it belongs.

One object is a legal, complete Sort.

And do not buy bins first.

Storage bought first just locks in the clutter.

Remove what does not belong, then buy for what is left.

If you even still need to.

You sorted by belonging.

Next we sort by need.

Go run one pass.
