## 1. Sort before you shop

The usual advice for a messy space is to go buy storage. Bins, baskets, a nice tray. It feels like progress, but storage bought first just organizes the clutter and locks it in place.

There is a free step that comes before that. Separate what belongs in the space from what does not, and carry the noes somewhere else. That is Sort, the first of the six S's.

Sort first, and most of the mess walks out the door before you buy a single bin.

## 2. It is an occupancy problem

The clutter on your entryway console is not a filing problem. It is an occupancy problem.

Most of what makes a space feel chaotic is not disorder. It is the sheer presence of things that were never supposed to be there. The charger, the read magazines, the loose homework, the bowl that quietly collects coins.

None of that needs a better system. It needs to leave. Notice the things that do not belong, and show them the way out.

## 3. The one question

Here is the whole test for any object in a space. Does this help the space do its job?

That is it. You hold up an object, you look at the job the space is for, and you ask whether this thing helps that job happen. Yes means it belongs here. No means it belongs somewhere else.

No more interrogating each object about your whole life. One small, answerable question, over and over, until the surface is clear.

## 4. Separating is the easy half

Sort has two halves, and you do them in order. First you separate what belongs from what does not. Then you remove what does not.

People love the first half and skip the second, which is why so many organizing afternoons end with the room exactly as full as it started, only rearranged.

As the chapter puts it: "Separating is thinking. Removing is the part that changes the room."

So do the removing for real.

## 5. Check the address, not the object

The usual way to declutter is to pick up each thing and put it on trial. Do I still like this. Will I use it someday. Who gave it to me.

The One Question skips all of that. It does not ask what the object means. It asks whether it helps this space do its one job.

A phone charger is genuinely useful, and for the entryway the answer is still no, because charging phones is not what the entryway is for. "You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address."

## 6. Removing is not throwing away

The thing that stalls a Sort is not the objects. It is guilt.

You pick up something that does not belong, and a simple move suddenly feels like a verdict. The bowl was a gift. The magazines cost money. So the hand hesitates, the thing goes back down, and the pass dies.

Keep this close: you are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here. Nothing has been donated, sold, or thrown out. It has moved. And it is completely reversible.

## 7. Most things already have a home

When an item belongs somewhere else, where does it go? For most of what you pull off a surface, the answer is easy, because the thing already has a home and just got lazy.

The charger goes to the desk. The screwdriver goes to the toolbox. The sweater goes to the closet. A ten-second walk each.

"You are not inventing homes. You are returning things to the ones they wandered from." The discipline is simply to make the walk, not to set the thing down two feet away and call it progress.

## 8. Park the maybes

A few objects will stump you. No obvious home, or you honestly cannot tell whether they belong here or not.

Do not solve those on the spot. If you stop to settle each hard one, the whole pass grinds to a halt. Set them aside in one place, a box or a corner, and keep moving.

A maybe is not a failure and it is not a decision. It is just a thing that waits in one known spot while you finish the clear calls, which are the great majority.

## 9. The needle finally moves

Through four chapters of preparing, nothing physical had changed, so a friction meter for the space held perfectly still. A gauge that swings when nothing moved is just flattery.

Then you Sort. You carry the magazines and the charger and the bowl out to their real homes, and the space is measurably emptier than it was this morning. That is the first real change, done by hand, no money spent.

It steps toward calm but stops partway, because Sort is only the first of six S's. That honest, partial move is the point.

## 10. Sort by belonging, then by need

Do one Sort pass and the surface changes. What is left all belongs here, in the sense that it helps the space do its job.

That does not yet mean you need to own all of it, or that three of those things are not quietly doing the same job. That is a sharper question, and it comes next.

You sorted by belonging. Next we sort by need. But belonging first, because removing what does not live here is the biggest single step.
