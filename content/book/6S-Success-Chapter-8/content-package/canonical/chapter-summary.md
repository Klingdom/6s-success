# Chapter 8 Summary: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three, Sort*

## One-line summary

Sort is the first S and the first physical move in the book: hold every object up to one question, does this help the space do its job, and carry out everything that answers no, so most of the mess leaves before you buy a single thing.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Chapter 8 opens Part Three and turns four chapters of preparation into action. Sort, the English name for Seiri, has one aim: separate what belongs in a space from what does not, then remove what does not. It is not tidying and not cleaning. The reader turns the purpose written in Chapter 7 into the One Question, does this help the space do its job, and runs a worked pass through the entryway drop zone: keys and umbrella stay, magazines and a stray charger go to a better home. Removing is only an address change, not a verdict. For the first time, the friction meter moves, and stops partway.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 8 begins Part Three, Sort, and delivers the payoff of all the preparing that came before. The reader chose the entryway drop zone in Chapter 4, scored it 14 out of 30 in Chapter 5, photographed it in Chapter 6, and gave it a purpose in Chapter 7. Now, for the first time, an object actually moves.

The chapter defines Sort carefully. It is not tidying, which only rearranges a pile, and not cleaning, which is Shine, three chapters away. Sort is the structural step before both: it asks of every object whether it lives here, keeps the yeses, and removes the noes. The idea is Seiri, the first of the factory five S's, and the reframe is that clutter is an occupancy problem, not a filing problem. Sort has two halves, separate and remove, and the chapter insists on doing the second, which most people skip.

The hero tool is the One Question, the operational form of the purpose: does this help the space do its job? A worked pass through the drop zone splits its contents into belongs here and belongs somewhere else, and the surface gets lighter with nothing bought. The clear items go to their real homes; the honest maybes wait in one spot for a system a few chapters later. A calm anti-shame section reframes removing as an address change, not a judgment of worth or a decision to throw anything away.

Then the friction meter, held still for four chapters, finally moves toward calm, and stops in the middle, because Sort is only the first of six S's. Next, Chapter 9 sorts what is left by need.
