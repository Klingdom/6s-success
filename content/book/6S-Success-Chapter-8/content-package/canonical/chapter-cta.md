# Chapter 8 Calls to Action: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

This is the first action chapter. Its strongest CTA is running a real Sort pass, even a one-object one. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: removing is an address change, not a verdict.

## Primary CTA
Run your first Sort pass. "Stand in your space, say its one job out loud, and ask one question of every object: does this help the space do its job? Carry out everything that answers no. Even one object is a legal, complete Sort. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Before you buy a single bin, remove what does not belong. Chapter 8 gives you one question that sorts any space fast, without the guilt. Read it free online, then Chapter 9 sorts what is left by need."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Stand in your space, say its one job out loud, then pick up the first thing your eye lands on and ask the One Question. If it does not help the space do its job, carry it to where it actually belongs. One object. That is a legal, complete Sort.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone grabs three things from the space that do not belong there and walks each one to its real home. Nine things gone in five minutes, no arguing about anyone's stuff but your own.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is doing a pass, however small. Lead with "ask one question and carry out the noes," which is concrete and can be done in minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and low-stakes. Removing is only an address change; the reader decides where their own things live.
- "Sort by address, not by fate" and "removing what was never supposed to be there" are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The claim is modest and true: remove what does not belong, and most of the mess leaves before you buy a single bin.
