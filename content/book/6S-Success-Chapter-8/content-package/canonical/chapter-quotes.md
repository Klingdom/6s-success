Pull quotes lifted directly from the manuscript and final HTML, ready for quote cards, social posts, and graphics. Every line below was grep-verified as present in chapter_08_manuscript.md. All are verbatim or lightly trimmed. Nothing here is invented.

## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "This is Sort, the first of the six S's, and it is the simplest one to describe and the most satisfying one to feel."

2. "Sort is not tidying."

3. "Sort is not cleaning either."

4. "The clutter is not a filing problem. It is an occupancy problem, and step one is simply to notice the squatters and show them the way out."

5. "Separating is thinking. Removing is the part that changes the room."

6. "Does this help the space do its job?"

7. "You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address."

8. "A better home means the place the object actually lives, and the pass works because most objects already have one."

9. "You are not inventing homes. You are returning things to the ones they wandered from."

10. "You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here."

11. "You are a person deciding where your belongings live, which is an ordinary, kind, and completely reversible thing to do, not a judge handing down sentences."

12. "When removing stops feeling like throwing away, you stop flinching, and when you stop flinching, you finish."

13. "The needle stops in the middle, pointed at the goal but not arrived, and there is a good reason for the restraint."

14. "most of what overwhelms us was never disorder to be organized, only surplus to be removed."

15. "You sorted by belonging. Next we sort by need."

## Definition box (canonical)

"Separate what belongs in a space from what does not, and remove what does not. Judge by one question, the space's job, not by the object's worth. Sort is not tidying and not cleaning. It is deciding what lives here."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"Sort by address, not by fate. Today you are only deciding where a thing lives, not whether to keep or release it. Move the clear ones, park the maybes in one spot, and do not let a single hard object stall the whole pass."

## One Idea to Keep (canonical close)

"You do not organize a space by adding storage. You organize it by removing what was never supposed to be there. Sort first, and most of the mess walks out the door before you buy a single bin."

## The friction-meter caption (for graphics)

"The needle moves for the first time. Nothing was bought and nothing was cleaned. Objects simply left, and the space is already lighter. Five S's still to come, so it stops partway, pointed at the goal."

## The One Question (for graphics)

The hero device: every object passes through one gate, "Does this help the space do its job?" Yes means "belongs here" (green calm-dot, it stays). No means "belongs somewhere else" and goes to "a better home" (arrow out of the space).
