# Chapter 8 Key Takeaways: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **Sort is the first S, and the first time you move a thing.** Four chapters of preparing are done. Sort has one aim: separate what belongs in a space from what does not, and carry the noes somewhere else.

2. **Sort is not tidying and not cleaning.** Tidying leaves the pile in place; cleaning is Shine, later. Sort is the structural step before both, deciding what actually lives here.

3. **Clutter is an occupancy problem, not a filing problem.** Most of what makes a space chaotic is the sheer presence of things that were never supposed to be there. Step one is to show them the way out.

4. **Sort has two halves, and you must do the second.** First separate what belongs from what does not, then remove. People love the separating and skip the removing, which is why rooms end up merely rearranged.

5. **The One Question tests every object.** Does this help the space do its job? Yes means it belongs here; no means it belongs somewhere else and goes to a better home. The purpose you wrote becomes a tool you can point at things.

6. **You are checking the object's address, not judging the object.** A phone charger is genuinely useful and still fails, because charging phones is not what the entryway is for.

7. **A better home is where the thing actually lives.** Most items already have one, so the move is a ten-second walk. The discipline is to make the walk, not to set the thing down two feet away and call it progress.

8. **The honest maybes wait in one spot.** Do not solve every hard object on the spot or the pass grinds to a halt. Park the "I am not sure" items together and keep moving; they get their own system a few chapters on.

9. **Removing is not throwing away.** Sorting an item out of a space is only an address change inside your home, not a decision to donate, sell, or discard it.

10. **Sort by address, not by fate.** You are not deciding what a thing is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here, which is ordinary, kind, and completely reversible.

11. **Guilt, not the objects, is what stalls a Sort.** When a simple move starts to feel like a verdict, the hand hesitates and the pass dies. When removing stops feeling like throwing away, you stop flinching, and you finish.

12. **The friction meter moves for the first time, and stops partway.** Objects actually moved, so the needle finally travels toward calm, done by hand with no money spent. It stops in the middle because Sort is only the first of six S's.

13. **Do not buy storage before you Sort.** Bins bought first just organize the clutter and lock it in. Remove what does not belong, then buy for what is left, if you even still need to.

14. **You sorted by belonging; next you sort by need.** Everything that stayed belongs here, but that does not yet mean you need to own all of it. That harder question is Chapter 9.
