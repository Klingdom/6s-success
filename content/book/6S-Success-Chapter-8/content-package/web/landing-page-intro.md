# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 8, Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

For four chapters you have prepared and not moved a single thing. You chose the drop zone by your door, gave it an honest score, took its before photo, and wrote it one job: this entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic. Today the waiting is over. Today you pick something up and it leaves.

This is Sort, the first of the six S's, and it is the simplest one to describe. It has one aim: separate the things that belong in a space from the things that do not, then carry the ones that do not somewhere else. That is the whole move. You are not going to buy anything or wipe a single surface. You are going to hold up each object on that console and ask one question, with the purpose sentence as the judge.

The question is the same for the keys and for the coupons and for the stray screwdriver: does this help the space do its job? If yes, it belongs here, and it stays. If no, it belongs somewhere else, and its job today is to go to a better home, the place it already lives. The charger goes to the desk. The sweater goes to the closet. Notice that this is much easier than the usual method, which picks up each object and interrogates its whole relationship to your life. The One Question does not ask what a thing is worth. It just checks its address.

And here is the part that lifts the weight off: removing is not throwing away. You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here. That is an ordinary, reversible thing to do, not a verdict. Move the clear ones, park the few maybes in one spot, and keep going. When it stops feeling like a judgment, you stop flinching, and you finish.
