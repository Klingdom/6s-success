## Four chapters of preparing. Today you actually move something.

You have decluttered that spot before. You made three piles, you agonized over each object, you got a headache, and by evening the surface was rearranged but just as full. So it drifted right back, and you quietly decided you were bad at this.

You are not bad at this. The usual method is exhausting because it asks the wrong thing. It picks up each object and interrogates its whole relationship to your life: do I still like this, will I use it someday, who gave it to me. That is a punishing way to spend a Saturday, and it is why so many of us stall out with half-sorted piles and no room left.

## One question sorts any space fast

Chapter 8 of *6S Success: Home Edition*, "Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong," opens Part Three, and it is the first doing chapter in the book. Sort has exactly one aim: separate the things that belong in a space from the things that do not, then carry the ones that do not somewhere else. It is not tidying and not cleaning. It is deciding what actually lives here.

The tool is one question you ask of every object: does this help the space do its job? If yes, it belongs here and stays. If no, it belongs somewhere else and goes to a better home, wherever it actually lives. A phone charger is genuinely useful and the answer is still no, because charging phones is not what an entryway is for. You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address.

## What you get in this chapter

- The One Question, a single test that replaces the whole "do I still like this" checklist
- A worked pass through a real entryway drop zone, object by object, that you can copy onto your own space
- Why removing is not throwing away, and the one sentence that takes the guilt out of every decision: you are not deciding what it is worth, you are only deciding it does not live here
- What to do with the honest maybes, so one hard object does not stall the whole pass
- Why buying bins first only locks the clutter in place, and what to do instead

You will not shop for anything. You will not wipe a surface. In an afternoon, with no money spent, you actually clear a space, and for the first time in the book the friction meter moves. It stops partway on purpose, because Sort is only the first of six S's, and that is exactly where an honest first step should stop.

Read the chapter, ask one question of everything on one surface, and carry out the noes. Even one object is a legal, complete Sort.
