# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

*Chapter 8, 6S Success: Home Edition. Use these for personal reflection or a group discussion. They are open-ended, with no single right answer.*

1. The chapter defines Sort as separating what belongs in a space from what does not, then removing what does not. It also says Sort is not tidying and not cleaning. Think of a time you "organized" a space. Were you sorting, or were you really just tidying and leaving the pile in place?

2. The chapter reframes clutter as an occupancy problem, not a filing problem, most of the mess being things that were never supposed to be there. Which space in your home is crowded less by disorder and more by squatters that belong somewhere else?

3. The two halves of Sort are separating and removing, and the chapter says people love the first and skip the second. Where have you done the thinking half and stalled before actually carrying anything out? What made you stop?

4. The One Question is: does this help the space do its job? How does asking that feel different from the usual questions, like do I still like this or will I use it someday?

5. The chapter says, "You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address." Pick one useful object sitting in the wrong space right now. Is it a genuinely good thing that simply lives at the wrong address?

6. Where "belongs somewhere else" goes is often obvious, and the discipline is to make the ten-second walk instead of setting the thing down two feet away and calling it progress. Where do you tend to set things down halfway rather than return them?

7. The chapter separates removing from getting rid of, and offers the sentence, "You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here." When has guilt about an object's worth or cost kept you from moving it, and how might that sentence change the moment?

8. The chapter says the guilt, not the objects, is what stalls a Sort, because a simple move starts to feel like a verdict. Where in your home would you flinch first, and what makes that particular object feel heavy to move?

9. For the honest maybes, the advice is to set them in one spot and keep moving rather than solving each on the spot. What would go in your maybe pile, and how do you usually let those uncertain items stall the whole afternoon?

10. For the first time in the book the friction meter moves, and it stops partway on purpose, because Sort is only the first of six S's. If you ran one real pass this week, how far would your own needle move, and what would still be left for the steps ahead, including the harder question in the next chapter of what you actually need to own?
