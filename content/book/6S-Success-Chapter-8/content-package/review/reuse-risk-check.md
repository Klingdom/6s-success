# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 8 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it. Every element named below was grep-verified in the manuscript before being listed.

## Same-day collision check
The calendar is built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, do not let the "you are only deciding it does not live here" reassurance and the "checking its address" line both land the same day, since they carry the same anti-shame beat.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same One Question content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version (`linkedin/linkedin-newsletter-version.md`) and the email newsletter version (`newsletter/newsletter-version.md`) share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, and the like) to point back with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Part 3, Sort)
- **The entryway drop zone is now a five-chapter running example.** It was chosen in Chapter 4, audited at 14 out of 30 in Chapter 5, photographed in Chapter 6, given a purpose in Chapter 7, and gets its first Sort pass here. When Part 2 and Part 3 assets are live in the same weeks, vary which step each post leads with so the same console does not feel overused. The number, the photo, and the purpose all pay off in this chapter; the friction-meter first move is the payoff of all four.
- **Keep Chapter 8 distinct from Chapter 9 inside Part 3.** Chapter 8 sorts by belonging ("does it belong in THIS space"). Chapter 9 sorts by need ("do you need to own it at all"). The manuscript itself draws the line, verbatim: "You sorted by belonging. Next we sort by need." Do not let Chapter 8 assets drift into the own-it-or-not question, and do not run the two chapters' posts so close that the Chapter 9 payoff is spoiled.
- **Hold the later Part 3 seams.** Chapter 10 (holding areas and the maybes system) and Chapter 11 (donate/sell/discard) are teased only lightly here ("the maybes get a system in a couple of chapters"). Keep those teasers as teasers in the social assets; do not let a post promise or build those systems early.
- **The friction meter is now a moving asset.** From Chapter 8 on it will keep stepping toward calm. Make sure Chapter 8's "first move, stops partway" framing is not contradicted by any asset that shows it further along than the manuscript does.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter itself is a draft authored in the book voice, not supplied source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft. If the author revises the chapter, refresh the assets so they do not drift from the published version.
- Nothing is borrowed from outside the book. The ten quote cards are verbatim from the manuscript (each string grep-verified). The two alternates at the end of `graphics/quote-card-copy.md` are labeled: one verbatim (the One Idea to Keep), one lightly trimmed (a leading capital added and the "that most of what..." lead-in dropped so the fragment stands alone). That labeling is accurate.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate the six CTA phrasings across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep the drop-zone example and the Part 2 to Part 3 seam from colliding in the same week, and keep Chapter 8 "sort by belonging" clearly separate from Chapter 9 "sort by need."
5. Preserve the "first move, stops partway" friction-meter framing in every asset that shows the gauge.
6. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
