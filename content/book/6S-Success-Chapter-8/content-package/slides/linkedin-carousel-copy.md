# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 8. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**Before you buy a single bin, do this.**

Sort first, and most of the mess walks out the door before you spend a cent.

Swipe for the whole move.

[VISUAL] The drop zone from the before photo, seen fresh. A few items wear a green calm-dot, a few throw a red spark, and a hand hovers, about to lift the first thing. Warm paper, slate chapter label.

---

### Slide 2

Storage bought first just locks the clutter in.

A neat bin around a room that was already holding too much is not progress.

It is contained overload.

[VISUAL] A tidy bin drawn around a still-crowded shelf, the bin outline in soft grey and crossed out. No triumph, just a quiet "not yet."

---

### Slide 3

It is an occupancy problem.

Clutter is not a filing problem. Most of the chaos is the sheer presence of things that were never supposed to be there.

[VISUAL] A crowded shelf where most items are faintly greyed and drifting toward an exit arrow. Slate label: "AN OCCUPANCY PROBLEM."

---

### Slide 4

Separate what belongs, remove what does not.

That is the whole aim of Sort, the first of six S's.

Buy nothing, clean nothing, just decide what lives here.

[VISUAL] Two panels: "SEPARATE" splits items into two soft groups, "REMOVE" walks the second group out a door. Footer strip: "BELONGS HERE · BELONGS SOMEWHERE ELSE."

---

### Slide 5

One question tests every object.

Does this help the space do its job?

Yes, it belongs here. No, it belongs somewhere else.

[VISUAL] The One Question gate. Objects flow toward "Does this help the space do its job?" and split: up into a green "BELONGS HERE" zone, down and out via an arrow into a slate "A BETTER HOME" zone.

---

### Slide 6

You are checking the address, not judging the object.

A phone charger is genuinely useful. For the entryway the answer is still no, because charging phones is not the job.

[VISUAL] A charger held to a small gate. A green "useful" tick beside it, the gate answer "no, belongs somewhere else," a slate out-arrow to a desk icon.

---

### Slide 7

Separating is thinking. Removing changes the room.

People love the split and skip the carry-out, which is why rooms end up rearranged, not lighter.

Do the removing for real.

[VISUAL] A split pile on the left, an arrow, and the "somewhere else" group actually leaving through a door on the right. Green dots on the stayers, slate arrows on the leavers.

---

### Slide 8

Most things already have a home.

Charger to the desk. Screwdriver to the toolbox. Sweater to the closet.

You are returning things to the ones they wandered from.

[VISUAL] A short trail of items walking back to labeled homes, desk, toolbox, closet, each arriving with a green calm-dot.

---

### Slide 9

Do not let one hard object stall the pass.

Set the honest maybes in one spot and keep moving.

They get their own system a couple of chapters on.

[VISUAL] Clear items moving briskly to their homes, and one small honey-labeled "MAYBES" box set calmly to the side. One tidy box, not a pile.

---

### Slide 10

You are not deciding what it is worth.

You are only deciding it does not live here. Nothing donated, sold, or thrown out. It moved.

Completely reversible.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. No sparks or dots; slate does the work.

---

### Slide 11

For the first time, the meter moves.

Objects actually left, so the friction needle steps toward calm.

It stops partway, because Sort is only the first of six S's.

[VISUAL] The friction meter, moved. The needle stepped into the honey middle toward calm, a faint dashed ghost of the old position on the friction side, the GOAL crosshair still ahead with its slate PURPOSE flag.

---

### Slide 12 (soft CTA)

You sorted by belonging. Next, by need.

This is from Chapter 8 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Read the full chapter and grab the One Question card, free in the online book, then Chapter 9 sorts what is left by need.

[VISUAL] The moved friction meter, smaller, beside a forward arrow to a slate "CH 9" tab. Footer: "You have removed what does not belong here. Next, whether you need it at all."
