### Slide 3: What Sort actually is

- Sort is not tidying. A tidied pile is still a pile.
- Sort is not cleaning. That is Shine, three chapters from now.
- Sort is the step before both: does this thing live here, yes or no? The yeses stay. The noes leave.

[VISUAL] Three stacked bands. Top two, "not tidying" and "not cleaning," shown faint and crossed through in soft #6A625A. Bottom band bright: "deciding what lives here" with one green calm-dot and one red friction-spark.

### Slide 4: It is an occupancy problem

- Sort is the English name for Seiri, the first of the factory five S's, where clutter was slow and unsafe.
- Before you organize anything, remove what does not belong in the area at all.
- The clutter is not a filing problem. It is an occupancy problem.

[VISUAL] A crowded shelf where most items are faintly greyed "squatters" drifting toward an exit arrow, and only a few crisp items remain. Soft #6A625A for the leaving group, ink for the stayers. Label in slate: "AN OCCUPANCY PROBLEM."

### Slide 5: Separate, then remove

- Sort has two halves, done in order. First separate what belongs here from what belongs somewhere else. Then remove.
- People love the first half and skip the second, so rooms end up rearranged, not lighter.
- Separating is thinking. Removing is the part that changes the room.

[VISUAL] Two panels connected by an arrow. Panel one, "SEPARATE," a split of items into two soft groups. Panel two, "REMOVE," the second group walking out a door. Footer strip: "BELONGS HERE · BELONGS SOMEWHERE ELSE."

### Slide 6: The One Question

- Turn the purpose you wrote into a single gate every item passes through.
- Does this help the space do its job? Yes, it belongs here, and it stays with a green calm-dot. No, it belongs somewhere else, and it goes to a better home.
- That is the entire test. Same question for the keys, the coupons, the screwdriver, the sweater.

[VISUAL] The hero device, straight from the book. Objects flow toward a central gate reading "Does this help the space do its job?" and split. Up into a green zone "BELONGS HERE" (calm-dots). Down and out via an arrow into a slate zone "A BETTER HOME." Footer, slate italic: "One gate. Two answers. The noes flow out."

### Slide 7: Check the address, not the object

- The usual way to declutter interrogates each object's whole relationship to your life. That is exhausting, and it stalls.
- The One Question refuses all of that. It asks one small, answerable thing: does this help this particular space do its one particular job?
- A phone charger is genuinely useful, and the answer is still no. You are not judging the charger. You are just checking its address.

[VISUAL] A charger held up to a small gate. A green "useful" tick beside it, but the gate answer reads "no, belongs somewhere else" with a slate out-arrow to a desk icon. Calm, almost diagrammatic. Terracotta accent line: "checking the address, not the worth."

### Slide 8: A pass through the drop zone

- Belongs here: keys, outgoing mail to post, sunglasses, the dog's leash, a folded reusable bag, the umbrella, a small tray for keys and wallet.
- Belongs somewhere else: already-read magazines, junk mail, a phone charger (desk), finished homework and school papers, a stray screwdriver, a shed sweater (closet), the coin-collecting bowl.
- Nothing was bought. The console now holds only its door-job things, and the surface is calmer.

[VISUAL] The worked pass, straight from the book. Two columns. Left, "BELONGS HERE," seven door-job items each with a green calm-dot. Right, "BELONGS SOMEWHERE ELSE," seven items each with a slate out-arrow and its real home in parentheses. Footer, slate italic: "Judged only by the job: out the door fast, soft landing back in."

### Slide 9: Where "belongs somewhere else" goes

- Most items already have an obvious home, so the move is a ten-second walk. A better home means the place the object actually lives.
- The discipline is to make the walk, not to set the thing down two feet away and call it progress.
- The honest maybes wait in one spot while you finish the clear calls. They get their own system a couple of chapters on.

[VISUAL] A short trail of items walking back to labeled homes (desk, toolbox, closet) with green arrival dots. Off to one side, a single small "maybes" box with a soft honey label, set aside and calm, not a pile. Slate caption: "Returning things to the ones they wandered from."

---

### Slide 10: It is not about throwing away

- The thing that stalls a Sort is not the objects. It is guilt: a simple address change starts to feel like a verdict, the hand hesitates, and the pass dies.
- You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here.
- The bowl can be lovely and still not belong on the launch pad. Deciding where your belongings live is ordinary, kind, and completely reversible.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel #FBF7EF. The line "You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here." set in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. No sparks or dots; slate does the work.

### Slide 12: Do not buy storage first

- Buying bins and baskets before you Sort just organizes the clutter and locks it in place.
- You do not organize a space by adding storage. You organize it by removing what was never supposed to be there.
- Sort first, and most of the mess walks out the door before you buy a single bin.
