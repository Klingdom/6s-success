# Visual Storyboard

## Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 8. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

---

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_08_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper #F7F2E9: slide and card backgrounds
- Panel #FBF7EF: card fill, definition and idea boxes
- Ink #2B2622: primary text, object outlines
- Soft #6A625A: captions, secondary axis labels
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: primary accent, chapter kicker, the first-move highlight
- Honey #DDA63A: secondary accent, the middle of the meter, the maybes box
- Slate #3C5A6B: chapter labels, the gate border, the GOAL flag, the "belongs somewhere else" side
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: belongs here, the stays side
- Friction spark #CB4B36: belongs somewhere else, the friction side of the meter
- Rules and hairlines #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8: dividers, dashed lines
- Display font Fraunces: titles, pull quotes, the gate question, the two headlines
- Body font Newsreader: worked pass, captions, running text
- Label font Inter: uppercase, wide letter-spacing, for kickers, tags, axis labels

**The recurring motif.** A green calm-dot #6E8B5B marks what belongs here and stays. A red friction-spark #CB4B36 marks what belongs somewhere else and leaves. This pairing carries the whole chapter's argument visually. Keep it consistent on every beat. Honey stays warm and neutral, never used to signal good or bad; here it also carries the middle of the meter and the maybes box, both of which are honest waypoints, not verdicts.

## Beat 1: The drop zone, seen fresh

The chapter and both decks open the same way. The entryway drop zone from the before photo, but now a few objects wear a green calm-dot and a few throw a red spark, and a hand hovers, about to lift the first thing.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm morning light, the same cluttered console as Chapter 6's before photo. A hand reaches in from the edge.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, ink #2B2622 for the clutter, green #6E8B5B dots on the keys tray and umbrella, red #CB4B36 sparks on the magazines, charger, and coin bowl.
- Motif: this is the first frame where dots and sparks appear on real objects. Keep the tagging light: a few of each, not every item.
- Caption line: "You have a number, a picture, and a purpose. Now, for the first time, you pick something up and it leaves."

---

## Beat 2: Not tidying, not cleaning

Set the definition by ruling out the near neighbors before stating what Sort is.

- Composition: three stacked bands. Top, "TIDYING," a neatened pile still fully present. Middle, "CLEANING," a polished but still-crowded surface. Both drawn faint and ruled through with a soft diagonal line in #6A625A.
- Bottom band bright and decided: "SORT: deciding what lives here," with one green calm-dot and one red friction-spark side by side.
- Palette: soft #6A625A for the ruled-out neighbors, ink and the two motif colors for the live band.
- Point: Sort is the structural step before both, not a gentler word for either.

---

## Beat 3: An occupancy problem

Establish the reframe that powers the whole chapter.

- Composition: a crowded shelf where the majority of items are faintly greyed, drifting toward an exit arrow at the right edge. Only a few crisp, ink-outlined items stay put.
- Motif: the leaving group is the surplus, not disorder. No sparks needed here; the greying does the work. Slate #3C5A6B label: "AN OCCUPANCY PROBLEM, NOT A FILING PROBLEM."
- Palette: soft #6A625A for the squatters, ink for the stayers, paper ground.
- Nod to origin: a small Inter footnote in soft grey, "Sort is the English name for Seiri," no logos, no overclaiming.

---

## Beat 4: The One Question gate

The chapter's hero device and the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: incoming objects on the left flow along a slate arrow into a central gate. The gate is a rounded panel #FBF7EF with a 3px slate #3C5A6B border reading, in Fraunces, "Does this help the space do its job?" with a small Inter line beneath, "yes stays · no leaves."
- YES branch: a green #6E8B5B path curves up to a pale green zone #E8EFE0 labeled "BELONGS HERE," carrying four green calm-dots and the items keys, umbrella, leash, bag.
- NO branch: a slate #3C5A6B path curves down to a zone "A BETTER HOME" with the items magazines, mail, charger, then an arrow leaving the frame entirely.
- Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "One gate. Two answers. The noes flow out."
- This is the clearest single frame of the dot-and-spark logic. Everything else echoes it.

## Beat 5: Checking the address

A quiet single-object beat that defends the method against guilt and overthinking.

- Composition: one phone charger held up to a small gate. A green "genuinely useful" tick sits beside the charger, yet the gate answer reads "no, belongs somewhere else" with a slate out-arrow to a desk icon.
- Motif: the tick and the "no" coexist on purpose. Useful and belongs-here are different questions. Terracotta #BC4B2A underline on the phrase "checking the address, not the worth."
- Palette: paper ground, slate for the gate and arrow, green only on the small tick, no red spark here because the point is that a no is not a judgment.

## Beat 6: The first pass, two columns

The worked example, used as a copyable template. Rebuild from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: one canvas split by a vertical dashed divider in #D9CDB8.
- Left, green header bar #6E8B5B "BELONGS HERE": seven rows, each with a green calm-dot #6E8B5B, in Newsreader: keys; outgoing mail to post; sunglasses; the dog's leash; a folded reusable bag; the umbrella; a small tray for keys and wallet.
- Right, slate header bar #3C5A6B "BELONGS SOMEWHERE ELSE": seven rows, each with a slate out-arrow and its real home in parentheses: already-read magazines; junk mail (recycling); phone charger (desk); homework and school papers; a stray screwdriver (toolbox); a shed sweater (closet); the coin-collecting bowl.
- Footer, slate #3C5A6B italic Newsreader: "Judged only by the job: out the door fast, soft landing back in."
- Note: the two header bars carry the frozen outcome words. Do not paraphrase "belongs here" or "belongs somewhere else."

## Beat 7: A better home, and the maybes

How the noes actually leave, and what to do with the honest maybes without pre-empting Chapter 10.

- Composition: a short trail of three items walking back to labeled homes, desk, toolbox, closet, each arriving with a green calm-dot #6E8B5B.
- Off to one side, a single small box with a soft honey #DDA63A label "MAYBES," set aside and calm. Draw it as one tidy box, not a pile, and do not build a system around it. A small Inter teaser in soft grey: "they get their own system a couple of chapters on."
- Palette: paper ground, ink items, green arrival dots, honey only on the maybes box.
- Caption idea: "You are returning things to the ones they wandered from."

---

## Beat 8: It is not about throwing away

The anti-shame center of the chapter. A pause beat, restrained.

- Composition: the book's pull-quote treatment. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here."
- Palette: no sparks, no dots. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the room going quiet.
- Tone note: the point is permission, not shame. An address change is reversible; keep the frame light.

---

## Beat 9: The needle moves

The landmark payoff, and the close of both decks. Rebuild from the HTML SVG, and make it visibly different from the four "held" chapters.

- Composition: the semicircular friction meter. The arc runs green #6E8B5B (CALM) on the left, honey #DDA63A in the middle, friction spark #CB4B36 (FRICTION) on the right.
- The move: the live needle has stepped off the friction side into the honey middle, pointing up toward calm, labeled NOW in ink. A faint dashed ghost in #B9AC96 shows where it sat before, tagged "was here," so the distance traveled is unmistakable.
- The GOAL marker: a slate #3C5A6B crosshair on the calm side, still ahead of the needle, carrying its slate PURPOSE flag from Chapter 7.
- Footer caption, verbatim: "The needle moves for the first time. Nothing was bought and nothing was cleaned. Objects simply left, and the space is already lighter. Five S's still to come, so it stops partway, pointed at the goal."
- Motif close: the needle moves because objects moved, and stops partway because Sort is only the first of six steps. Honest, not triumphant.

## Carrying it across the set

- Open every sequence with the drop zone seen fresh; close every sequence with the moved friction meter. The two bookend the first action chapter.
- Use Fraunces only for the load-bearing lines: titles, the gate question, the pull quote, the two column headers. Keep running copy in Newsreader so the slides breathe.
- Never let honey signal correctness. Belongs is green, belongs-somewhere-else is red, and that two-color logic should be legible even in a thumbnail. Honey carries only neutral waypoints: the middle of the meter and the maybes box.
- The frozen outcome words "belongs here" and "belongs somewhere else" and "a better home" appear on the gate and the two-column figures. Copy them exactly.
- Keep generous paper margins and a single idea per frame. The chapter's voice is calm, concrete, and anti-shame; the visuals should feel the same, decided but never loud, with no force or hype imagery.
