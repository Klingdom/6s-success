# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 8

**"Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are
working specs, not finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in
`chapter_08_final.html`: the tagged drop zone, the One Question gate, the worked pass, and
the friction meter moved for the first time.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`,
ink `#2B2622`, soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`,
calm green `#6E8B5B`, friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`.

Scope fence: this chapter sorts by "does it belong in this space," not by "do you need to
own it" (Chapter 9), and it does not use red tags or a holding-area system (Chapter 10) or
decide donate, sell, or discard (Chapter 11). "A better home" always means where the thing
already lives, an address change, never the giveaway pile.

---

## 1. The One Question (the hero tool)

**Purpose:** Show the whole device in one glance: one gate, two answers, the noes flowing
out of the space. This is the diagram the chapter is built around.

**What it shows:** Every object in the space meets the same single question, and the answer
routes it. Yes keeps it. No sends it to a better home.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE ONE QUESTION`.
- Left: a loose cluster of small drop-zone glyphs (keys, an envelope, a charger, a folded
  bag) labeled beneath in sans, soft gray: "everything in the space". A slate flow arrow
  carries them right into the gate.
- Center: a slate-bordered rounded gate card. Fraunces in ink: "Does this help the space
  do its job?" Helper line beneath in sans, soft gray: "yes stays · no leaves".
- Up-right branch, green: a green-bordered zone tab `BELONGS HERE` with four green calm
  dots and a Newsreader italic line "keys, umbrella, leash, bag".
- Down-right branch, slate: a slate-bordered zone tab `A BETTER HOME` with a Newsreader
  italic line "magazines, mail, charger" and an out-arrow leaving the frame.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "One gate. Two answers. The noes flow out."

---

## 2. Separate, Then Remove (the two halves, in order)

**Purpose:** Make visible the step people skip. Separating is thinking; removing is the
part that changes the room. The chapter insists you do both.

**What it shows:** Two panels read left to right as a sequence, joined by a slate arrow.

**Rough layout:**
- Left panel, header sans caps soft gray `1 · SEPARATE`: the console surface with items
  mentally split into two soft groups, a green-tinted "belongs here" pool and a slate-tinted
  "belongs somewhere else" pool. A small thought-bubble glyph marks this as the thinking
  half. Footer in Fraunces, slate: "Separating is thinking."
- A slate arrow across the seam, labeled in sans: "then".
- Right panel, header sans caps soft gray `2 · REMOVE`: the same console, now lighter, with
  the slate group walking out on out-arrows to their real addresses (desk, closet, toolbox
  shown as small tagged doorways). The surface holds only the green-dotted keepers. Footer
  in Fraunces, green: "Removing changes the room."
- Bottom center, Newsreader italic, slate: "People love the first half and skip the second.
  Do both."

---

## 3. The First Pass (the worked split)

**Purpose:** Give the reader a clean two-column model to copy onto their own space, judged
only by the job. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The drop-zone objects sorted into two labeled columns, each keeper wearing
a green calm-dot, each mover wearing an out-arrow and its real home in parentheses.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE FIRST PASS`. A dashed bone divider down the middle.
- Left column, green header bar `BELONGS HERE`, seven Newsreader rows each with a green
  calm-dot: keys · outgoing mail to post · sunglasses · the dog's leash · a folded reusable
  bag · the umbrella · a small tray for keys and wallet.
- Right column, slate header bar `BELONGS SOMEWHERE ELSE`, seven Newsreader rows each with a
  slate out-arrow and a soft-gray address in parentheses: already-read magazines · junk mail
  (recycling) · phone charger (desk) · homework and school papers · a stray screwdriver
  (toolbox) · a shed sweater (closet) · the coin-collecting bowl.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "Judged only by the job: out the door fast, soft landing
  back in."

---

## 4. Tidying, Cleaning, Sort (what Sort actually is)

**Purpose:** Draw the line the chapter draws in words. Sort is not tidying and not cleaning.
It is the structural step before both.

**What it shows:** Three small cards in a row, the same cluttered surface treated three ways,
so the difference is obvious at a glance.

**Rough layout:**
- Card 1, header sans caps soft gray `TIDYING`: the pile neatened into a straighter pile,
  same count of items. Small soft-gray note: "still a pile". A faint spark dot marks that
  nothing left. Footer Newsreader italic: "Neater, not smaller."
- Card 2, header sans caps soft gray `CLEANING`: a sponge wiping the same crowded surface,
  the items still present. Honey art-note tag: "that is Shine, three chapters on". Footer
  Newsreader italic: "Polishing what should not be there."
- Card 3, header sans caps soft gray `SORT`, drawn with a slate border so it reads as the
  answer: the surface now genuinely lighter, a few green-dotted keepers left, the rest gone
  on out-arrows. Footer in Fraunces, green: "Smaller and truer."
- Bottom center, Newsreader italic, slate: "The clutter is not a filing problem. It is an
  occupancy problem."

## 5. The Friction Meter Moves (the first-time payoff)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the landmark. For the first time in the book the needle
steps off the friction side, because objects actually moved. It stops partway, because Sort
is only the first of six S's. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge, but visibly different from the four
"held" chapters: the live needle has stepped into the honey middle, and a dashed ghost marks
where it used to sit on the friction side, so the distance traveled is unmistakable.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark
  (right), on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- A dashed, muted ghost needle at the old deep-friction position, tagged in small sans, muted
  gray: "was here".
- The live needle in ink, moved into the honey middle zone, pointed up toward calm, tagged
  `NOW`. It rests short of the goal, not at it.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully aimed on the green calm side, still clearly ahead of the
  needle, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag from Chapter 7.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "One S done. The needle finally steps, and stops
  partway, pointed at the goal."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** the needle must read as *moved partway*, resting in the
middle, not swung all the way to calm and not still held on the friction side. The whole
point of the figure is a real first step that has not yet arrived. Five S's are still ahead.
