# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 8

**"Sort: Remove What Does Not Belong"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across
all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single
warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for tension. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth,
real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the entryway drop zone, the console by the front door, the
same surface carried since Chapter 4. This chapter is the first time an object actually moves.
Nothing is bought, nothing is cleaned; things simply leave for where they already live.
Keep the tone calm and kind: removing is an address change, never a verdict, and never a purge.

---

## 1. Lifting the first thing (the opener)

A close, low-angle photograph of a hand hovering over a cluttered entryway console at early
morning, about to lift the first object off it. The surface is genuinely busy: a tangle of
keys, a stack of already-read magazines, a phone charger and cable, a decorative bowl
collecting loose coins, an umbrella leaning at the end. Warm low dawn light rakes in from a
side window, long soft shadows across the wood grain. Palette of warm paper cream, ink brown,
a touch of honey on the brass keys, slate blue in the shadowed wall. Editorial cookbook-style
photography, shallow focus on the hovering hand, the clutter soft behind it, human and quiet.
Nothing is being cleaned or bought; the only thing about to happen is that one object leaves.

- Mood: earned readiness, the first real move after four chapters of preparing.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, a garbage bag
  or bin in frame, any sense of throwing away, dramatic mess for shock value.

## 2. The console after one pass (belongs here, and nothing else)

A tidy, lived-in entryway console photographed in soft morning light, holding only what helps
the space do its one job: a small tray with keys and a wallet, a dog's leash on a hook, one
folded reusable bag, an umbrella, tomorrow's outgoing mail set to ride out the door. The
surface is calm because everything on it earns its place. Warm cream wall, honey-toned wood,
a slate blue runner, a faint sprig of green for life. Natural window light, gentle shadows,
real wear on the wood. The same console as the opener, now measurably lighter, with no bins,
no baskets, and no new storage added.

- Mood: calm competence, a launch pad that actually launches.
- Avoid: showroom minimalism, sterile white, harsh flash, new storage props, oversaturated
  color, anything staged or catalog-perfect.

## 3. The ten-second walk (returning a thing to where it lives)

A warm editorial photograph of a person mid-stride in a hallway, carrying a single phone
charger from the entryway toward a desk where it plainly belongs, cable trailing gently. The
frame catches the small, ordinary act of returning one object to its real address. Soft warm
side light, honey and paper tones, ink-brown shadows, shallow depth on the charger in hand.
Documentary and unstaged, calm rather than urgent. The point is a short, easy walk, not a
big decision, not a discard.

- Mood: light discipline, the small move that actually changes the room.
- Avoid: a trash can or donation box as the destination, urgency or strain, clutter drama,
  cold clinical lighting, tilt-shift toy-camera effect.

## 4. Held against the job (the purpose as ruler)

A close still-life photograph of a hand holding a small handwritten index card up beside an
object lifted off the console, comparing the two. The card reads, in plain ink handwriting,
"out the door fast, soft landing back in". The object being weighed against it is an
everyday one, a decorative bowl. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown handwriting, honey
light on the wood, a single warm-red note only if tension is needed. Editorial, tactile,
shallow focus shared between the card and the object. The image should read as checking an
address, calm and unhurried, not judging the object's worth.

- Mood: quiet, fair appraisal against a plain written standard.
- Avoid: a face frowning in judgment, price tags, guilt cues, harsh contrast, glossy
  magazine retouching, floating typeset text.

## 5. The needle steps (the friction meter, moved for the first time)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle
gauge dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on
the right. The dark needle has stepped for the first time off the red friction side into the
honey middle, pointed up toward calm but resting short of the goal. A faint dashed ghost of
the needle's old position remains on the friction side, so the distance traveled is
unmistakable. A slate crosshair labeled the goal sits fully aimed on the green calm side,
clearly ahead of where the needle now rests. Warm paper cream background, ink line work,
consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish. The gauge should look visibly changed
from a version where the needle sat still.

- Mood: an honest, earned first step, real but not finished.
- Avoid: the needle swung all the way to calm, the needle still pinned on the friction side,
  celebratory confetti or hype, neon color, a completion or victory feel. Five S's remain.
