## The single named target (carried through Part 1 to 2)
The **entryway drop zone** — chosen in Ch 4 (the tie was broken in its favor because you see it every day), audited in Ch 5 at **14 out of 30** (weakest on Standardize and Sustain), photographed in Ch 6 (the before photo), and given its purpose in Ch 7.

## The purpose sentence = the judge (frozen, copy verbatim from Ch 7)
> This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic.

## Hero device (frozen): The One Question
Every object in the space passes through one gate:
> **Does this help the space do its job?**
- If yes → **it belongs here.** (mark with a green calm-dot)
- If no → **it belongs somewhere else.** (it leaves this space and goes to a better home)
This is the operational form of Ch 7's purpose. The purpose is the standard; the One Question is how you apply it, object by object.

## Outcome vocabulary (frozen — use these exact words, not synonyms)
- Items that pass: **"belongs here."**
- Items that fail: **"belongs somewhere else"** / goes to **"a better home."**
- The act: **"Sort"** = separate what belongs here from what does not, and **remove** what does not. Sort is not tidying and not cleaning.
- Do NOT use "keep vs toss/trash/throw out" (that is disposal = Ch 11) and do NOT use "necessary vs unnecessary" (that is Ch 9). Removing an item from this space is only deciding it does not LIVE here, not deciding its fate.

## Worked pass on the drop zone (the concrete example)
Judged against the purpose (fast out the door, soft landing on the way in):
- **Belongs here:** keys, the outgoing mail to post, sunglasses, the dog's leash, a folded reusable bag, the umbrella, a small tray to catch keys and wallet.
- **Belongs somewhere else:** a stack of already-read magazines, junk mail, a phone charger that lives better at a desk, the kids' finished homework and school papers, a stray screwdriver, a sweater that belongs in the closet, a decorative bowl that just collects coins and clutter.
The drop zone gets lighter. Only the door-job things remain.

## Friction meter — the FIRST MOVE (frozen caption)

> **Frozen figure caption:** The needle moves for the first time. Nothing was bought and nothing was cleaned. Objects simply left, and the space is already lighter. Five S's still to come, so it stops partway, pointed at the goal.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> You do not organize a space by adding storage. You organize it by removing what was never supposed to be there. Sort first, and most of the mess walks out the door before you buy a single bin.

## In-chapter callouts (frozen wording for the two actions)
- **Quick Win:** Stand in your space, say its one job out loud, then pick up the first thing your eye lands on and ask the One Question. If it does not help the space do its job, carry it to where it actually belongs. One object. That is a legal, complete Sort.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone grabs three things from the space that do not belong there and walks each one to its real home. Nine things gone in five minutes, no arguing about anyone's stuff but your own.

## Scope discipline (do NOT pre-empt later chapters)
- **Ch 9 (Necessary vs. Unnecessary):** Ch 8 sorts by "does it belong in THIS space," not by "do you need to own it at all." An item can belong somewhere else and still be necessary. Do not open the own-it-or-not question.
- **Ch 10 (Red Tags, Holding Areas, Sorter's Remorse):** do NOT introduce red tags or a formal holding-area system. For the genuinely-homeless or "I am not sure" items, one short forward-teaser is allowed ("the maybes get their own system in a couple of chapters"), but do not build it here.
- **Ch 11 (Donate/Sell/Store/Recycle/Throw):** Ch 8 removes items from the space; it does not decide donate/sell/discard. "A better home" means where it lives now, not the giveaway pile.
- Mirror how Ch 7 set up the sort without doing it: Ch 8 sets up the downstream decisions without doing them.

## Voice and anti-shame guardrails
- Warm, plain, concrete, quietly confident, gently witty, non-prescriptive.
- Removing is not throwing away. Frozen reassurance line: **"You are not deciding what it is worth. You are only deciding it does not live here."**
- NO force/violence metaphors ("purge," "attack the pile," "pull the trigger," "war on clutter"). NO hype words.
- Lexical discipline: do not lean on one payoff word. Likely temptations here: "lighter," "belongs," "space," "gone." Cap any single payoff word at ~3 to 4 uses and vary. Vary the signature sentence cadence; do not reuse a loaded praise-word on both sides of a contrast.
