# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (you can write out exactly what "tidy" means and still lose the argument, because words about how a thing should look are almost infinitely arguable, so the fix is not better words but a photo: take one picture of the space on the day it was right, tape it where the space is, and the argument evaporates) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. The photo is framed as a kindness that ends arguments, never a control imposed on the family. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small thing that quietly ended a running argument in my house, and then I want to hear from you all.

For years I tried to explain what "tidy" meant on our open kitchen shelves. I even wrote it on a card taped inside the cupboard: mugs on the left, handles facing out, tallest at the back, bowls stacked no more than four high. Everyone read the same card, and everyone still built a slightly different shelf. I used to think that was people not paying attention.

Here is the line that finally landed for me.

"The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium."

That was exactly it. The words were fine. The trouble is that words about how a thing should look are almost infinitely arguable, because each of us reads the same careful sentences and pictures something a little different at the other end.

So the book hands you a different tool, and it is already in your pocket.

"A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like. For anything you would struggle to put into words, that is the difference between a standard people argue about and one they can simply see and match."

Here is what I actually did, and it took about a minute. On a rare day when the shelf was exactly right, I took a photo of it and taped the photo inside the cupboard door.

"The most powerful standard in a home is often a single photograph: the shelf, the counter, the entryway, captured on the day it was exactly right. From then on, tidy is not an opinion and not an argument. It is a picture on the cupboard door, and the room either matches it or it does not."

The arguing just stopped. There is nothing left to interpret. You look at the picture, you look at the shelf, you match the two. Even our youngest, too young to read the word "tidy", can do it.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

Is there a spot in your home where "put it back the way it should be" starts a small argument every single time, because everyone pictures "right" a little differently? If you took one photo of it on a good day and stuck that photo right where the space is, do you think the argument would just quietly end?

I will go first in the comments. 😄
