## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 25 is the third and final chapter of Part Seven and completes Standardize, the fifth of the six S's. You already have standards in words (Chapter 23) and the three tools that make them run (Chapter 24), and for most recurring work that is enough. But you have met the edge where words strain: try to write down exactly what a tidy shelf looks like and you pile up clauses that still miss it. Some things simply resist description, and for those the problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium.

The fix is the visual standard, the standard you show instead of tell. A written standard tells you what to do; a visual standard shows you what right looks like. The flagship is the photo of the ideal state: photograph a space when it is exactly right, put the picture where the space is, and from then on tidy is not an opinion but a picture the room either matches or does not. It is the optimistic mirror image of the before photo. For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph. The craft is to make it match-able: shoot from the angle and light of use, post it at the point of use, keep it honest rather than laminated.

This completes the fifth S. The needle ticks a small, honest step to the Standardize-complete milestone, the fifth on the dial, and the anti-backslide catch is fully engaged. Five of six S's are done; only Sustain remains.
