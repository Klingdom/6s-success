# Chapter 25 Calls to Action: Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards

This is the third and final chapter of Part Seven, and it completes the fifth S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea: photograph one space when it is exactly the way you want it, print it small, and tape it up where the space is, so "put it back the way it should be" stops being an instruction anyone has to remember or interpret and becomes a picture to match. The CTAs match the chapter's tone, warm and human, never corporate: a visual standard is a kindness that ends arguments, not a control imposed on the family, and the value is entirely in the picture being seen at the point of use, not in it being beautiful or laminated. The move costs about a minute, because the value is turning a standard that lived in words or in one person's head into a picture anyone, even a child too young to read, can see and match.

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Photograph one space when it is exactly the way you want it, print it small, and tape it up where the space is. Now "put it back the way it should be" is not an instruction anyone has to remember or interpret. It is a picture to match.
- **Family Challenge:** Let the children take the ideal-state photos and put them up at their own height. A child who took the picture of the tidy shelf is far more likely to put that shelf back the way the picture shows, because the standard is theirs.
- **6S Tip:** Shoot the standard photo from the exact angle and in the same light someone will see the space from when they go to set it right. A picture shot from an odd angle or in different light is hard to match, and a standard that is hard to match is one people quietly stop using.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: photograph one space at its best and tape the picture where the space is. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in about a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of warmth and relief. A home whose standards live only in words, or only in one person's head, is the normal case; a picture is a kindness that ends arguments, not a control imposed on the family.
- The rule (some things are easier to show than to say; when a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard), the show-do-not-tell reframe (a written standard tells you what to do, a visual standard shows you what right looks like), and the photo-of-the-ideal-state reframe are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid corporate framing, hype, and any force or control language (no enforce, comply, audit in the punitive sense, lock down). A visual standard shares clarity and ends arguments; it is not factory signage or a management dashboard imposed on the household. Avoid brand names (no named cameras, apps, or label products).
- Scope note: this completes Standardize with the visual standard (the photo of the ideal state and the storyboard); it does not re-teach the concept or the three tools (Chapters 23 and 24), the visual controls that make a storage spot self-explaining (Chapter 14, labels, outlines, boundaries, the Glance Test), or the matched-pair before-photo technique (Chapter 6), and it does not build the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30). It does not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety. Because this completes the fifth S, the goal is now only a step away, one S remaining, Sustain.
