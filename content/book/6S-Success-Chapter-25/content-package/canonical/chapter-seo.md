## Search intent
Making the hardest-to-describe parts of a home easy to keep, after the reader has the concept of a standard (Chapter 23) and the three tools that run it (Chapter 24) and has hit the edge where words strain. Readers arriving here have a home that is cleared, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized in words, and they have discovered that some things, what a tidy shelf looks like, how the cables coil, where each thing sits when the room is right, are far easier to show than to say. They are tired of arguing about what "tidy" means and of writing careful instructions everyone reads slightly differently. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: the visual standard, the standard you show instead of tell, delivered as a photo of the ideal state (photograph a space when it is exactly right and that photo becomes the standard anyone can match reality against at a glance) and a storyboard (for a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph); and the rule, some things are easier to show than to say, so when a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard. It gives the craft of making a visual standard match-able (shoot from the angle and light of use, post it at the point of use, keep it honest rather than laminated); reads the friction meter as a STANDARDIZE COMPLETE milestone, the fifth on the dial, with the anti-backslide catch fully engaged; and carries the Part 7 retrospective. It completes the fifth S; five of six S's are done and the goal is a step away, because one S remains, Sustain (Chapters 26 to 30).

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Explains why written instructions for how a space should look so often fail, naming the real cause, the medium, rather than blaming the family: everyone reads the same sentences and rebuilds a slightly different picture, so a shared photo, not better writing, is what closes the gap
- Gives the flagship move plainly: photograph a space on the day it is exactly right and post the picture where the space is, so "tidy" stops being an opinion and becomes a picture the room either matches or does not
- Distinguishes a visual standard (the ideal state or result of a space or task, what "done right" looks like) from a visual control (a label, outline, or boundary that makes a storage spot self-explaining), two devices often blurred together
- Introduces the storyboard for tasks with steps, a short strip of a few frames in order that carries a sequence better than a numbered paragraph, and makes clear four phone snaps or stick figures are plenty
- Gives the craft of making a visual standard genuinely match-able, shoot from the angle and light of use and post it at the point of use, rather than the common failure of a beautiful laminated binder filed in a drawer
- Frames the ideal-state photo as the optimistic mirror image of the before photo from Prepare, the positive "this is right" after, without re-teaching the matched-pair technique
- Reads the friction meter honestly at a milestone: the needle ticks only a small step because Standardize secured what was built rather than clearing new ground, and the real change is the anti-backslide catch fully engaged, so the reader is not sold a false forward leap
- Keeps the register warm and human, never corporate: a visual standard is a kindness that ends arguments, not factory signage or a control imposed on the household, with no force or control language, no hype, and no brand names
