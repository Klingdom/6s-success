"Some things are easier to show than to say. When a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard." A written standard about how a shelf should look is endlessly arguable. The problem is the medium. Read the free chapter.

3/
"A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like." For anything you would struggle to put into words, that is the gap between a standard people argue about and one they can simply see and match. Read the free chapter.

(263 chars)

4/
Photograph a space when it is exactly right, and that photo becomes the standard: not a description of the ideal state, but a picture of it, that anyone can match their reality against at a glance.

(200 chars)

---

5/
The most powerful standard in a home is often a single photograph, captured on the day the shelf was exactly right. From then on, tidy is not an opinion and not an argument. It is a picture on the cupboard door. Read the free chapter.

(237 chars)

---

6/
For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph. Show the few frames that matter, in order, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word.

(181 chars)

---

7/
Give one space a picture-standard today. Photograph it when it is exactly right, print it small, tape it up where the space is. Now "put it back the way it should be" is not something to remember or interpret. It is a picture to match. Read the free chapter.

(261 chars)

---

8/
Shoot the standard photo from the angle and in the light someone will actually have when they set the space right. A photo that is subtly hard to match is one people quietly stop using. Put it where the work happens, not in a drawer. Read the free chapter.

(259 chars)

---

9/
Let the children take the ideal-state photos and put them up at their own height. A child who took the picture of the tidy shelf is far more likely to put that shelf back the way the picture shows, because the standard is theirs. Read the free chapter.

(255 chars)
