2/
"Some things simply resist description." How the cables coil, where each thing sits when the room is right. A written standard about how things should look is almost infinitely arguable. The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium.

(259 chars)

---

3/
So you stop describing the image and simply show it. "The moment you stop describing the image and simply show it, the argument evaporates. There is nothing left to interpret." That is the whole move: a visual standard, the standard you show instead of tell.

(261 chars)

---

4/
The reframe: "A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like." For anything you would struggle to put into words, that is the difference between a standard people argue about and one they can simply see and match.

(260 chars)

---

5/
Here is the definition. A visual standard is the ideal state of a space or a task, captured as a picture rather than described in words: a photo of what "done" looks like, or a short storyboard of the steps.

(210 chars)

---

6/
The flagship is the photo of the ideal state. "Photograph a space when it is exactly right, and that photo becomes the standard: not a description of the ideal state, but a picture of it, that anyone can match their reality against at a glance."

(248 chars)

---

7/
"The most powerful standard in a home is often a single photograph: the shelf, the counter, the entryway, captured on the day it was exactly right. From then on, tidy is not an opinion and not an argument." Tidy becomes a picture the room either matches or it does not.

(272 chars)

---

8/
It is the optimistic mirror of the before photo you took at the start: not the mess at its worst, but the space at its best, and what you mean to keep. A child too young to read the word tidy can match a shelf to a photo.

(224 chars)

---

9/
For a task with steps, show it. "For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph. Show the few frames that matter, in order, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word." That is the storyboard.

(239 chars)

---

10/
Borrowed from the way films are planned: a few simple frames, each one key step, in order. For coiling the cables or sorting the recycling, four phone snaps beat a numbered list. "The value is entirely in the sequence being visible, not in the pictures being beautiful."

(274 chars)

---

11/
Make it match-able. Shoot from the angle and in the light the person will actually have when they set the space right. A photo taken from an odd angle or in different light is subtly hard to match, and subtly hard to match means quietly abandoned.

(251 chars)

---

12/
And put it where the work happens. "a visual standard filed away is a contradiction in terms, because its entire value is being seen at the point of use." A few honest photos taped where they are needed beat a laminated binder in a drawer.

(243 chars)

---

13/
The smallest proof of the whole idea: photograph one space when it is exactly right, print it small, tape it up where the space is. Now "put it back the way it should be" is not an instruction anyone has to remember or interpret. It is a picture to match.

(259 chars)
