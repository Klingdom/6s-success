2. Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities
   The setup, and the chapter just behind this one. Chapter 24 gave a written standard its three text-based tools, a checklist so nothing is forgotten, a schedule so the work has a time, and a name so someone owns it, and turned a hopeful card on the fridge into a small piece of machinery that runs on a tired Tuesday. But some standards are carried better by a picture than by any list. Read how the three tools were delivered, then see how Chapter 25 adds the last kind of standard, the one a picture carries better than words, and completes the fifth S.

3. Give one space a picture-standard (the chapter action)
   Photograph one space when it is exactly the way you want it, print it small, and tape it up where the space is. Now "put it back the way it should be" is not an instruction anyone has to remember or interpret. It is a picture to match. It costs about a minute, and it is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea: a standard that lived in words or in one person's head becomes a picture anyone, even a child too young to read, can see and match.

4. The Ideal State Photo card (free resource)
   A printable and phone wallpaper that shows how to shoot a standard photo, the angle and the light of use, and where to post it, so any space can get a picture-standard anyone can match. Turn "tidy" from an opinion into a picture the room either matches or does not.

5. The Storyboard template (free resource)
   A printable short strip of blank frames to sketch or photograph a task's key steps in order, so a sequence can be shown instead of written. Four frames are plenty, because the value is in the steps being visible, not the pictures being beautiful, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word.
