# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 25, Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (some things are easier to show than to say)

- og:title: Some Things Are Easier to Show Than to Say.
- og:description: Some things simply resist description. Try to write down exactly what a tidy shelf looks like and you pile up clauses that still miss it, and everyone reads the same sentences and rebuilds a slightly different picture. The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium. So you stop describing the image and simply show it: photograph a space when it is exactly right, and that photo becomes the standard anyone can match reality against at a glance. When a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard.
- twitter:title: Some Things Are Easier to Show Than to Say.
- twitter:description: When a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard. Photograph a space at its best, and the room either matches the picture or it does not. From Chapter 25 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A warm line-and-flat-color scene of a small photograph taped to a cupboard door, at a child's height, showing a shelf at its tidiest, with the real shelf just below it in the same warm light, matching. Someone is setting one last item back to match the picture. Behind it, softly out of focus, the ordinary room it belongs to. Nothing corporate, no laminated binder, no clipboard, no signage. It reads as a kindness that ends an argument, a picture anyone, even a child too young to read the word tidy, can see and match. Along the bottom: A written standard tells you what to do; a visual standard shows you what right looks like.

## Variant B (a written standard tells you what to do; a visual standard shows you what right looks like)

- og:title: A Written Standard Tells You What to Do. A Visual Standard Shows You What Right Looks Like.
- og:description: A visual standard is the ideal state of a space or a task, captured as a picture rather than described in words: a photo of what done looks like, or a short storyboard of the steps. Photograph a space when it is exactly right and post it where the space is, and tidy stops being an opinion and becomes a picture the room either matches or does not. For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph. With this, the fifth S is complete: your home no longer runs on your memory, it runs on what you have made visible.
- twitter:title: A Written Standard Tells You What to Do. A Visual Standard Shows You What Right Looks Like.
- twitter:description: A visual standard is a photo of the ideal state, or a short storyboard of the steps. It shows what right looks like, so anyone can see and match it. This completes the fifth S. From Chapter 25 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A calm line-and-flat-color diptych, two plain panels side by side. On the left, a single ideal-state photo of a counter captured on the day it was exactly right, taped where the counter is. On the right, a short storyboard, three or four simple frames in order showing the key steps of a small task, phone snaps or stick figures, plenty and honest rather than beautiful. Warm daylight, unhurried, nothing corporate, no binder. Footer: Photograph the ideal state, or storyboard the steps. When words fall short, show the result instead.
