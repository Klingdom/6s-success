## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 25, the third and final chapter of Part Seven, Standardize, and it completes the fifth of the six S's. The last two chapters built standards in words: Chapter 23 gave you the concept, the best way you know today written down, and Chapter 24 gave that standard its three tools, a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who. For most recurring work that is enough. But you have met the edge where words strain: try to write down exactly what a tidy shelf looks like, how the cables should coil, or where each thing sits when the room is right, and you pile up clauses that still miss it. Some things simply resist description, and for those the problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium. Everyone reads the same careful sentences and rebuilds a slightly different picture at the other end, so a written standard about how things should look is almost infinitely arguable. This chapter delivers the fix, the visual standard, the standard you show instead of tell, and it comes in two forms. The photo of the ideal state: photograph a space when it is exactly right, put the picture where the space is, and from then on tidy is not an opinion and not an argument, it is a picture on the cupboard door and the room either matches it or it does not, the optimistic mirror image of the before photo you took at the very start. And the storyboard: for a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph, the few frames that matter shown in order, so the next person follows the pictures without reading a word, and four phone snaps or stick figures are plenty because the value is in the sequence being visible, not the pictures being beautiful. The craft is to make it match-able: shoot from the angle and in the light the person will actually have when they set the space right, put it at the point of use, and keep it honest rather than laminated, because a visual standard filed away is a contradiction in terms, its entire value being seen at the point of use. A visual standard is distinct from a visual control: a label, an outline, or a boundary makes a storage spot self-explaining, while a visual standard shows what done right looks like, so a person can match reality against a picture. With this, the fifth S is complete. The friction meter reads a milestone: the needle reaches the Standardize-complete mark, the fifth on the dial after Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Safety, and the tick forward is honestly small, because Standardize was never about clearing big new friction, its whole gift was quieter. You see it in the catch, not the needle. The small catch that first appeared when you began to standardize is fully engaged now, locked: everything is written down, carried by tools, and shown in pictures, held against the slide back about as firmly as a home can be. The needle barely moved, and the grip behind it is complete. Five of the six S's are done, and the goal is a step away on the calm side, because one S still remains, Sustain, the work of keeping a held home held over the years. The rule to carry out: some things are easier to show than to say; when a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard.
