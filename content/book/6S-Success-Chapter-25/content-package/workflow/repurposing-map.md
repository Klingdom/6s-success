# Chapter 25 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- You already have standards in words (Chapter 23) and the three tools that make them run (Chapter 24), and for most recurring work that is enough, but you have met the edge where words strain: try to write down exactly what a tidy shelf looks like, how the cables should coil, or where each thing sits when the room is right, and you pile up clauses that still miss it, because some things simply resist description
- A written standard about how things should look is almost infinitely arguable, because everyone reads the same careful sentences and rebuilds a slightly different picture at the other end; the problem is not the people and not the standard, it is the medium
- The show-do-not-tell reframe and the definition box (frozen): the fix is the visual standard, the standard you show instead of tell. A written standard tells you what to do, a visual standard shows you what right looks like; a visual standard is the ideal state of a space or a task, captured as a picture rather than described in words, a photo of what "done" looks like or a short storyboard of the steps
- The flagship, the photo of the ideal state (frozen pictures line): photograph a space when it is exactly right, and that photo becomes the standard, not a description of the ideal state but a picture of it, that anyone can match their reality against at a glance; put it where the space is, and from then on tidy is not an opinion and not an argument but a picture on the cupboard door the room either matches or does not; it is the optimistic mirror image of the before photo, so low a bar that a child too young to read the word "tidy" can match a shelf to a photo
- The storyboard (frozen storyboard line): for a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph; show the few frames that matter, in order, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word; borrowed without ceremony from the way films are planned, four phone snaps or stick-figure sketches are plenty, because the value is in the sequence being visible, not the pictures being beautiful
- The make-it-match-able craft: a visual standard only works if it is genuinely easy to match, so shoot from the angle and in the light of use (a photo taken from a stepladder when everyone else sees the pantry from standing height is subtly hard to match, and subtly hard to match means quietly abandoned), post it at the point of use, and keep it honest rather than laminated, because a visual standard filed away is a contradiction in terms and a few slightly imperfect photos taped where they are needed beat a beautiful binder in a drawer
- The rule, frozen (hero): some things are easier to show than to say; when a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard; the completion reframe, with this the fifth S is complete, the best way you know is written down, carried by a checklist and a schedule and a name, and, where words fall short, shown in a picture, so your home no longer runs on your memory, it runs on what you have made visible; the One Idea to Keep, when words fall short, show the result instead, photograph a space at its best or storyboard a task's steps and put the picture where the work happens, and right stops being an argument and becomes something anyone can see and match
- The friction meter reaches the STANDARDIZE COMPLETE milestone, the fifth mark on the dial after Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Safety; the needle takes a small, honest tick forward, and that smallness is honest because Standardize was never about clearing big new friction, its gift is quieter and you see it in the catch; the anti-backslide catch behind the needle is now fully engaged and locked, its firmest state, because the gains are written down, carried by tools, and shown in pictures; the needle barely moved and the grip behind it is complete; five of six S's are done and the goal is a step away, because one S remains, Sustain

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the third and final chapter of Part Seven and it COMPLETES Standardize, the fifth S, by adding the last kind of standard, the one a picture carries better than any list, to the concept from Chapter 23 and the three tools from Chapter 24. It USES the finished home the first four S's built (sorted, arranged, clean, safe) and the written standards with their tools from Chapters 23 and 24, but does not re-open any of them. Do not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety (Chapters 16 to 22); the before-photo callback ("the optimistic mirror image of the before photo") is a one-line reuse, not a re-teach.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 23, Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today): the CONCEPT of a standard (the best way you know today, written down) belongs to that chapter. Chapter 25 assumes you already have written standards and adds the visual kind; do NOT re-deliver the concept here.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities): the three tools that make a written standard run, a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who, belong to Chapter 24. Chapter 25 references them in a line (the completion reframe) but does NOT re-deliver them.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 14, Straighten's visual controls): the labels, outlines, boundaries, and the Glance Test that make a storage spot self-explaining belong to Chapter 14. A visual standard is a different device: it shows the ideal state or result of a space or a task, what "done right" looks like, so a person can match reality against a picture. Note only that a label is a tiny visual standard while the ideal-state photo is the full one; do NOT re-teach visual controls here.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 6, the before photo): the matched-pair before-photo technique belongs to Prepare. Chapter 25 uses it only as the optimistic mirror image (not "this is what it looked like at its worst" but "this is what it looks like at its best, and this is what we are keeping it like"); do NOT re-teach the before-photo method.
- CRITICAL fence (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30, later S): the long-term adherence, audits, and habit-as-a-system belong to Sustain. Chapter 25 completes Standardize and does NOT build the adherence or audit system. Standardize is the fifth S; Sustain is the sixth.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home whose standards live only in words, or only in one person's head, is the normal case, not a failing; the problem at the edge where words strain is the medium, not the people, and the bar is set so low that a child too young to read the word "tidy" can match a shelf to a photo. Keep it warm, relief and a load shared, not lecture; a visual standard is a kindness that ends arguments.
- The visual standard frees the household, it does not police it: the danger word is corporate, so keep every asset human, warm, and light, a tool that shares clarity and ends arguments, never factory signage or a management dashboard. The value is entirely in the picture being seen at the point of use, not in it being beautiful or laminated. No force or control language (no enforce, comply, audit in the punitive sense, crack down, lock down), no hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "the cupboard door", "a phone photo", or "a printed picture", not a named camera, app, or label product); teach the concept, the definition box, and the two forms (the ideal-state photo and the storyboard), not a shopping list.
- The source's Standardize step (make the standard visual, so the ideal state and the correct sequence can be seen and matched at a glance) is drawn from the 6S source, home-scaled into the ideal-state photo and the storyboard, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to standard work and Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed. This is the final Standardize move; the long-term adherence work maps onto Sustain, Chapter 26 onward.
- The friction-meter reading is specific to Ch 25: it reaches the STANDARDIZE COMPLETE milestone, the fifth mark on the dial after Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Safety. The needle takes a small, honest tick forward, and that smallness is honest, because Standardize was never about clearing big new friction the way Sort, Straighten, and Shine were; its gift is quieter and you see it in the catch, not the needle. The anti-backslide catch BEHIND the needle, the one that first appeared when you began to standardize and grew stronger when standards got their tools, is now FULLY ENGAGED and LOCKED, its firmest state, because the gains are written down, carried by tools, and shown in pictures, held against the slide back about as firmly as a home can be.
