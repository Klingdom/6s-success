# Chapter 25 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, human, quietly proud voice; a visual standard is a kindness that ends arguments, not a control imposed on the family, and its whole value is being seen at the point of use, not being beautiful or laminated; no force or control words, no hype, no brand names.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You wrote the standard. Then everyone argued about what "tidy" meant.

**Body:** Some things simply resist description. Try to put into words exactly what a tidy shelf looks like and you pile up clauses that still miss it. Swipe for the last kind of standard, the one a picture carries better than any list.

---

## Slide 2: The medium is the problem

**Headline:** The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium.

**Body:** A standard about how things should look is almost endlessly arguable, because everyone reads the same careful sentences and rebuilds a slightly different picture at the other end. Words are simply the wrong tool for some standards.

---

## Slide 3: The rule

**Headline:** Some things are easier to show than to say.

**Body:** When a picture carries the standard better than words can, the picture is the standard. The fix for an arguable standard is not better words. It is to stop using words and show the thing instead.

---

## Slide 4: Show, do not tell

**Headline:** A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like.

**Body:** For anything you would struggle to put into words, that is the difference between a standard people argue about and one they can simply see and match. The moment you show the image, there is nothing left to interpret.

---

## Slide 5: What a visual standard is

**Headline:** The ideal state, captured as a picture rather than described in words.

**Body:** A visual standard is a photo of what "done" looks like, or a short storyboard of the steps. Two everyday forms, and neither needs anything you do not already own.

---

## Slide 6: The photo of the ideal state

**Headline:** Photograph a space when it is exactly right, and that photo becomes the standard.

**Body:** Not a description of the ideal state, but a picture of it, that anyone can match their reality against at a glance. Get the space right on a good day, take the photo before it drifts, and put it where the space is.

---

## Slide 7: Tidy stops being an argument

**Headline:** The most powerful standard in a home is often a single photograph.

**Body:** The shelf, the counter, the entryway, captured on the day it was exactly right. From then on, tidy is not an opinion and not an argument. It is a picture on the cupboard door, and the room either matches it or it does not.

---

## Slide 8: The optimistic mirror of the before photo

**Headline:** You did a version of this at the very start.

**Body:** The before photo was the honest record of the mess. The ideal-state photo is its optimistic mirror: not "this is what it looked like at its worst" but "this is what it looks like at its best." A child too young to read "tidy" can match a shelf to a photo.

---

## Slide 9: The storyboard

**Headline:** For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph.

**Body:** Show the few frames that matter, in order, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word. Three or four phone snaps for coiling the cables or setting up the table. The value is in the sequence being visible, not the pictures being beautiful.

---

## Slide 10: Make it easy to match

**Headline:** Shoot it from the angle and in the light of use.

**Body:** A photo taken from a stepladder, when everyone else sees the pantry from standing height, is subtly hard to match, and subtly hard to match means quietly abandoned. Match-able is the whole job.

---

## Slide 11: Put it where the work happens

**Headline:** A visual standard filed away is a contradiction in terms.

**Body:** Its entire value is being seen at the point of use. A few slightly imperfect photos, taped honestly where they are needed, do more than a beautiful laminated binder that lives in a drawer and helps no one.

---
