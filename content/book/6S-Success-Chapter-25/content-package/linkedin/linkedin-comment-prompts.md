# Chapter 25 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, human, quietly proud voice, reassurance and permission not compliance: a home whose standards live only in words, or only in one person's head, is the normal case, and a picture is a kindness that ends arguments, not a control imposed on the family. No corporate framing, no force or control language, no brand names.

---

1. Some things simply resist description. Try to write down, in sentences, exactly what a tidy shelf looks like, and you pile up careful clauses that still miss it, and then everyone reads them and pictures something slightly different. Be honest with me: what is the one thing in your home that you have tried to describe in words, again and again, that never quite lands the way a picture would? Is it a shelf, a counter, a way of coiling something, or where each thing sits when a room is right?

2. The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium. So much of what looks like a family not following the rules is really just words being the wrong tool for the job. Has that reframe changed anything for you? Think of a standard in your home that keeps getting argued over, and ask yourself honestly: would a single photo taped where the space is end the argument faster than one more carefully worded note?

3. A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like. The most powerful standard in a home is often a single photograph of the shelf or the counter, captured on the day it was exactly right, so tidy stops being an opinion and becomes a picture the room either matches or does not. Have you ever done this, even by accident? And if you have not, which one space would you photograph first, on the next day it is exactly the way you want it?

4. For a task with steps, a short strip of pictures beats a paragraph: three or four phone snaps of the key frames, in order, and the next person can follow the pictures without reading a word. The value is in the sequence being visible, not the pictures being beautiful. Which task in your home would you storyboard, the one you keep re-explaining, the one where a numbered list never quite carries the order? Setting up the folding table, coiling the cables, sorting the recycling?

5. A visual standard filed away is a contradiction in terms, because its entire value is being seen at the point of use. A few slightly imperfect photos taped where they are needed do more than a beautiful laminated binder in a drawer. Here is the warmer half of the question: if the children took the ideal-state photos and put them up at their own height, do you think the standard would finally become theirs? For those of you who have tried it, did letting a child photograph the tidy shelf change whether they put it back that way?
