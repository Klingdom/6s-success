# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 25 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 25 is the THIRD and FINAL chapter of Part Seven and COMPLETES Standardize, the fifth of the six S's. Chapter 23 opened the fifth S with the concept of a standard (the best way you know today, written down), and Chapter 24 gave standards the three tools that make them run (a checklist, a schedule, a name); four of the six S's were already done going in (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety). Chapter 25's job is to name the edge where words strain (try to write down exactly what a tidy shelf looks like and you pile up clauses that still miss it, because "the problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium") and to deliver the last kind of standard, the one a picture carries better than any list: the ideal-state photo and the short storyboard. Because a visual standard could sound like factory signage or a management dashboard, the register must stay warm and freeing, not corporate: not "post the approved reference image and audit against it," but a kindness that ends arguments and a load taken off the one person who used to hold the picture in their head. The voice must stay anti-shame (a standard that lives only in words, or only in one person's head, is the normal case, not a failing; the bar is so low that "a child too young to read the word 'tidy' can match a shelf to a photo"), frame the picture as relief rather than control (right "becomes something anyone can see and match"), keep the tool honest rather than impressive (a few phone snaps taped where the work happens beat a laminated binder in a drawer), and read the meter honestly (the needle ticks a small, honest step to the STANDARDIZE COMPLETE milestone, the fifth on the dial, with the anti-backslide catch fully engaged, five of six S's done and one, Sustain, still ahead).

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored manuscript and final HTML)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," stands the reader at the far edge of the fifth S, and pictures the ordinary marks of a visual standard: a photograph "taped inside the cupboard door or stuck to the wall beside the shelf," a shelf a child can match, "four phone snaps of your own hands doing the steps," the entryway reset shown in "three frames" (shoes on the rack, bags on the hooks, surface clear).
- **Concrete over abstract.** The idea is taught through the two-kinds-of-standard opener (the same shelves, once as a wordy card someone has to decode and once as a single photo), the tidy-argument diagnosis ("my tidy and your tidy are genuinely different pictures"), the flagship photo of the ideal state, the storyboard "borrowed, without any ceremony, from the way films are planned," and the make-it-match-able craft (shoot from "the angle and in the light that the person will actually have," post it "where the work happens").
- **Anti-shame.** A written standard that everyone reads differently "fails not because it is wrong but because it is written"; the fix removes the blame directly ("The problem is not the people and not the standard. It is the medium"); and the bar is set as low as it goes ("A child too young to read the word 'tidy' can match a shelf to a photo").
- **Warm and freeing, not corporate (the register the chapter turns on).** The visual standard is framed as ending arguments, not policing them: "the argument evaporates. There is nothing left to interpret." It is kept honest rather than impressive ("keep it honest rather than impressive"), and the closing image is a home "that shows everyone, plainly, what right looks like," not an inspection regime.
- **Honest about the milestone and the meter.** The needle reaches the Standardize-complete tick, "and that is honest": the forward tick is deliberately small because "Standardize was never about clearing big new friction." What changed is behind the needle: the catch "is fully engaged now, locked." "The needle barely moved, and the grip behind it is complete." The goal is kept openly ahead "because one S still remains, Sustain."

## Voice risks to watch
- **Hero vocabulary is dense.** "standard," "visual," "picture," "photo," "photograph," "storyboard," "match," "tidy," "home" recur because they are the subject; expected, not a tic, but vary the surrounding language when excerpting, and watch "picture," "photo," and "match" as the payoff words most likely to stack.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the LinkedIn and X sign-offs; several close on "Read the free chapter" / "Read it free online" / "Read the chapter free in the online book."
- **Keep the register warm and freeing, not corporate.** The danger word for this chapter, as for Chapters 23 and 24, is "corporate": a reference photo posted at the point of use can slide into factory signage or a management dashboard, so any asset must not tip from "a kindness that ends arguments" into inspection, enforcement, or a compliance board. The frozen framing keeps it anti-shame (a standard that lived only in words is the normal case, the problem is the medium not the people) and keeps the picture a shared thing to match, not a control imposed on the family.
- **"Match" and "right" are topic words, not control words.** They are used warmly throughout (the room either matches the photo or it does not, a picture anyone can see and match); watch that excerpts never let them tip into inspect, audit (punitive), enforce, or grade. No force or control language appears in the package.
