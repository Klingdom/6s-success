## Definition box (frozen)
> A visual standard is the ideal state of a space or a task, captured as a picture rather than described in words: a photo of what "done" looks like, or a short storyboard of the steps. Where a written standard tells you what to do, a visual standard shows you what right looks like.

## The show-do-not-tell reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> A written standard tells you what to do. A visual standard shows you what right looks like. For anything you would struggle to put into words, that is the difference between a standard people argue about and one they can simply see and match.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> When words fall short, show the result instead. Photograph a space at its best, or storyboard a task's steps, and put the picture where the work happens. Right stops being an argument and becomes something anyone can see and match.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Photograph one space when it is exactly the way you want it, print it small, and tape it up where the space is. Now "put it back the way it should be" is not an instruction anyone has to remember or interpret. It is a picture to match.
- **Family Challenge:** Let the children take the ideal-state photos and put them up at their own height. A child who took the picture of the tidy shelf is far more likely to put that shelf back the way the picture shows, because the standard is theirs.
- **6S Tip:** Shoot the standard photo from the exact angle and in the same light someone will see the space from when they go to set it right. A picture shot from an odd angle or in different light is hard to match, and a standard that is hard to match is one people quietly stop using.
- **Common Mistake:** Building an elaborate laminated binder of visual standards that looks impressive and helps no one, instead of a few honest photos stuck where the work happens. The whole value of a visual standard is being seen at the point of use; a beautiful one filed away is worth nothing.
