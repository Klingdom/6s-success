- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** To make a standard run without you, answer three questions. What: a checklist, so nothing is forgotten. - **The checklist reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful. It is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head. Pilots and surgeons live by them, and not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not.
- **The schedule reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A task with no time does not get done on a rhythm. It gets done in a panic, once it has become a crisis. A schedule is how you trade the occasional emergency for the small, dull, ordinary upkeep that quietly prevents it.
- **The responsibility reframe (frozen, pull-quote, callback to Chapter 17):** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. You met that line back in Shine, and it is even truer of a standard: the fastest way to make sure something never happens is to leave it unowned, so that every person assumes some other person has it handled.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** the checklist (what) / the schedule (when) / the responsibility (who) / a list, a time, and a name. Chapter 23 wrote the standard down; Chapter 24 gives it the three tools that make it happen.

## Motifs
- **A recipe does not cook itself.** A written standard is necessary but not sufficient; it needs tools to run.
- **What, when, who.** The three questions a standard must answer to happen: a list, a time, a name.
- **A list does not forget.** Checklists are for everyone, because everyone forgets; not a sign of weakness.
- **Upkeep versus crisis.** Work without a time gets done in a panic; a schedule trades the emergency for the dull, ordinary upkeep.
- **A job with no name.** A standard nobody owns is a standard that waits forever; the callback to Chapter 17.
- **The catch reinforced.** The three tools make the anti-backslide catch stronger, so the gain is held more firmly.

## Emotional purpose
Warm, practical relief, and the continued lifting of a private burden. Chapter 23 let the reader set down the weight of being the only one who knows how the home works; this chapter lets them set down the weight of being the only one who remembers to do things and the only one nagging others to help. The register works hard to keep checklists, schedules, and responsibilities feeling human and freeing rather than corporate or controlling: these are tools that share the load, not a compliance system imposed on the family. No shame (a written-but-unfollowed standard is normal), no force or control framing.

## Practical purpose

Do NOT build the long-term adherence / audit system or the daily 6S time ritual (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30). Do NOT re-teach the earlier S's. Avoid brand names (no named apps or products).
