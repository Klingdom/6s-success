# Chapter 24 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, human, freeing voice; the three tools take a load off you and share it, not a compliance system imposed on the household, and responsibilities are chosen, not handed down; no force or control words, no hype, no brand names.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You wrote the standard. Then it got ignored for a week.

**Body:** A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself. Writing down the best way was the crucial first move, and it is not, by itself, enough. Swipe for the three tools that make a standard actually run.

---

## Slide 2: Knowing is not doing

**Headline:** Knowing what good looks like is not the same as doing it.

**Body:** The card can be perfect and still sit there. To actually run, a standard needs three things writing it down does not supply: someone has to know exactly what to do, the work has to have a time, and someone has to own it.

---

## Slide 3: The three places a standard fails

**Headline:** A standard nobody follows fails in exactly three places.

**Body:** Memory: six steps, four remembered, two quietly dropped. Timing: no particular moment, so it drifts to "later", where good intentions go to die. Ownership: it belongs to everyone, so to no one. Each has a tool.

---

## Slide 4: One question, one tool

**Headline:** A checklist answers what. A schedule answers when. A responsibility answers who.

**Body:** Give a standard all three and it stops being a hopeful card on the fridge and becomes a small piece of machinery that runs whether or not you are thinking about it.

---

## Slide 5: The checklist reframe

**Headline:** A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful.

**Body:** It is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head. Pilots and surgeons live by them, and not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not.

---

## Slide 6: Keep the checklist short

**Headline:** The one you will actually run beats the thorough one you will not.

**Body:** A checklist is how the competent stay reliable when they are tired, rushed, or interrupted, which at home is roughly always. Keep it to the few steps that change the result, in plain words.

---

## Slide 7: The schedule reframe

**Headline:** A task with no time waits to become urgent.

**Body:** A task with no time does not get done on a rhythm. It gets done in a panic, once it has become a crisis. A schedule is how you trade the occasional emergency for the small, dull, ordinary upkeep that quietly prevents it.

---

## Slide 8: Hang it off a cue you already have

**Headline:** Do not invent a new slot. Borrow one that already runs.

**Body:** The bins go out the night the show you watch ends. The plants get watered when the Sunday coffee is brewing. Attached to a rhythm that already runs, a task stops depending on anyone remembering to schedule it.

---

## Slide 9: The responsibility reframe

**Headline:** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one.

**Body:** You met that line back in Shine. The fastest way to make sure something never happens is to leave it unowned, so every person assumes some other person has it handled. The fix is a name, one name, per standard.

---

## Slide 10: Chosen, not handed down

**Headline:** At home, you do not hand out the jobs. You let people pick.

**Body:** A job someone chose is a job they will actually do. What is chosen gets done; what is imposed gets subverted, slowly, and usually with excellent excuses. Lay out the standards that need owners, and let people choose.

---

## Slide 11: Put all three on one standard

**Headline:** The after-dinner reset becomes a fifteen-minute machine.

**Body:** A short checklist, a schedule that is a cue you already have (when the dinner plates are cleared, every night), and chosen names for each part. A list, a time, and named parts that run on a tired Tuesday when nobody can be bothered.
