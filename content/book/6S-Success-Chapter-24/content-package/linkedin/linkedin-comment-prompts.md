# Chapter 24 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, human, freeing voice, reassurance and permission not compliance: a written standard that gets ignored is the normal case, and the three tools take a load off you and share it, not a rule to be enforced. Responsibilities are chosen, not handed down. No corporate framing, no force or control language, no brand names.

---

1. A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself. Most of us have written down a good way of doing something, taped it up, and then watched it get ignored for a week. Be honest with me: what is one standard or routine you actually wrote down or agreed on in your home, that still somehow never happens? And when you look at it, is the missing piece memory, timing, or ownership?

2. A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful. Pilots and surgeons live by them, not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not. A lot of us still resist a checklist at home out of a quiet sense that needing one is admitting a weakness. Does reframing it as how the competent stay reliable when tired and interrupted change how it lands for you? What is one home task you would quietly run better with a short list stuck where you do it?

3. A task with no time does not get done on a rhythm. It gets done in a panic, once it has become a crisis: the gutter you clean in the rain because it overflowed, the fridge you empty in a hurry when something has gone off. Which bit of upkeep in your home keeps becoming an emergency because it never had a time? And is there a rhythm you already have, a show that ends, a coffee that brews, that you could quietly hang it off?

4. A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. The fastest way to make sure something never happens is to leave it unowned, so every person assumes some other person has it handled. What is the task in your home that sits in exactly that blind spot, everybody's, therefore nobody's? And here is the warmer half of the question: if the household got to choose their own standards rather than be handed them, which one do you think would finally get picked up?

5. What is chosen gets done; what is imposed gets subverted, slowly, and usually with excellent excuses. This is where a home is not a workplace: you do not hand out the jobs, you let people pick. For those of you who have tried it, did letting someone choose the part they own actually change whether it got done? And for the youngest in the house, what is the very important small job they would happily claim as theirs?
