# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (writing the best way down was the right move, but a recipe on a card does not cook itself, so a written standard only actually runs once it has three small tools: a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. The three tools are framed as a way to share the load and free you, never corporate or a compliance system. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small idea that quietly fixed something in my house, and then I want to hear from you all.

A while back I did the thing everyone says to do: I wrote down the best way to run our after-dinner cleanup, five plain steps, and taped it inside a cupboard. I felt very organized. And it changed almost nothing, because the card just sat there while I kept doing the whole reset myself.

Here is the line that finally landed for me.

"A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself."

That was exactly it. The steps were perfect and completely ignored. The book says a written-down task fails in one of three places: people forget a step, it never gets an actual time, or it belongs to everyone and so to no one. And each gap has a tiny fix.

"This chapter gives every standard its three tools: a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who."

The one that stuck with me most was the checklist, because I always felt a bit silly needing one.

"A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful. It is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head. Pilots and surgeons live by them, and not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

Is there a job in your home that you have written down or explained a hundred times and it still only happens when you personally make it happen? And if you gave it a real time (tied to something that already happens, like when the dinner plates are cleared) and put one name on it, do you think it would finally run without you?

I will go first in the comments. 😄

(If anyone wants it written out properly, this is from Chapter 24 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the idea that got the after-dinner cleanup out of my hands and made it run on its own.)
