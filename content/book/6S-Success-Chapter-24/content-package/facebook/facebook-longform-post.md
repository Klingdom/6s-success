# Facebook Longform Post: I Wrote the Standard Down. It Still Did Not Get Done.

For a couple of weeks last winter I had a standard taped to the inside of a cupboard door. Five plain steps for the after-dinner reset, written the way the book said to write them. I was quietly proud of it. And it did almost nothing, because a card on a door does not do the dishes.

That is the honest gap this chapter names.

"A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself."

The steps were right. Everyone could read them. And still the kitchen only got reset on the nights I stood there running it, because writing the best way down tells everyone what good looks like, and knowing what good looks like has never once been the same as doing it.

So the chapter asks a plain question: when a written standard gets ignored, where exactly does it fall down? The answer is always one of three places. A step gets forgotten. The task drifts to "later" and never gets a real time. Or it belongs to the whole house, which means it belongs to nobody. Three gaps, and each one has a tool.

"This chapter gives every standard its three tools: a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who."

A checklist, so nothing gets forgotten. This was the part that surprised me most.

"A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful. It is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head. Pilots and surgeons live by them, and not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not."

A schedule, so the work gets a time. I stopped hunting for a free slot and just hung the reset off a cue we already had: it starts when the dinner plates are cleared, every night.

And a name, so someone owns it. Not handed down, chosen: one person took the kitchen, one took the living room, the youngest got the light switches. What is chosen gets done.

That was it. The same card, three small additions, about five minutes. It turned into a fifteen-minute machine that runs on a tired Tuesday when nobody can be bothered, which is the only test any of this has to pass.
