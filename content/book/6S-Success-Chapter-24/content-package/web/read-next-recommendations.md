# Read Next Recommendations: Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities

1. Chapter 25, Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards
   The next step, and the piece that completes Standardize. Chapter 24 gave a written standard its three text-based tools, a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who. But some standards are carried better by a picture than by any list: a photo of the ideal state, a simple storyboard. Chapter 25 adds that last kind of standard and completes the fifth S. Chapter 24 gives the tools in words; Chapter 25 gives the one a picture carries better than words.

2. Chapter 23, Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today
   The setup, and the chapter just behind this one. Chapter 23 opened Part Seven and the fifth S with the concept of a standard, the best way you know today written down, a recipe and not a rule, and had you write one plain card as proof. But a card on the fridge does not follow itself, which is exactly where this chapter begins. Read how the concept was delivered, then see how Chapter 24 gives that standard the three tools that make it actually run.

3. Give one standard its three tools (the chapter action)
   Take the one standard you wrote last chapter and give it the three tools in five minutes: turn it into a short checklist, hang it off a time or an existing cue, and put one name against it. That is the difference between a standard that exists and a standard that happens. It costs about five minutes, and it is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea.
