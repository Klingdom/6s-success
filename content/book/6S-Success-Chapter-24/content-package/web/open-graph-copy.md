# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 24, Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (a recipe on a card does not cook itself)

- og:title: A Recipe on a Card Does Not Cook Itself.
- og:description: A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself. Writing down the best way tells everyone what good looks like, but knowing what good looks like has never once been the same as doing it. A standard runs when three things are true: a checklist so nothing is forgotten, a schedule so the work has a time, and a name so someone owns it. Give a standard all three and it stops being a good idea on a card and becomes something the home does on its own.
- twitter:title: A Recipe on a Card Does Not Cook Itself.
- twitter:description: A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself. Give it a checklist, a schedule, and a name, and it runs without you. From Chapter 24 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A warm line-and-flat-color scene of a single small card stuck to the side of a fridge, the way a household would actually stick one up. The card has a plain title, four or five short checklist lines in a friendly hand, one line noting when it happens (it starts when the dinner plates are cleared), and a name beside each part. Behind it, softly out of focus, the ordinary evening it belongs to, a kitchen after dinner in warm light. Nothing corporate, no binder, no clipboard, no chore-chart app. It reads as a small machine a family runs together. Along the bottom: A checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, a name for the who.

## Variant B (a standard is the best way, three tools make it happen)

- og:title: A Standard Is the Best Way. Three Tools Make It Happen.
- og:description: A standard is the best way. A checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen. To make a standard run without you, answer three questions. What: a checklist, so nothing is forgotten. When: a schedule, so the work has a time and happens on a rhythm. Who: a responsibility, so one name owns it. A standard with all three stops being a good idea on a card and becomes something the home does on its own.
- twitter:title: A Standard Is the Best Way. Three Tools Make It Happen.
- twitter:description: A standard is the best way. A checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen. From Chapter 24 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: A calm line-and-flat-color triptych, three plain panels side by side, each holding one small icon and one word: a short checklist for What, a clock or evening cue for When, and a single name tag for Who. Under the three, one standard card ties them together, stuck where the work happens. Warm daylight, unhurried, nothing corporate, no clipboard. Footer: A standard is the best way; a checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen.
