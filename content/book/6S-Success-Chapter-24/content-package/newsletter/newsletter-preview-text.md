# Newsletter Preview Text (Preheader): Checklists, Schedules, and Responsibilities

Ten options, each 35 to 90 characters, each paired with a subject line. Char counts noted.

1. Pairs with #1 (A standard is the best way. A checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen.)
   "The list, the time, and the name that make a standard actually run." (66 chars)

2. Pairs with #2 (A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself.)
   "You wrote the best way down. Here is how to make it actually happen." (67 chars)

3. Pairs with #3 (A checklist answers what. A schedule answers when. A responsibility answers who.)
   "Three plain questions, three small tools: what, when, who." (58 chars)

4. Pairs with #4 (A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful.)
   "Pilots and surgeons live by them. Not because they forget more." (62 chars)

5. Pairs with #5 (A task with no time doesn't wait to be convenient. It waits to become urgent.)
   "A task with no time doesn't wait to be convenient. It waits to become urgent." (76 chars)

6. Pairs with #6 (A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one.)
   "Put one chosen name on it, and it stops being nobody's job." (58 chars)

7. Pairs with #7 (What is chosen gets done; what is imposed gets subverted.)
   "Let people choose the job they'll own. Chosen work gets done." (60 chars)

8. Pairs with #8 (The after-dinner reset, turned into a fifteen-minute machine.)
   "A list, a time, and named parts: a machine for a tired Tuesday." (62 chars)

9. Pairs with #9 (The needle did not advance. The grip behind it got tighter.)
   "The needle held. The grip on everything behind it just got tighter." (66 chars)

10. Pairs with #10 (Give one standard its three tools in five minutes.)
    "Take the standard you already wrote. Give it three tools today." (62 chars)
