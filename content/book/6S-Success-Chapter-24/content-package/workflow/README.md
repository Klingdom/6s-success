## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
Last chapter you wrote a standard, the best way you knew put on a card where anyone could follow it, and that was the crucial first move, but it is not by itself enough: a standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself, because knowing what good looks like has never once been the same as doing it. Watch a written standard that nobody follows and it falls down in exactly one of three places, memory (steps get quietly dropped), timing (it drifts to "later", where good intentions go to die), and ownership (it belongs to the household in general, so to no one in particular); none of those is a failure of the standard itself, what was missing was a way to carry it, and each gap has one tool. The chapter's hero is the three tools a standard needs to run without you, each the answer to one plain question: a checklist answers what, so nothing is forgotten; a schedule answers when, so the work has a time and happens on a rhythm; a responsibility answers who, so one name owns it (the definition box and the three-tools line). The checklist is not a sign you are forgetful, it is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head, the way pilots and surgeons work, kept short and real, because a checklist you will actually run beats a thorough one you will not, every time. The schedule is how you trade the occasional emergency for the small, dull, ordinary upkeep that quietly prevents it, and the easiest way to give recurring work a time is to hang it off a cue that already happens, so a task stops depending on anyone remembering to schedule it. The responsibility is the callback to Shine, a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, so put one name per standard, chosen not handed down, because what is chosen gets done and what is imposed gets subverted, slowly and usually with excellent excuses. Put all three on the after-dinner reset and it stops being a vague good intention and becomes a fifteen-minute machine with a list, a time, and named parts that will run on a tired Tuesday when nobody can be bothered, which is the only real test any of this has to pass. The friction meter HOLDS and does not move, because these are tools rather than new clearing and no fresh friction was cleared, but the catch that appeared behind the needle last chapter is stronger now, where it was a single tooth this chapter has given it three, and the backslide it prevents is barred more firmly, because the good way is now carried, run on a rhythm, and owned; the needle did not advance, the grip on everything behind it got noticeably tighter, and that is precisely the work of this S. The goal stays ahead on the calm side, because one piece of Standardize remains and Sustain still follows. Hands off to Chapter 25 · Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards, which adds the one kind of standard a picture carries better than any list and completes the fifth S.
