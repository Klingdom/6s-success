# Chapter 24 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- Last chapter you wrote a standard, the best way you knew put on a card, and that was the crucial first move, but it is not by itself enough: a standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself, because knowing what good looks like has never once been the same as doing it
- A written standard that nobody follows falls down in exactly one of three places: memory (steps get quietly dropped), timing (it drifts to "later", where good intentions go to die), and ownership (it belongs to the household in general, so to no one in particular); none is a failure of the standard itself, what was missing was a way to carry it
- The three tools (the hero): to make a standard run without you, answer three questions, and each has a tool. A checklist answers what, so nothing is forgotten. A schedule answers when, so the work has a time and happens on a rhythm. A responsibility answers who, so one name owns it. A standard with all three stops being a good idea on a card and becomes something the home does on its own; the three-tools line, frozen: this chapter gives every standard its three tools, a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who
- The checklist reframe (frozen): a checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful, it is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head; pilots and surgeons live by them, not because they are bad at their jobs but because everyone forgets and a list does not; it is how the competent stay reliable when tired, rushed, or interrupted, kept short and real, because a checklist you will actually run beats a thorough one you will not, every time
- The schedule reframe (frozen): a task with no time does not get done on a rhythm, it gets done in a panic once it has become a crisis; a schedule is how you trade the occasional emergency for the small, dull, ordinary upkeep that quietly prevents it; the easiest way to give recurring work a time is to hang it off a cue that already happens, so a task stops depending on anyone remembering to schedule it
- The responsibility reframe (frozen, callback to Shine): a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, you met that line back in Shine, and it is even truer of a standard; the fix is one name per standard, chosen not handed down, because what is chosen gets done and what is imposed gets subverted, slowly and usually with excellent excuses
- Put all three on one standard: the after-dinner reset gets a four or five line checklist, a schedule that is a cue you already have (it starts when the dinner plates are cleared), and chosen responsibilities (one person the kitchen, another the living room, the youngest the light switches); on one small card stuck to the fridge it is no longer a vague good intention, it is a fifteen-minute machine with a list, a time, and named parts, and it will run on a tired Tuesday when nobody can be bothered, which is the only real test any of this has to pass
- The rule, frozen (hero): a standard is the best way; a checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen; the One Idea to Keep, a written standard is a good idea, give every standard you care about a list so nothing is forgotten, a time so it gets done on a rhythm, and a name so someone owns it, and it will run without you
- The friction meter HOLDS and does NOT move, because these are tools, not new clearing; but the catch that appeared behind the needle last chapter is REINFORCED, where it was a single tooth it is now three, and the backslide it prevents is barred more firmly, because the good way is now carried, run on a rhythm, and owned; the needle did not advance, the grip on everything behind it got noticeably tighter; the goal stays ahead because one piece of Standardize remains and Sustain still follows

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the second chapter of Part Seven and it CONTINUES Standardize, the fifth S, by giving the written standard from Chapter 23 its three tools. It USES the finished home the first four S's built (sorted, arranged, clean, safe) and the one written standard from Chapter 23, but does not re-open any of them. Do not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety (Chapters 16 to 22); the Shine callback ("a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one") is a one-line reuse, not a re-teach.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 23, Standardize: The Best Way We Know Today): the CONCEPT of a standard (the best way you know today, written down) and the act of writing one plain standard belong to the previous chapter. Chapter 24 assumes you already have a written standard and gives it the three tools; do NOT re-deliver the concept or the write-one-standard walkthrough here.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 25, Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards): the visual and pictorial standards (the one kind of standard a single picture carries better than any list) belong to Chapter 25, which completes the fifth S. Chapter 24 gives the three text-based tools (lists, times, names); do NOT build visual standards here.
- CRITICAL fence (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30, later S): the long-term adherence, audits, and habit-as-a-system belong to Sustain. Chapter 24 may note in a line that a standard becomes habit through repetition, but does NOT build the adherence or audit system. Standardize is the fifth S; Sustain is the sixth.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a standard that is written but never followed is the normal case, not a failing; a checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful but how the competent stay reliable when tired, rushed, or interrupted. Keep it warm, relief and a load shared, not lecture; good intentions turned into small machines so the home keeps itself.
- The three tools free the household, they do not police it: the danger word is corporate, so keep every asset human, warm, and light, tools that take a load off you and share it, never a compliance regime. Responsibilities are chosen, not handed down (what is chosen gets done; what is imposed gets subverted). No force or control language (no enforce, comply, crack down, lock in, hold accountable in the punitive sense), no hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "the fridge", "the back of a door", or "a card", not a named app or chore-chart product); teach the concept, the definition box, and the three tools, not a shopping list.
- The source's Standardize step (assign accountability, integrate the standard into daily work, and let it become habit through repetition) is drawn from the 6S source, home-scaled into the checklist, the schedule, and the chosen name, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to standard work and Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed. The remaining Standardize step (visual and pictorial standards) maps onto Chapter 25.
- The friction-meter reading is specific to Ch 24: it HOLDS and does NOT move, because these are tools, not new clearing, so the needle stays exactly where Standardize left it last chapter (four of six S's done, Standardize under way). But the catch that appeared BEHIND the needle last chapter is REINFORCED: where it was a single tooth it is now three, and the backslide it prevents is barred more firmly, because the good way is now carried (a checklist), run on a rhythm (a schedule), and owned (a name). The needle did not advance, the grip on everything behind it got noticeably tighter, which is precisely the work of this S. This is a HOLD with the anti-backslide catch reinforced, NOT a move and NOT a milestone; no new marker on the dial, and the goal stays ahead on the green calm side, because one piece of Standardize remains (Chapter 25) and Sustain still follows.
