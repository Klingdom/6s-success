## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 24 is the second chapter of Part Seven and continues Standardize, the fifth of the six S's. Last chapter you wrote a standard, the best way you knew put on a card where anyone could follow it. That was the crucial first move, and it is not, by itself, enough. A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself; knowing what good looks like has never once been the same as doing it.

Watch a standard nobody follows and it falls down in one of exactly three places: memory (steps get dropped), timing (it drifts to "later"), and ownership (it belongs to everyone, so to no one). None of those is a failure of the standard itself; what was missing was a way to carry it. Those three carriers are the chapter's three tools. A checklist answers what, so nothing is forgotten, and it is not a crutch for the incompetent but how the competent stay reliable when tired, rushed, or interrupted. A schedule answers when: a task with no time gets done in a panic once it is a crisis, so hang it off a cue that already happens. A responsibility answers who: a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, so put one chosen name per standard, because what is chosen gets done.

Put all three on the after-dinner reset and it becomes a fifteen-minute machine that runs on a tired Tuesday. The needle holds, and the catch behind it is stronger now: one tooth became three.
