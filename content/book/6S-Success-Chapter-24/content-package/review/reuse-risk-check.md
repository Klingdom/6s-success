# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 24 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line or image hitting the same follower until it feels canned. This check flags where that can happen.

## High-frequency elements
| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A standard is the best way. A checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make the best way actually happen (the rule) | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, quote card, graphics, HTML | A follower on several platforms sees the hero rule repeatedly | Calendar staggers it; vary the phrasing around it per platform |
| A standard is a recipe on a card, and a recipe on a card does not cook itself (the gap) | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics | Medium to High | The problem the chapter opens on; the most portable line. Use across formats, not stacked on one day. |
| This chapter gives every standard its three tools: a checklist for the what, a schedule for the when, and a name for the who (three-tools line) | Canonical, subtitle, LinkedIn, X, quote card | Medium to High | The hero device named in one sentence; vary the surrounding worked example (the after-dinner reset). |
| A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful ... a list does not (checklist reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card | Medium | The turn that removes the shame; pair with a different framing (short and real / the competent stay reliable) each time. |
| A task with no time does not get done on a rhythm ... quietly prevents it (schedule reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card | Medium | The quiet economy of a time; vary the worked cue (bins off the show, plants off the Sunday coffee). |
| A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one ... has it handled (responsibility reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card | Medium | The chosen-name heart, a callback to Shine; pair with "what is chosen gets done" rather than restating whole. |
| Take the one standard you wrote last chapter and give it the three tools in five minutes (Quick Win / primary CTA) | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, graphics | Medium | The frozen action; lead with a fresh hook before the full instruction. |
| The friction-meter caption ("The needle still does not move ... one piece of Standardize remains and Sustain still follows") | Canonical, graphics, HTML | Low to Medium | Long; it is the honest-HOLD beat, specific to this chapter. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording ("Read the free chapter", "Read it free online", "Read the chapter free in the online book") | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings; most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision
The calendar avoids two assets sharing a hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push one back a day so the rule or the recipe-on-a-card line does not land twice in one day.

## Cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same three-tools content. Post only one to LinkedIn (use the copy-ready slides file; keep the outline as planning).
- The LinkedIn newsletter (`linkedin/linkedin-newsletter-version.md`) and the email newsletter share structure; do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- Web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 24)
- Chapter 24 is the SECOND chapter of Part Seven and the CONTINUE-THE-FIFTH-S chapter. Keep it distinct from Chapter 23 behind it, which opened Standardize with the CONCEPT of a standard and one written standard. Chapter 24 uses that one written standard as its starting point, but assets must NOT re-derive the concept of a standard, re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, or Safety (a one-line callback, such as the Shine line "a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one," is fine, a re-teach is not), or re-tell the 5S+1 history. Grep confirms the assets deliver the three tools and the applied pass and do not rebuild the concept or the earlier S's.
- Do not let this chapter slide FORWARD into the visual standards or the making-it-last work. Ch 24 assets keep it to the three TEXT-BASED tools (lists, times, names) and must NOT build the visual and pictorial standards (Chapter 25, a photo of the ideal state, a storyboard, the standard a picture carries better than words), or the long-term adherence and audit system (Sustain, Chapters 26 to 30). This fence is held: the visual standards and the making-it-last work are named in the package only as "the next chapters" and the S's still ahead, never built. The schedule here gives recurring work a time and integrates it into daily work, but is NOT the Sustain daily-6S-time habit.
- The friction-meter beat is specific to this chapter: the needle HOLDS at (67.6,154.9) where Chapters 22 and 23 left it and does NOT move, because building tools clears no new friction; Sort, Straighten, Shine, and Safety complete are passed markers; and the anti-backslide catch introduced last chapter is REINFORCED from a single tooth into a three-tooth ratchet (the three tools), the "no backslide" arrow barred more solidly, with the GOAL crosshair still ahead at (67,155). This is a HOLD with the anti-backslide catch reinforced, NOT a move and NOT a milestone. Do not imply the needle advanced, that a new milestone was reached, or that the goal is near; the goal stays ahead because one piece of Standardize remains (Chapter 25 completes it) and Sustain still follows.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter is a draft authored in the book voice, grounded in the 6S source (Steps to Complete Standardize: establish checklists and schedules, assign accountability, and integrate standard activities into daily work, tied lightly to standard work and Toyota and the wider use of checklists in aviation and surgery), not supplied verbatim source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft; re-sync if the chapter is revised.
- Quote cards are verbatim: every card in `graphics/quote-card-copy.md` verified byte-identical to a quoted line in `canonical/chapter-quotes.md` (0 mismatches). Nothing borrowed from outside the book; no brand-name products are named, and the Standardize steps, standard work, Toyota origin, and the pilots-and-surgeons checklist reference are used lightly, home-scaled, not overclaimed. The three tools are framed as sharing the load and freeing the reader, warm and human; "responsibility" and "own it" are topic words used warmly, never enforce, comply, or hold accountable in the punitive sense (the force / control scan returned zero control uses across the whole package).

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording (highest-value, lowest-effort fix; several posts close on "Read the free chapter").
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical.
4. Keep Chapter 24 from blurring into its neighbors: distinct from Chapter 23 behind it (the concept of a standard, already delivered) and distinct from the visual standards ahead of it (Chapter 25) and the making-it-last work (Sustain). Chapter 24 is the CONTINUE-THE-FIFTH-S chapter: the three tools that make a written standard run, the applied after-dinner-reset pass, and the honest HOLD with its reinforced anti-backslide catch.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
