When: a schedule, so the work has a time and happens on a rhythm. Who: a responsibility, so one name owns it. A standard with all three stops being a good idea on a card and becomes something the home does on its own.
- **The checklist reframe (frozen):** A checklist is not a sign that you are forgetful. It is what lets a capable person do a complicated thing perfectly without holding all of it in their head. Pilots and surgeons live by them, and not because they are bad at their jobs, but because everyone forgets, and a list does not.
- **The schedule reframe (frozen):** A task with no time does not get done on a rhythm. It gets done in a panic, once it has become a crisis. A schedule is how you trade the occasional emergency for the small, dull, ordinary upkeep that quietly prevents it.
- **The responsibility reframe (frozen, callback to Shine):** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. You met that line back in Shine, and it is even truer of a standard: the fastest way to make sure something never happens is to leave it unowned, so that every person assumes some other person has it handled.
- **One Idea to Keep (frozen):** A written standard is a good idea; a checklist, a schedule, and a name are what make it happen. Give every standard you care about a list so nothing is forgotten, a time so it gets done on a rhythm, and a name so someone owns it, and it will run without you.
