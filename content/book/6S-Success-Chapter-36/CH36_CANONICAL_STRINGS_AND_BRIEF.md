## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The six zones (frozen, structural list):** The family room is six small zones, not one big job: the primary media zone, the toy and play zone, the board game and puzzle zone, the blanket and comfort zone, the charging and device zone, and the craft and activity zone. Do them one at a time and the room the family actually lives in comes back to you a zone at a time.

- **The clean-and-dry rule (frozen, family-room Shine principle):** The family room is the hardest-working soft room in the house, and here clean means two things at once: genuinely clean, and genuinely dry. What lands on this floor goes in a small mouth or under a bare knee, and anything put away damp, a toy, a bin, a throw, quietly breeds mildew. So you wash and dry things properly, you lift the dry mess before you reach for a wet cloth, and you finish every zone on your hands and knees, checking the floor a child shares.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished family room is one a child can play in on the floor and an adult can relax in at the end of a long day: the screen and every vent clear so the consoles run cool, toys in open picture-labelled bins a child can put away alone, games that are complete and playable tonight, throws washed and folded in one basket, devices charging on a hard surface, and a craft surface that clears for dinner in one motion.

- **The dry-mess reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Dried glue, glitter, and paint defeat a wet cloth, which just smears them into a film. In the family room you lift the dry mess first, scraping it off with a plastic edge and lifting glitter out of the seams with a brush, and only then do you wipe. The dry mess comes up clean; the same mess, wetted, becomes a job you fight.

- **The floor-a-child-shares reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** In this room the last step of every zone is the most important, and it happens on your hands and knees: the sweep of the floor a crawling child shares. The marble, the snapped-off wheel, the stray die, the seed bead are all choke-sized, and a cracked plastic edge is sharp enough to open a knee. You are not just tidying the floor here, you are clearing it of the things a small child finds first.

- **The heat-with-nowhere-to-go reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Two quiet fire risks live in this room, and both are heat with nowhere to go. Dust matted across a console vent traps the heat that cooks it, exactly as it does a television, so the vents are the real cleaning. And a tablet or a battery pack charging on a cushion or under a blanket cannot shed its heat, so nothing in this room charges on soft material, ever.

- **The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure):** This is the family room the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the screen and vents clear, the toys and games where a child can reach and return them, the throws washed and folded, and the floor clear of anything small. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a family room a child can play in on the floor and an adult can relax in at night is the one that stays good, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A family room is not clean because the surfaces are wiped. It is clean because you washed and dried the toys and throws properly, cleared the console vents, scraped the dried glue before you wiped, and finished on your hands and knees clearing the floor a child shares. Do one zone this weekend, the toy zone or the media zone, and give the family a room that is genuinely clean and genuinely safe to live in.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / clean and dry, both / dry mess first, then the floor a child shares. The nine-rule Shine method from Chapter 31 still governs; the family room adds washing and drying, the dried-mess scrape, the child-safety sweep, and heat with nowhere to go.

## The four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win:** Do the one thing that saves your consoles. With the brush head on the vacuum, clear the vent slots and back panels of the television, every console, the streaming box, and the soundbar. If dust has packed a slot solid, loosen it with a soft detail brush, then lift it. Wipe the screen with a dry cloth only. The machines run cool instead of throttling and dying.
- **Family Challenge:** Give the shelf to the child. Put the active toys in five or six open bins with no lids on the lowest shelf, one category each, with a photo of the contents taped to the front where a child looking down can see it. Then a child can put the room away without an adult, and the standard survives the people who never read this book.
- **6S Tip:** Two rules make this room work. First, clean means clean and dry: wash the toys, the bins, and the throws, and dry them fully, because a damp bin breeds mildew and a wet toy goes in a mouth. Second, lift the dry mess before the wet cloth: scrape dried glue and paint and brush glitter out of the seams before you wipe, or you just smear it into a film.
- **Common Mistake:** Wiping the surfaces and calling the room done, so the console vents stay packed, the toys go back into damp bins, and the small parts stay on the floor a crawler shares. And wetting a dried-glue mess, which turns a two-minute scrape into a smeared film. Clean and dry, dry mess first, and finish on your hands and knees.

## The room: source facts (from the Micro Zone Manual, Family Room, 6 zones)
Carry specific nouns. Each zone's full Inputs + numbered Steps + inspect-and-flag are authored in the manuscript from the Manual's shine_detail.

1. **Primary media zone.** Job: the TV, consoles, and controllers, so anyone can start watching or playing without hunting a remote. Done: one console per shelf with its docked controllers, disc cases spine out, every cable labelled at both ends, the strip off the carpet, no vent slot covered. Shine (clean is really heat and dust; screen back to vents to floor): the screen (DRY cloth only, straight passes, never spray); the TV back panel and rear vents (brush head, lift the dust mat); the console/streaming box/soundbar vent slots and tops (brush head + soft detail brush in the grooves); the controllers/remotes/thumbstick collars (barely damp then dry, no moisture into a stick housing); the disc-case spines; the shelf under each device (lift it off, wipe, dry); the cable bundle and label tags; the strip body and the floor behind. Call: the console with the save files (plug it in now, move the saves or keep it docked, do not leave it dead behind the new one). Hazards: a flat screen on a low unit tipping (fall/cut/crush); a blanket over a console vent (burn/fire); cables across the open floor (fall). Inspect: a cracked/scorched cable near the plug; a vent that packs solid fast or a console hot to the touch; a shifting mount/shelf; a drink ring over live connections; a warm or browning power strip.
2. **Toy and play zone.** Job: the toys in active rotation, at a height and form a child can put away alone. Done: five or six open lidless bins on the lowest shelf, one category each, a photo of the contents on the front face, the play mat rolled against the wall, clear floor for two children to build. Shine (everything goes in a small mouth or under a bare knee, so clean AND dry): the top of the shelf and its edges (dry cloth, left to right); the bin front faces and photo labels (damp then dry, the child must still read the picture); the shelf boards under the bins (lift each bin, wipe, dry, spills hide under bins); the open bin interiors (empty, wash in mild soapy water, rinse, dry fully, a damp bin keeps a block damp); the hard-plastic toys and blocks (wash, rinse, air-dry fully); the fabric dolls and soft blocks (mesh wash bag through the machine, dry fully); the play mat both faces (vacuum top, lift, vacuum underside and floor, wipe a wipe-clean mat, dry); the floor around and under the shelf (corners and the foot line). Call: the gift nobody plays with (the shelf belongs to the child, not the giver; move it out). Hazards: marbles, small figure accessories, snapped-off wheels (poison/choke/strangle); cracked hard plastic sharp enough to cut, and a top-heavy low shelf (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: a cracked sharp plastic edge; a choke-sized loose part on a shared floor; a soft/musty bin base (a wet toy went back damp); a rocking shelf; a peeling/faded photo label.
3. **Board game and puzzle zone.** Job: games and puzzles that are complete and playable tonight. Done: boxes flat in stacks no more than five high, a tape strip naming each game on the short edge, every loose piece bagged inside its own lid, no box that failed its count. Shine (a greasy film in an eating room; the bottom box gives way): the top of the shelf and edges (dry cloth first); the box lids and top faces (damp then dry at once, do not soften the cardboard); the short edges and the tape name strips (dry, keep the name readable); the bottom box of each stack (lift the stack, wipe, check the corners); the shelf boards (wipe and dry, restack no more than five high); the shelf front edges; the floor beneath (dice, tokens, puzzle pieces escaped from an open box). Call: the game with one piece missing (substitutable pieces replace; game-defining pieces mean the box goes). Hazards: a five- or six-box stack pulled off a high shelf (fall/cut/crush); dice and pawns scattering during play (poison/choke/strangle). Inspect: soft/bowed/crushed corners on a bottom box; a lid film that keeps returning (food handled over the shelf); a stray die or metal piece on the shared floor; a sagging shelf board; a damp patch on a back edge against an outside wall.
4. **Blanket and comfort zone.** Job: the throws and floor cushions people actually reach for, in a container that tells the truth about how many. Done: one basket of four throws folded to the same rectangle, two floor cushions stacked beside it, nothing draped over the sofa back, radiator, or chair arm. Shine (pet hair and popcorn oil concentrate here, so wash everything; check the warm surfaces a blanket drifts onto): the basket exterior and rim (damp or vacuum a woven basket, dry); the throws (wash every one, even the clean-looking, dry fully, fold to one rectangle); the floor cushions (vacuum both faces and seams, wash removable covers, spot-clean fixed, dry); the basket interior corners (hair and crumbs); the radiator top, heater grille, lamp shade, and sofa back a stray throw drifts onto (damp on the radiator, brush head on the grille never a wet cloth, dry cloth on the shade); the floor around and under the basket. Call: the ugly favourite (keep the throw the house actually uses, not the handsome one nobody unfolds). Hazards: a throw over a radiator/heater/lamp shade (burn/fire); a throw sliding off the arm to the floor (fall). Inspect: a scorched/melted/stiffened patch on a throw off a heater; a warm/discoloured patch on a lamp shade; a musty throw put away damp; pet hair and oily film in the basket base; a cracked or loosening woven-basket rim.
5. **Charging and device zone.** Job: charge the household's phones, tablets, controllers, and headphones in one place, on a surface that can take the heat. Done: one strip on a hard surface, four or five labelled cables each ending at a named slot, the empty slots visible by day, no charger plugged in anywhere else in the room. Shine (lint and a stressed cable end fail before a device does): the side-table top (lift the devices and strip off, wipe the sticky drink ring, dry); the strip body and switch (brush/vacuum, damp away from the sockets, dry); the cable runs and ends (dry cloth, feel the last few centimetres near the plug); the charging ports on the devices (blow or soft-brush the lint out, no liquid, a plug stops seating from lint long before the cable wears); the devices (barely damp, moisture off the port, a hand on each to feel for heat); the named slots and empty positions; the floor and the cable drop. Call: the old phone with the photos on it (offload the photos, then recycle it). Hazards: a tablet/battery pack charging on a cushion or under a blanket (burn/fire); a cable long enough to hang to the floor (poison/choke/strangle); drinks on the same table as the strip (water/electricity). Inspect: a cracked/heat-stiffened sheath near the plug; lint packed so a plug will not seat; a warm or browning strip; a cable end that wobbles loose; a drink ring beside the strip.
6. **Craft and activity zone.** Job: craft supplies grouped by activity so a project starts in one motion and the surface clears for dinner just as fast. Done: four lidded labelled bins (paper and card, drawing, glue and tape, kits in progress), scissors point-down in a cup out of the youngest child's reach, one clear work surface, exactly one project bin out. Shine (dried glue and glitter defeat a wet cloth, so scrape and lift dry first): the work surface top (scrape dried glue and paint with a plastic scraper, then wipe and dry); the cracks and seams (vacuum or brush the glitter and flecks out, a cloth skates over them); the bin lids and labels (damp then dry, lids seal flat); the bin interiors (tip out, wipe corners of dried glue crumbs, loose beads, pen lids); the brushes and the scissor cup (wash brushes, stand bristle up, wipe the cup); the glue-gun cooling tile (unplugged and cold, peel the dried strings, wipe); the floor under and around the table (beads, glitter, paper scraps travel furthest here). Call: the half-finished project (a spoken test: will you finish it by a named date, or does it go). Hazards: a glue gun left plugged in (burn/fire); craft knives and sharp scissors blade-up or flat (fall/cut/crush); seed beads, googly eyes, pompoms in a room a crawler shares (poison/choke/strangle). Inspect: a scorch/melt ring where the glue gun cools; a glue-gun cord cracked near the tip; a leaking glue or marker staining a bin base or a solvent smell; a tippy scissor cup; escaped beads/eyes/pompoms on the shared floor.

## The kit for the family room (product TYPES; no brand names)
- **Clean and shine (the inputs that do the work here):** a compact vacuum with attachments (the upholstery tool, crevice tool, and brush head, the room's key input, for the throws, the vents, and the floor); a colour-coded microfiber cloth set, with a dedicated dry cloth kept only for screens; a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner; a floor-material-compatible cleaner; a soft detail brush (vent grooves, glitter in seams, and around charging ports); a plastic scraper (dried glue and paint); mild dish soap and warm water (washing toys and bins); a mesh wash bag (soft toys and covers through the machine); a puff of compressed air or a soft brush for the charging ports.
- **Sort and routing:** a Keep, Relocate, Donate, Recycle, Trash container set.
- **Homes (Storage & Organization):** open-front bins in medium and small, and toy bins with picture labels, at child height; a front-facing book bin; a controller dock and a remote control tray; board-game component bags; a portable project tray for the craft in progress; a pegboard panel for craft tools; a throw basket; medium clear lidded bins for craft supplies and overflow; a cable management kit.
- **Standardize (Visual Controls):** a portable label maker; picture and photo labels for the toy bins; a cord label set (every cable at both ends); a game piece count-and-date card; shelf location labels; floor boundary tape to mark the clear play floor.
- **Safety:** anti-tip anchors and hardware (the television, the toy shelf, and the game shelf, all tip-over risks a child pulls or climbs); a surge protector with overload protection (the one charging strip, on a hard surface, nothing chained); a child-resistant latch and a drawer safety latch (craft solvents and blades, up and out of reach); a stable step stool (a high shelf, never a low bin edge); a moisture absorber or humidity monitor (damp in the toy bins or the throw basket).
