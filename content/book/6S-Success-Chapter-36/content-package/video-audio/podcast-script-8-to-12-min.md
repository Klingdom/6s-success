# Chapter 36 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Family Room

Single host, warm and practical.

## Cold open
Here is something that surprises new parents: in the family room, wiping is not cleaning. This is the room the whole family lives in, where everything goes in small hands and small mouths, so clean has to mean two things at once, clean and dry. Today we clean the whole room the way it actually needs it.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. Today, the family room, the everyday cousin of the living room, the one the household actually lives in. The standard here is not a showroom. It is a room that is genuinely clean, genuinely dry, and genuinely safe to sit on the floor in. So the method adds three things: you wash and dry things properly, because a wet toy goes in a mouth and a damp bin breeds mildew; you lift the dry mess, the glue and glitter and paint, before any wet cloth; and you finish every zone on your hands and knees, sweeping the floor a crawling child shares.

## Part 2: The six zones
The room is six zones: the media zone, the toys, the board games, the blankets, the charging station, and the craft table. Start with the toys or the media zone.

## Part 3: The media zone
A console does not die of age, it dies of dust. The dust matted across its vents traps the heat and cooks it, so the real cleaning here is the vents and the back panel, not the screen. Brush and vacuum every slot. The screen itself gets a dry cloth only.

## Part 4: The toy zone
Everything here goes in a small mouth or under a bare knee, so clean means clean and dry. Empty and wash every bin in soapy water and dry it fully, because a damp bin keeps a damp block damp. Wash the hard toys, run the soft ones through the machine in a mesh bag, and lift each bin to clean the board beneath, where the spills hide. Set the toys up in open picture-labelled bins at child height, so a child can put the room away alone.

## Part 5: The rest
The board games want to be complete and playable, so count them and check the corners of the box at the bottom of the stack. The blankets all go in the wash, even the clean-looking ones, and you check the radiator and the lamp shade a stray one drifts onto. The charging station charges on a hard surface, never soft material, and you clear the lint from the ports. And the craft table, you scrape the dried glue and brush the glitter before any wet cloth.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
The safety list is long here: strap the TV and the toy and game shelves, never cover a vent or charge on soft material, unplug the glue gun, and get the blades and beads up off the shared floor. And the calls are mostly things kept for a person: the console with the save file, the gift nobody plays with, the incomplete game, the ugly favourite everyone actually uses.

## Close
A family room is not clean because the surfaces are wiped. It is clean because you washed and dried the toys and throws, cleared the console vents, scraped the dried glue, and finished on your hands and knees clearing the floor a child shares. Do one zone this weekend. Next time: the primary bedroom.
