# Chapter 36 Summary: The Family Room

The SIXTH room playbook of Part Nine. The heart of the chapter is exactly how to clean and shine each of the six family-room zones, with the exact inputs to gather and the numbered steps to follow.

The family room is the everyday, hardest-working soft room, lived in by the whole household, kids and pets and all, so the standard is not a showroom: it is a room that is genuinely clean, genuinely dry, and genuinely safe to sit on the floor in. On top of the nine-rule Shine method, three habits govern the room. Clean means clean and dry: you wash the toys, bins, and throws and dry them fully, because a damp bin breeds mildew and a wet toy goes in a mouth. You lift the dry mess first: dried glue, glitter, and paint defeat a wet cloth, so you scrape and brush them off before you wipe. And you finish every zone on your hands and knees, sweeping the floor a crawling child shares of the choke-sized things it finds first.

The six zones, each with its inputs, numbered steps, and inspect-and-flag list: the primary media zone (the real cleaning is the vents that keep the consoles cool, not the screen); the toy and play zone (wash and dry every toy and bin, and set them up so a child can put them away alone); the board game and puzzle zone (complete and playable tonight, and check the corners of the box at the bottom of a stack); the blanket and comfort zone (wash everything, and check the warm surfaces a stray blanket drifts onto); the charging and device zone (clear the lint from the ports, and never charge on soft material); and the craft and activity zone (scrape the dried glue and glitter before you wipe).

The room's hard calls are mostly things kept for a person rather than the room, and its two big fire risks are heat with nowhere to go: a covered console vent, and a device charging on soft material.
