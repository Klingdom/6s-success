# Facebook Longform Post: Clean, Dry, and Safe on the Floor

A story-driven post from Chapter 36 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the family room.

---

Here is a thing that surprises new parents about cleaning: in the family room, wiping is not enough.

This is the room the whole family actually lives in, with children on the floor and everything going in small hands and small mouths. So the standard is not a showroom. It is a room that is genuinely clean, genuinely dry, and genuinely safe to sit on the floor in, and each of those words changes how you clean.

Clean and dry, both. You wash the toys, the bins, and the throws and dry them fully, rather than just wiping them, because a wet toy goes straight into a mouth and a damp bin breeds mildew that keeps a damp block damp. Hard toys get soapy water; soft toys go through the machine in a mesh bag; and nothing goes back until it is bone-dry.

Lift the dry mess first. Dried glue, glitter, and paint defeat a wet cloth, which just smears them into a film. So you scrape the glue with a plastic edge and brush the glitter out of the seams first, and only then wipe.

And the most important step of every zone happens on your hands and knees: the sweep of the floor a crawling child shares. The marble, the snapped-off wheel, the stray die, and the seed bead are all choke-sized, and a cracked plastic edge is sharp enough to open a knee.

Two more, both about heat with nowhere to go. Dust matted across a console vent cooks the machine, so clean the vents, not just the screen. And never charge a tablet or a battery pack on a cushion or under a blanket, because it cannot shed its heat.

Do one zone this weekend, the toy zone or the media zone, and give the family a room that is genuinely clean and genuinely safe to live in.
