# Chapter 36 LinkedIn Posts: The Family Room

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. Clean, dry, and safe on the floor
The family room is not a showroom. It is the room the whole family lives in, so the standard is different: genuinely clean, genuinely dry, and genuinely safe to sit on the floor in.

"The family room is the hardest-working soft room in the house, and here clean means two things at once: genuinely clean, and genuinely dry. What lands on this floor goes in a small mouth or under a bare knee, and anything put away damp, a toy, a bin, a throw, quietly breeds mildew."

## 2. A wet toy goes in a mouth
The reason you wash and dry the toys and bins properly, rather than just wiping them, is simple: a wet toy goes straight into a small mouth, and a damp bin breeds mildew that keeps a damp block damp. Wash the hard toys in soapy water, run the soft ones through the machine in a mesh bag, and dry everything fully before it goes back. Clean means clean and dry, both.

## 3. Lift the dry mess first
"Dried glue, glitter, and paint defeat a wet cloth, which just smears them into a film. In the family room you lift the dry mess first, scraping it off with a plastic edge and lifting glitter out of the seams with a brush, and only then do you wipe. The dry mess comes up clean; the same mess, wetted, becomes a job you fight."

## 4. The most important step is on your hands and knees
"In this room the last step of every zone is the most important, and it happens on your hands and knees: the sweep of the floor a crawling child shares. The marble, the snapped-off wheel, the stray die, the seed bead are all choke-sized, and a cracked plastic edge is sharp enough to open a knee."

## 5. Your consoles are dying of dust
Everyone cleans the screen. Nobody cleans the vents. But dust matted across a console vent traps the heat that cooks it. So the real cleaning at the media zone is the vents and the back panel, not the screen. Brush and vacuum every slot on the TV, the consoles, the streaming box, and the soundbar. Wipe the screen with a dry cloth only.

## 6. Never charge on soft material
Two fire risks live in this room, and both are heat with nowhere to go. A covered console vent is one. The other: a tablet or a battery pack charging on a cushion or under a blanket cannot shed its heat. So nothing in this room charges on soft material, ever. One strip, on a hard surface, one named slot per device.

## 7. Give the shelf to the child
"Put the active toys in five or six open bins with no lids on the lowest shelf, one category each, with a photo of the contents taped to the front where a child looking down can see it. Then a child can put the room away without an adult, and the standard survives the people who never read this book."

## 8. Six zones, not one room
"The family room is six small zones, not one big job: the primary media zone, the toy and play zone, the board game and puzzle zone, the blanket and comfort zone, the charging and device zone, and the craft and activity zone."

Start with the toy zone or the media zone for the biggest win.

## 9. Strap the three that tip
A family room has three tall, top-heavy things a child pulls or climbs: the television, the toy shelf, and the game shelf. All three are tip-over risks. While the shelves are bare for cleaning, do the one thing that matters most and strap each to a wall stud, with the weight low.

## 10. A room the family can live in
"A family room is not clean because the surfaces are wiped. It is clean because you washed and dried the toys and throws properly, cleared the console vents, scraped the dried glue before you wiped, and finished on your hands and knees clearing the floor a child shares."
