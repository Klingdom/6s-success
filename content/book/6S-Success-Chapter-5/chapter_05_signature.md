## Signature Overview

**Length:** 16 pages (8 spreads)
**Part:** Part 2, Prepare (the opening chapter of Part 2; it follows Chapter 4's chosen target and hands off to Chapter 6, Photograph Before You Fix)
**Reading time:** 9 to 12 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 58% text, 42% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Teach the reader to look at the target they chose in Chapter 4 and see it clearly, with numbers, before they change anything. Replace the dread of the word "audit" with the friendliness of a flashlight. Give a fast, non-judgmental way to rate a single space on all six S's, find the lowest score (the biggest opportunity), and record a baseline they can beat later. Do not start the work. That is Part 3, Sort, beginning in Chapter 8. This chapter measures; the next chapter photographs; then Chapter 7 defines purpose, and only then does the sorting begin.

**Throughline space:** the entryway drop zone, the first target you chose in Chapter 4 (it tied on Chapter 4's scorecard, and you broke the tie in its favor because you see it every day). Using the reader's likely chosen space keeps continuity and lets the audit feel personal. The drop zone is rich in all six S dimensions: too much stuff (Sort), no homes (Straighten), dust and a wobbly leg (Shine), a trip hazard and scissors in reach (Safety), no obvious system (Standardize), and no habit holding it (Sustain).

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapters 1 to 4. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data and for Safety. Green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns and is load-bearing (strong axes get calm-dots, weak axes get sparks). The friction meter appears at the close.

**New hero motif introduced:** **the 6S Snapshot.** A six-spoke radar (hexagon) with one axis per S, rated 0 to 5. Connect the dots and the lopsided shape shows, at a glance, where a space is strong and where it is starving. Safety rendered in slate to match the book. This is a reusable diagnostic the reader will run on every space in the book, and the Part openers can show it filling out over time.

**Friction meter call:** holds steady, baseline taken. The reader does not move objects in this chapter, so the needle does not earn a move (book rule). Instead, a small marker labeled "baseline" is placed on the dial at the chosen area's current reading (toward the friction side, since an unaudited drop zone scores low). Chapter 4 added a crosshair on the calm side (the aim). Chapter 5 adds the baseline marker on the friction side (where the space is now). Together they frame the gap the work will close. Movement starts in Part 3.

## Page-by-Page Layout

### PAGE 1 (recto) | Chapter Opener
- **Spread objective:** Defuse the word "audit" immediately, before the reader's shoulders climb to their ears. Reframe it as seeing, not grading.
- **Layout:** Full-bleed image. Title lower third. Kicker "Part 2 · Prepare."
- **Visual:** [VISUAL: Full-page photo] A person crouched in front of the entryway drop zone with a small notebook and a coffee, just looking, not touching. Calm morning light. Curiosity, not dread.
- **Caption:** "An audit is not a test you can fail. It is a flashlight. Before you change a single thing about your target, you are going to see it clearly, on purpose, with numbers."

### PAGE 2-3 (Spread A) | The Word Nobody Likes
- **Spread objective:** Name and disarm the dread. An audit at home is not the IRS. It is two honest minutes that tell you where the work should go, so you do not waste effort on the wrong S.
- **Layout:** Left page narrative. Right page a small spot illustration of the drop zone with both green calm-dots and red friction-sparks scattered, signaling "some of this is fine, some is not, and we are about to tell which."
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Opening" plus the top of "Why Measure Before You Move," roughly 480 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > You cannot improve what you have not honestly seen. Two minutes of looking saves an hour of fixing the wrong thing.

### PAGE 4-5 (Spread B) | Why Measure Before You Move
- **Spread objective:** Make the case for a baseline. Without a number, "better" is a feeling that fades, and you cannot tell whether the work held. A baseline turns a vague mess into a specific, fixable, measurable thing, and gives you a number to beat.
- **Layout:** Text with a simple before/after meter spot (a needle with no reading vs a needle with a marked baseline).
- **Body copy:** "Why Measure Before You Move," continued, roughly 420 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Skipping the audit because you already know the space is bad. You know it feels bad. You do not yet know which of the six things is actually broken, and that is the only useful information.

### PAGE 6-7 (Spread C) | The Six-S Snapshot
- **Spread objective:** Hand the reader the tool. One plain question per S, a zero-to-five score, and a shape that shows where the space is starving. This is the hero spread.
- **Layout:** Short intro text, then the hero radar infographic dominating the spread.
- **Body copy:** "The Six-S Snapshot," roughly 440 words plus the six questions.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The 6S Snapshot." A six-spoke radar, one axis per S (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety in slate, Standardize, Sustain), rated 0 to 5, with an example drop-zone reading plotted as a lopsided polygon. Green calm-dot on the strongest axis, red friction-spark on the weakest. Center note: the dents are where the work goes.
- **Boxed definition:**
  > **The audit in one line**
  > Score your space zero to five on each S, add it up, and write the number down. The lowest score is your biggest opportunity. The total is the baseline you will beat.

### PAGE 8-9 (Spread D) | What the Shape Tells You
- **Spread objective:** Teach the reader to read their own snapshot. A space low on Sort is buried. A space low on Straighten has no homes. A space fine on Sort but low on Sustain was fixed once and drifted. The shape names the real problem.
- **Layout:** Text left, a small gallery of three example snapshot shapes (the buried space, the homeless space, the drifted space), each a tiny radar with a one-line read.
- **Body copy:** "What the Shape Tells You," roughly 420 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > Your lowest score is not a failure. It is a map. It is telling you exactly where an hour of effort will buy the most relief.

### PAGE 10-11 (Spread E) | Run the Audit on Your Target
- **Spread objective:** Walk the reader through scoring their own chosen space, fast and honestly, with the six questions written plainly and a worked example on the entryway drop zone.
- **Layout:** Text with the Home 6S Audit scorecard illustration (six questions, a 0 to 5 column, a worked drop-zone example, a total out of 30).
- **Body copy:** "Run the Audit on Your Target," roughly 460 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The Home 6S Audit." The six questions in a column, each with a 0 to 5 scale and a worked drop-zone score, Safety row in slate, a Baseline total of 14 out of 30 at the bottom.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Score your target space right now, in two minutes, from memory if you have to. An imperfect number beats no number. You can always re-score with the space in front of you.

### PAGE 12-13 (Spread F) | Honest, Fast, and Kind
- **Spread objective:** Set the right spirit. Score the space, not yourself. Use gut numbers, not agonized ones. And remember that a low baseline is good news, because it means there is a lot of relief available, cheaply.
- **Layout:** Text with a small spot of a calm hand writing "14 / 30" in a notebook, a green calm-dot beside it.
- **Body copy:** "Honest, Fast, and Kind," roughly 380 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Auditing the whole house at once. The audit is per area. Score the one space you chose in Chapter 4. The house gets audited one target at a time, the same way it gets fixed.

### PAGE 14-15 (Spread G) | Your Baseline
- **Spread objective:** Lock the number and what it means. The reader now has a score, a weakest S, and a target total to beat after the work. This is the prepared starting line.
- **Layout:** A short synthesis section and a compact "Your baseline" checklist.
- **Body copy:** "Your Baseline," roughly 340 words.
- **Checklist:**
  > **Take your baseline**
  > - Score your target zero to five on each S, using the six questions.
  > - Add the six scores for a total out of thirty.
  > - Circle your lowest score. That is where the work will mostly go.
  > - Write the total and the date in your notebook or phone.
  > - Keep it. You will re-score after the work and watch the number climb.
- **Callout:**
  > **Family Challenge**
  > Each person audits the space they nominated last chapter and reports its weakest S at dinner. Lowest single score wins the right to go first, because that space has the most relief waiting in it.

### PAGE 16 (verso) | Chapter Close and What's Next
- **Spread objective:** Land the baseline warmly, and tee up the photograph in Chapter 6.
- **Layout:** Short close, "One Idea to Keep" box, friction-meter spot (holds, baseline marked), "Next" strip.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Close," roughly 240 words.
- **Boxed recap:**
  > **One Idea to Keep**
  > An audit is not a grade you take. It is a flashlight you aim. The lowest number is not bad news about you. It is good news about how much relief is sitting there, waiting to be claimed.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Friction-meter spot] The meter holding where it sat, with a small slate "baseline" marker now placed on the dial at the chosen area's current reading, and the Chapter 4 crosshair still resting on the calm side as the goal. The gap between them is the work.
- **Next teaser:** "Next: Chapter 6 · Photograph Before You Fix. You have the number. Now take the picture, because the camera sees what the eye has already learned to forgive."

## Production Notes / Review Flags
- This chapter measures; it does not fix. Keep the reader from sorting here. The work begins in Part 3 (Chapter 8). Part 2 is Prepare: audit (Ch 5), photograph (Ch 6), purpose (Ch 7).
- The radar/snapshot is a new diagnostic device. Confirm the 0 to 5 scale and the six plain-language questions. They are written to be answerable from memory in two minutes; if review prefers a 1 to 5 scale or different question wording, both relabel easily.
- The friction meter gains a baseline marker on the friction side, complementing Chapter 4's crosshair on the calm side. Confirm this read. If review prefers the meter to stay simpler, the marker is easy to drop.
- Keep "audit" friendly throughout. The book has a later chapter, "6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections," so the warm, non-judgmental framing established here should carry forward.
- Reuse Chapters 1 to 4's exact type, palette, and callout styling. The 6S Snapshot should be drawn so it can be shown filling out across later Part openers.
