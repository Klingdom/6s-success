**Hook line:** "The word audit makes everyone flinch. Here is why it should not."

The word audit makes everyone flinch. It is a clipboard word. A bad-news word. But an audit at home is not a test you can fail. It is a flashlight. You are not grading yourself. You are just looking at one space, on purpose, and putting a few honest numbers on it before you change a single thing. Two minutes of looking. That is the whole job.

## SHORT 2: A Low Score Is Good News

**Length:** about 45 seconds
**Hook line:** "Your space scored low? Good. That is the best news you will get all week."

[VISUAL] A hand writes "14 / 30" in a small notebook. A green dot drawn beside it.

[SPOKEN]
Your space scored low. Good. I mean it. A low baseline is the best news you will get all week. Here is the math nobody says out loud. A space that scores fourteen out of thirty has sixteen points of relief just sitting in it, available, and mostly cheap to claim. A space that already scores a twenty-eight? It is pretty good. There is barely any joy left to mine from it. The worst spaces give the best wins. So when your number comes back low, do not wince. That is just a measure of how much better your day is about to get for one focused hour.

## SHORT 3: You Are About to Fix the Wrong Thing

**Length:** about 50 seconds
**Hook line:** "You are about to spend an hour fixing the wrong thing. Here is how to not."

[VISUAL] A person enthusiastically dumping drop-zone clutter into a donation box. Fast, satisfying, a little ruthless.

[SPOKEN]
You are about to spend a whole hour fixing the wrong thing. Watch. Your drop zone feels chaotic, so you do the obvious thing. You throw stuff away. An hour later, you step back. And it is still a mess. Because the problem was never too much stuff. The problem was that nothing had a home. So now you have less stuff and the exact same chaos, and you decide 6S does not work. It works. You just aimed at the wrong S, because you never measured. A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways. Measure first, and you find the one that actually needs you.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- "Step 1: throw stuff away"
- "Step 2: still a mess"
- "The problem was no homes, not too much stuff."
- "Measure before you move."

## SHORT 4: The Six Questions

**Length:** about 60 seconds
**Hook line:** "Six questions. Two minutes. You will know exactly where to start."

[VISUAL] A blank six-spoke radar on a warm background. Each question pops one axis at a time as it is asked.

[SPOKEN]
Six questions, two minutes, and you will know exactly where to start in any messy space. Score each one zero to five. Sort. How much of what is here do you actually use? Straighten. Does everything have an obvious home you can find fast? Shine. Is it clean, no hidden dust, leaks, or wear? Safety. Could anything here hurt someone, especially a child? Standardize. Is the right way obvious to anyone, or only in your head? Sustain. Is any habit keeping it up, or does it just drift back? Now add the six up. That total out of thirty is your baseline, and your lowest score is exactly where to start.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- "Sort: do you use it?"
- "Straighten: does it have a home?"
- "Shine: is it clean?"
- "Safety: could it hurt someone?" (in slate)
- "Standardize: is the right way obvious?"
- "Sustain: is a habit holding it?"
- "Total / 30 = your baseline"

[VISUAL] The radar fills into a lopsided shape as each score lands. A green calm-dot on the high axis, red friction-sparks on the two low ones.

## SHORT 5: Read the Shape

**Length:** about 55 seconds
**Hook line:** "The shape of your mess tells you what is actually wrong. Here is how to read it."

[VISUAL] Three different radar shapes appear one at a time, each a clear silhouette.

[SPOKEN]
The shape of your mess tells you what is actually wrong, and once you can read it you will diagnose a room in seconds. Shape one caves in on Sort. That space is buried. Too much stuff. Nothing else can be fixed until the volume comes down, so start by removing. Shape two is full on Sort but collapses on Straighten. That space is not too full. It just has no homes. It does not need a purge, it needs a key dish and a hook. And shape three is great early, then falls off a cliff on Standardize and Sustain. That one was organized before, and it keeps drifting back because nothing was built to hold the fix. The shape is not an insult. It is a map.

[ON SCREEN TEXT]
- "Caved in on Sort = buried. Remove."
- "Low on Straighten = no homes. Assign homes."
- "Drops off at the end = keeps drifting. Build a habit."
- "Your lowest score is a map, not a failure."

[VISUAL] Each shape gets its one-line read stamped beside it, then the third shape glows as "the most common one."
