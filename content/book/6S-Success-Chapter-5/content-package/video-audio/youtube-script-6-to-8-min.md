# YouTube Script: The 6S Home Audit

**Chapter 5, 6S Success: Home Edition (Part Two, Prepare)**
**Slug:** the-6s-home-audit
**Estimated runtime:** 6 to 8 minutes (roughly 1,050 spoken words)
**Format:** Alternating [HOST] narration with [VISUAL] / [B-ROLL] / [ON SCREEN TEXT] direction

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00 to 0:45)

[VISUAL] Tight shot on a cluttered entryway drop zone. Keys, mail, a single stray glove, sunglasses, a tangle of charging cables. Slightly desaturated, a little tense.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "AUDIT"

[HOST]
Nobody has ever been excited to hear the word audit. It is a cold word. A clipboard word. It shows up with bad news and reading glasses. So before your shoulders climb up to your ears, let me take that word away from you for a second and hand it back as something a lot friendlier.

[B-ROLL] Slow push in on a person crouching down in front of that drop zone, a small notebook in one hand, a coffee on the floor beside them. They are just looking. Not touching anything. Warm morning light starts to bleed in. The tension drains out of the frame.

[HOST]
An audit at home is not a test you can fail. It is a flashlight. Today, before you change one single thing about the space you chose, you are going to see it clearly, on purpose, with numbers. That is the whole job.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "An audit is a flashlight, not a grade."

---

## THE SETUP (0:45 to 1:45)

[VISUAL] Title card. "The 6S Home Audit." Warm paper texture, terracotta and honey accents.

[HOST]
Quick recap. Last chapter you picked a target. One small, high-friction, low-effort space, written down and waiting for you. Maybe it is the entryway drop zone, that little table by the door where the keys, the mail, the sunglasses, and that one lonely glove all go to argue. I am going to use that drop zone as our example today, but everything works on whatever space you actually chose.

[B-ROLL] Hands hovering over the clutter, eager, then pulling back. A gentle "not yet" gesture.

[HOST]
You are motivated. You are ready. And the natural urge right now is to skip straight to fixing it. Resist that for about ten more minutes, because there is one quick step that makes every minute of fixing actually land where it helps.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Measure before you move."

[HOST]
Here is the thing nobody tells you about a messy space. It is never equally broken in all six ways. One or two of the S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem. And if you cannot tell which, you will pour your energy into the parts that were never the issue, and then wonder why the space still feels off.

---

## WHY MEASURE FIRST (1:45 to 3:00)

[VISUAL] Split screen. Left, a person scrubbing and tidying happily. Right, the same space three weeks later, drifting back to chaos.

[HOST]
Let me make the case for the number, because skipping it feels efficient and it really is not. Without a baseline, better is just a feeling, and feelings fade. You will spend a satisfying hour on your drop zone, step back, feel good, and three weeks later you will have no honest way to tell whether it actually improved or whether you just remember it fondly.

[B-ROLL] Close on a hand purging items into a donation box, fast and a little ruthless.

[HOST]
And worse, you will not know whether the thing you fixed was the thing that was broken. Picture this. Your drop zone feels chaotic, so you spend your hour throwing things away. But the real problem was never too much stuff. It was that nothing had a home. So now you have less stuff and the exact same chaos, and you walk away thinking 6S does not work. It works. You just aimed at the wrong S, because you never measured.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "A number protects you from fixing the wrong thing."

[HOST]
A baseline does three quiet things. It makes the problem specific. It gives you a number to beat. And it stops you from fixing the wrong thing. A carpenter measures twice and cuts once, and nobody calls the carpenter a procrastinator. Your two minutes of scoring is the measure-twice of home organizing. You are not stalling. You are aiming.

---

## THE 6S SNAPSHOT, BUILT AXIS BY AXIS (3:00 to 4:45)

[VISUAL] A blank six-spoke radar fades in on screen, a clean hexagon. One empty axis per S. Center is zero, the rim is five.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "The 6S Snapshot"

[HOST]
Here is the tool. It is one plain question per S, a score from zero to five, and a shape. You already know the six S's, so you are not learning anything new. You are just pointing each one at your space like a flashlight and asking, honestly, how is this doing on this one thing. Let me build it with you.

[VISUAL] As the host names each S, that axis lights up and a dot lands at the example score. The polygon draws itself, point by point.

[HOST]
Sort. Of everything in this space, how much do you actually use? On the drop zone, most of it earns its place, so that is a four.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Sort ... 4"

[HOST]
Straighten. Does everything have an obvious home you can find fast? Mostly, but not quite. Call it a three.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Straighten ... 3"

[HOST]
Shine. Is it clean, with no hidden dust, leaks, or wear? Decent. Another three.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Shine ... 3"

[HOST]
Safety. Could anything here hurt someone, especially a child? There are scissors at grabbing height. That is a two.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Safety ... 2" (rendered in slate)

[HOST]
Standardize. Is the right way obvious to anyone, or does the system live only in your head? Only in your head. That is a one.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Standardize ... 1"

[HOST]
Sustain. Is any habit keeping this up, or does it just drift back to chaos between heroic cleanups? It just drifts. One.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Sustain ... 1"

[VISUAL] The finished radar. A lopsided polygon, full on top, caved in at the bottom. A green calm-dot glows on the strong Sort axis. Red friction-sparks flicker on Standardize and Sustain.

[HOST]
Add it up. Fourteen out of thirty. And look at that shape. This space is not generally bad. It is specifically starving on Standardize and Sustain.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Baseline 14 / 30. The dents are where the work goes."

---

## READ THE SHAPE (4:45 to 6:00)

[VISUAL] A small gallery of three different radar shapes appears side by side.

[HOST]
The reason it is a shape and not just a list is that the shape tells a story, and once you can read it you can diagnose a space in seconds.

[VISUAL] Shape one caves in hard at the top, on Sort.

[HOST]
A space that caves in on Sort is buried. There is just too much in it. Nothing else can be fixed until the volume comes down. The shape is telling you to start by removing, not arranging.

[VISUAL] Shape two is full on Sort but collapses on Straighten.

[HOST]
A space that is fine on Sort but low on Straighten is the opposite. It does not have too much. It has no homes. The right amount of stuff is there, it just floats. That space does not need a purge. It needs a key dish, a mail tray, a hook. Assign homes. Do not throw things away.

[VISUAL] Shape three, our drop zone, full early then falling off a cliff at the end.

[HOST]
And then there is our drop zone. Good early, then the floor drops out on Standardize and Sustain. This is the most common shape in a home that has been organized before. It keeps sliding back because nothing was ever built to hold the fix in place. No visible standard. No small habit. It got tidied, and tidying always expires.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Your lowest score is not a failure. It is a map."

[HOST]
See what just happened. You have not touched the space, and you already know that throwing things away would waste your afternoon, and that the real work is a simple labeled system and one ten-second habit. That is the snapshot earning its keep.

---

## LOW SCORE IS GOOD NEWS, AND THE CTA (6:00 to 7:30)

[VISUAL] Close on a calm hand writing "14 / 30" in a small notebook. A green calm-dot drawn beside it.

[HOST]
Now, fourteen out of thirty. Notice what that number is and is not. It is not a grade on your character or your housekeeping. It is a measurement of one small table by a door, taken so you can make it better and prove that you did. So three quick rules. Be honest. Score it as it actually is on an ordinary Tuesday, not the rare day company is coming. Be fast. About ten seconds a question. Your first instinct is almost always right.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Honest. Fast. Kind."

[HOST]
And be kind, which is the one people forget. A low baseline is good news. Read that again, because it runs against every instinct. A space that scores a fourteen has sixteen points of relief sitting in it, available, mostly cheap. A space that scores a twenty-eight is already pretty good and there is not much joy left to mine from it. The worst spaces give the best wins. That is a genuinely lovely thing about all of this.

[VISUAL] The friction meter appears. The needle holds steady, no objects have moved. A small slate marker labeled NOW sits on the friction side. A crosshair labeled GOAL rests on the calm side. The gap between them glows softly.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "NOW ........ GOAL. The gap is the work."

[HOST]
The audit moved no objects, so the needle holds. But now it has a reading. NOW marks where your space sits today. GOAL marks where it is going. Everything from here is just closing that gap.

[B-ROLL] The person stands up from the drop zone, notebook closed, calm and a little lighter. They glance back at the space, no dread, just a plan.

[HOST]
So here is your two-minute homework. Go score the space you chose. Zero to five on each S, using the six questions. Add it up for a total out of thirty. Circle your lowest score, because that is where the work will go. Then write the total and today's date somewhere you will find it again, because we are going to beat that number together.

[ON SCREEN TEXT] "Take your baseline. Read Chapter 5 free in the online book."

[HOST]
That is the whole audit. Not a clipboard. A flashlight. Take your baseline today, and next chapter we take the before photo, because the eye forgives the pile you have stopped noticing, and the camera does not. I will see you there.

[VISUAL] End card. "The 6S Home Audit. 6S Success: Home Edition." Soft CTA: "Read the chapter free online. Next: Photograph Before You Fix."
