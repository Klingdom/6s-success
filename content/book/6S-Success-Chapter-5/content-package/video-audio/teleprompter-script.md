# Teleprompter Script: The 6S Home Audit

**Chapter 5, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-6s-home-audit
**Length:** about 650 words, roughly 4.5 minutes spoken
**Read naturally. Short lines. Breathe at the breaks.**

---

Nobody has ever been excited
to hear the word audit.

It is a cold word.
A clipboard word.
It shows up with bad news
and reading glasses.

So let me hand it back to you
as something friendlier.

An audit at home
is not a test you can fail.
It is a flashlight.

Before you change a single thing
about the space you chose,
you are going to look at it,
on purpose,
and put a few honest numbers on it.

That is the whole audit.
Two minutes of looking.

Here is what nobody tells you
about a messy space.

It is never equally broken
in all six ways.

One or two of the S's
are usually fine.
One or two are the real problem.

And if you cannot tell which,
you will pour your energy
into the parts
that were never the issue,
and wonder why the space
still feels off.

So let me make the case
for the number.

Without a baseline,
better is just a feeling.
And feelings fade.

You will spend a satisfying hour,
step back, feel good,
and three weeks later
have no honest way to tell
if it actually improved.

Worse,
you might fix the wrong thing.

Picture a drop zone
that feels chaotic.
So you throw things away.

But the problem
was never too much stuff.
The problem was
that nothing had a home.

Now you have less stuff
and the same chaos.

You did not fail.
You just aimed at the wrong S,
because you never measured.

A carpenter measures twice
and cuts once.
Nobody calls the carpenter
a procrastinator.

You are not stalling.
You are aiming.

So here is the tool.
One plain question per S.
A score from zero to five.

Sort.
How much of what is here
do you actually use?

Straighten.
Does everything have a home
you can find fast?

Shine.
Is it clean,
with no hidden dust or wear?

Safety.
Could anything here
hurt someone,
especially a child?

Standardize.
Is the right way obvious to anyone,
or only in your head?

Sustain.
Is any habit keeping it up,
or does it just drift?

Now add the six scores together.

In our example,
the entryway drop zone
comes back fourteen out of thirty.

And here is the part that matters.
The shape tells a story.

It is not failing at everything.
It is fine on Sort.
It is decent in the middle.
And it is starving
on Standardize and Sustain.

That tells you something real.
This space was organized before.
It keeps drifting back
because nothing was built
to hold the fix in place.

So you already know.
Do not throw things away.
Build a simple system
and one small habit.

Same hour.
Completely different result.

Now,
three quick rules.

Be honest.
Score it as it is
on an ordinary Tuesday,
not the day company is coming.

Be fast.
About ten seconds a question.
Your first instinct
is almost always right.

And be kind.

A low baseline is good news.

A space that scores a fourteen
has sixteen points of relief
sitting in it,
available,
and mostly cheap.

The worst spaces
give the best wins.

So here is where you are.
You have a number,
a shape,
and a weakest S.

That is what prepared means.
Not perfect.
Not even started.
Just seen clearly,
measured honestly,
and aimed correctly.

So go score the space you chose.
Add it up.
Circle your lowest score.

Write the total
and today's date
somewhere you will find it again.

Because we are going to beat
that number together.

An audit is not a grade you take.
It is a flashlight you aim.

Take your baseline today.

And next,
we take the before photo.

Because the eye is generous.
The eye forgives.
The eye edits out the pile
you have stopped noticing.

The camera does not.

I will see you there.
