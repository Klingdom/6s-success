**2.**
A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways. One or two of the S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem. If you cannot tell which, you will fix the part that was never the issue.

*(approx 201 chars)*

**3.**
Without a baseline, better is just a feeling, and feelings fade. A number turns a vague, guilty "this space is a mess" into a specific, almost cheerful "this is a 14, and the low scores are Standardize and Sustain."

*(approx 219 chars)*

**4.**
A baseline protects you from fixing the wrong thing. A drop zone that feels chaotic might have a perfectly fine amount of stuff. The real problem might be that nothing has a home. Measure before you move.

Chapter 5, 6S Success: Home Edition. Free online.

*(approx 255 chars)*

**5.**
Score each S from 0 to 5. Zero means "this is the whole problem." Five means "this is genuinely handled." Do not agonize. A gut number you write down in ten seconds beats a perfect number you never get around to.

*(approx 216 chars)*

---

**6.**
The 6S Snapshot turns six scores into a shape. Plot Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain from 0 to 5, connect the dots, and the lopsided shape shows at a glance where the space is starving.

*(approx 211 chars)*

---

**7.**
Read the shape. Caved in on Sort means the space is buried, so remove first. Fine on Sort but low on Straighten means it has no homes, so assign them. Good early then low on Standardize and Sustain means it was tidied before and drifted back.

*(approx 244 chars)*

**8.**
Your lowest score is not a failure. It is a map. It points directly at the cheapest relief in the room, the one S where an hour of effort will buy the most calm.

**9.**
Be honest. Score the space as it is on an ordinary Tuesday, not on the rare day company is coming. A flattering audit is a lie you tell only yourself, and it points your effort in the wrong direction.
