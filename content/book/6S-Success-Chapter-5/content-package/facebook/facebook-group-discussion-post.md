# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (a messy space is never equally broken in all six ways, and a low score is good news), then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, written so it does NOT read like an ad.

---

Small idea that quietly changed how I look at a messy room, and I want to hear your take.

For ages I treated my worst spots as just "bad." The drop zone by the door, the junk drawer, the shelf that collects everything. One big undifferentiated mess. So I would attack the whole thing, usually by throwing stuff away, and somehow it never quite got better.

Then I picked up a reframe that made it click: a messy space is never equally broken in all the same ways. One part is usually fine. One part is the actual problem.

So now, before I touch anything, I give a space a quick gut score from 0 to 5 on a few things. Do I actually use what is here? Does everything have an obvious home? Is it clean? Is anything a safety issue? Is the right way to keep it obvious, or does it only live in my head? Is any habit holding it together, or does it just drift back?

Nine times out of ten, the mess is not "too much stuff." It is that nothing has a home, or that I tidied it once and never built a habit to keep it. Totally different fixes. Knowing which one saves me from purging a space that never needed purging.

The other thing that surprised me: a low score is good news. A space that scores terribly has the most relief just sitting in it, waiting, usually for cheap. The worst spots give the best wins.

Okay, here is what I actually want to know from you all:

Think of your worst room. Of those six (using what is there, having homes, cleanliness, safety, an obvious system, and a habit that keeps it up), which one would score the lowest? Be honest. Mine is "a habit that keeps it up." I am great at the one-time fix and terrible at maintaining it.

Drop yours in the comments. I am curious whether we all have the same weak spot or wildly different ones. 🙂

(For anyone who asks, this scoring idea comes from Chapter 5 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, it is just the most useful way to look at a messy room that I have run into lately and I wanted to share it.)
