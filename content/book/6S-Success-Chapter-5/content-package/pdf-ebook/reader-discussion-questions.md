# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: The 6S Home Audit

Ten open-ended questions grounded in Chapter 5. There are no wrong answers. They work for a solo reader with a notebook or a group talking it through together.

1. The chapter calls "audit" a cold, clipboard word that arrives with bad news and reading glasses. What does the word bring up for you, and did the flashlight reframe change how it felt by the end of the chapter?

2. Think of a time you fixed a space and it drifted back, or you fixed the wrong thing entirely. Looking back, which of the six S's was actually the problem, and would measuring first have changed what you did?

3. The chapter says "better" without a baseline is just a feeling that fades. Where in your home have you trusted a tidy feeling instead of a number, and how did that turn out?

4. Pick one space and score it out loud on all six S's, zero to five. Which question was hardest to answer honestly, and why?

5. When you connect your scores into a shape, what does the shape tell you? Is your space buried (low on Sort), homeless (low on Straighten), or fixed-before-and-drifting (low on Standardize and Sustain)?

6. The worked drop zone scores fourteen out of thirty and is weakest at Standardize and Sustain. Does that pattern sound like any space in your own home? What would you build to hold the fix in place?

7. "A flattering audit is a lie you tell only yourself." Where are you tempted to round up out of pride, and what would an honest score of that space actually be on an ordinary Tuesday?

8. The chapter insists a low baseline is good news, because the worst spaces give the best wins. Do you believe that? What makes it easy or hard to see a low number as opportunity instead of a verdict?

9. The audit scores the space, never yourself. How well were you able to keep those separate while scoring, and what helps you score a space without it feeling like a grade on your character?

10. The chapter measures but moves nothing, on purpose. How does it feel to deliberately not start fixing yet? Is waiting to aim harder or easier for you than starting the work right away?
