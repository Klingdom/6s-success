# Chapter 5 Calls to Action: The 6S Home Audit

This chapter measures; it does not fix. Its strongest CTA is the audit itself: score your chosen space and write down the baseline. The CTAs match the chapter's friendly, non-judgmental tone.

## Primary CTA
Take your baseline. "Score your target space zero to five on each S, using the six questions. Add them up for a total out of thirty. Circle your lowest score; that is where the work will go. Write the total and the date down. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Before you fix the space you chose, spend two honest minutes seeing it clearly. Chapter 5 shows you how to audit it with a flashlight, not a clipboard. Read it free online, then Chapter 6 takes the before photo."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Score your target space right now, in two minutes, from memory if you have to. An imperfect number beats no number.
- **Family Challenge:** Each person audits the space they nominated and reports its weakest S at dinner. Lowest single score goes first.

## Soft CTAs for social posts (rotate these)
- "This is from Chapter 5 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online."
- "An audit is a flashlight, not a grade. The full chapter is free in the online book."
- "Score your space on the six S's and find your weakest one. The chapter walks you through it."
- "Grab the free Home 6S Audit Form and take your baseline."
- "Next after your baseline: Chapter 6, the before photo."

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the signature)
- **The Home 6S Audit Form:** a printable with the six questions, the zero-to-five scale, space for the total and date, and a blank 6S Snapshot radar to plot.
- **The 6S Snapshot card:** a printable and phone wallpaper of the blank radar, for scoring any space on the fly.

## CTA writing notes
- The audit is the action. Lead with "score your space and write down the baseline," which is concrete, free, and two minutes.
- Keep the tone friendly and non-judgmental. The whole chapter exists to take the dread out of "audit," so the CTAs must not add pressure.
- "A low score is good news" is the most counterintuitive, shareable hook in this chapter. Lead social with it.
- Avoid hype. The number is honest, the relief is real, the claims stay the size of the chapter.
