# Chapter 5 Summary: The 6S Home Audit

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Two, Prepare*

## One-line summary

Before you fix the target you chose, spend two honest minutes scoring it zero to five on each of the six S's, so you find the one S that is actually dragging the space down and record a baseline you can beat.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Chapter 5 opens Part Two by taking the dread out of the word "audit." It is a flashlight, not a grade. The reader scores their chosen target space on all six S's using one plain question each, plots the scores on the 6S Snapshot (a six-spoke radar), and reads the lopsided shape to find the weakest S. A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways, so the audit shows exactly where an hour of effort buys the most relief. The reader records a baseline total out of thirty, a number to beat after the work. The example drop zone scores fourteen.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 5 is the first chapter of Part Two, Prepare. The reader has chosen a target (Chapter 4). Before changing anything, this chapter teaches them to see it clearly and put numbers on it.

It starts by disarming "audit." At home it is not a clipboard or a test. It is two minutes of looking that tell you where the work should go. The chapter argues for measuring before moving: without a baseline, "better" is a feeling that fades, you cannot tell whether the work held, and worst of all you might fix the wrong thing. A drop zone that feels chaotic might have a fine amount of stuff and simply lack homes; purge it and you will have less stuff and the same chaos.

The hero tool is the 6S Snapshot, a six-spoke radar rated zero to five on each S, with one plain question per S. Connect the scores into a shape and the shape tells a story: a space caved in on Sort is buried and needs removing; a space fine on Sort but low on Straighten needs homes, not a purge; a space that scores well early and collapses on Standardize and Sustain was organized before and keeps drifting because nothing held the fix.

The reader scores their own target (the worked example, an entryway drop zone, totals fourteen out of thirty, weakest at Standardize and Sustain), records the baseline and date, and circles the lowest score as the marching order. The spirit is honest, fast, and kind: a low baseline is good news, because the worst spaces give the best wins. Next, Chapter 6 photographs the space.
