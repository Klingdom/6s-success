# Chapter 5 Key Takeaways: The 6S Home Audit

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **An audit at home is a flashlight, not a grade.** It is two honest minutes of looking, taken so you can make a space better and prove that you did. Score the space, never yourself.

2. **A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways.** One or two S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem. The audit tells you which.

3. **Measure before you move.** Without a baseline, "better" is a feeling that fades, and you cannot tell whether the work held.

4. **A baseline protects you from fixing the wrong thing.** A space that feels chaotic might just lack homes. Purge it and you get less stuff and the same chaos, then wrongly conclude 6S does not work.

5. **The 6S Snapshot turns six scores into a shape.** Rate the space zero to five on each S with one plain question, and the lopsided shape shows where it is starving.

6. **Read the shape to find the real problem.** Caved in on Sort means buried, start by removing. Fine on Sort but low on Straighten means no homes, assign homes. Good early then collapsing on Standardize and Sustain means it was organized before and keeps drifting.

7. **Your lowest score is a map, not an insult.** It points directly at where an hour of effort buys the most relief.

8. **The total is a number to beat.** Add the six scores for a baseline out of thirty, write it down with the date, and re-score after the work to watch it climb.

9. **Be honest.** Score the space as it is on an ordinary Tuesday, not the rare day company is coming. A flattering audit is a lie that points your effort the wrong way.

10. **Be fast.** Ten seconds per question. Your first instinct is almost always right; agonizing just talks you into a kinder number.

11. **A low baseline is good news.** A fourteen has sixteen points of relief sitting in it, mostly cheap. The worst spaces give the best wins.

12. **The audit is per area, not the whole house.** Score the one space you chose. The house gets audited one target at a time, the same way it gets fixed.
