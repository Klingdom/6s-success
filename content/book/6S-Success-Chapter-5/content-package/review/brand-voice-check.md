# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 5 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 5 adds a specific job: it takes the dread out of a measurement step. It asks the reader to score their space honestly without turning the score into a verdict on themselves. The voice must stay friendly and never clinical.

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," uses a recognizable space (the entryway drop zone where the keys and mail argue).
- **Non-judgmental.** "Score the space, not yourself." "A low number is not a verdict."
- **Plain over technical.** A radar chart becomes "a shape with a story," not a data visualization.
- **Concrete and specific.** A clipboard, a notebook, a key dish, a wobbly leg, an ordinary Tuesday.
- **Gently witty.** "A word that arrives with bad news and reading glasses." "The one stray glove."
- **Honest about scope.** No objects move, so the friction meter holds. The chapter measures, it does not fix.

## Channel-by-channel check

| Channel | Voice held? | Notes |

## Specific voice wins
- The "flashlight, not a grade" reframe is preserved everywhere and never turns clinical, which matters because the chapter exists to make a measurement feel kind.
- "A low score is good news" survives intact as the chapter's most counterintuitive line, across the thread, a quote card, and the article.
- "Score the space, not yourself" carries the anti-shame thread that runs from Chapter 1's "rooms are not messy people."

## Voice risks to watch
- **Contractions on X.** The book uses few; X uses several. Deliberate platform choice. Revise only if brand guidelines forbid contractions everywhere.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the sign-offs so they do not feel templated.
- **Do not let the radar go technical.** A few graphics and video notes could tip into "data visualization" language. Spot-checked: the assets keep it as "a shape with a story." Keep it that way.
- **Emoji on Facebook.** Used sparingly. Confirm it matches your brand's emoji policy.

## Banned-language audit
- Widened dash scan is clean: no em dashes, no en dashes, and no spaced-hyphen " - " used as a dash anywhere (verified, zero hits as separators).
- No prohibited hype words as filler. "Transform" appears only as SVG code attributes in the publishable HTML, never in prose.
- No "in today's fast-paced world" or similar.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all twelve packages, and the take-the-dread-out-of-audit job of Chapter 5 is honored throughout. Approve the voice. The chapter itself still needs author sign-off, since it was authored rather than supplied.
