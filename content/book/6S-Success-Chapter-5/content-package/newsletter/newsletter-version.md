# Newsletter: The 6S Home Audit

**Subject line (placeholder):** A low score is good news (here is why)

**Preview text (placeholder):** Two honest minutes with a flashlight, not a clipboard.

---

Hi friend,

Nobody has ever been excited to hear the word "audit." It is a cold word, a clipboard word, a word that shows up with bad news and reading glasses. So before your shoulders climb to your ears, let me hand it back to you as something friendlier.

An audit at home is not a test you can fail. It is a flashlight. Before you change a single thing about the space you chose last chapter, you are going to see it clearly, on purpose, and put a few honest numbers on it. That is the whole thing. Not a grade. Just a calm look at a space you have been walking past on autopilot for months.

Here is the part nobody tells you. A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways. One or two of the S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem. And if you cannot tell which, you will pour your energy into the parts that were never the issue and wonder why the space still feels off.

**Measure before you move, or you might fix the wrong thing.**

Without a baseline, "better" is just a feeling, and feelings fade. You spend a satisfying hour on the entryway drop zone, step back, feel good, and three weeks later you cannot honestly say whether it improved or whether you just remember it fondly. Worse, you cannot tell whether the thing you fixed was the thing that was broken. A drop zone that feels chaotic might have a perfectly fine amount of stuff. Its real problem might be that nothing has a home. Purge it, and you end up with less stuff and the same chaos, then conclude that 6S does not work. It works. You just aimed at the wrong S because you never measured.

So here is the tool: the 6S Snapshot. One plain question per S, a score from zero to five, and a shape.

- **Sort.** How much of what is here do you actually use?
- **Straighten.** Does everything have an obvious home you can find fast?
- **Shine.** Is it clean, with no hidden dust, leaks, or wear?
- **Safety.** Could anything here hurt someone, especially a child?
- **Standardize.** Is the right way obvious to anyone, or only in your head?
- **Sustain.** Is any habit keeping it up, or does it just drift?

Score each one in about ten seconds, no agonizing. Then connect the six numbers into a shape, and the lopsided shape shows you, at a glance, where the space is starving.

In the chapter, the worked example is that entryway drop zone. It scores Sort 4, Straighten 3, Shine 3, Safety 2, Standardize 1, Sustain 1. A baseline of 14 out of 30. The space is fine on Sort, decent in the middle, and starving on Standardize and Sustain. It has been organized before, and it keeps sliding back because nothing was ever built to hold the fix. That number tells you to skip the purge and spend your hour building a simple, visible system and one small habit instead. Same hour, completely different result.

And here is the kind part, the one that runs against every instinct. A low baseline is good news. A space that scores a fourteen has sixteen points of relief sitting in it, mostly cheap. A space that scores a twenty-eight is already pretty good. The worst spaces give the best wins.

**Try this week:** Score one space, the one you already chose. Six questions, zero to five, two minutes, from memory if you have to. Add it up, circle your lowest score, and write the total and today's date in a notebook or your phone. That is your baseline. You will beat it after the work, and the jump will feel like proof.

That is all "prepared" means in this book. Not perfect. Not even started. Just seen clearly, measured honestly, and aimed correctly.
