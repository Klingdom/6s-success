# Diagram Ideas: The 6S Home Audit

**Chapter 5, "The 6S Home Audit"**
**6S Success: Home Edition, Part Two, Prepare**

Five diagrams, each drawn in the book's flat line-and-color style: roughly 3px stroke, rounded corners, warm paper, Fraunces labels, Newsreader notes. Palette is the locked set (paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark `#CB4B36`). All five already exist or are implied in the final HTML, so these match the canonical art.

---

## 1. The 6S Snapshot radar (the hero)

**Purpose:** Turn six separate scores into one shape so the reader sees, instantly, that a space is not generally bad but specifically starved on one or two S's.

**What it shows:** A six-spoke radar on a hexagon grid, center 0 to rim 5. Axes in book order: Sort top, then clockwise Straighten, Shine, Safety (in slate), Standardize, Sustain. The worked drop-zone reading plotted and connected: Sort 4, Straighten 3, Shine 3, Safety 2, Standardize 1, Sustain 1. A green calm-dot ring on the Sort vertex (strongest), a red friction-spark on Standardize and Sustain (weakest). Baseline badge `14 / 30`.

**Rough layout:** Radar centered on a panel card. Three faint grid rings. Terracotta polygon at 14 percent fill with solid vertex dots. Axis labels outside the rim, score numerals just inside each vertex. Italic center note: "The dents are where the work goes." Slate-outlined baseline pill top-right.

---

## 2. The Home 6S Audit scorecard

**Purpose:** Give the reader a fill-in-the-blank model they can copy exactly onto their own space, and show that a "bad" space is really a specific pattern of scores.

**What it shows:** Six rows, one per S, each with its plain question and a 0 to 5 score for the example drop zone. A bold baseline total row at the bottom.

**Rough layout:** A clean table on a panel card. Header strip: `ASK ONE QUESTION PER S` left, `0 TO 5` right, a slate rule under it. Each row: S name in Fraunces terracotta, question in Newsreader ink, score far right in Fraunces. The Safety row tinted slate (`#ECF1F4`) with slate type. The two 1s (Standardize, Sustain) in friction red. Bottom: a slate-outlined `BASELINE  14 / 30` badge. The questions, verbatim:

- Sort: How much of what is here do you actually use? (4)
- Straighten: Does everything have an obvious home you can find fast? (3)
- Shine: Is it clean, with no hidden dust, leaks, or wear? (3)
- Safety: Could anything here hurt someone, especially a child? (2)
- Standardize: Is the right way obvious to anyone, or only in your head? (1)
- Sustain: Is any habit keeping it up, or does it just drift? (1)

## 3. Three example snapshot shapes, side by side

**Purpose:** Teach the reader to read the shape of their own snapshot, so they know whether to remove, to assign homes, or to build a standard and a habit.

**What it shows:** Three small radars in a row, each a different lopsided shape with a one-line read underneath.

- **The buried space:** caves in toward the center on Sort. Read: "Too much in it. Start by removing, not arranging."
- **The homeless space:** fine on Sort, collapses on Straighten. Read: "The right amount of stuff, but nothing has a home. Assign homes, do not purge."
- **The drifted space:** good on the first few S's, falls off a cliff on Standardize and Sustain (this is the drop zone). Read: "Organized before, keeps sliding back. Build a visible standard and one tiny habit."

**Rough layout:** Three equal panel cards across. Each radar small, no score numerals, just the shape and a single red spark on the weakest axis. Fraunces caption title and one Newsreader read line per card. Keep them visually identical except for the shape, so the dents do the talking.

---

## 4. Fixing the wrong thing, before and after

**Purpose:** Show, concretely, why measuring first matters: purge a space that only lacked homes and you end up with less stuff and the same chaos.

**What it shows:** A two-panel before/after of the same drop zone, framed as the wrong fix.

- **Left, "Purged without auditing":** the table with far fewer items but still scattered, a red friction-spark still hovering, a caption: "Less stuff. Same chaos. The problem was never the amount."
- **Right, "Audited first":** the same table with a key dish, a mail tray, and a hook added, items in their homes, a green calm-dot. Caption: "A home for everything and a small habit to hold it. Same hour, different result."

**Rough layout:** Two side-by-side line-art spots on a single panel, divided by a thin slate rule with a small arrow. Both drawn in the book's drop-zone style (console table, line-art objects). The contrast is in the dots: red spark left, green calm-dot right. Header strip: "Aim before you act."

---

## 5. The friction meter with NOW and GOAL

**Purpose:** Honor the book rule that the meter moves only when objects move, while showing the chapter produced a real result: a measured starting point and a known target.

**What it shows:** The recurring friction-meter gauge holding steady, with a baseline marker on the friction side and a goal crosshair on the calm side. The gap between them is the work ahead.

**Rough layout:** A 180-degree arc gauge. The arc runs green (calm) on the left, honey in the middle, friction red on the right. The needle holds where it sat, no move. A slate diamond marker labeled `NOW` on the friction side. A slate crosshair labeled `GOAL` (carried from Chapter 4) on the calm side. Labels `CALM` left, `FRICTION` right. Italic note under the gauge: "The gap between NOW and GOAL is the work." Compact, about 420 x 260, set small at the chapter close.
