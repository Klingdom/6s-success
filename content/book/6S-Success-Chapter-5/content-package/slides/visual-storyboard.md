# Visual Storyboard

## The 6S Home Audit

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 5. Ties the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

This storyboard maps each key beat to the recommended image or diagram, the palette cues that keep it on-brand, and the recurring green calm-dot and red friction-spark motif. The rule for the motif is simple and load-bearing throughout the book: green calm-dots sit on the strong axes (what is already working), red friction-sparks sit on the weak axes (where the work goes). Use them sparingly so each one reads.

---

## The palette, in one place

- Warm paper background: #F7F2E9. Panel and card fill: #FBF7EF. Ink for text and line art: #2B2622.
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: primary accent, S-labels, the data polygon, headline emphasis.
- Honey #DDA63A: secondary accent, the friction-meter mid-band, art notes.
- Slate #3C5A6B: all data framing and the Safety axis and row, always. Safety is never terracotta.
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: strength markers on strong axes.
- Red friction-spark #CB4B36: problem markers on weak axes, and the friction end of the meter.
- Fonts: Fraunces for display and S-labels, Newsreader for body and questions. Inter-style sans only for small all-caps labels (BASELINE, NOW, GOAL).
- Strokes: warm line-and-flat-color, about 3px, rounded corners, matching Chapters 1 to 4.

## Beat 1: The calm audit (opener / hook)

- Recommended image: a person crouched in front of the entryway drop zone with a small notebook and a coffee on the floor, just looking, not touching. Calm morning light, curiosity not dread.
- Deck slide 1, carousel slide 2.
- Palette cues: warm paper and dawn-gradient light (#FBE9C8 fading to #FBF7EF), ink line work, a honey-toned table.
- Motif: one green calm-dot near a part of the table that is fine, one or two red friction-sparks on the trip-hazard shoe and the cluttered corner. Mixed dots say: some of this is fine, some is not, and we are about to tell which.

## Beat 7: The 6S Snapshot radar (hero)

- Recommended diagram: the hero six-spoke radar. Sort at top, then clockwise Straighten, Shine, Safety (slate), Standardize, Sustain. Axes 0 at center to 5 at rim. The example drop-zone reading (Sort 4, Straighten 3, Shine 3, Safety 2, Standardize 1, Sustain 1) plotted as a lopsided polygon.
- Deck slide 7, carousel slide 6. This is the chapter's hero and a reusable book diagnostic, so draw it cleanly enough to reuse on later Part openers as it fills out.
- Palette cues: warm-paper or panel field, rule-colored grid rings (#E2D8C4), terracotta polygon at about 14 percent fill with a 3px terracotta stroke, terracotta vertex dots. The Safety label in slate. A white "BASELINE 14 / 30" badge framed in slate.
- Motif: a green calm-dot ring on the strong Sort vertex, a red friction-spark (a small four-line star) on the starved Standardize and Sustain vertices. Center note in italic Newsreader: the dents are where the work goes.

## Beat 8: Three example shapes (reading the shape)

- Recommended diagram: three tiny radars in a row, each a recognizable silhouette.
  - "Buried": deeply dented at Sort, full elsewhere. Read: too much, start by removing.
  - "Homeless": full on Sort, collapsed at Straighten. Read: no homes, add a key dish, a tray, a hook.
  - "Drifted": full on the first axes, falling off a cliff at Standardize and Sustain. Read: organized before, kept drifting.
- Deck slide 8, carousel slide 7.
- Palette cues: same grid and terracotta polygons, scaled down. Keep labels minimal, one read-line each in Newsreader.
- Motif: one red friction-spark on each shape's weakest axis so the eye lands on the problem instantly. The "drifted" shape is the drop-zone family and should match the hero.

## Beat 13: The friction meter, baseline taken (close)

- Recommended diagram: the recurring friction meter holding steady. A slate NOW baseline marker on the friction side, a slate GOAL crosshair on the calm side, the gap between them clearly the work.
- Deck slide 13, carousel slide 10.
- Palette cues: the arc in green-honey-red (#6E8B5B, #DDA63A, #CB4B36), ink needle holding, slate markers, the "the gap between NOW and GOAL is the work" line in italic Newsreader.
- Motif: NOW label in friction-spark red, GOAL label in green calm. The needle has not moved, because no objects moved, but it finally has a reading. This honors the book rule that the meter moves only when objects move.

---

## Recurring-motif checklist (apply across both decks)

- Green calm-dot only on strong axes and settled, finished states (Sort vertex, the locked baseline, the relief-available field).
- Red friction-spark only on weak axes and unsolved problems (Standardize and Sustain vertices, the surviving spark in the wrong-fix trap, the NOW marker).
- Safety is always slate, never terracotta, on every axis, row, and label.
- One BASELINE 14 / 30 readout recurs across the radar, the scorecard, and the close, so the number becomes familiar.
- Keep markers sparse. Two calm-dots and two sparks per busy frame at most, so each one still means something.
