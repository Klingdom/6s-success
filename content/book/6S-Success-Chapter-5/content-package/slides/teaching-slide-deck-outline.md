# Teaching Slide Deck Outline

## The 6S Home Audit

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 5, Part Two: Prepare*

A 13-slide teaching deck. Two honest minutes with a flashlight, not a clipboard. Score your chosen space, find the one S dragging it down, and write down a baseline you can beat.

Design system: warm paper (#F7F2E9), panel (#FBF7EF), ink (#2B2622). Fraunces for display, Newsreader for body. Terracotta (#BC4B2A) and honey (#DDA63A) accents, slate (#3C5A6B) for data and for Safety. Green calm-dot (#6E8B5B) on strong axes, red friction-spark (#CB4B36) on weak ones.

---

### Slide 1 | An audit is not a test you can fail

- Nobody has ever been excited to hear the word audit. It is a cold, clipboard word.
- At home it is something friendlier. It is a flashlight, not a grade.
- Before you change one thing about your target, you see it clearly, on purpose, with numbers.

[VISUAL] Full-bleed calm-audit photo: a person crouched in front of the entryway drop zone with a small notebook and a coffee on the floor, just looking, not touching. Warm morning light. Title in the lower third over the image.

---

### Slide 2 | The flashlight reframe

- An audit at home is not the IRS. It is two honest minutes of looking.
- It tells you where the work should go, so you do not waste effort on the wrong S.
- You are not stalling. You are aiming.

[VISUAL] A simple flashlight beam falling across a line-art drop zone, the lit cone picking out specific spots. Terracotta beam edge on warm paper.

---

### Slide 3 | A space is never equally broken

- A messy space is never equally broken in all six ways.
- One or two S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem.
- If you cannot tell which, you pour energy into the parts that were never the issue.

[VISUAL] The drop zone in line art with mixed markers scattered over it: green calm-dots on the parts that are fine, red friction-sparks on the parts that are not. The caption: some of this is fine, some is not, and we are about to tell which.

---

### Slide 4 | Measure before you move

- Without a baseline, "better" is just a feeling, and feelings fade.
- A number does three things: makes the problem specific, gives you a score to beat, and protects you from fixing the wrong thing.
- The two minutes you spend scoring your space is the measure-twice of home organizing.

[VISUAL] Two friction-meter spots side by side: a needle with no reading, then a needle with a marked baseline. Honey and slate dial, ink needle.

---

### Slide 5 | The wrong-fix trap

- The drop zone that feels chaotic might have a perfectly fine amount of stuff.
- Its real problem might be that nothing has a home.
- Purge it and you get less stuff and the same chaos, then wrongly conclude 6S does not work. It works. You aimed at the wrong S.

[VISUAL] Before/after line art: a purged but still-chaotic drop zone, with a red friction-spark still sitting on the Straighten problem. Caption: less stuff, same chaos.

---

### Slide 6 | The six questions

- One plain question per S, scored zero (the whole problem) to five (genuinely handled).
- Sort: how much of what is here do you actually use? Straighten: does everything have an obvious home you can find fast? Shine: is it clean, with no hidden dust, leaks, or wear?
- Safety: could anything here hurt someone, especially a child? Standardize: is the right way obvious to anyone, or only in your head? Sustain: is any habit keeping it up, or does it just drift?

[VISUAL] Six stacked question rows, each with a 0-to-5 scale. The Safety row tinted slate. Terracotta S-labels in Fraunces, Newsreader questions.

---

### Slide 7 | The 6S Snapshot

- Connect the six scores into a shape, and the shape tells you more in one glance than an hour of staring.
- Sort at top, then clockwise: Straighten, Shine, Safety (in slate), Standardize, Sustain. Each axis runs 0 at the center to 5 at the rim.
- The dents are where the work goes.

[VISUAL] The hero radar: a six-spoke hexagon with the example drop-zone reading (Sort 4, Straighten 3, Shine 3, Safety 2, Standardize 1, Sustain 1) plotted as a lopsided terracotta polygon. Green calm-dot on the strong Sort axis, red friction-spark on the starved Standardize and Sustain axes. Baseline 14 / 30 badge in the corner.

---

### Slide 8 | Reading the shape

- The shape has a story. Three common ones:
- Caved in on Sort means buried. There is too much. Start by removing, not arranging.
- Fine on Sort but low on Straighten means no homes. It needs a key dish, a mail tray, a hook, not a purge.
- Good early then a cliff at Standardize and Sustain means it was organized before and keeps drifting. Build a visible standard and one tiny habit.

[VISUAL] Three small radar shapes in a row: "buried" (dented at Sort), "homeless" (dented at Straighten), "drifted" (full early, collapsing at Standardize and Sustain). A red friction-spark on each shape's weak axis, a one-line read under each.

---

### Slide 9 | The worked example: the drop zone

- The entryway drop zone scores 14 out of 30. Sort 4, Straighten 3, Shine 3, Safety 2, Standardize 1, Sustain 1.
- It is not failing at everything. It is fine on Sort, decent in the middle, and starving on Standardize and Sustain.
- Without the audit you might purge a space that did not need it. With it, you know to build a simple visible system and one small habit to hold it.

[VISUAL] The Home 6S Audit scorecard: six question rows with the worked scores down the right, Safety row in slate, a bold "Baseline 14 / 30" badge at the bottom. The two 1s in friction-spark red.

---

### Slide 10 | Your lowest score is a map

- Your lowest score is not a failure. It is a map.
- It points directly at where an hour of effort buys the most relief.
- You have not touched the space, and you already know the real work is a labeled system and a ten-second habit.

[VISUAL] A single radar with a red friction-spark on the weakest axis and an arrow leading from it to a small label: "the cheapest relief in the room."

---

### Slide 11 | A low score is good news

- A low baseline is good news. Read that again, because it runs against every instinct.
- A space that scores a fourteen has sixteen points of relief sitting in it, available, mostly cheap.
- A space that scores a twenty-eight is already good and there is not much joy left to mine. The worst spaces give the best wins.

[VISUAL] A meter or simple bar showing a 14 with sixteen points of "relief available" shaded ahead of it in green calm tones. Honest, warm, not hype.

---

### Slide 12 | Take your baseline

- Score your target zero to five on each S, using the six questions.
- Add the six scores for a total out of thirty. Circle your lowest score. That is where the work will mostly go.
- Write the total and the date in your notebook or phone. Keep it. You will re-score after the work and watch the number climb.

[VISUAL] A hand writing "14 / 30" and today's date in a small notebook, the space softly out of focus behind, a green calm-dot beside the number. Be honest, be fast, be kind.

---

### Slide 13 | A number to beat

- The audit moved no objects, but you can no longer look at your target as a vague, guilty mess. You see a specific shape with specific dents.
- An audit is not a grade you take. It is a flashlight you aim. The lowest number is good news about how much relief is waiting to be claimed.
- Soft CTA: Score your space, write down the baseline, and keep it. The full chapter is free in the online book. Next, Chapter 6 takes the before photo.

[VISUAL] The friction meter holding steady, a slate NOW baseline marker on the friction side and a GOAL crosshair on the calm side. Caption: the gap between NOW and GOAL is the work.
