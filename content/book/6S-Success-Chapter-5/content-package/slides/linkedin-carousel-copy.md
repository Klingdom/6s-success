## The 6S Home Audit

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 5. On-slide copy, ready to set.*

10 slides. Slide 1 is the hook, slide 10 is the soft CTA. Keep the text short and let the design system carry the rest: warm paper, Fraunces headlines, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for Safety and data, green calm-dots on strong axes, red friction-sparks on weak ones.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**A low score is good news.**

You read that right. The messiest spot in your home is the one with the most relief waiting in it.

[VISUAL] Big Fraunces headline on warm paper. A small radar shape, dented and lopsided, in the corner with one red friction-spark on its weakest axis.

---

### Slide 2

**Nobody likes the word "audit."**

It is a cold, clipboard word. So let us hand it back as something friendlier.

At home, an audit is a flashlight. Not a grade.

[VISUAL] A flashlight beam crossing a line-art drop zone, lighting up a few specific spots. Terracotta beam edge.

---

### Slide 3

**A messy space is never equally broken.**

One or two of the six S's are usually fine. One or two are the real problem.

The audit tells you which. Guessing wastes your afternoon.

[VISUAL] The drop zone in line art with mixed markers: green calm-dots on the parts that work, red friction-sparks on the parts that do not.

---

### Slide 4

**Measure before you move.**

Without a baseline, "better" is just a feeling, and feelings fade.

A number makes the mess specific, gives you a score to beat, and stops you fixing the wrong thing.

[VISUAL] Two small friction meters: one needle with no reading, one with a marked baseline. Honey and slate dial.

---

### Slide 5

**Six questions. Ten seconds each.**

Sort: how much of this do you actually use?
Straighten: does everything have an obvious home?
Shine: is it clean, no hidden dust or wear?
Safety: could anything here hurt someone?
Standardize: is the right way obvious, or only in your head?
Sustain: is any habit keeping it up, or does it just drift?

[VISUAL] Six stacked rows, each with a 0-to-5 scale. Safety row tinted slate. Terracotta S-labels.

---

### Slide 6

**Connect the scores. Get a shape.**

The 6S Snapshot turns six numbers into one lopsided picture.

The dents show, at a glance, exactly where your space is starving.

[VISUAL] The hero radar: hexagon, Sort at top, the example drop-zone polygon (4, 3, 3, 2, 1, 1). Green calm-dot on Sort, red friction-sparks on Standardize and Sustain. "14 / 30" badge.

---

### Slide 7

**Read the shape.**

Caved in on Sort? Buried. Start by removing.
Low on Straighten? No homes. Add a key dish, a tray, a hook.
Strong early, then a cliff on Standardize and Sustain? Organized before, kept drifting.

[VISUAL] Three tiny radar shapes in a row, each with a red friction-spark on its weak axis and a one-line read underneath.

---

### Slide 8

**The drop zone scores a 14.**

Fine on Sort. Decent in the middle. Starving on Standardize and Sustain.

So the fix is not a purge. It is a simple visible system and one small habit.

[VISUAL] The Home 6S Audit scorecard with the worked scores, Safety in slate, the two 1s in friction-spark red, a bold "Baseline 14 / 30."

---

### Slide 9

**Your lowest score is a map.**

It is not a verdict on you. It points straight at the cheapest relief in the room.

A 14 has sixteen points of relief sitting in it, mostly cheap. The worst spaces give the best wins.

[VISUAL] A single radar with an arrow from the weakest axis to a label: "the cheapest relief in the room." Green calm tones.

---

### Slide 10 (soft CTA)

**Take your baseline today.**

Score your space zero to five on each S. Add it up out of thirty. Circle the lowest number and write it down with the date.

This is from Chapter 5 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Read it free in the online book.
