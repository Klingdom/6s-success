## Why each held image is held

### `ch5-image4a.png` and `ch5-image6a.png` · scale conflict

Both explicitly define the scale as **"1 (not good) to 5 (excellent)"**. The chapter defines it
as **zero to five**, and the zero is load-bearing: *"Score it zero, for this is the whole
problem."* Six paragraphs then hang off that ("A zero means it is mostly things that belong
somewhere else, or nowhere", and five more).

We considered changing the text instead. The scale appears in ~8 places across the source trio
**and in 37 files of the content package** (quote cards, LinkedIn, Facebook, newsletter, the
publishable HTML, the infographic spec, and the appendix Home 6S Audit Form). Regenerating two
plates is far cheaper than a package rebuild, so the **text stands and the plates change**.

These two are otherwise excellent. `4a` is titled **"The 6S Snapshot"**, the chapter's hero
motif by name, and both are canon-correct with Safety fourth.

### `ch5-image3a.png` · contradicts the canonical worked example

The chapter's worked example for the entryway drop zone is fixed:

> **Sort 4 · Straighten 3 · Shine 3 · Safety 2 · Standardize 1 · Sustain 1 = 14 / 30**
> Weakest: Standardize and Sustain, both at 1.

Figure 3a prints **"14 out of 30"** but then states **"Lowest scores: Standardize (2) and
Sustain (2)"**, and its radar plots Safety as one of the *strongest* axes. That inverts the
narrative: in this chapter Safety scores 2 and the floor is Standardize/Sustain at 1. The
plotted values also do not visibly sum to 14.

Otherwise a strong figure and the closest match to the chapter's "feeling versus measuring"
argument. It needs the canonical six values and nothing else.

### `ch5-image2a.png` · misattributed quotation, plus voice issues

Carries **"What gets measured gets managed." — Peter Drucker.**

This is one of the most frequently misattributed lines in management writing. The Drucker
Institute has stated it does not appear in his published work; it is often traced instead to
V. F. Ridgway's 1956 paper, which was in fact a *critique* of measurement. **Verify or remove
before print** — a misattributed epigraph is a factual error in a published book, and this one
is well known enough that readers will catch it.

`ch5-image3a.png` carries a companion quote, **"What gets measured gets improved." — Tom
Peters**, of similarly uncertain provenance. Recommend dropping both attributions and keeping
the lines unattributed, or replacing them with something from the project's own source material.

Also in 2a: the contraction "you're", and an **em dash** before the attribution.

### `ch5-image5a.png` · radar data would clash with the retained hero

"Reading the Shape" is a good match for the *What the Shape Tells You* section, but its example
radar carries yet another set of values. With the canonical hero radar retained as SVG two
sections earlier, the reader would meet two different snapshots of the same audit. It should be
regenerated as part of one consistent radar family alongside 4a.

### `ch5-image7a`, `8a`, `9a` · partial series

Two typos to fix if these are reused: 8a reads **"Easy yo return"** (should be "to"), and
**"cables and charges"** (should be "chargers").

## Regeneration brief

Regenerate as one consistent family. Every radar in the chapter must plot the same numbers.

**1. The scale is ZERO to five.** Not 1 to 5. Label the innermost ring 0. The zero must be
reachable and must read as "this is the whole problem." Five reads as "this is genuinely
handled." Total out of 30.

**2. The canonical worked example (entryway drop zone), to be used in every figure that shows
one:**

| S | Score |
|---|---|
| Sort | 4 |
| Straighten | 3 |
| Shine | 3 |
| **Safety** | **2** |
| Standardize | 1 |
| Sustain | 1 |
| **Total** | **14 / 30** |

Lowest scores are **Standardize and Sustain, both 1**. Safety is 2. Sort is the strongest at 4.

**3. Six S's, in order, Safety fourth:** Sort · Straighten · Shine · **Safety** · Standardize ·
Sustain. (All the "a" plates already get this right.)

**4. No attributed quotations** unless the attribution has been verified. Drop the Drucker and
Tom Peters lines.

**5. House voice:** no contractions ("is not", not "isn't"), and **no em or en dashes**. The
"a" set uses contractions throughout; the chapter text uses none anywhere.

**6. Still needed, not yet drawn:** per-S deep dives for **Safety, Standardize and Sustain** to
complete the 7a/8a/9a series, and a closing figure for *A Number to Beat*.

## A gap in the project's quality gates

Chapter 4 surfaced that baked-in em dashes bypass the dash scan because validation cannot read a
PNG. Chapter 5 shows the same blind spot is much wider than punctuation. **None of these are
catchable by any gate the project currently runs:**

- a scale that disagrees with the text
- worked-example numbers that disagree with the manuscript
- a misattributed quotation
- contractions against house voice
- typos inside artwork

Every one has to be caught by a human, or by a model, actually reading the plates. Worth
treating "read every figure's own text against the chapter" as a required production gate
rather than an optional pass.
