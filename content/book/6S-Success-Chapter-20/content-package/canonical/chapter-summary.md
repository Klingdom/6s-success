## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 20 is the first chapter of Part Six and opens Safety, the fourth of the six S's, the "plus one" that American practice added to 5S. Chapter 19 completed Shine: the home is cleared by Sort, arranged by Straighten, and kept clean and read by Shine. This chapter turns from the state of the room to the people in it.

It begins with the reframe the whole part rests on: a clean home is not a safe home. Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean; none asked whether it could hurt someone, so a rug at the top of the stairs, medicine within a child's reach, and a dead smoke alarm pass every earlier test while staying dangerous. A light 5S+1 history explains why safety was pulled out and named its own S: something this important should never be left to chance.

The hero is the safety lens, one swapped question asked of the whole home: could this hurt someone. Paired with it is protect people first, a triage that ranks hazards by how badly and how soon they could hurt someone, fixing the worst and soonest first. Heinrich's pyramid is the reason to act now, while hazards are still only near misses. Safety is framed as love, not fear: handle the few real dangers so you can stop worrying. One first pass handles the top dangers today. The needle takes its first Safety step; the goal is still ahead.
