## Search intent
Making a home safe without alarm. Readers arriving here already have a home that is cleared, arranged, and kept clean and read (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Chapters 1 to 19), and they sense, correctly, that clean is not the same as safe. They want a way to see the dangers they have walked past for years, decide what to fix first, and handle the worst ones without turning the home into a place of constant dread. The chapter answers with one hero device and one triage: the safety lens, a single new question asked of the whole home, could this hurt someone; and protect people first, ranking hazards by how badly and how soon they could hurt someone and fixing the worst and soonest first. It gives the light 5S+1 history, the hazard definition, and Heinrich's pyramid as the calm reason to act now, frames safety as love not fear, and makes one first high-value pass. Because a part opener that removes real dangers, the friction meter takes its first Safety step off the Shine-complete milestone, with the goal still ahead.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Separates tidy from safe, naming the invisible gap the first three S's never ask about, rather than assuming a clean home is a safe one
- Tells the light, accurate 5S+1 history of why safety was pulled out and named its own S, tied to Toyota and the Toyota Production System, without overclaiming
- Makes safety a matter of the question you ask, the safety lens, so readers can see hazards they have habituated to, instead of a generic checklist
- Gives a plain triage, protect people first, ranking by how badly and how soon, so readers know what to fix first instead of trying to fix everything at once
- Uses Heinrich's pyramid as a calm reason to act now, while hazards are still only near misses, rather than as a frightening statistic
- Reframes safety as love, not fear, so the whole step reduces worry rather than stoking it, with no fear-mongering, no graphic injury, and no alarmist numbers
- Keeps scope tight: this opens Safety with the lens, the triage, and one first pass; it does not build the systematic room-by-room hazard hunt (Chapter 21), ongoing safety systems (Chapter 22), documented standards (Standardize), or long-term adherence (Sustain)
- Takes the first Safety step honestly, the fourth S has only begun and the goal is still ahead, rather than claiming the home is finished or safe
