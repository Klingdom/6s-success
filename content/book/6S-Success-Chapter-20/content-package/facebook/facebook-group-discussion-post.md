# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (a clean, tidy home is not automatically a safe one, and seeing the difference takes a new question rather than more cleaning) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small idea that genuinely changed how I look at my house, and then I want to hear from you all.

For years my measure of a good home was that it was clean and tidy. Clear the clutter, give everything a place, keep it clean, and I felt like the job was done and the house was in good shape. Safe, even. It turns out those are not the same thing, and it took me an embarrassingly long time to see it.

Here is the line that did it.

"A clean home is not a safe home. Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean. None of them asked whether it could hurt someone, so a home can be cleared, arranged, and spotless and still hold every danger it started with."

That stopped me. The rug that always slid a little at the top of the stairs was never messy. The cleaning bottles under the sink at my toddler's exact height were never misplaced. The smoke alarm with the dead battery was perfectly tidy. Every one of them passed the tidy test, and every one of them could hurt someone. Tidy and safe are just different questions, and I had only ever been asking one.

So I did one slow walk of the house asking a different thing: not is this tidy, but could this hurt someone. I did not try to fix everything, just the worst few. Fresh battery in the alarm. Bottles up high behind a latch. Grip tape under that rug. It took maybe twenty minutes, and I set down a background worry I did not even know I was carrying.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

When you walk your home asking not whether it is tidy but whether it could hurt someone, what is the first thing that jumps out at you? And for the parents here, what is the one hazard you handled that let you finally breathe out about it?

I will go first in the comments. 😄

(If anyone wants it written out properly, this is from Chapter 20 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the question that changed how I see my own house.)
