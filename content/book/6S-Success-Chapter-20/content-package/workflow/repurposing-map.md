# Chapter 20 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- By every measure the first three S's gave you the home is finished, and it is good, and it could still send someone to the hospital tonight, not because you did anything wrong but because nothing you have done so far was ever aimed at that
- A clean home is not a safe home: Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean, none asked whether it could hurt someone, so tidy and safe are different questions and a hazard does not announce itself the way a mess does
- Why safety is its own S, the light 5S+1 history: the method began with five S's, and safety was pulled out and named its own S because something this important should never be left to chance ("safety runs through everything" has a failure mode, what belongs to everyone can end up owned by no one)
- "First" is priority, not sequence: you did Safety fourth and that order is fine, but protecting people sits above the list, so when a safety fix, a tidiness improvement, and an efficiency tweak all want your Saturday, safety wins every time
- The hero is the safety lens: change the question, and ask of the whole home not whether it is tidy but whether it could hurt someone; this is developing safety consciousness, teaching your eye a new question until it becomes automatic, and what you are building is not fear but a habit of noticing
- The triage is protect people first: rank what you find by two plain measures, how badly it could hurt someone and how soon or how likely it is to, and fix the worst and soonest first
- The pyramid is why you act now, Heinrich home-scaled: a large pile of small hazards produces mostly near misses and, every so often, one serious injury, and you can rarely tell which one, so remove them while they are still near misses
- Safety is love, not fear: handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop carrying them around as worry; safety done well is quiet, it is what lets a parent breathe out
- One first pass, kept small: the smoke alarm gets tested and a fresh battery, the cleaning bottles go up high or behind a latch, the rug at the top of the stairs gets grip tape or goes, even the launch pad gets the new question; not a tidier home, a safer one
- The friction meter takes its FIRST Safety step off the Shine-complete milestone, small but honest, because removing the chance of harm is the deepest kind of friction gone, with the goal still ahead

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the first chapter of Part Six and it OPENS Safety, the fourth S, the "plus one" added to 5S; it turns from the state of the room (Sort, Straighten, Shine) to the people in it. Do not re-teach cleaning or inspection (Chapters 16 to 18) or the reset (Chapter 19); safety is a different lens on the same home. The chapter USES the finished home the first three S's built (a home cleared, arranged, and kept clean and read) but does not re-open any of them.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 21, Find and Remove Hazards): Chapter 20 opens Safety with the lens, the triage, and ONE first high-value pass on the worst and most obvious dangers. The systematic room-by-room hazard HUNT, the full inventory, and the complete safety-questions checklist are Chapter 21. Do not build the thorough hunt here; hold it as a teaser and handoff only.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 22, Improve Overall Safety): the ongoing, systemic layer, lasting safety consciousness, emergency preparation, and the home versions of safety training, goals, and review are Chapter 22. Do not build ongoing safety systems or emergency prep here.
- CRITICAL fence (Standardize and Sustain, later S's): do NOT write documented standards (Standardize) or long-term adherence systems (Sustain). Chapter 20 only begins the fourth S.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space; the fix is a new question and a short triage, not guilt or fear. Keep it calm, loving attention and relief, not lecture.
- Safety is love, not fear: no alarmist statistics, no graphic injury, no fear-mongering. Heinrich's Principle is the calm reason to act now while hazards are still near misses, not a threat, and its ratios are not overclaimed. No hype words and no force or violence metaphors; nothing is attacked, blitzed, blasted, waged war on, or purged; a few real dangers are simply handled. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements (say "a cabinet latch", not a brand); teach the lens and the triage, not a shopping list.
- The 6S Safety Step, the 5S+1 history, the hazard definition, and Heinrich's Principle are drawn from the source, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed.
- The friction-meter movement is specific to Ch 20: it takes its FIRST Safety step off the Shine-complete milestone, small but honest, because nothing was cleaned or tidied but the room's exposure to harm dropped, and removing the chance of harm is the deepest kind of friction gone. Be honest that this is a real but small STEP (not a new milestone, not arriving); three of six S's are done, Safety has only begun, and the goal is still ahead on the green calm side, because Standardize and Sustain still follow. A home that is clean is not the same as a home that is clean and safe.
- Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical; point syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium) back to it.
- Keep the framing plain: "safety", "hazard", "the lens", "the question", "protect people first", "worst and soonest first". Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone.
