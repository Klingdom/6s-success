# Safety: Protect People First

**Part Six, Safety**
Chapter 20 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

Subtitle · A home can be cleared, arranged, and spotless and still hold every danger it started with, because tidy and safe are different questions. The fourth S turns from the state of the room to the people in it: one new question, could this hurt someone, and you fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, first. This opens the fourth S.

## Chapter descriptor

The first chapter of Part Six, and Safety opens. Sort cleared the home, Straighten arranged it, and Shine kept it clean and read (Chapters 16 to 19), and by every measure the book has given you it is finished, and good. It could still hold every danger it started with, because none of the first three S's ever asked whether a thing could hurt someone. Today you turn from the state of the room to the people in it. You meet the safety lens, one new question asked of the whole home, could this hurt someone, which the source calls developing safety consciousness, and the hazard definition it goes looking for. Because you cannot fix everything at once, you protect people first, ranking hazards by how badly and how soon they could hurt someone and fixing the worst and soonest first, with Heinrich's pyramid as the calm reason to act now, while the dangers are still only near misses. Nothing new is taught about tidying; Safety is a different lens on the same home, and it asks the least tidying of any of the S's.

## How to read this chapter

This is the first chapter of Part Six, Safety, and it picks up exactly where the last one left off, with a home that is cleared, arranged, and kept clean and read on fifteen minutes a day, finished by every measure so far, and still, in a few quiet places, dangerous. It is not about cleaning again and it opens no new tool beyond the lens and the triage. It is about one turn: looking at your finished home through the one question the first three S's do not carry, and handling the worst of what it shows you first.

Read it through once for the idea, then take the lens for a slow walk through the home you already have. The idea is small and rests on one swapped question. For three parts you looked at your home asking, in various forms, is this in order; now you ask the same home something else entirely, could this hurt someone, and a question decides what you are able to see. Paired with it is the triage, protect people first, because ask the safety question honestly and you will find more than you expected, so you rank what you find by two plain measures, how badly it could hurt someone and how soon or how likely it is to, and you fix the worst and soonest first. The pyramid, Heinrich home-scaled, is why you act on the top of that list now rather than someday: a large pile of small hazards gives mostly near misses and, rarely, one serious injury, and you cannot tell in advance which is which, so you remove them while they are still harmless. And the register throughout is love, not fear. You handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop carrying them around as worry.

Keep the first pass small, because protecting people first means the first pass is the worst few things handled now, not a thorough hunt. Test the smoke alarm and put a fresh battery in before anything else; put the cleaning bottles up high or behind a latch; put grip tape under the rug at the top of the stairs, or take the rug up; and give even the launch pad the new question. Nothing here re-teaches cleaning or inspection; that was Chapters 16 to 19, and Safety is a different lens on the same home. Nothing here builds the systematic, room-by-room hazard hunt or the full safety-questions checklist; that is Chapter 21, Find and Remove Hazards. Nothing here builds ongoing safety systems or emergency preparation; that is Chapter 22, Improve Overall Safety. Nothing here writes documented standards, which is Standardize, or solves long-term adherence, which is Sustain. Because a part opener that removes real dangers, the friction meter takes its first Safety step off the marked Shine-complete milestone toward the goal, small but honest, because nothing was cleaned or tidied and yet the room's exposure to harm dropped, with the goal still ahead because Safety has only begun and Standardize and Sustain still follow.

If you only do one thing: walk one room, find the single thing most likely to seriously hurt someone, the unlatched medicine, the rug that slides at the top of the stairs, the smoke alarm with a dead battery, and fix that one thing today. You just protected people first. That small proof is the whole chapter, and it is what opens Safety.
