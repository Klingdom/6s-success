# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 20

**"Safety: Protect People First"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Six · Safety*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_20_final.html`: the
finished, cared-for home from the end of Shine drawn genuinely lovely with five quiet red hazard marks
tidiness never caught; the identical living room drawn twice, headed "Is it tidy?" under a soft green
wash and "Could this hurt someone?" with the same unmoved objects now carrying five red hazard marks;
a two-axis triage plot, how badly up and how soon or likely across, with the worst-and-soonest corner
ringed "fix first" and a small Heinrich pyramid inset; and the friction meter, which takes its FIRST
small Safety step this chapter, off Chapter 19's marked SHINE COMPLETE milestone toward calm, with the
goal still ahead.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`. Incidental fills used in the figures: green wash
`#EDF1E4`, gauge track `#EDE3CE`, cream `#FCF8F0`, quiet fill `#F3EEE2`, wood-warm `#EFE7D6`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the FIRST chapter of Part Six, and it OPENS Safety, the fourth S, the
"plus one" added to the original five. It does one turn: it moves the book from the state of the room
to the people in it. It teaches the tidy-is-not-safe reframe (a clean home is not a safe home), why
safety is its own S (the 5S+1 history), the safety lens (the single swapped question, could this hurt
someone, and the habit it builds, developing safety consciousness), protect-people-first triage (rank
by how badly and how soon or likely, worst and soonest first), Heinrich's Principle home-scaled (a pile
of small hazards, mostly near misses, rarely one serious injury, so remove them while still near
misses), a first small safety pass, and the emotional core (safety is love, not fear). It USES the
finished home from Shine (Chapters 12 to 19) and does NOT re-teach Sort, Straighten, or Shine. CRITICAL
fence: this is the OPENING pass, the worst few dangers handled today, NOT the thorough room-by-room
hazard hunt (Chapter 21 · Find and Remove Hazards, teaser and handoff only), NOT the work of making
safety lasting (the chapter after that), and NOT the documented standard-work of Standardize or the
long-term adherence of Sustain (later S's, named only as still to come). It opens no safety tool beyond
the lens and the triage. Because a few real dangers were removed, not because anything was cleaned or
tidied, the friction needle takes its FIRST small Safety step off the marked Shine-complete milestone,
honoring the book's rule that the meter moves only when the home really changes; the step is small and
honest and the goal is still ahead. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER
20 · SAFETY`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their
internal hyphen: worst-and-soonest, near-miss, home-scaled, two-axis, fix-first, first-pass, safety-
lens, launch-pad, knee-height, low-cupboard, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, Sort-complete,
line-and-flat-color, calm-dot, hazard-mark, breathe-out. Anti-shame throughout: a home holds hazards
because they are quiet and orderly and have become furniture to the eye, not because anyone was
negligent; the fix is a new question asked on purpose, not vigilance or blame. Safety is love, not
fear, so no diagram carries an alarm tone, an injury statistic, or graphic harm; the register is calm,
warm, and reassuring. No force or violence framing anywhere; no war on hazards, no blitz, no purge; the
whole move is one lens, one walk, the worst dangers handled first. No hype words. Avoid brand names and
specific product endorsements; teach the habit, not a shopping list.

## 1. The Finished Home, Quietly Dangerous (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole premise in one image. The home is clean, arranged, and cared for,
and every one of a handful of hazards was there the entire time, invisible to the first three S's
because none of them asked the safety question. A finished home, by every measure so far, is also, in
five quiet places, dangerous. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The cleared, arranged, freshly cleaned home from the end of Shine, drawn warm and
genuinely lovely, everything in its place and gleaming. Laid over that tidy scene, quiet and easy to
miss, are five small red hazard marks on things that tidiness never questioned: a rug curling up at the
very top of a staircase, a low cupboard standing open with a cleaning bottle inside and a toddler a step
away, a smoke alarm on the ceiling with its little indicator light gone dark, a heavy box perched near
the edge of a high shelf, and a cord run taut across a walkway. At a glance the room reads finished and
safe. Look again through the red marks and it is quietly full of danger.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm dawn wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint Newsreader
  italic line across the top, muted: `a finished home · lovely, and quietly dangerous`.
- A single ink floor line across the base, faint `#E2D8C4` side walls, the whole home read left to right
  as one continuous space, warm and lived-in rather than staged.
- Centered high on the wall, the smoke alarm in cream `#FCF8F0` with an ink outline, its little
  indicator light drawn dark grey `#6A625A` rather than lit, a muted Newsreader italic tag beneath, `a
  smoke alarm gone dark`, a small red hazard-mark just above it.
- Lower left, a low cupboard standing open in wood-warm `#EFE7D6` with an ink edge: a plain cleaning
  bottle inside at a small child's height and a toddler a step away, a small red hazard-mark above the
  open door, a muted italic line, `a low cupboard, a bottle in reach`.
- Left of the stairs, the top of a staircase with a rug in `#D9CDB8` curling up at the very top step, a
  small red hazard-mark above it, a muted italic line, `a rug at the top of the stairs`.
- Right, a high shelf with a heavy box perched near the edge, a small red hazard-mark above it, a muted
  italic line, `a heavy box near the edge`.
- Low across a walkway, a cord run taut in honey, a small red hazard-mark above it, a muted italic line,
  `a cord across the walk`.
- A small sans caps line low across the scene, terracotta: `TIDY IS NOT THE SAME AS SAFE`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "A finished home, by every measure so far. It is also, in five quiet
  places, dangerous. None of these were messy, misplaced, or dirty, so none of them showed up in Sort,
  Straighten, or Shine. Seeing them takes a different question, and asking it is the fourth S."

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is that the home is genuinely finished and lovely, so draw
it warm and cared for and truly tidy, then lay the five hazards over it quiet and easy to miss, each one
a thing tidiness never questions. Hold the spark-red discipline: the five small hazard-marks are the
only warm-red on the image, used to mark the quiet dangers, never to dramatize gore or a mess; nothing
here is dirty or misplaced. Draw no injury and no accident and no person at fault; the hazards are
patient, not happening, and the toddler near the cupboard is calm and safe, simply a step too close.
The register is a quiet second look at a good home, not a warning poster. Warm line-and-flat-color
style, the register of a gentle noticing.

## 2. The Two Lenses (the hero, one room two questions)

**Purpose:** Show that safety is a matter of the question you ask, not the state of the room. The same
tidy room hides a different, dangerous set of facts the moment you look at it through the safety lens.
Nothing moves between the two panels; only the question changes, and with it, what you can see. This is
the chapter's hero image. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The identical living room drawn twice, side by side. On the left, headed "Is it
tidy?", the room reads calm and finished under a soft green wash, everything apparently fine. On the
right, the same room headed "Could this hurt someone?", the same objects are unchanged but five of them
now carry small red hazard marks: the rug at the stair top, a low cupboard with a bottle in it, the dark
smoke alarm on the ceiling, a cord across the floor, and a pan handle turned outward over the stove.
Nothing in the room moved between the two panels; only the question changed.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `ONE ROOM ·
  TWO QUESTIONS · NOTHING MOVES BUT WHAT YOU SEE`.
- Left panel, a soft green wash `#EDF1E4` with a muted green outline, headed in Fraunces `Is it tidy?`
  in green `#5F7A4F`, a Newsreader italic subhead `the room passes` in green. Inside, the room drawn
  calm and finished: the smoke alarm on the ceiling with a small green indicator dot, a low cupboard, a
  stove with a pan, a rug at the stair top, a cord along the floor, all reading fine. A faint green calm
  wash over the whole panel. Footer italic, green: `reads calm · everything in its place`.
- Right panel, panel fill `#FBF7EF` with a `#E2D8C4` outline, headed in Fraunces `Could this hurt
  someone?` in slate, a Newsreader italic subhead `the same room, five hazards` in terracotta. The
  identical room, same geometry, unmoved, but five small spark-red hazard marks now sit on the rug at
  the stair top, the low cupboard with its bottle, the dark smoke alarm (its indicator now drawn grey,
  not green), the cord across the floor, and the pan handle turned outward. Footer italic, terracotta:
  `five hazards, unmoved · now visible`.
- Draw the two rooms with byte-for-byte identical object geometry, so the eye reads at once that nothing
  moved; the only difference is the heading, the wash, and the five red marks.
- Caption (from the chapter): "One room, two questions. Nothing between the panels changed except what
  you are looking for. Asked whether it is tidy, the room passes. Asked whether it could hurt someone, it
  gives up five answers the first question could never see."

**Note for the illustrator:** the entire argument lives in the sameness, so draw the two rooms
identically, object for object, and change only the question, the wash, and the five marks; if a viewer
suspects the right room was tidied differently, the image has failed. Keep the left panel calm and green
and genuinely fine, not smug; keep the right panel the same room, not a messier one, with the five
spark-red marks the only warm-red on the image, each on a thing that was there all along. The dark smoke
alarm indicator is the one shared object that visibly differs, grey on the right where it reads green on
the left, because a dead alarm is the same object in either lens and only the safety question notices.
Warm line-and-flat-color style, slate headings, the register of a quiet reveal, not an alarm.

## 3. Protect People First (the triage plot and the Heinrich pyramid)

**Purpose:** Make the triage concrete and give the reader a way to rank real hazards, and show, via the
pyramid, why the top of the list is worth acting on now. Rank by two plain measures, how badly and how
soon, fix the worst-and-soonest corner today, and let the minor-and-unlikely wait; the pyramid is why
the top corner cannot wait. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A simple ranking diagram in two parts. The main part is a two-axis plot: the vertical
axis "how badly it could hurt someone" from minor at the bottom to serious at the top, the horizontal
axis "how soon or likely" from unlikely on the left to imminent on the right. Sample home hazards sit as
small dots: a dead smoke alarm and unlatched medicine in the top-right corner, ringed and labeled "fix
first, today"; a slightly loose bannister lower-left, labeled "later"; a cord across a walkway and a
wobbling shelf in the middle. A small inset on the right shows Heinrich's pyramid home-scaled: a wide
base of near misses, a thinner band of minor hurts, and a single cap of serious injury, with a note that
removing hazards at the base shrinks the whole shape.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `RANK BY
  HOW BADLY, AND BY HOW SOON`.
- Two slate axes `#3C5A6B` with small arrowheads. Vertical axis labeled in slate sans, rotated, `how
  badly it could hurt someone`, tagged `serious` at the top and `minor` at the base in Newsreader
  italic; horizontal axis labeled `how soon or likely`, tagged `unlikely` at the left and `imminent` at
  the right.
- A faint dashed terracotta diagonal from the lower-left toward the top-right corner, at low opacity, as
  a quiet priority hint that harm rises toward the worst-and-soonest corner.
- A fix-first ring: a terracotta ellipse at low fill-opacity around the top-right corner, a small sans
  caps `FIX FIRST · TODAY` above it in terracotta and a Newsreader italic line `the dead smoke alarm, the
  unlatched medicine`. Two spark-red dots sit inside the ring for those two hazards.
- Middle dots in slate: `wobbling shelf` and `cord across the walk`, each a slate dot with a small italic
  label. Lower-left, a green dot for `loose bannister` with a small sans caps `LATER` in green beneath.
- Pyramid inset: a dashed `#E2D8C4` bordered panel on the right, headed in small sans caps `HEINRICH,
  HOME-SCALED`. A three-band pyramid: a single spark-red cap labeled `serious injury` (label set outside,
  with a small leader line), a honey middle band labeled `minor hurts`, and a wide green base labeled
  `near misses`. Beneath, a Newsreader italic note in soft gray, two lines: `Remove the base, / and the
  whole shape shrinks.`
- Caption (from the chapter): "Rank by two things: how badly, and how soon. The worst-and-soonest corner
  gets fixed today; the minor-and-unlikely can wait. The pyramid is why the top corner cannot: the same
  pile of small hazards that mostly gives near misses is the pile that, once in a rare while, gives the
  serious one."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a picture of a principle, not a dataset, so keep it warm and
legible at a glance, calm slate axes and only a handful of dots. The two axes carry the whole method:
harm rising up the side, likelihood across the bottom, so the worst-and-soonest corner is unmistakably
the top-right. Hold the color discipline: one warm terracotta for the fix-first ring and its label, spark
red only on the two fix-first dots and the pyramid's single serious-injury cap, slate for the middle dots
and the axes, green for the loose-bannister dot and the pyramid base. The pyramid must read as home-
scaled and calm, a wide base of near misses tapering to a single rare cap, never as an injury statistic
or a scare; its whole message is that you shrink the shape by clearing the base while it is still just
near misses. Do not draw the loose bannister as neglect or the fix-first corner as a disaster; both are
honest, and triage simply means the worst first. Calm slate axes, warm accents, the register of a quiet
proof.

## 4. Safety Is Love, Not Fear (the worry set down, no HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's emotional core as a picture: safety done well is quiet, and every
hazard removed is a worry you get to set down and never pick up again. This diagram has no direct HTML
twin; it renders the "Safety Is Love, Not Fear" section as a calm before-and-after of the mind, and it
hands off to Chapter 21 without teaching it. It is the reassuring heart of the chapter, the beat that
turns a small knot of anxiety into a breath let out.

**What it shows:** Two calm facing panels of the same parent in the same finished home, read left to
right as before and after the worrying is done. Panel one, THE WORRY CARRIED: a parent stands in the
lovely home with a soft cloud of small grey worry-thoughts hovering above them, each thought a tiny
faded hazard, the bleach, the alarm, the stairs, drawn as quiet grey question-shapes, the register low
and tiring, the vigilance that does not scale. Panel two, THE WORRY SET DOWN: the same parent in the
same home, the grey cloud gone, the few real hazards now marked with small green calm-dots where they
were handled, the parent's shoulders eased, a single soft line reading as a breath let out. Beneath the
two panels, a quiet strip carries the emotional-core line and a small dim handoff to what comes next.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `HANDLE IT ONCE, WITH YOUR HANDS`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath
  carrying the section's spine: "You handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so that you can stop
  carrying them around as worry."
- Two warm cream `#FCF8F0` panels with ink outlines, left to right, each headed with a small slate tab.
  Panel `THE WORRY CARRIED`: the parent in the finished home with a soft grey cloud of small faded worry-
  thoughts above, a muted Newsreader italic note, `watching for everything, forever`. Panel `THE WORRY
  SET DOWN`: the same parent, the cloud gone, small green calm-dots on the few handled hazards, a muted
  italic note, `the dangers dealt with, not feared`.
- A single soft green breath-line between the panels or over the second, quiet, to read as a parent
  breathing out; no faces of fear, just ease.
- To the right, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `CHAPTER 21 · NEXT` in muted honey sans
  caps, a muted italic note beneath, `Safety gets thorough: the hazards a first pass always misses`, with
  two fainter panel outlines behind it, unlabeled, for the lasting-safety chapter and, further off,
  Standardize and Sustain. Hold the whole right group quiet and low-contrast, clearly ahead, not yet
  reached.
- Beneath the panels, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen One Idea to Keep: "Protect people
  first. Walk the home asking not whether it is tidy but whether it could hurt someone, fix what could
  hurt someone worst and soonest before anything else, and you trade a low hum of worry for the quiet of
  a home whose real dangers have been handled."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "That is the fourth S in a single pass: not a tidier home, a safer
  one."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the anti-shame, anti-fear heart of the chapter, so keep it warm
and reassuring, never a scare; the point is relief, a parent who can breathe out because the worrying is
done with the hands, once, instead of carried in the mind forever. Draw the grey worry-cloud faded and
quiet, small and tiring, not dramatic, and make the second panel visibly lighter, the cloud gone and the
calm-dots settled, so the eye reads the trade from a low hum of worry to quiet. Hold the color
discipline: grey for the carried worry, green calm-dots for the handled hazards and the breath let out,
slate for the tabs and the strip, and no spark red on this calm, reassuring beat. Keep the Chapter 21
panel dim, unlit, and clearly ahead; you may name it as next, but teach nothing of it, no hazard hunt,
no method, only that Safety gets thorough. The register is love rather than order: not vigilance, but
the ease that comes when the real dangers have been handled and can finally be set down.

## 5. The Needle Takes Its First Safety Step (the friction meter)

**Purpose:** Deliver the honest first-Safety-step beat and close the chapter. For the first time the
needle moves not because daily friction dropped but because the chance of harm did; a few real dangers
that were in this home are now gone. The live needle takes a small step off Chapter 19's marked SHINE
COMPLETE milestone toward calm, deliberately small because the earlier S's already removed most of what
this meter measures and what remains is protecting and locking in, in smaller increments. It must read
as a real, honest step and NOT as arriving; the goal is still ahead, because Safety has only begun.
Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML, and drives the live data-driven gauge.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle a small step past Chapter 19's
clearly marked "Shine complete" milestone tick, toward the calm side, notably close to the Shine-complete
position because the step is small on purpose. Chapter 19's Shine-complete position is now a faint dashed
ghost just behind the live needle; Chapter 18 and the earlier Shine steps of Chapters 16 and 17 are
fainter still; the Straighten-complete milestone tick and, further back, the Sort-complete milestone tick
sit as small passed markers. A terracotta marker labels the reached position SAFETY · STEP 1. The GOAL
crosshair, tagged purpose, still sits further out on the green calm side, ahead of the live needle,
because Safety has only begun and Standardize and Sustain still follow.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark (right), on a
  pale `#EDE3CE` track. The gauge is a live, data-driven SVG, consistent with prior chapters. Pivot at
  (210,198); the LIVE needle reaches the Safety-step-one position at about (69.5,154.5), a small step off
  the Shine-complete mark toward the goal; Chapter 19's Shine-complete position (71,154) is now the dashed
  ghost just behind; Chapter 18 (73,153.5) a fainter ghost behind that; Chapters 16 and 17 fainter still;
  the Straighten-complete tick near (82,161) and the Sort-complete tick near (133,110) as small passed
  markers further back; the GOAL crosshair near (67,155) on the green calm side, still AHEAD, with the gap

- The `SORT COMPLETE` milestone in slate at reduced opacity, a chip and leader furthest back as ground
  covered many chapters ago.
- The `STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE` milestone in slate, a passed marker further back, its chip and leader reading

- The `SHINE COMPLETE` milestone in slate, drawn clearly as the passed marker just behind the live
  needle, its chip and leader reading as the milestone this Safety step is measured from.
- Chapters 16 and 17 drawn as a fainter dashed ghost needle in soft gray, tagged `Ch 16 · 17`; Chapter 18

- Caption (frozen): "The needle takes its first Safety step, off the Shine-complete mark toward calm.

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a FIRST STEP, small and honest, and the smallness is the
whole point: the earlier S's already cleared most of what this meter measures, so what remains is
protecting and locking in, which moves the needle in smaller, quieter increments than the first sweep of
clearing and arranging did. Mark the reached position clearly as SAFETY · STEP 1 in terracotta, a step
off the Shine-complete milestone rather than a milestone of its own, and keep it visibly close to the
Shine-complete mark. The live needle must NOT reach the goal crosshair; leave clear room between the live
needle and the goal on the green calm side, room that the rest of Safety, then Standardize and Sustain,
will take. Draw Chapter 19's Shine-complete position as a dashed ghost just behind the live needle, with
Chapter 18, the earlier Shine steps, and the Straighten-complete and Sort-complete milestones reading as
passed ground further back. A real step and a long road still ahead are both true at once. No completion
glow, no confetti; the dial has most of Safety and two more S's to travel, and the step moved for the
best of reasons: not a tidier home, a safer one.
