## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 20, the first chapter of Part Six, Safety, and it opens the fourth of the six S's, the "plus one" American practice added to 5S. For nineteen chapters the book was about the state of the room; Safety is about the people in it, which is a different thing entirely and, when the two ever compete, the more important one. Chapter 19 completed Shine: the home is cleared by Sort, arranged by Straighten, and kept clean and read on a rhythm that runs almost on its own. This chapter turns from that finished home to the people who live in it, and it begins with the reframe the whole part rests on: a clean home is not a safe home. Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean; none of them asked whether it could hurt someone, so a home can be cleared, arranged, and spotless and still hold every danger it started with, and safety is the question the first three S's never ask. Tidy and safe are different questions, and where they part ways the gap can be dangerous precisely because it is invisible: a hazard does not announce itself the way a mess does, it sits there, tidy and patient, until the day it does not. A light 5S+1 history explains why safety was pulled out and named its own S: the method began with five S's, and as it spread beyond the factory floor people saw that not one of the five, on its own, guaranteed the people in the space were safe, because something this important should never be left to chance. That is why this S carries the word first in its title. It is not first in sequence; you do it fourth, and that order is fine. It is first in priority: when a safety fix, a tidiness improvement, and an efficiency tweak all want your Saturday, safety wins, every time, because protecting people is not one item on the list, it sits above the list. The hero is the safety lens, one swapped question asked of the whole home, could this hurt someone, which the professionals call developing safety consciousness, teaching your eye a new question until asking it becomes automatic; what you are building is not fear, it is a habit of noticing. A hazard is any thing or situation in a home that could hurt someone, the loose rug, the pills within reach, the dead smoke alarm, the wobbling shelf, and it does not have to look like a mess, because most hazards are perfectly tidy. Because you cannot fix everything at once, you protect people first: rank what you find by two plain measures, how badly it could hurt someone and how soon or how likely it is to, and fix the worst and soonest first, without apology. Heinrich's Principle is the calm reason to act now: a large pile of small hazards produces mostly near misses and, every so often, out of the same pile, one serious injury, and you can rarely tell in advance which one it is, so you remove them while they are still only near misses. And the whole S is love, not fear: you handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop carrying them around as worry, because every hazard you remove is a worry you get to set down and never pick up again. The chapter makes one first high-value pass, keeps it small, and closes by reading the friction meter, which takes its first Safety step off the Shine-complete milestone toward the goal. That step is honest in a new way: nothing was cleaned or tidied this time, but a few real dangers are simply gone, and removing the chance of harm is the deepest kind of friction there is. The step is deliberately small, because the earlier S's already cleared most of the daily friction the meter measures; what remains protects and locks in, in smaller increments. This chapter does not re-teach cleaning (Chapters 16 to 19), does not build the systematic room-by-room hazard hunt or the full safety-questions checklist (Chapter 21), and does not build ongoing safety systems or emergency preparation (Chapter 22); it is the first pass, the lens and the triage, not the documented Standardize system or the long-term Sustain work. It is a real, earned first step and only a beginning: Chapter 19's Shine-complete milestone is the passed marker just behind the live needle, and the goal is still ahead, because Safety has only begun and Standardize and Sustain still follow.
