## Key extracted assets
- **Title:** Safety: Protect People First
- **Part:** Part Six, Safety (the first chapter of Part 6; it OPENS Safety, the fourth S, the "plus one" added to 5S)
- **Hero device:** the safety lens (the one new question, could this hurt someone)
- **The rule (frozen):** Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone.
- **The triage (frozen):** Protect people first: fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, before anything else.
- **The hazard definition (frozen):** A hazard is any thing or situation in a home that could hurt someone: the loose rug, the pills within reach, the dead smoke alarm, the wobbling shelf. It does not have to look like a mess. Most hazards are perfectly tidy.
- **The tidy-is-not-safe reframe (frozen):** A clean home is not a safe home. Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean. None of them asked whether it could hurt someone, so a home can be cleared, arranged, and spotless and still hold every danger it started with. Safety is the question the first three S's never ask.
- **The 5S+1 history (frozen):** The method began with five S's. When it spread beyond the factory floor, people saw that not one of the five, on its own, guaranteed the people in the space were safe, so safety was added as its own S, the plus one in what was first called 5S+1.
- **Heinrich's pyramid (frozen):** A large pile of small hazards produces mostly near misses and minor hurts, and, every so often, out of the same pile, one serious injury. You remove the hazards while they are still only near misses, because the whole aim is to never find out which one it was.
- **Safety is love, not fear (frozen):** You handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so that you can stop carrying them around as worry. Safety done well is quiet: it is what lets a parent breathe out.
- **One Idea to Keep (frozen):** Protect people first. Walk the home asking not whether it is tidy but whether it could hurt someone, fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest before anything else, and you trade a low hum of worry for the quiet of a home whose real dangers have been handled.
