# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 20 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line or image hitting the same follower until it feels canned. This check flags where that can happen.

## High-frequency elements
| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone | Canonical, web, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics, slides | A follower on several platforms sees the hero rule repeatedly | Calendar staggers it; vary the phrasing around it per platform |
| A clean home is not a safe home (the tidy-is-not-safe reframe) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics | Medium to High | The gap the chapter opens; the most shareable turn. Use across formats, not stacked on one day. |
| Protect people first: fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, before anything else | Canonical, CTA, LinkedIn, X, quote card, graphics | Medium | The triage; pair with a different worked example (smoke alarm / medicine / rug) each time. |
| A hazard is any thing or situation in a home that could hurt someone... (definition) | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, quote card | Medium | The frozen definition box; long, so lead with a fresh hook before it. |
| Safety done well is quiet: it is what lets a parent breathe out | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card | Medium | The safety-is-love-not-fear line; vary the surrounding worry it sets down. |
| The friction-meter caption ("The needle takes its first Safety step...") | Canonical, graphics, HTML | Low to Medium | Long; it is the honest first-step beat, specific to this chapter. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording ("Read it free online", "Read the free chapter", "the online book") | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings; most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision
The calendar avoids two assets sharing a hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push one back a day so "Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone." does not land twice in one day.

## Cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same Safety content. Post only one to LinkedIn (use the copy-ready slides file; keep the outline as planning).
- The LinkedIn newsletter and the email newsletter share structure; do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- Web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 20)
- Chapter 20 is the FIRST chapter of Part Six and the OPEN-SAFETY chapter. Keep it distinct from Chapters 16 to 19 behind it: Sort cleared the home, Straighten arranged it, and Shine keeps it clean and read; Chapter 20 turns from the state of the room to the people in it with one new question. Assets must USE the finished home as the starting point but must NOT re-teach cleaning, inspection, or the reset. Grep confirms the assets open Safety with the lens and the triage and do not rebuild the earlier S's.
- Do not let this chapter slide FORWARD into the thorough hunt. Ch 20 assets must not build the systematic, room-by-room hazard HUNT, the full inventory, or the complete safety-questions checklist (that is Chapter 21). This is a CRITICAL fence and it is held: the thorough search is named in the package only as "the next chapter" and "not yet done," never built. The first pass is deliberately the worst few things handled today, not a thorough sweep.
- Do NOT drift into ongoing safety systems, lasting safety consciousness, or emergency preparation (Chapter 22), documented standards (Standardize), or long-term adherence (Sustain); these are noted lightly at most, not built.
- Do NOT re-open Sort, Straighten, or Shine.
- The friction-meter first-Safety-step is specific to this chapter (a real but small first step to (69.5,154.5), off Chapter 19's Shine-complete mark at (71,154), which is now the dashed ghost behind it, with a slate SHINE COMPLETE marker for the passed Chapter 19 position and a FIRST SAFETY STEP label for the new live position). For the first time the needle moved because the chance of harm dropped, not because daily friction did. Do not reuse Chapter 19's "SHINE COMPLETE milestone" framing or imply a new milestone was reached; it is a STEP, not arriving. Keep the goal honestly ahead (three of six S's done; Safety just begun, with the thorough hunt, Standardize, and Sustain still to come).

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter is a draft authored in the book voice, grounded in the 6S source ("Principles of 6S: Safety Step," the 5S+1 history, the hazard definition, and Heinrich's Principle), not supplied verbatim source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft; re-sync if the chapter is revised.
- Quote cards are verbatim: every card in `graphics/quote-card-copy.md` verified byte-identical to a quoted line in `canonical/chapter-quotes.md` (0 mismatches). Nothing borrowed from outside the book; no brand-name products are named, and the Safety Step / 5S+1 / Heinrich origin is referenced lightly, home-scaled, not overclaimed, with no alarmist statistics or graphic injury.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording (highest-value, lowest-effort fix; several posts close on "Read it free online").
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical.
4. Keep Chapter 20 from blurring into its neighbors: distinct from Chapters 16 to 19 behind it (the clean, arranged, kept home) and distinct from the thorough hazard hunt ahead of it (Ch 21) and the later S's (Standardize, Sustain). Chapter 20 is the OPEN-SAFETY chapter: one new question, a short triage, and the worst few dangers handled first.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
