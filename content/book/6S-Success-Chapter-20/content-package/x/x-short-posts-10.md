# X Short Posts: Safety: Protect People First

Chapter 20 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters, counting its number line, and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Chapter 20 of 6S Success: Home Edition opens Safety, the fourth S. You cleared the home, arranged it, keep it clean. By every measure so far it is finished, and it is good. And it could still hurt someone tonight. Safety asks the one question the others never did. Free online.

2/
"Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone."

That single swapped question is the whole tool, because a question decides what you are able to see. Ask is it tidy, and your eye slides over the dead smoke alarm. Read the free chapter.

(255 chars)

3/
"A cluttered counter is an annoyance. A cabinet a child can open is a catastrophe waiting for a quiet afternoon."

Tidy and safe are different questions, and where they part ways the gap is dangerous precisely because it is invisible. Read the free chapter.

(260 chars)

---

4/
"A hazard is any thing or situation in a home that could hurt someone: the loose rug, the pills within reach, the dead smoke alarm, the wobbling shelf. It does not have to look like a mess. Most hazards are perfectly tidy."

(226 chars)

---

5/
"Protecting people is not one item on the list competing with the others. It sits above the list."

First is priority, not sequence. When a safety fix, a tidy-up, and an efficiency tweak all want your Saturday, safety wins, every time.

(238 chars)

---

6/
Ask the safety question honestly of a whole home and you find more than you expected. The answer is not to look away; it is to put it in order. Rank by how badly it could hurt someone, and how soon. Fix the worst and soonest first. The rest can wait its turn.

(262 chars)

---

7/
"What you are building is not fear. It is a habit of noticing."

The professionals call it developing safety consciousness: teaching your eye a new question until asking it becomes automatic. Not vigilance, which exhausts. A habit.

(234 chars)

---

8/
Reframe, do not renovate. The first pass is not a thorough hunt; it is the worst few things, handled now. Test the smoke alarm. Latch the poisons. Grip-tape the rug at the top of the stairs. A few things move, purely because they could hurt someone. Read the free chapter.

(275 chars)

---

9/
Every hazard you remove is not just a harm prevented; it is a worry you get to set down and never pick up again. A safe home is not a fearful one. It is the one where you have already done the worrying, once, with your hands. Safety done well is what lets a parent breathe out.

(280 chars)

The friction meter takes its first Safety step, off the Shine-complete mark toward calm. Nothing was cleaned or tidied; a few real dangers were simply removed. The goal is still ahead, because Safety has only begun.
