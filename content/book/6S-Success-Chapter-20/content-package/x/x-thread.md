6/
The word first in its title is priority, not sequence. You do Safety fourth, and that order is fine. But when a safety fix, a tidiness improvement, and an efficiency tweak all want your Saturday, safety wins, every time. Protecting people sits above the list, not on it.

7/
The tool is one swapped question. For three parts you looked at your home asking, in various forms, is this in order. Now you ask the same home something else. A question decides what you are able to see: ask could this hurt someone, and the dead smoke alarm lights up.

8/
"Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone."

The professionals call this developing safety consciousness: teaching your eye a new question until asking it becomes automatic. What you are building is not fear. It is a habit of noticing.

(259 chars)

9/
"A hazard is any thing or situation in a home that could hurt someone: the loose rug, the pills within reach, the dead smoke alarm, the wobbling shelf. It does not have to look like a mess. Most hazards are perfectly tidy."

(226 chars)

10/
Ask the question honestly and you find more than you expected. The answer is not to look away; it is to put it in order.

"Protect people first: fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, before anything else."

(218 chars)

11/
Why act now? A large pile of small hazards produces mostly near misses, and, every so often, out of the same pile, one serious injury. You can rarely tell which is the dangerous one. So you remove them while they are still near misses. That is the whole aim.

(262 chars)

---

12/
None of this is about living afraid or wrapping your home in foam. It is the opposite. You handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop carrying them around as worry. Safety done well is quiet: it is what lets a parent breathe out.

(249 chars)

13/
Make a start today and keep it small. Test the smoke alarm; if it is silent, a fresh battery before anything else. The cleaning bottles at a child's height go up or behind a latch. The rug at the top of the stairs gets grip tape, or goes. The worst few things, handled now.
