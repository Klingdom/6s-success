# Chapter 20 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Safety: Protect People First

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, calm, anti-shame voice, reassurance and permission not alarm: a home full of unnoticed hazards is the normal case, because adults habituate to their own space, and the fix is a new question and a short triage, not guilt or fear. No graphic injury, no scary numbers.

---

1. Be honest with me here. Your home might be clean, arranged, and cared for, and still hold a danger none of that ever asked about: a rug that slides at the top of the stairs, a smoke alarm you cannot remember testing, medicine within a small reach. None of those look like a mess. What is the one thing in your home that is perfectly tidy and still not quite safe?

2. A clean home is not a safe home, because tidy and safe are different questions. Sort, Straighten, and Shine asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean. None of them asked whether it could hurt someone. When you swap the question and walk your home asking only "could this hurt someone?", what is the first thing that lights up?

3. Here is the whole idea in one line: protect people first, fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, before anything else. If you had to name the single thing in your home most likely to seriously hurt someone, and fix only that one thing this week, what would you fix first?

4. Safety is love, not fear. The goal is not to live watching for danger; it is to handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop worrying about them. Every hazard you remove is a worry you get to set down and never pick up again. What is one worry about your home you would most like to be able to set down for good?

5. A useful trick: get down to a child's height and look. Most of the worst home hazards, low cupboards, dangling cords, small swallowable things, sit in a band of space adults never look at because they never live there. Have you ever seen your own home from down there? What did you notice that you had walked past for years?
