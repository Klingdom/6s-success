# Chapter 20 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Safety: Protect People First

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, calm, anti-shame voice; safety is love, not fear; a note of quiet reassurance as this opens the fourth S, without hype or alarm; no force metaphors, no graphic injury, no scary statistics.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** A clean home is not a safe home.

**Body:** A home can be cleared, arranged, and spotless and still hold every danger it started with. Swipe for the one question that tells the difference, and open the fourth S.

---

## Slide 2: Tidy and safe are different questions

**Headline:** Sort, Straighten, and Shine never asked whether it could hurt someone.

**Body:** They asked whether a thing belonged, where it lived, and whether it was clean. Safety is the question the first three S's never ask.

---

## Slide 3: A hazard does not announce itself

**Headline:** A hazard is patient.

**Body:** It does not announce itself the way a mess does. It sits there, tidy and patient, until the day it does not. Most hazards are perfectly tidy.

---

## Slide 4: Why safety is its own S

**Headline:** Something this important should never be left to chance.

**Body:** The method began with five S's. People saw that none of them, on its own, guaranteed the people in the space were safe. So safety was pulled out and named: the plus one in 5S+1.

---

## Slide 5: First is priority, not sequence

**Headline:** You do Safety fourth. It is first in priority.

**Body:** When a safety fix, a tidiness improvement, and an efficiency tweak all want your Saturday, safety wins, every time. Protecting people sits above the list.

---

## Slide 6: The hero device

**Headline:** The safety lens: could this hurt someone?

**Body:** For three parts you asked, is this in order. Now you ask the same home something else entirely. A question decides what you are able to see.

---

## Slide 7: The rule

**Headline:** Ask not whether it is tidy, but whether it could hurt someone.

**Body:** The professionals call this developing safety consciousness: teaching your eye a new question until asking it becomes automatic. What you are building is not fear. It is a habit of noticing.

---

## Slide 8: Protect people first

**Headline:** You cannot fix everything at once. So you triage.

**Body:** Protect people first: fix what could hurt someone worst and soonest, before anything else. Rank by two plain measures, how badly and how soon.

---

## Slide 9: Why act now

**Headline:** Remove hazards while they are still only near misses.

**Body:** A large pile of small hazards gives mostly near misses and, rarely, one serious injury. You cannot tell which. So you act now, because the whole aim is to never find out which one it was.

---

## Slide 10: Safety is love, not fear

**Headline:** It is what lets a parent breathe out.

**Body:** None of this is about living afraid. You handle the few real dangers plainly, once, so you can stop carrying them around as worry. Every hazard removed is a worry you set down for good.

---

## Slide 11: A first pass, kept small

**Headline:** One lens, one walk, the worst dangers handled first.

**Body:** The dead smoke alarm gets a battery. The cleaning bottles go up or behind a latch. The rug at the stair top gets grip tape. Not a tidier home, a safer one.

---
