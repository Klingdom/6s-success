# Chapter 12 Calls to Action: Straighten: A Place for Everything

This is the first chapter of Part Four, and it opens Straighten, the second S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible design move: give one everyday thing a home within arm's reach and return it there for three days. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: you were never messy, your things simply had nowhere to go, and the fix is design, not willpower.

## Primary CTA
Give one thing a home. "Pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most, and give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it. Return it there for three days and watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Sort decided what stays; now every keeper gets one clear home. Chapter 12 opens Part Four with a single tool, the Home Test: a home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. Read it free online, then Chapter 13 places those homes by how often you reach for each thing."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most, and give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it. Return it there for three days. Watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone names the one thing they can never find. Together, give each of those things one obvious home, and agree out loud that it now only ever lives there. Losing it becomes almost impossible.
- **6S Tip:** A good home is closer than the floor. If a thing keeps landing on the counter, its home is too far away or too much work. Move the home, not your willpower.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small design move, not a burst of effort: give one thing a home and let the home do the work. Lead with "give one thing a home," which is concrete and provable in three days.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame. You were never messy; the fix is a better home, not more discipline. Never frame past mess as a character flaw.
- The reframe (you were never messy) and the Home Test are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is giving each keeper one clear home judged by an easy return.
- Scope note: this gives each keeper a home; it does not place homes by frequency of use (Chapter 13), add labels or visual cues (Chapter 14), or map the whole room (Chapter 15). The keepers are settled; Sort is not re-opened.
