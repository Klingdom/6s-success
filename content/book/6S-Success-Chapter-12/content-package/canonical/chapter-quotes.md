# Chapter 12 Quotes: Straighten: A Place for Everything

Pull quotes lifted directly from the manuscript and final HTML, ready for quote cards, social posts, and graphics. Every line below was grep-verified as present in chapter_12_manuscript.md and chapter_12_final.html. All are verbatim or lightly trimmed. Nothing here is invented.

## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "A place for everything, so everything can find its way back."

2. "A surface can be clear and still be one busy morning away from a mess."

3. "A room emptied of the wrong things will refill with the right things left loose, because a keeper with nowhere to go is just tomorrow's clutter arriving early."

4. "It is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching."

5. "When putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it will get set down where you stand, every time, and no amount of trying harder changes that arithmetic. You are not failing the room. The room is failing you."

6. "The problem was never your willpower. The problem was the design. And a design problem has a design fix, which is the good news, because you can change a home far more easily than you can change your character."

7. "So the standard is one easy motion."

8. "The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using."

9. "A home that takes effort is a home that fails."

10. "A home does the remembering for you."

## The core principle (frozen)

"A place for everything, so everything can find its way back."

## The Home Test (frozen, hero)

"A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you."

## The reframe (frozen, anti-shame pull-quote)

"You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second."

## Definition box (canonical)

"Straighten, the second S, gives every keeper one clear home, a single spot where it lives and returns to. A place for everything, and everything in its place, so the space stays easy without effort."

## One Idea to Keep (canonical close)

"Tidy is not a chore you repeat forever. It is a set of homes good enough that things find their own way back. Build the homes once, and the room helps keep itself."

## Quick Win (canonical)

"Pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most, and give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it. Return it there for three days. Watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"A good home is closer than the floor. If a thing keeps landing on the counter, its home is too far away or too much work. Move the home, not your willpower."

## Common Mistake (canonical)

"Giving something a home that is technically \"away\" but hard to reach, behind a door, under a lid, across the room. If putting it back takes three steps and a decision, it will not go back. A home that takes effort is a home that fails."

## Family Challenge (canonical)

"Everyone names the one thing they can never find. Together, give each of those things one obvious home, and agree out loud that it now only ever lives there. Losing it becomes almost impossible."

## The friction-meter caption (for graphics)

"The needle steps again, the first step of Straighten. Nothing left the house this time; instead, everything that stayed got a home, so the room grew easier to keep. Sort-complete sits just behind the needle now. The goal is still ahead, because Straighten has only begun and four more S's follow."
