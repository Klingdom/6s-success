## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 12 is the first chapter of Part Four, and it opens Straighten, the second of the six S's. Sort emptied the entryway of what did not belong, but the same sentence that closed the last part opens this one: the keepers are still exactly where the pass left them, sitting loose with no proper places of their own. Deciding what stays and deciding where it lives are two different acts, and only the first is done. So the work turns from removing to arranging. The chapter names its fence plainly: it does not re-open Sort, and it does not yet place homes by frequency, add labels, or map the room.

The principle is small enough to hold in a sentence: a place for everything, so everything can find its way back, because the point of a home is the return trip. Straighten asks almost nothing of you emotionally; it is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching. Things do not get put away because the home is too far, behind a door, under a lid, or never decided, not because the person is lazy. The one tool is the Home Test: a home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else, and takes no more than one easy motion.

The entryway keepers each get a home, keys to a tray or hook, leash to a hook, umbrella to a stand or hook, bag to a hook, mail to an outgoing slot. You were never messy; your things simply had nowhere to go. Nothing left the house, yet the friction meter takes its first Straighten step.
