# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 12

**"Straighten: A Place for Everything"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working
specs, not finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in
`chapter_12_final.html`: the cleared entryway console holding only its loose keepers each under
a faint question mark, the Home Test drawn as a green one-motion return beside a bad home that
fails, the keepers mapping each to one assigned home, and the friction meter stepped one notch
past the Sort-complete milestone into the first step of Straighten.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter opens Part Four, the second S, and gives each keeper that Sort left
one clear home, a single spot where it lives and returns to. It does not re-open Sort or
re-decide what stays; the keepers are settled. It does not place homes by how often a thing is
used (that is Chapter 13, Store by Frequency of Use), it does not add labels or visual cues
(Chapter 14), and it does not map a whole room's homes at once (Chapter 15). Today has one
narrow task: give each keeper a home judged only by an easy return. Separator discipline:
labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 12 · STRAIGHTEN`, never a floating hyphen or a
floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: one-motion, Sort-complete,
anti-shame. Anti-shame throughout: the mess was never a verdict on the reader; a thing that
never gets put away has a bad home or no home, not a lazy owner, and the fix is design, not
willpower. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no guilt cues, no force or pressure framing anywhere.

## 1. The Homeless Keepers (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's starting point in one image. The right things are here, the
console is clear, and yet not one keeper has a place of its own. The empty hook and empty tray
promise where the work is headed. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The cleared entryway console after Sort, holding only its five keepers
sitting loose where they landed, a small faint question mark floating above each, with an empty
hook on the wall and an empty tray on the surface waiting to be used.

**Rough layout:**
- Warm daylight wash, a low floor line, the surface clear but not yet settled.
- The console across the frame in soft wood fill `#E7C58B`, rounded corners, a small terracotta
  panel label `THE CONSOLE, AFTER SORT`.
- The five keepers resting loose, spaced unevenly as if each was set down where a hand happened
  to leave it: a set of keys, the dog's leash, a folding umbrella, a reusable shopping bag, and
  a small stack of tomorrow's mail. Each keeper carries a faint Fraunces italic question mark
  above it in muted `#C6BCAA`, the same question over every one.
- On the wall, an empty hook with a small honey sans note `an empty hook`. On the surface, an
  empty tray with a soft gray sans note `an empty tray`. Both are drawn open and waiting, not
  yet holding anything.
- A Newsreader italic line low in the frame, soft gray: "cleared by Sort, but not yet settled".
- Caption idea: "After Sort, the console holds only its keepers, but each one is sitting
  wherever it landed. The question above every item is the same: where does this live?"

**Note for the illustrator:** the keepers are the right things, already chosen and settled, so
draw them calmly and well kept, not as clutter or a mess. Nothing here is up for another vote.
The tension in the frame is quiet and gentle: a clear surface that is still one busy morning
away from scattering, because nothing has a place of its own yet. The empty hook and empty tray
are the promise, so make them read as ready and inviting, not as a scolding about what is
missing.

## 2. The Home Test (the hero device)

**Purpose:** Make the hero device legible at a glance: a home is only as good as the return
trip, and the test is whether putting the thing back is easier than putting it down where you
stand. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A left-versus-right contrast on one item, the keys. On the left, a good home:
the keys drop into a tray in a single green arc, one easy motion, so they go back every time. On
the right, a bad home: the same keys face a spot that is far, behind a door, and under a lid, a
long broken path marked with a friction-spark, so they never make it there.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE HOME TEST`.
- Left half, the good home: a Fraunces green header "A good home", Newsreader italic beneath
  "the return is one easy motion". A tray drawn open on the console, a smooth green arc from the
  hand to the tray, a green calm-dot and a small sans tag `one motion`, and a green sans caps
  line at the base `IT GOES BACK`.
- Right half, the bad home: a Fraunces spark-red header "A bad home", Newsreader italic beneath
  "far, behind a door, under a lid". The path drawn long and broken across the room, past a
  door and toward a lidded box, small spark tags `three steps` and `and a decision`, a
  friction-spark burst where the path breaks, and a spark sans caps line at the base
  `IT DOES NOT GO BACK`.
- A center divider rule so the two homes read as a fair side-by-side comparison of the same
  item.
- Beneath both, a Newsreader italic line in slate carrying the whole idea: "The only question a
  home has to pass: is putting it back easier than setting it down where you stand?"
- Caption idea: "A home is only as good as the return trip. Left, the keys drop into a tray in
  one easy motion. Right, the same keys face a home that fails, so they never go back."

**Note for the illustrator:** the contrast is the lesson, so keep it plain and non-accusatory.
The bad home is not the reader's fault and not a bad person's shelf; it is simply a spot placed
wrong, too far and too much work. The frozen test line does the teaching: a home works when
putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else, and if returning it takes
more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not the person. Keep it calm and non-coercive, no
guilt cues.

## 3. Giving the Keepers Homes (the worked example)

**Purpose:** Make the method trustworthy by watching it run on the real entryway keepers, each
matched to one assigned home that passes the Home Test, so the surface stays clear on its own.
Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The five keepers in a left column, each joined by a single green one-motion
arrow to its one assigned home in a right column, with a small side panel confirming that every
home passes the test.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `GIVING THE KEEPERS HOMES`.
- A left column of keepers, each a Newsreader item name, and a right column of homes, each a
  green calm-dot and a sans home label, joined one to one by a single green arrow tagged
  `one motion`:
  - "the keys" green arrow to `a tray or hook at the door`.
  - "the leash" green arrow to `a hook by the door`.
  - "the umbrella" green arrow to `a stand at the door`.
  - "the reusable bag" green arrow to `a hook beside the leash`.
  - "tomorrow's mail" green arrow to `an outgoing slot at the door`.
- A right-side panel `EVERY HOME PASSES THE TEST`, three green calm-dot lines: "one easy
  motion", "right where it is used", "no door, no lid, no drawer". Beneath it a Newsreader
  italic line in slate: "so the surface stays clear on its own".
- Caption idea: "Each keeper gets one home, and every home is a single easy motion from where
  the thing is used, so the console stays clear without a nightly tidy."

**Note for the illustrator:** each keeper links to exactly one home, a clean one-to-one match,
never a keeper with two homes or a shared spot. Every home lands within reach of the door
because that is where these particular things are used and let go, but do not label any home as
prime or rank them by how often a thing is used; that placement work is Chapter 13, not this
frame. No labels or color-coded cues on the homes either; a plain green calm-dot is enough. The
payoff is the calm right-side note: build the homes once and the room does the tidying. Keep it
warm and matter-of-fact.

## 4. Why Things Do Not Get Put Away (the reframe)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's central reframe as a picture: the difference between the thing
that always goes back and the thing that never does is the home, not the person. This diagram
has no direct HTML twin; it renders the "Why Things Do Not Get Put Away" section as a
paired-card figure.

**What it shows:** Two facing cards. The left is the fork that always gets put away, its home
near and open and obvious. The right is the thing that lives on the counter, its home too far
or too much work, or never decided at all.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `WHY THINGS DO NOT GET PUT AWAY`, with a Fraunces ink line
  beneath: "the difference is the home, not you".
- Left card, green header bar `ALWAYS GOES BACK`: a small fork glyph, sans helper "near, open,
  obvious, reachable in one motion". Newsreader italic beneath: "the home is the path of least
  resistance". A green calm-dot in the corner.
- Right card, slate header bar `NEVER GETS PUT AWAY`: a small counter glyph with a thing resting
  on it, sans helper "across the room, behind a door, under a lid, or never decided". Newsreader
  italic beneath: "so it lands wherever the hand lets go". A single small friction-spark at the
  snag, not a red wash.
- Between the two, a slate rule and the Fraunces line, ink: "The problem was never your
  willpower. The problem was the design."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "A design problem has a design fix, so move the home, not
  your willpower."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the anti-shame heart of the chapter, so keep the right
card kind, not a scene of failure. The counter thing is not evidence about the reader; it is
evidence about a home placed wrong. Draw the fork card as effortless and the counter card as
simply badly served by its spot, two designs rather than two kinds of person. No guilt cues, no
mess drama, no force framing. The lesson is gentle: you can change a home far more easily than
you can change your character.

## 5. The Friction Meter, First Step of Straighten (the milestone)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest first-Straighten-step move. Nothing left the
house this time, but giving the keepers homes cut the room's friction, so the live needle steps
one notch past the Sort-complete milestone toward calm, the first step of the second S, with the
goal still ahead. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle stepped a little further
toward calm than the Sort-complete tick, that tick now sitting just behind the needle as a
passed milestone, Chapter 11's position drawn as a faint dashed ghost, and the goal crosshair
still further ahead on the calm side.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark
  (right), on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- The `SORT COMPLETE` tick in slate, marked on the band with a solid slate chip, now sitting
  just behind the live needle as a milestone already passed.
- The live needle in ink, stepped one clear notch past the Sort-complete tick toward calm, its
  new resting spot flagged by a green marker `STRAIGHTEN BEGINS` with a small green dot, naming
  it the first step of the second S.
- Behind the live needle, a faint dashed gray needle at Chapter 11's Sort-complete position,
  tagged in small sans `Ch 11`, set to about 55 percent opacity so it clearly reads as the
  ground just covered, not the live reading.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still
  clearly ahead of the live needle, because Straighten has only begun and four more S's follow.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The needle steps again, into Straighten. The goal is
  still ahead."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is the FIRST step of a new S, not a hold and not an
arrival. The live needle must sit one clear notch past the marked Sort-complete tick, with that
tick now just behind it and Chapter 11's position drawn as the faint dashed ghost. The needle
must not reach the goal crosshair; the goal stays clearly ahead on the green side, because the
rest of Straighten (Chapters 13 to 15) and Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain all still
lie ahead. Do not draw a held needle with no move, and do not swing it all the way to calm. The
honest message is exact: nothing left the house, yet giving the keepers homes made the room
easier to keep, so the needle stepped once, plainly, and stopped well short of the end. No
confetti, no completion glow.
