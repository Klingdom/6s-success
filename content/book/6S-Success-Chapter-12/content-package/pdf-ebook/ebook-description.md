# 6S Success: Home Edition (Ebook Description)

Most decluttering advice ends at the decision and leaves you standing over a cleared surface that still will not stay clear. You worked out what to keep, you hauled out the rest, and three weeks later the room looks the way it did before you started. Nothing was wrong with your effort. Clearing and keeping-clear are separate jobs, and the second one was never done. Chapter 12 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is that quiet second half.

"Straighten: A Place for Everything" is the first chapter of Part Four, and it opens Straighten, the second of the six S's. Sort emptied the entryway of what did not belong, but the keepers are still sitting loose where they landed, with no places of their own. Deciding what stays and deciding where it lives are two different acts, and Sort only did the first. So the work turns from removing to arranging. The chapter names its fence plainly: it does not re-open Sort, and it does not yet place homes by frequency, add labels, or map the whole room. Today has one narrow task, give each keeper a home.

The principle fits in a sentence: "A place for everything, so everything can find its way back." The point of a home is the return trip. And Straighten is lighter than Sort was. It asks almost nothing of you emotionally. "It is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching." You are not judging your possessions or yourself, you are building a few small landing spots and matching each keeper to one.

Before handing out homes, the chapter answers the question most tidying advice skips: why do things not get put away in the first place? The usual answer is a character one, that you are lazy or disorganized or just messy, and it is both unkind and wrong. The thing that always goes back has a good home, near and open and reachable in a single motion. The thing that lives on the counter has a bad home or none at all. "When putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it will get set down where you stand, every time, and no amount of trying harder changes that arithmetic. You are not failing the room. The room is failing you." "The problem was never your willpower. The problem was the design. And a design problem has a design fix, which is the good news, because you can change a home far more easily than you can change your character."

That leaves one tool the whole chapter turns on, the Home Test: "A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you." The standard is one easy motion. The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it; the coat lands on the hook in the same swing that takes it off. "The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using." And a home that is technically away but hard to reach, behind a door, under a lid, across the room, fails: "A home that takes effort is a home that fails."

Then the chapter walks the entryway keepers into homes one at a time, each held up to the test. Keys to a tray or a hook at the door. Leash to a single hook, grabbed on the way out and hung on the way back. Umbrella to a stand or hook at the door, not the back of a hall closet, which is the one people get wrong most reliably. Reusable bag to a hook beside the leash. Tomorrow's mail to one small outgoing slot, a launching place rather than a resting one, so it rides out in your hand. Every home landed within reach of the door, none is clever, none needs a special product, and each was chosen for one reason only, that returning the thing there is a single easy motion.

The anti-shame message is said out loud: "You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second." This is a design fix, not a willpower fix. "A home does the remembering for you," and it keeps working while you are tired, distracted, or running late, which is most of the time.

Nothing leaves the house in this chapter, yet the friction meter takes its first Straighten step, past the Sort-complete milestone toward calm, because giving the keepers homes cuts how much thought the room costs you. Chapter 11's position becomes a faint dashed ghost, and the goal crosshair is still ahead, because Straighten has only begun and four more S's follow. You did not become tidier today. You built small homes that do the tidying that used to fall to you. Every keeper has a home now, but not every home is in the best spot yet, and that is where Chapter 13 goes next.
