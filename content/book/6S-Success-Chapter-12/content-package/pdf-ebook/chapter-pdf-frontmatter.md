## Chapter descriptor

The first chapter of Part Four, and the one that opens Straighten, the second S. Sort emptied the entryway, but the keepers are still sitting loose where they landed, with no places of their own. Today you give each one a home, a single spot where it lives and returns to, and judge each home by one plain test, that putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. The work turns from removing to arranging, and nothing has to leave the house.

## How to read this chapter

This is the first chapter of Part Four, Straighten, and it picks up exactly where Sort left off, with the surface cleared but the keepers sitting loose where they landed. It is not about deciding what stays; you made those calls in Part Three, and they are done. It is about giving each thing that stayed one clear home, so the surface holds without a nightly tidy.

Read it through once for the idea, then go to your own drop zone and take the keepers one at a time. For each, pick one home and hold it up to the Home Test: is putting the thing back there easier than putting it down anywhere else, in one easy motion? If yes, the home is good. If returning it takes three steps and a decision, the home is wrong, not you, so move the home closer or make it more open. Keys to a tray or hook at the door, leash to a hook, umbrella to a stand or hook and not the back of a closet, bag to a hook, tomorrow's mail to one small outgoing slot. Every home within reach of where the thing is used and let go.

Nothing here re-opens Sort; the keepers are settled and stay. Nothing here places homes by how often you reach for each thing, that is the next chapter, Chapter 13, and we do not touch it today. Nothing here adds labels or visual cues, or maps the whole room; those are Chapters 14 and 15. Today has one narrow task: give each keeper a home judged by an easy return.

If you only do one thing: pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most, and give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it. Return it there for three days, and watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision.
