## Straighten: A Place for Everything

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 12. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*

### Slide 1 (hook)

**The surface is clear. So why are the keys still on it?**

A cleared surface is not finished.

Swipe for the second S, and the one test that keeps a surface clear on its own.

### Slide 2

**Clear, and still one morning from a mess.**

Choosing to keep a thing is not the same as deciding where it lives.

So the keepers sit loose, a tidy-looking pile that scatters again by Thursday.

[VISUAL] The same console, softened, its five keepers still loose under faint question marks. A soft rule reads, verbatim: "A surface can be clear and still be one busy morning away from a mess."

---

### Slide 3

**Clearing and keeping-clear are separate jobs.**

A weekend clean-out only does the first. That is why so many of them quietly undo themselves.

The quiet second half is giving each keeper a home.

[VISUAL] A two-panel on paper ground: "REMOVING," the out box leaving, greyed and finished; "ARRANGING," the loose keepers reaching toward waiting spots. A slate rule between them: "two separate jobs, and only the first is done."

---

### Slide 4

**A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.**

The point of a home is the return trip.

A thing with a home has a default, so the answer to where it goes is already decided.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A single green calm-dot beside a small tag: "the point of a home is the return trip."

---

### Slide 5

**This asks almost nothing of you.**

Sort asked hard questions. Straighten is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching.

You are not judging your things; you are building a few small landing spots.

[VISUAL] The definition box, panel with a slate border: "Straighten, the second S, gives every keeper one clear home, a single spot where it lives and returns to." A single green calm-dot in the corner.

---

### Slide 6

**The difference is almost never you. It is the home.**

The fork has a good one, near and open and reachable in a single motion. The thing on the counter has a bad home or none at all.

When putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it gets set down where you stand, every time.

[VISUAL] A quiet contrast: the fork by an open drawer with a short green arc, "always goes back"; a lone object on a counter with a broken friction-spark path to a far cabinet, "never goes back." No scolding; the frame is plain.

---

### Slide 7

**You are not failing the room. The room is failing you.**

The problem was never your willpower. It was the design.

And a design problem has a design fix. You can change a home far more easily than you can change your character.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the line in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Beneath, the 6S Tip in a slate-bordered callout: "A good home is closer than the floor. Move the home, not your willpower." No sparks.

---

### Slide 8

**The Home Test.**

A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else.

If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.

[VISUAL] The hero device, a contrast: on the left, keys drop into a tray in one green arc, "IT GOES BACK"; on the right, the same keys face a home far, behind a door, under a lid, the path broken in friction spark, "IT DOES NOT GO BACK." A slate band beneath: "is putting it back easier than setting it down where you stand?"

---

### Slide 9

**The standard is one easy motion.**

The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it. The return is folded into a motion you were already making.

The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using.

[VISUAL] One clean green arc: a hand releasing keys into a tray in a single motion, tagged "one easy motion." Beneath, the Common Mistake in a friction-spark callout: "A home that takes effort is a home that fails."

---

### Slide 10

**Every home landed within reach of the door.**

Keys to a tray. Leash to a hook. Umbrella to a stand. Bag to a hook. Mail to an outgoing slot.

None is clever. None needs a special product. Each was chosen because returning the thing there is a single easy motion.

[VISUAL] The five keepers in a column, each mapped by a single green arrow to one assigned home. A slate panel lists what every home passes: "one easy motion · right where it is used · no door, no lid, no drawer." Footer: "so the surface stays clear on its own."

---

### Slide 11

**You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go.**

Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second.

The pile by the door was never evidence about you. It was evidence about the door.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the reframe set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A soft green glow, no sparks. This slide should feel like permission.
