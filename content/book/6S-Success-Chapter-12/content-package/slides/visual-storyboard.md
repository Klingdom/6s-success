# Visual Storyboard

## Straighten: A Place for Everything

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 12. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_12_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper #F7F2E9: slide and card backgrounds
- Panel #FBF7EF: card fill, definition and idea boxes
- Ink #2B2622: primary text, object outlines
- Soft #6A625A: captions, secondary labels, the empty tray label
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: the chapter kicker
- Honey #DDA63A: the empty hook waiting, the middle of the meter
- Slate #3C5A6B: chapter labels, the GOAL crosshair and PURPOSE flag, the Sort-complete milestone tick
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: a good home, the completed outcome, the calm side of the meter, "IT GOES BACK"
- Friction spark #CB4B36: a bad home, the friction side of the meter, "IT DOES NOT GO BACK"
- Ghost needle #8A8073: the faint dashed ghost of Chapter 11's position
- Rules and hairlines #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8: dividers, dashed lines
- Display font Fraunces: titles, pull quotes, definition and idea boxes, column headers
- Body font Newsreader: worked pass, captions, running text
- Label font Inter: uppercase, wide letter-spacing, for kickers, tags, axis labels

**The recurring motif.** The hero device of the chapter is The Home and The Home Test. Every keeper gets one designated home, a single spot where it lives and returns to, and what separates a good home from a bad one is the effort of the return. Green #6E8B5B marks a good home and the completed outcome, a return that is one easy motion; the friction spark #CB4B36 marks a bad home, one that is far, behind a door, or under a lid, so the thing never makes it back. Honey #DDA63A stays warm and neutral, carrying the empty hook that waits at the start and the middle of the meter. Slate #3C5A6B carries the chapter labels, the GOAL crosshair, and the Sort-complete milestone tick. Terracotta is spent only on the chapter kicker. The ghost needle #8A8073 is Chapter 11's position on the meter.

**Two fences to hold visually.** First, this is placing, not deciding: never redraw the Sort pass or reweigh a keeper, because Sort is finished and every keeper stays. Chapter 12 only gives each keeper one home. Second, this gives each keeper A home judged by an easy return; do not draw homes ranked by how often a thing is used (that is Chapter 13), do not add labels, outlines, or colour-coding to the homes (that is Chapter 14), and do not map homes across a whole room (that is Chapter 15). Keep the focus on the single easy return.

## Beat 1 · The keepers, home yet homeless

The chapter and both decks open the same way: the cleared console holding only its keepers, each sitting loose, each asking the same question.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm daylight. The cleared entryway console after Sort holds only its keepers, sitting loose where they landed: a set of keys, the dog's leash, a folding umbrella, a reusable bag, and a small stack of tomorrow's mail. A small, faint question mark floats above each thing. On the wall beside the console an empty honey #DDA63A hook waits, and on the surface an empty tray sits ready, both quietly asking to be used.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, ink #2B2622 outlines, honey #DDA63A on the empty hook, soft #6A625A on the empty tray label and the faint question marks. The surface clear but not yet settled, because nothing here has a place of its own.
- Motif: the right things are here; none of them has a home yet. The empty hook and empty tray promise where the work is headed.
- Caption line, from the book: "After Sort, the console holds only its keepers, but each one is sitting wherever it landed. The question above every item is the same: where does this live?"

## Beat 2 · Clear, and still one morning from a mess

The in-between state a cleared surface lands in, given a quiet framing.

- Composition: the same console, softened, its five keepers still loose, each under a faint ink #2B2622 question mark. A thin soft #6A625A rule carries the line: "A surface can be clear and still be one busy morning away from a mess."
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, no verdict colours yet; the empty honey #DDA63A hook and the empty tray wait at the edges of the frame.
- Point: choosing to keep a thing is not the same as deciding where it lives, so the keepers sit loose, a tidy-looking pile that will scatter again by Thursday. Sort was about removing; Straighten is about arranging. Nothing has to leave today.

## Beat 3 · From removing to arranging

The turn the whole part of the book makes, drawn as two separate jobs.

- Composition: a two-panel on paper ground. Left panel, labelled in Inter "REMOVING," shows the out box leaving through the door, greyed and finished. Right panel, labelled "ARRANGING," shows the five loose keepers with soft arrows reaching toward waiting spots.
- A slate #3C5A6B rule between the panels: "two separate jobs, and only the first is done." Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "a keeper with nowhere to go is just tomorrow's clutter arriving early."
- Palette: greyed ink on the finished left panel, live ink on the right, slate on the rule. No green or spark yet; those arrive with the Home Test.
- Point: clearing and keeping-clear are separate jobs, which is why so many clean-outs quietly undo themselves. Name the fence: not reopening Sort, not yet choosing the best spot, adding a label, or planning the room. Today gives each keeper a home.

## Beat 5 · The principle

The line the chapter turns on, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "A place for everything, so everything can find its way back."
- Beneath it, a single green calm-dot #6E8B5B and a small Inter tag: "the point of a home is the return trip."
- Palette: green marks the return; otherwise slate does the work. No sparks.
- Point: the point of a home is the return trip. A thing that has a home has a default, so the moment you are done with it the answer to where it goes is already decided.

## Beat 6 · Why things do not get put away

The quiet contrast at the center of the chapter's reframe.

- Composition: a two-side contrast on paper ground. Left, the fork by an open drawer with a short green #6E8B5B arc, tagged in Inter "always goes back." Right, a lone object marooned on a counter with a broken friction spark #CB4B36 path to a far cabinet, tagged "never goes back." Between them a slate #3C5A6B rule: "the difference is the home, not the person."
- Palette: green on the good home, friction spark on the bad path, slate on the rule. Keep the frame plain, never scolding.
- The frozen reframe may sit beneath, in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "The problem was never your willpower. The problem was the design. And a design problem has a design fix, which is the good news, because you can change a home far more easily than you can change your character."
- 6S Tip, on a slate-bordered callout with a slate #3C5A6B dot: "A good home is closer than the floor. If a thing keeps landing on the counter, its home is too far away or too much work. Move the home, not your willpower."
- Point: when putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it will get set down where you stand, every time. You are not failing the room; the room is failing you.

## Beat 7 · The Home Test

The chapter's hero device and the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Composition: the Home Test drawn as a contrast, a slate #3C5A6B divider down the middle. On the left, headed in Fraunces "A good home," a set of keys drops into a tray in a single green #6E8B5B arc marked "one motion," a green calm-dot and the words "IT GOES BACK." On the right, headed "A bad home," the same keys face a home that is far, behind a door, and under a lid; the path is long and broken in friction spark #CB4B36, marked "three steps and a decision," with a friction spark and the words "IT DOES NOT GO BACK."
- Beneath, a band on panel #FBF7EF carries the test question in Newsreader italic slate #3C5A6B: "The only question a home has to pass: is putting it back easier than setting it down where you stand?"
- The frozen Home Test carries the beat, and may sit as the pull-quote above the drawing, in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you."
- Palette: green on the good home and its one-motion arc, friction spark on the bad home's broken path, slate on the divider and the test band. Paper ground.
- Fence: the test is about the effort of the return, not neatness or cleverness. Do not draw the good home as prettier, only as closer and more open.

## Beat 8 · One easy motion

The standard the test sets, kept to a single clean return.

- Composition: one clean green #6E8B5B arc, a hand releasing keys into a tray in a single motion, tagged in Inter "one easy motion." Beneath, the Common Mistake in a friction-spark #CB4B36 bordered callout showing a small far cabinet behind a door and under a lid.
- Common Mistake, verbatim: "Giving something a home that is technically "away" but hard to reach, behind a door, under a lid, across the room. If putting it back takes three steps and a decision, it will not go back. A home that takes effort is a home that fails."
- Palette: green on the one-motion arc, friction spark on the callout border, paper ground. Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "the best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using."
- Point: the return is folded into a motion you were already making, which is exactly why it happens without fail.

## Beat 9 · Giving the keepers homes

The worked example, five keepers, kept short. Use it as a copyable template. Rebuild from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: the five keepers in a column on paper ground, each mapped by a single green #6E8B5B arrow to one assigned home. The keys to a tray or hook at the door. The leash to a hook by the door. The umbrella to a stand at the door. The reusable bag to a hook beside the leash. Tomorrow's mail to an outgoing slot at the door.
- A panel on the right, slate #3C5A6B rule, lists what every home passes: "one easy motion · right where it is used · no door, no lid, no drawer." Footer, Newsreader italic slate #3C5A6B: "so the surface stays clear on its own."
- Palette: green on every arrow and every home fill, slate on the panel rule and captions. Paper ground.
- Note: keep it to these five keepers only, and keep every home within reach of the door. None is clever, none needs a special product, and each is chosen for one reason only, that returning the thing there is a single easy motion. Do not rank the homes by how often each thing is used; that placement is Chapter 13.

## Beat 10 · You were never messy

The anti-shame center of the chapter, given the pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the reframe in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second."
- Beneath, two small callouts: the Quick Win with a green #6E8B5B dot and the Family Challenge with a honey #DDA63A dot. A soft green glow, no sparks.
- Palette: green marks the permission and the completed outcome; honey stays warm on the Family Challenge. No verdict colours; this beat should feel like permission, not scolding.
- Point: the pile by the door was never evidence about you. It was evidence about the door. This asks you to become a slightly better designer of your own small spaces. A home does the remembering for you.

The close of the chapter and the soft handoff, given the One Idea to Keep pull-quote treatment.

- Composition: the settled console on paper ground, warm and light. The keys are in their tray, the leash and bag hang on their hooks, the umbrella stands by the door, tomorrow's mail sits in its outgoing slot, each home marked with a small green calm-dot #6E8B5B.
- The frozen One Idea to Keep, in Fraunces medium #2B2622 on panel #FBF7EF with slate #3C5A6B rules: "Tidy is not a chore you repeat forever. It is a set of homes good enough that things find their own way back. Build the homes once, and the room helps keep itself."
