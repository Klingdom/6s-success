# Chapter 12 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Straighten: A Place for Everything

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, no force metaphors.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** The surface is clear. So why are the keys still on it?

**Body:** A cleared surface is not finished. Swipe for the second S, and the one test that keeps a surface clear on its own.

---

## Slide 2: The in-between state

**Headline:** Clear, and still one morning from a mess.

**Body:** Choosing to keep a thing is not the same as deciding where it lives. So the keepers sit loose, a tidy-looking pile that scatters again by Thursday.

---

## Slide 3: Two different jobs

**Headline:** Clearing and keeping-clear are separate jobs.

**Body:** A weekend clean-out only does the first. That is why so many of them quietly undo themselves. The quiet second half is giving each keeper a home.

---

## Slide 4: The principle

**Headline:** A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.

**Body:** The point of a home is the return trip. A thing with a home has a default, so the answer to where it goes is already decided.

---

## Slide 5: Lighter than Sort

**Headline:** This asks almost nothing of you.

**Body:** Sort asked hard questions. Straighten is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching. You are not judging your things; you are building a few small landing spots.

---

## Slide 6: Why things do not get put away

**Headline:** The difference is almost never you. It is the home.

**Body:** When putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it gets set down where you stand, every time.

---

## Slide 7: The reframe

**Headline:** You are not failing the room. The room is failing you.

**Body:** The problem was never your willpower. It was the design. And a design problem has a design fix.

---

## Slide 8: The Home Test

**Headline:** The Home Test.

**Body:** A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.

---

## Slide 9: One easy motion

**Headline:** The standard is one easy motion.

**Body:** The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it. The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using.

---

## Slide 10: Five keepers, five homes

**Headline:** Every home landed within reach of the door.

**Body:** Keys to a tray. Leash to a hook. Umbrella to a stand. Bag to a hook. Mail to an outgoing slot. None is clever. None needs a special product.

---

## Slide 11: You were never messy

**Headline:** You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go.

**Body:** Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second.

---

## Slide 12: Soft CTA

**Headline:** Build the homes once, and the room helps keep itself.
