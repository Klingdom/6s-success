## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
Sort is finished, but the keepers are sitting loose where they landed, with no homes of their own. Straighten, the second S, gives every keeper one clear home. A home is good when returning the thing is easier than putting it down anywhere else, the **Home Test**, one easy motion. The failure to put things away is a design problem, not a character flaw: you were never messy, your things simply had nowhere to go. The entryway keepers each get a home (keys to a tray or hook, leash to a hook, umbrella to a stand, bag to a hook, mail to an outgoing slot). Nothing leaves the house, yet the friction meter takes its first Straighten step past the Sort-complete milestone, because a home cuts the thought a room costs. Hands off to Chapter 13 (place the homes by frequency of use).

## Frozen strings (copy verbatim; do not paraphrase)
- Core principle: **A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.**
- The Home Test: **A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.**
- Anti-shame reframe: **You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second.**
- One Idea to Keep: **Tidy is not a chore you repeat forever. It is a set of homes good enough that things find their own way back. Build the homes once, and the room helps keep itself.**
