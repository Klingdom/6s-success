# Teleprompter Script: Straighten: A Place for Everything

**Chapter 12, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** straighten-a-place-for-everything
**Length:** about 700 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come stand with me at the entryway.

The console is clear.

And yet the keys are on it.

So is the leash, the umbrella, the bag, tomorrow's mail.

Every one earned its place during Sort.

Not one has a place on this table.

Choosing to keep a thing

is not the same as deciding where it lives.

So the keepers sit here loose,

wherever the hand set them down.

A surface can be clear

and still be one busy morning away from a mess.

Welcome to the second S.

Sort was about removing.

Straighten is about arranging.

We are done taking things out of the room.

From here on, the things that remain get somewhere to belong.

One clear spot each.

Nothing has to leave today.

Today, the keepers get homes.

Here is the principle it turns on.

A place for everything,

so everything can find its way back.

Notice it says find its way back.

Because the point of a home is the return trip.

A thing with a home has a default,

so the moment you are done with it,

where it goes is already decided.

Now, one question tidying advice skips.

Why do things not get put away?

The usual answer is a character one.

You are lazy, or disorganized, or messy.

That answer is unkind and wrong.

Think of the thing that always goes back.

The forks. The hook the good coat hangs on.

It goes back every time,

because its home is right there,

and using it is the path of least resistance.

Now the thing that lives on the counter.

The difference is almost never you.

It is the home.

When putting a thing away is more work

than setting it down where you stand,

it will get set down where you stand.

You are not failing the room.

The room is failing you.

The problem was never your willpower.

The problem was the design.

And a design problem has a design fix.

So here is the one tool.

The Home Test.

A home works when putting the thing back

is easier than putting it down anywhere else.

If returning it takes more than one easy motion,

the home is wrong, not you.

So the standard is one easy motion.

The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it.

The coat lands on the hook

in the same swing that takes it off.

The best homes are the ones

you never notice yourself using.

Now let us give the keepers homes.

Keys, to a tray or a hook at the door.

Leash, to a hook, grabbed and hung in one motion.

Umbrella, to a stand at the door.

Not the back of a closet.

A home you reach past three coats to use, fails.

Bag, to a hook beside the leash.

Tomorrow's mail, to a small outgoing slot,

so it rides out in your hand.

Every home landed within reach of the door.

None is clever.

None needs a special product.

Each was chosen for one reason.

Returning the thing there is a single easy motion.

Now let me say the thing plainly.

You were never messy.

Your things simply had nowhere to go.

Give them a home,

and putting them away

stops taking willpower and starts taking a second.

The pile by the door was never evidence about you.

It was evidence about the door.

Change the door, and the pile goes away.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Because it moves today.

But nothing left the house this time.

Not one thing was carried to the curb.

And still the needle steps again.

Past the Sort-complete tick, toward calm.

How, if nothing left?

Because friction is not only about how much is in a room.

It is about how much thought the room costs you.

And giving the keepers homes cuts that cost.

This is the first step of the second S.

But be honest about what the meter still shows.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

And it is still a fair distance off.

Straighten has only begun.

Four more S's follow.

So here is your job.

Pick the one thing you use every day.

Give it a single home within arm's reach.

Return it there for three days.

Watch how fast putting it back

stops being a decision.

Tidy is not a chore you repeat forever.

It is a set of homes good enough

that things find their own way back.

Build the homes once,

and the room helps keep itself.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go give one thing a home.
