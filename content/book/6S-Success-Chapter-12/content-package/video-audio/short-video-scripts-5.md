## SHORT 1: A place for everything
**Topic: the second S and its core principle. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Your surface is clear, and nothing on it has a home yet.

**Script:**
The console is clear, and yet the keys are on it. So is the leash, the umbrella, the bag, tomorrow's mail. Every one earned its place during Sort. Not one has a place on this table. Welcome to the second S, Straighten. Sort was about removing. Straighten is about arranging. And here is the principle it turns on: a place for everything, so everything can find its way back. Notice it says find its way back, because the point of a home is the return trip. A thing with a home has a default, so the moment you are done with it, where it goes is already decided. Nothing has to leave today. Today, the keepers get homes.

- 0s: "The surface is clear. Nothing on it has a home."
- 12s: "Sort removes. Straighten arranges."
- 24s: "A place for everything, so everything can find its way back."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The cleared entryway console holding only its keepers, each with a faint question mark. An empty hook and empty tray wait. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 2: You were never messy
**Topic: the anti-shame reframe. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The pile by the door was never evidence about you.

**Script:**
You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second. For years, mess in a home gets read as a verdict on the person living in it. That reading is cruel, and it is useless, because it points you at the one thing you cannot easily change, your character, and away from the one thing you can, the arrangement of your stuff. The pile by the door was never evidence about you. It was evidence about the door, which had no tray on it and no hook beside it. Change the door, and the pile goes away, without a single ounce of extra resolve.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "You were never messy."
- 12s: "Your things simply had nowhere to go."
- 26s: "The pile was evidence about the door, not you."
- 34s: "Change the door, and the pile goes away."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A bare door, then a small tray and a hook added beside it. Keys drop into the tray in one motion. The surface stays clear behind. Warm, unhurried.

## SHORT 3: The Home Test
**Topic: the hero device, the one-motion return. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A good home is not the neat one. It is the one you barely notice using.

**Script:**
Everything depends on knowing a good home from a bad one, and it comes down to one thing: the effort of the return. Here is the Home Test. A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you. The test is not whether a thing can be returned. It is whether returning it beats dropping it on the nearest surface. So the standard is one easy motion. The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it. The coat lands on the hook in the same swing that takes it off. The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "How do you know a good home?"
- 10s: "Is putting it back easier than putting it down?"
- 24s: "The standard: one easy motion."
- 34s: "The best homes are the ones you never notice using."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Split screen. Left, an item drops into a good home in one green, effortless motion. Right, the same item facing a bad home, far, behind a door, under a lid, that fails.

## SHORT 4: The umbrella people get wrong
**Topic: the common mistake, home that takes effort. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The closet feels right for an umbrella. That is exactly why it fails.

**Script:**
Here is the home people get wrong most reliably: the umbrella. The hall closet feels like the proper, grown-up place for it to go. But a home you have to open a door and reach past three coats to use is a home that fails the test, so the umbrella ends up leaning in the corner instead. Put it where you actually pass on your way out, a stand or a hook right at the door, and it will go back where it goes. The rule underneath is simple. Giving something a spot that is technically away but hard to reach does not work. If putting it back takes three steps and a decision, it will not go back. A home that takes effort is a home that fails.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "The umbrella people get wrong"
- 10s: "A home you reach past three coats to use, fails"
- 22s: "Put it where you pass on your way out"
- 30s: "A home that takes effort is a home that fails."

**[VISUAL] direction:** An umbrella shoved into a crowded closet, then leaning in a corner. Cut to a simple stand by the door where it drops in one motion. Warm light.

## SHORT 5: The needle steps again
**Topic: the worked entryway pass and the first Straighten step. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Nothing left the house, and the room still got easier to keep.

**Script:**
Watch the entryway keepers each get a home. Keys to a tray at the door. Leash to a hook, grabbed on the way out and hung on the way back. Umbrella to a stand at the door. Bag to a hook beside the leash. Tomorrow's mail to a small outgoing slot, so it rides out in your hand. Every home landed within reach of the door, chosen for one reason: returning the thing there is a single easy motion. Then look at the friction meter. Nothing left the house this time, yet the needle steps again, past the Sort-complete tick toward calm, because giving the keepers homes cut how much thought the room costs you. This is the first step of the second S. The goal is still ahead, because Straighten has only begun.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Give each keeper one home"
- 12s: "Keys, leash, umbrella, bag, mail, each at the door"
- 30s: "Nothing left the house. The needle still steps."
- 40s: "The first step of the second S"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Each keeper connected to one assigned home. Cut to the friction meter: the live needle stepped past a marked "Sort complete" tick, a faint dashed ghost just behind it, the GOAL crosshair still ahead on the green side.
