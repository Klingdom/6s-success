## SECTION 1: FROM REMOVING TO ARRANGING

[HOST]
Let me name the turn we are making, because it is the one most clean-outs miss.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Sort was about removing. Straighten is about arranging.]

[HOST, voice over]
Deciding what stays and deciding where it lives are two different acts, and Sort only did the first. You spend a hard weekend clearing a room, you stand back and admire it, and three weeks later it looks the way it did before. Nothing was wrong with your effort. Clearing and keeping-clear are just separate jobs, and the second one was never done.

[HOST, on camera]
Here is why. A room emptied of the wrong things will refill with the right things left loose, because a keeper with nowhere to go is just tomorrow's clutter arriving early. A surface can be clear and still be one busy morning away from a mess.

---

## SECTION 2: WHAT STRAIGHTEN IS

[HOST]
Welcome to the second S. Its old name is seiton, a place for everything, and everything in its place.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.]

[HOST, voice over]
That is not a slogan about neatness. A room does not stay orderly because someone keeps tidying it. It stays orderly when every thing in it has one known spot, so being in order is simply the resting state. Here is the principle the whole chapter turns on: A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.

[HOST, on camera]
Notice it says find its way back, because the point of a home is the return trip. And this work asks almost nothing of you emotionally. It is closer to carpentry than to soul-searching. You are not judging your possessions or yourself. You are building a few small landing spots and matching each keeper to one.

---

## SECTION 3: WHY THINGS DO NOT GET PUT AWAY

[HOST]
Before we hand out homes, one question most tidying advice skips. Why do things not get put away in the first place?

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The problem was never your willpower. It was the design.]

[HOST, voice over]
The usual answer is a character one: you are lazy, or disorganized, or just messy. That answer is unkind and wrong. Think of one thing that always gets put back without a thought, the forks, the hook the good coat hangs on. It goes back every time because its home is right there and using it is the path of least resistance.

[HOST, on camera]
Now the thing that never gets put away, the one that lives on the counter. The difference is almost never you. It is the home. When putting a thing away is more work than setting it down where you stand, it will get set down where you stand, every time, and no amount of trying harder changes that arithmetic. You are not failing the room. The room is failing you.

---

## SECTION 4: THE HOME TEST

[HOST]
So everything depends on knowing a good home from a bad one, and that comes down to one measurable thing: the effort of the return.

[VISUAL: One item returned to a good home in a single easy motion, green and effortless, beside the same item facing a bad home that is far, behind a door, under a lid.]

[HOST, voice over]
Here is the one tool this chapter turns on. The Home Test: A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The Home Test: is putting it back easier than putting it down?]

[HOST, on camera]
Read that twice. The test is not whether a thing can be returned. It is whether returning it beats dropping it on the nearest flat surface. So the standard is one easy motion. The keys drop into the tray as your hand passes over it. The coat lands on the hook in the same swing that takes it off your shoulders. The best homes are the ones you never notice yourself using.

---

## SECTION 5: GIVING THE KEEPERS HOMES

[HOST]
Let us make some homes, because the test is easier to trust once you have watched it work.

[B-ROLL: The five keepers each connected to one assigned home. Keys to a small tray at the door, leash to a hook, umbrella to a stand by the door, bag to a hook beside the leash, tomorrow's mail to a small outgoing slot.]

[HOST, voice over]
Keys go to a small tray or a hook right at the door, so the hand coming in drops them in the same motion it closes the door. The leash gets one hook, grabbed on the way out and hung on the way back. The umbrella wants a stand or hook at the door, not the back of a closet, because a home you have to reach past three coats to use is a home that fails. The reusable bag is a hook too, ideally beside the leash. And tomorrow's mail gets one small outgoing slot by the door, so it rides out in your hand when you leave.

[HOST, on camera]
Notice what happened. Every home landed within reach of the door. None is clever. None needs a special product. Each was chosen for one reason only, that returning the thing there is a single easy motion. And a home that takes effort is a home that fails.

---

## SECTION 6: YOU WERE NEVER MESSY

[HOST]
Now the thing this whole chapter has been circling.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go.]

[HOST, voice over]
You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second. The pile by the door was never evidence about you. It was evidence about the door, which had no tray on it and no hook beside it.

[HOST, on camera]
Change the door and the pile goes away, without a single ounce of extra resolve. This does not ask you to become a more disciplined person. It asks you to become a slightly better designer of your own small spaces. A home does the remembering for you. Build it once, and it keeps working while you are tired, distracted, or running late.

## SECTION 7: THE NEEDLE STEPS AGAIN

[VISUAL: The friction meter. The live needle has stepped past a marked tick labeled "Sort complete" and a little further toward the calm side. Chapter 11's position is a faint dashed ghost just behind it. The GOAL crosshair still sits further out on the green calm side.]

[HOST, voice over]
Go look at the friction meter, because it moves today. But nothing left the house this time. Not one thing was carried to the curb. And still the needle steps again, past the Sort-complete tick. How, if nothing left? Because friction is not only about how much is in a room. It is about how much thought the room costs you, and giving the keepers homes cuts that cost sharply.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: The first step of the second S.]

[HOST, on camera]
And be just as honest about what the meter still shows. The goal is still ahead, out on the green calm side, and it is still a fair distance off. Straighten has only begun. We gave the keepers homes today, but we have not asked whether each home is in the best spot, or made any easier to keep at a glance, or mapped a whole room. Those are the next three chapters, and four more S's follow.
