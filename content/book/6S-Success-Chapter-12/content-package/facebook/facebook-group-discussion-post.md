# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the Home Test and the one-motion standard), then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, the tiny idea that finally made my entryway stay clear on its own, and then I want to hear about the one thing you can never keep put away.

For years I thought I was just messy. The keys landed on the counter, the leash ended up wherever, the umbrella leaned in a corner because the closet was a whole production to open. I moved it all a hundred times. It always came back.

Here is what actually changed it, and it had nothing to do with trying harder. I gave each thing one home, and I judged every home by a single question. A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.

That flipped the whole thing for me. The reason a thing lives on the counter is almost never you. It is that its home is too far, behind a door, under a lid, or never decided. The fork always goes back because its home is near and open and takes one motion. Make the other thing's home just as easy and it goes back too.

So the keys got a tray right at the door. The leash and the bag got hooks. The umbrella got a stand where I actually pass on the way out. None of it clever, all of it one easy motion. And the surface stayed clear without me thinking about it.

The line I keep coming back to: you were never messy, your things simply had nowhere to go.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the one thing in your house that never gets put away? And be honest about its current home. Is it too far, behind a door, under a lid, or has it just never been given a spot at all? What is one easy home you could give it this week?

I will go first in the comments with my keys, which spent years living everywhere except one place. 😄
