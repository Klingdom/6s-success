# Facebook Longform Post: The Keys That Finally Had a Place

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 12 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the chapter that opens the second S. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA to give one thing a home. No em dashes.

---

My entryway console was clear, and the keys were still on it. So was the leash, and the umbrella, and the bag, and tomorrow's mail. Every one of them had earned its place during Sort. Not one of them had a place on the table.

That is the odd state a cleared surface lands in. The right things are there, but each is just resting wherever my hand set it down, a tidy-looking pile that scatters again by Thursday. A surface can be clear and still be one busy morning away from a mess.

So I stopped removing and started arranging. That is the whole turn of the second S. Nothing had to leave. The keepers just needed homes.

Here is the only tool I used. A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you. That is it. Not how neat the spot looks, not how clever the container is. Only the effort of the return.

So I went one at a time. Keys to a small tray right at the door, dropped in the same motion that closes it. Leash to a single hook, grabbed on the way out and hung on the way back. Umbrella to a stand by the door, not the back of a closet I have to reach past three coats to open. The bag to a hook beside the leash. Tomorrow's mail to one small outgoing tray, so it rides out in my hand.

None of it was clever. Each spot was chosen for one reason, that returning the thing there is a single easy motion. And now the console stays clear on its own, without a nightly tidy.

Here is the part I want you to actually believe. You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go. Give them a home, and putting them away stops taking willpower and starts taking a second.

So here is your low-pressure homework for the comments. Pick the one thing you reach for most, give it a single home within arm's reach, and return it there for three days.
