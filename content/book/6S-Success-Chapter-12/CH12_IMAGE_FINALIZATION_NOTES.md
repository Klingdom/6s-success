## Fix at next regeneration (none blocking)

### 1. The Home Test wording is paraphrased, not frozen

`ch12-image4` prints:

> "A home is good when putting it back is easier than putting it down."
> "If it takes more than one natural motion, it will not get done."

The frozen strings are:

> "A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down **anywhere else**."
> "If returning it takes more than one **easy** motion, **the home is wrong**."

Meaning is preserved and the plate is entirely usable, but the chapter prose carries the frozen
version, so a reader meets both wordings within a few centimetres of each other. Worth aligning,
particularly the dropped "anywhere else", which is the phrase that makes the test bite.

### 2. Real book titles · `ch12-image0a` only

Two spines are legible on the lower shelf: **"THE KINFOLK HOME"** and **"LIVE BEAUTIFULLY"**.
This is the chapter opener, so it is the most prominent plate in the chapter. Recommend
retitling or blurring. No other plate in the set shows brands, products or logos.

### 3. Contractions on three kept plates

- `0a`: "It's the starting point for better living."
- `0b`: "Let go of what you don't need."
- `3`: "That's the path people will always choose."

House voice uses none. Low severity, whole-set fix.

### 4. Figure numbering is inconsistent

Only `0a` (12–01, with an **en dash**) and the held plates 10 to 15 (12-10 to 12-15, with
**hyphens**) carry numbers. There are no figures 12-02 through 12-09. If figure numbers are to
appear at all, they need reissuing across the chapter in one consistent style.

### 5. The frozen reframe appears nowhere

> "You were never messy. Your things simply had nowhere to go."

This is the chapter's anti-shame pull-quote and its emotional centre. No plate states it.
`ch12-image2` comes closest in spirit. Worth a dedicated plate.

### Two systemic problems in the held group

**Rival numbered systems.** Six separate five-item systems compete for authority: "The Entryway
Reset in 5 Steps", "The Five Principles of Home Design", the five-part Reset Checklist, "The
1-Minute Reset", "5 Habits to Maintain Your Entryway", and "5 Ways to Celebrate". In the chapter
that introduces the **second S**, only the Home Test should govern.

**Vocabulary collision.** `image9` is titled "The Five Principles of Home **Design**" and uses
"home" to mean *the dwelling*. The frozen definition is the opposite scale: a home is *the one
designated spot for one keeper*. `image8`, `image12` and `image15` make the same slip in body
copy. In the chapter that defines the term, this would undo it.

**Internal contradiction on visibility.** `image5` (kept) marks a high shelf and a closed drawer
as **bad** homes because they are out of sight. `image10` says "Use baskets, hide the mess",
`image13` recommends "hidden storage", and `image15` praises "functional storage, keeps items out
of sight". These cannot both be true, and the kept plate has the right of it.

### Redundancy

Beyond the fences, the held group repeats itself: `image7` and `image10` are the same five-part
Reset in two layouts; `image11`, `image12` and `image14` all deliver daily-maintenance content;
`image12` through `image15` reuse the same base photograph with different overlays, with `14` and
`15` near-identical down to the framed wall art.

**Reassignment rather than regeneration** may suit several of these. The frequency material in
`image9` and `image10` is close to Chapter 13's job, the labelled bins in `image8` are close to
Chapter 14's, and the habit plates would serve Part 8.

## What was recovered, and the reasoning

`ch12-image9` ("The Five Principles of Home Design") was originally held because two of its five
panels cross fences: panel 2 ("Frequently used items belong within easy reach") is Chapter 13, and
panel 4 ("Make it visible") is Chapter 14.

On a second look at the plate itself rather than the audit summary, the panel breakdown is:

| Panel | Content | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Everything Has a Home | "If it does not have a home, it creates clutter." | **Chapter 12** |
| 2 · Keep It Close | Frequency and easy reach | Chapter 13 |
| 3 · Make It Easy | "The easier it is to put away, the more it stays that way." | **Chapter 12** (this is the Home Test) |
| 4 · Make It Visible | Visual cues | Chapter 14 |
| 5 · Make It Beautiful | Aesthetics | Neither, a matter of taste |

Two of the five panels are Chapter 12's own content, and the frozen brief **explicitly permits
teasers** for Chapter 14 ("That is the next tool. Teaser only") and Chapter 15. So the plate is
usable as a **map of Part Four** rather than as a set of principles to apply today, provided the
caption says which panels are today's work and which are the chapters ahead. The caption now does
exactly that, and names the fifth panel as taste rather than method.

It was placed in **What Straighten Is**, which was the chapter's only section with no figure at
all, so the recovery fills a real gap rather than crowding an existing one.

**Not cropped.** Removing the two forward-looking panels was considered and rejected: they are not
adjacent, so no rectangular crop isolates panels 1 and 3, and cropping away the title band would
leave the only untitled plate in the book.

**Still worth fixing at regeneration:** the title "The Five Principles of Home **Design**" uses
"home" to mean the dwelling, where this chapter defines a home as the one designated spot for one
keeper. Having now viewed it in place, the collision is mild rather than confusing, because panel 1
uses the term correctly and "home design" reads as an ordinary category label. Rewording to
something like "Five Principles of a Well-Designed Space" would remove it entirely.
