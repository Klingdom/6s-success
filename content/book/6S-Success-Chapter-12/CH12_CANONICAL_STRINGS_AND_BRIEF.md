## Hero device (frozen): The Home + The Home Test
Every keeper gets one designated **home**, a single spot where it lives and returns to. What separates a good home from a bad one is the effort of putting the thing back.
> **The Home Test (frozen):** A home works when putting the thing back is easier than putting it down anywhere else. If returning it takes more than one easy motion, the home is wrong, not you.

## The core principle (frozen)
> A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.

## The reframe (anti-shame, frozen, set as a pull-quote)

## Worked example (the entryway keepers)
After Sort, the drop zone holds only its keepers: the keys tray, the dog's leash, the umbrella, a reusable bag, and tomorrow's mail, but they are sitting loose where they landed, each without a settled home. Give each one a home that passes the Home Test:
- **Keys** → a small tray or a hook right at the door, so they drop or hang in one motion on the way in.
- **Leash** → a single hook by the door, grabbed on the way out and hung on the way back.
- **Umbrella** → a stand or hook at the door, not the back of a closet.
- **Reusable bag** → a hook, so it is there when you leave and back in one motion when you return.
- **Tomorrow's mail** → one small outgoing slot or tray, so it rides out the door in your hand.
Each home is chosen so returning the thing is one easy motion. That is what makes the surface stay clear on its own, without a nightly tidy.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most, and give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it. Return it there for three days. Watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone names the one thing they can never find. Together, give each of those things one obvious home, and agree out loud that it now only ever lives there. Losing it becomes almost impossible.
- **6S Tip:** A good home is closer than the floor. If a thing keeps landing on the counter, its home is too far away or too much work. Move the home, not your willpower.
- **Common Mistake:** Giving something a home that is technically "away" but hard to reach, behind a door, under a lid, across the room. If putting it back takes three steps and a decision, it will not go back. A home that takes effort is a home that fails.
