## Hero device: The Home + The Home Test
Every keeper gets one designated home, a single spot where it lives and returns to. What separates a good home from a bad one is the effort of the return, and the Home Test measures exactly that: putting it back must be easier than putting it down anywhere else, in a single easy motion. A home that takes more than that fails, and the fault is in the home, not the person. This is the home-scaled version of *seiton*, the second S, "a place for everything, everything in its place." Reference lightly, do not overclaim.

## Motifs
- **A place for everything, so everything can find its way back.** The point of a home is the return trip; the answer to "where does this go" is decided in advance.
- **The return is one easy motion.** A good home wins the quiet contest against setting the thing down where you stand; a bad home loses it, every time.
- **Design, not discipline.** Things do not get put away because of a missing or bad home, not a flawed character. Change the home, not your willpower.
- **The needle steps without anything leaving.** Nothing left the house; the keepers got homes, the room's friction dropped, and the meter honestly reflects it with the first Straighten step.

## Emotional purpose
Relief and self-forgiveness. The mess by the door was never a verdict on the reader; it was a verdict on the door, which had nowhere for the keys to go. The chapter lifts the old story that clutter means a discipline problem and replaces it with a story the reader can act on: build a few small homes and the room does the remembering. The mood is lighter than Sort on purpose, because the work is carpentry rather than soul-searching, and nothing has to leave.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to give each already-decided keeper one home and to check it with the Home Test. Walk the entryway keepers, keys, leash, umbrella, bag, mail, each to a home within a single easy motion of where it is used and let go. Show why one-motion homes keep a surface clear on their own, without a nightly tidy. Do NOT re-decide what stays (Ch 9 to 11), place homes by frequency of use (Ch 13), add labels or visual controls (Ch 14), or map the whole room (Ch 15).

## Callouts (inventory; frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** pick the one thing you use every day and reach for most; give it a single home within arm's reach of where you use it; return it there for three days; watch how fast putting it back stops being a decision.
- **Family Challenge:** everyone names the one thing they can never find; together give each of those things one obvious home and agree out loud it now only ever lives there; losing it becomes almost impossible.
- **6S Tip:** a good home is closer than the floor; if a thing keeps landing on the counter, its home is too far away or too much work; move the home, not your willpower.
- **Common Mistake:** giving something a home that is technically "away" but hard to reach, behind a door, under a lid, across the room; if putting it back takes three steps and a decision, it will not go back; a home that takes effort is a home that fails.
- **Definition box:** "Straighten, the second S."
- **One Idea to Keep** (frozen box).
