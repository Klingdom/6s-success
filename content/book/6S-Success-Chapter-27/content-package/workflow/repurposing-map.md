# Chapter 27 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- You have the keeping mindset now, from Chapter 26, and here is the first concrete way to do the keeping: a regular check-in on the home that catches drift while it is still small. The obvious word for it, audit, makes almost everyone tense up, because it arrives carrying tax inspectors, surprise checks, someone official with a clipboard deciding whether you passed; any keeping-practice that feels like being put on trial gets quietly abandoned within a month, so the first and most important job is to take the word apart and hand it back meaning something completely different
- The inspection-versus-check-in reframe (frozen): an inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them, a check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early, it can be the same walk through the same rooms and feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift; the mechanical act can be identical, what changes everything is the orientation of the looking
- The hero, a health check for the house: a short, warm, regular look at the house, not a report card for the family; you already own an instrument, if you want one, in the simple snapshot from your baseline audit, but the instrument matters far less than the spirit, and the spirit lives in the four things; and the definition box (frozen): a 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up, what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small, it checks the home, not the people, and it is a health check, not a test
- The four things (frozen): what turns an inspection into a check-in, it looks at the home, not the people, it is light and quick, it is regular and expected, and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped; each defuses a different part of the dread, miss any one and the audit tips back toward inspection, hold all four and it becomes an easy, welcome habit of care
- The no-blame move, the first of the four and the one everything hangs on (frozen): the moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected, keep it about the shelf, a shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character, it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right; blame makes the household defensive and teaches people to hide the mess before audit day, so you train your family to fake a clean house on a schedule; a drifted shelf is a neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre, and you simply top it up
- Catch it early, keep it light (frozen): the whole value of a regular check-in is timing, a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix, the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend, you do not audit the home to judge it, you audit it so that it never again has to be rescued; look often and lightly (a five-minute check every week or two), keep it genuinely light because only the light version survives, reuse the simple snapshot from your baseline audit, and let each person check their own zone, because people are honest about their own drift when nobody is standing over them with a pen
- The rule (frozen, hero): an audit checks the home, not the household, what it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame; and the One Idea to Keep (frozen): keep the gains with a light, regular check-in that looks at the home and never at the people, a drift caught small is a small fix, a drift left to grow is an overhaul, audit the house kindly and often, and it will never again need rescuing
- The friction meter takes its FIRST Sustain step, a small MOVE along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from the held position toward the goal, the first movement since Standardize; this is NOT a milestone and honestly small, because an audit clears nothing on its own, it scrubs no surface and removes no clutter, but it makes the home a little more able to keep itself, because drift is now met while it is small instead of discovered when it is large; Chapter 26's held position is a faint ghost just behind the needle, the dashed path continues shorter now to the goal crosshair, the anti-backslide catch stays fully engaged, five of six S's are done, and the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- This is the second chapter of Part Eight and it CONTINUES Sustain, the sixth and final S, giving the first concrete way to keep the gains, the recurring, drift-catching check-in. It USES the keeping mindset Chapter 26 built (a home is kept and not finished, on small shared habits) without re-teaching it. Do not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, or Standardize (Chapters 16 to 25); a one-line callback is a reuse, not a re-teach.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 5, The 6S Home Audit): Chapter 5 introduced the audit as a ONE-TIME BASELINE snapshot (the 6S Snapshot radar, score each S 0 to 5 for a total out of 30, the worked example 14/30). Chapter 27 is the RECURRING, drift-catching audit as a Sustain habit, so reference Chapter 5 in ONE light line only (you can reuse its simple snapshot) and do NOT re-teach the radar, the 0 to 5 scoring, or the out-of-30 baseline.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 28, Family Problem Solving): the audit FINDS drift and problems and fixes the small ones on the spot; the METHOD for solving the bigger, recurring problems together as a household belongs to Chapter 28. Chapter 27 may NAME that the audit turns up bigger problems, but does NOT build the problem-solving method here.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 29, Daily 6S Times): the specific daily rhythm and defined times for 6S belong to Chapter 29. Chapter 27's audit is a periodic, lighter check-in (a weekly or so walk), NOT the daily-times ritual.
- CRITICAL fence (Chapter 30, Your Next 6S Event): the recurring reset event and the book's close belong to Chapter 30. Chapter 27's audit is the small regular check, not the event; the goal is reached only at the very end of the book.
- Anti-shame register throughout: drift is normal and expected, that is the whole reason for a regular check-in, and finding drift is a success of the audit, not a failure of the household; a shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character, it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, like a low tyre you simply top up. Keep it warm, relief and a load shared, never a lecture; the reader and their family are never judged and never scolded, and nobody is on trial.
- The audit is care, it does not police the household: the danger word is corporate and, above all, surveillance, so keep every asset human, warm, and light, a kindness a household does for its home, never a management dashboard, a performance review, or a parent inspecting children. Use inspection ONLY as the negated thing the audit is not. No force, control, or surveillance language (crack down, hold people accountable in the punitive sense, keep tabs on, no hiding), no hype words (unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, audit-proof). Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements; teach the reframe, the definition box, the four things, and the no-blame and catch-it-early moves, not a shopping list.
- The source's first Sustain step (Shitsuke, schedule 6S audits, develop a sense of responsibility and self-discipline, continuous improvement) is drawn from the 6S source, home-scaled into the friendly, drift-catching check-in and reframed so it feels like care rather than inspection, referenced lightly and accurately, tied to standard work and Toyota and the Toyota Production System, and not overclaimed. This chapter is the operational home of that first step; the remaining Sustain mechanisms map onto Chapters 28 to 30.
- The friction-meter reading is specific to Ch 27: it takes its FIRST Sustain step, a small MOVE along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from the held position toward the goal, the first movement since Standardize. This is NOT a milestone and honestly small: an audit does not clear a single thing on its own, it scrubs no surface and removes no clutter; what it does is subtler and, over years, larger, it makes the home a little more able to keep itself, because drift is now met while it is small instead of discovered when it is large.
