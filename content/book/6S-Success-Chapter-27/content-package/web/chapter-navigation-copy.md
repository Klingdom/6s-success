## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 27, the second chapter of Part Eight, Sustain, which continues the sixth and final S. Chapter 26 opened Sustain with the mindset: your home is now sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized, and held against sliding back, but held is not the same as kept, because the world is relentless and nothing stays good on its own. You do not finish 6S, you keep it, on small shared habits rather than willpower, until keeping becomes second nature. Chapter 27 gives the first concrete way to do that keeping: a regular check-in on the home. The trouble is the name. Audit is a word that makes almost everyone tense up, arriving with tax inspectors and clipboards and grades, and any keeping-practice that feels like being put on trial gets quietly abandoned within a month. So this chapter takes the word apart and hands it back meaning something completely different. A home audit is not an inspection; it is a check-in: a quick, regular, warm look at the house, not the people, to see how the good things you built are holding up and to catch small drift before it becomes a large mess. It is closer to feeling a child's forehead for a temperature than to a tax audit, an act of care rather than an act of judgment. What changes everything is the orientation of the looking. The mechanical act can be identical, someone walks through the house and looks, but an inspection points its attention at people and asks who failed, so it produces defensiveness, hiding, and resentment, while a check-in points its attention at the home and asks how the house is holding up, so it produces small, cheerful fixes and a household that does not mind at all. Four things turn one into the other: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped. The first of the four is the one everything hangs on: keep the attention on the room and off the roommate, because the moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, and people start hiding the mess before audit day. A drifted shelf is a neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre, and you simply top it up. And the whole value of a regular check-in is timing: a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix, the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend, so you look often and lightly, keep it genuinely light because only the light version survives, and let each person check their own zone. The friction meter takes its first Sustain step. The needle moves for the first time since Standardize, but only a little, a short step along the dashed last-gap path that appeared in Chapter 26, because a home with a regular, kindly check-in needs a little less minding, drift caught while it is small instead of found when it is large. An audit clears nothing on its own; it scrubs no surface and removes no clutter, so this is a small move and not a milestone; the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. The rule to carry: an audit checks the home, not the household, and what it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame.
