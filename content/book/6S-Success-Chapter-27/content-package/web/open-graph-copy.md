Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (an audit checks the home, not the household)

- og:title: An Audit Checks the Home, Not the Household.
- og:description: You have the keeping mindset from Chapter 26; here is the first concrete way to keep the gains, a regular check-in on the home. But the word audit makes everyone flinch, because it sounds like a test with an inspector and a grade. A home audit is the opposite: a quick, regular, friendly look at the house, not the people, to see what is still working and catch small drift before it grows. An audit checks the home, not the household, so what it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame. Caught this week, a drift is a two-minute fix; left for six months, it is a lost weekend. Audit the house kindly and often, and it will never again need rescuing.

## Variant B (a home audit is not an inspection)

- og:title: A Home Audit Is Not an Inspection.
- og:description: An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift. So keep it about the shelf, never the shelf-slipper: a shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, like a low tyre you simply top up. An audit is a kindness you do for your home, not a judgment you pass on your family.

On the right, run as a check-in: a warm figure giving small green ticks to what is holding and quietly noting one small drift, the family loose and even helping. Warm daylight, unhurried, nothing corporate, no shame in either panel, only honesty. Footer: The same walk through the same rooms. The only difference is whether the looking is aimed at the people or at the home.
