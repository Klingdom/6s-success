# Chapter 27 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, kind, de-frightening voice; the audit is affectionate care for the home, not an inspection of the people; drift is normal and never scolded, nobody is on trial; no force, control, or surveillance words, no hype, no brand names.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** You built the good home. Now here is the first real thing you do to keep it, and the word for it makes everyone flinch.

**Body:** The word is audit. It sounds like a test with an inspector and a grade. It is the opposite. Swipe for the first concrete way to keep the gains, a check-in, not an inspection.

---

## Slide 2: The humble practice of looking

**Headline:** A home is kept by the humble, powerful act of looking.

**Body:** A home holds its shape when someone, now and then, simply looks at it with clear eyes and notices what has begun to slip, while the slipping is still small and a two-minute matter.

---

## Slide 3: Why "audit" makes everyone flinch

**Headline:** The word arrives carrying suitcases.

**Body:** Tax inspectors, surprise checks, someone official with a clipboard deciding whether you passed. Any keeping-practice that feels like being put on trial gets quietly abandoned within a month. So the first job is to take the word apart.

---

## Slide 4: Not an inspection

**Headline:** A home audit is not an inspection. It is a check-in.

**Body:** Closer to feeling a child's forehead for a temperature than to a tax audit. An act of care, not judgment. Get that distinction right and a whole household will happily do it. Get it wrong and it dies on contact.

---

## Slide 5: The same walk, two feelings

**Headline:** The same walk through the same rooms, twice, and it feels like the opposite thing.

**Body:** An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up. One is looking for fault, the other only for drift.

---

## Slide 6: The definition

**Headline:** A health check for the house, not a report card for the family.

**Body:** A 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up: what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small.

---

## Slide 7: The four things

**Headline:** Four small things turn an inspection into a check-in.

**Body:** It looks at the home, not the people. It is light and quick. It is regular and expected, never a surprise. And it notices what is working, not only what has slipped. Miss one and it tips back toward inspection.

---

## Slide 8: The rule

**Headline:** An audit checks the home, not the household.

**Body:** What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame. The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected. Keep it about the shelf.

---

## Slide 9: A drifted shelf is a low tyre

**Headline:** A drifted shelf is a neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre.

**Body:** You simply top it up. It is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right.

---

## Slide 10: Catch it early

**Headline:** A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix.

**Body:** The same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. Drift compounds, so you look often and lightly. You do not audit the home to judge it. You audit it so that it never again has to be rescued.

---

## Slide 11: Keep it light, check your own zone

**Headline:** Only the light version survives.

**Body:** A five-minute check every week or two beats a two-hour reckoning once a year, because the small one actually keeps happening. Let each person check their own zone, and it becomes quiet pride rather than being inspected.
