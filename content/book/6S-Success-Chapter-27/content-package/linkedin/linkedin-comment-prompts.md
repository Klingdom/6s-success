# Chapter 27 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, kind, de-frightening voice: drift is normal and expected, finding it is a success of the audit and not a failure of the household, nobody is on trial, and the audit is care rather than inspection. No corporate framing, no force, control, or surveillance language, no scolding, no brand names.

---

1. Be honest with me: what does the word audit do to you when you hear it applied to your own home? For a lot of us some part of the body braces, as if we are about to be tested and found wanting, and that bracing is exactly the thing that kills the habit. Where do you think that flinch comes from, and can you imagine the same short walk through your rooms feeling like a check-in on the house rather than a verdict on the people in it?

2. An audit checks the home, not the household, and what it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame. That line is easy to nod at and surprisingly hard to live. When something in your home has drifted, a shelf, a counter, a pile by the door, is your first thought about the system or about who let it slip? And have you noticed what changes in the room, and in the people, depending on which one you reach for first?

3. Here is the timing claim I want to test against your life: a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix, and the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. Can you think of a spot in your home where a tiny thing you left alone quietly hardened into a whole dreaded project? What would it have taken to catch it while it was still small, and what has stopped a regular, light look from happening?

4. The counterintuitive part is that the thorough, solemn audit is the one that dies, and the short, low-key one is the one that lasts, because only the light version actually keeps happening. Think about the keeping-routines you have tried. Were they built to be impressive or built to be repeatable? If you had to design a five-minute check you could genuinely do every week or two, forever, what would you strip out to make it that light?

5. Here is the warmer half of the question. So many homes have one person quietly walking around noticing everything that has slipped and fixing it alone, which is inspection by another name and it wears that person down. What if instead each person checked their own zone, nobody grading anyone, just quiet pride in your own patch? For those who have tried sharing the looking rather than carrying it, did people turn out to be honest about their own drift, and did it actually make the keeping lighter?
