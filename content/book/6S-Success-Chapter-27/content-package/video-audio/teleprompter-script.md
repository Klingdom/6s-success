# Teleprompter Script: 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections

**Chapter 27, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** 6s-audits-that-do-not-feel-like-inspections
**Length:** about 770 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the second chapter of Sustain.

Last time, you took on the mindset.

That a home is kept, not finished.

On small shared habits, until keeping is second nature.

That is the ground.

Now we lay the first stone on it.

The first concrete thing you do to keep the gains.

And it is the humblest practice there is.

Looking.

A home holds its shape when someone looks at it.

With clear eyes.

And notices what has begun to slip.

While the slipping is still small.

The word for this, in 6S, is audit.

And that word is the whole problem.

Audit makes almost everyone tense up.

It arrives carrying tax inspectors and clipboards.

Someone official deciding whether you passed.

Anything that feels like being put on trial.

Gets quietly abandoned within a month.

So let me take the word apart.

A home audit is not an inspection.

It is a check-in.

An inspection is something done to people.

To catch them out and grade them.

A check-in is something done for a home.

To see how it is holding up and help it early.

It can be the same walk through the same rooms.

And it will feel like the opposite thing.

Because one is looking for fault.

And the other is only looking for drift.

So here is the practice, with the dread removed.

A short, warm, regular look at the house.

It checks the home, not the people.

It is a health check, not a test.

And its whole spirit is four small things.

It looks at the home, not the people.

It is light and quick.

It is regular and expected.

And it notices what is working.

Not only what has slipped.

Each one defuses a part of the dread.

The home, not the people, so no one is on trial.

Light and quick, so it is never a burden.

Regular and expected, so it is never an ambush.

Notices what works, so people leave encouraged.

Miss any one, and it tips back into an inspection.

Hold all four, and it is a welcome habit of care.

Now, the first of those four is everything.

Keep the attention on the room.

And off the roommate.

The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip.

It dies.

Because nobody volunteers to be inspected.

So keep it about the shelf.

A drifted shelf is not a failure of character.

It is a small system that needs a small adjustment.

And blame does not just feel bad.

It wrecks the audit.

People get defensive and go quiet about drift.

And they start hiding the mess before audit day.

Keep it about the shelf, and none of that happens.

A drifted shelf is a neutral fact.

Like a low tyre. You simply top it up.

Now, why do you do this regularly, and keep it small.

It is entirely about timing.

A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix.

The same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend.

You do not audit the home to judge it.

You audit it so it never again has to be rescued.

Because drift compounds.

One thing in the wrong place invites a second.

So look often and lightly.

A five-minute check every week or two.

Catches drift while it is still trivial.

And keep it genuinely light.

Because only the light version survives.

If you want, let each person check their own zone.

That is quiet pride in your own patch.

Not being inspected.

Now go and look at the friction meter.

It moves again, just a little.

The first movement since Standardize.

Last chapter the needle held.

And revealed the last gap to the goal.

This chapter you laid down the first keeping-machinery.

A regular, kindly check-in.

So the needle takes its first Sustain step.

A short move along that dashed path.

It is small, and honestly so.

Because an audit clears nothing on its own.

What it does is subtler, and over years, larger.

It makes the home more able to keep itself.

A home that catches its own drift early.

Needs less minding to stay good.

So here is the rule that holds it all.

An audit checks the home, not the household.

What it finds is always a system to fix.

Never a person to blame.

So here is your job. It is one of the smallest in the book.

Do one two-minute audit right now.

Walk a single room.

And ask, of the room and not of anyone in it.

What has drifted since we set this up.

Fix the first small thing you find.

That is a whole audit.

And it was not frightening at all.

An audit is a kindness you do for your home.

Not a judgment you pass on your family.
