## SHORT 1: A home audit is not an inspection
**Topic: the hero reframe, the same walk can feel like two opposite things. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The word audit makes everyone flinch. It sounds like a test with an inspector and a grade. So let me take that word apart and hand it back to you.

**Script:**
You have the mindset now: a home is kept, not finished. So here is the first concrete thing you actually do to keep it, and it is the humblest practice there is: looking. A home holds its shape when someone, now and then, simply looks at it with clear eyes and notices what has begun to slip. In 6S, the word for that is audit, and audit makes almost everyone tense up, because it arrives carrying tax inspectors and clipboards and grades. So here is the whole idea. A home audit is not an inspection. It is a check-in. An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift.

- 0s: "The word audit makes everyone flinch."
- 14s: "A home audit is not an inspection. It is a check-in."
- 30s: "One is looking for fault."
- 44s: "The other is only looking for drift."

## SHORT 2: A health check for the house
**Topic: the definition and the four things that turn an inspection into a check-in. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Four small things turn a frightening word into an easy, welcome habit. Miss any one, and the audit tips back into an inspection.

**Script:**
Here is what a home audit actually is, with the dread removed. A 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up: what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small. It checks the home, not the people, and it is a health check, not a test. And the whole spirit of it lives in four small things. What turns an inspection into a check-in: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped. Each one defuses a different part of the dread. It looks at the home, so no one is on trial. It is light and quick, so it is never a burden. It is regular and expected, so it is never an ambush. And it notices what works, so people leave it encouraged, not scolded. Hold all four, and a frightening word becomes an easy, welcome habit of care.

- 0s: "A health check, not a test."
- 14s: "The home, not the people. Light and quick."
- 30s: "Regular and expected. Notices what works."
- 44s: "Hold all four. The word stops being frightening."

## SHORT 3: Keep it about the shelf
**Topic: the no-blame move, blame wrecks the audit two ways. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies. Nobody volunteers to be inspected. So keep it about the shelf.

**Script:**
Of all the things that make an audit work, this is the one everything hangs on: keep the attention on the room and off the roommate. The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected. Keep it about the shelf. A shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right. And this is not soft-heartedness, it is what actually works. The instant you assign blame, two things happen and both wreck it. People get defensive and go quiet about drift, so you lose the very information the audit exists to gather. And worse, they start hiding the mess before audit day, so you have trained your family to fake a clean house on a schedule. Keep it about the shelf, and none of that happens. A drifted shelf is a neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre, and you simply top it up.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Keep it about the shelf."
- 14s: "Not who let it slip."
- 30s: "Blame teaches people to hide the mess before audit day."
- 44s: "A drifted shelf is a low tyre. You just top it up."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The defensive household going quiet and the mess hidden before audit day, then the attention turning off the roommate and onto the shelf, then a drifted shelf topped up like a low tyre, neutral and blameless. Warm, human, slate and honey.

## SHORT 4: Catch it early, keep it light
**Topic: timing, a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix. The same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. That gap is the whole reason you look often.

**Script:**
Here is why you do this regularly and keep it small, and it is entirely about timing. The whole value of a regular check-in is timing. A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. You do not audit the home to judge it. You audit it so that it never again has to be rescued. Because drift compounds. One thing left in the wrong place invites a second; a system used slightly wrong quietly becomes used wrong by default. Caught in its first week, almost any drift is a shrug. Left for half a year, it has hardened into the new normal and turned into a whole dreaded project. So look often and lightly. A five-minute check every week or two catches drift while it is still trivial. And keep it genuinely light, because only the light version survives. If you want, let each person check their own zone, so it is quiet pride in your own patch, not being inspected.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A drift caught this week: a two-minute fix."
- 12s: "The same drift in six months: a lost weekend."
- 28s: "Drift compounds. So look often and lightly."
- 40s: "Only the light version survives."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Drift compounding, one thing recruiting a second, then the same drift at two ages, a two-minute fix beside a lost weekend, then the light audit walk through the zones with green "holding" ticks and one honey "drifted, quick fix" note, a few minutes on the clock. Warm, calm.

## SHORT 5: The needle takes its first Sustain step
**Topic: the friction meter moves for the first time since Standardize, honestly small. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** For the first time since Standardize, the needle moves. Just a little. And that small honest move is the whole point.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, because it moves again, just a little, and it is the first movement since Standardize. Last chapter the needle held, and the dial revealed the last small gap to the goal as a dashed path. This chapter you laid down the first real piece of keeping-machinery, a regular, kindly check-in, and so the needle takes its first Sustain step, a short move along that dashed path. It is a small step, and honestly so, because an audit does not clear a single thing on its own. It scrubs no surface and removes no clutter. What it does is subtler and, over years, larger: it makes the home a little more able to keep itself, because drift is now met while it is small instead of discovered when it is large. And a home that catches its own drift early is a home that needs less minding to stay good. So hold the rule that carries it: an audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame. The goal is reached only at the very end of the book.
