# X Short Posts: 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections

Chapter 27 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters, counting its number line, and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Chapter 27 of 6S Success: Home Edition gives the first concrete way to keep the gains: a regular check-in on the home that catches drift while it is still small. It hangs on one word almost everyone flinches at, and takes the dread off it: audit. Free online.

---

2/
"A home audit is not an inspection." It is a check-in: a quick, regular, warm look at the house, not the people, to see how the good things you built are holding up and catch small drift before it grows. Read the free chapter.

(229 chars)

---

3/
"An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early." Same walk, same rooms, opposite feeling: one looks for fault, the other only for drift.

(257 chars)

---

4/
"An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame." That is the whole spirit of a home audit: care for the house, never judgment on the family. Read the free chapter.

(230 chars)

---

5/
Keep it about the shelf. "A shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right." Read the free chapter.

(229 chars)

---

6/
"A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend." So you look often and lightly. You do not audit the home to judge it; you audit it so that it never again has to be rescued. Read the free chapter.

(253 chars)

---

7/
"What turns an inspection into a check-in: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped." Hold all four and a frightening word becomes an easy habit.

(254 chars)

---

8/
Keep it short and regular: a five-minute check every week beats a two-hour reckoning once a year, and only the short one keeps happening. Let each person check their own zone. A self-audit you own beats an inspection you resent. Read the free chapter.

(254 chars)

---

9/
Do one two-minute audit right now. Walk a single room and ask, of the room and not of anyone in it, "what has drifted since we set this up?" Fix the first small thing you find. That is a whole audit, and it was not frightening at all.

(237 chars)

10/
The needle takes its first Sustain step, its first since Standardize, a short way along the dashed last-gap path. An audit clears nothing on its own; it just makes the home keep itself, drift caught small instead of found large.
