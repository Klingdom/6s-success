# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the thing that quietly keeps a home good is not another big cleanup but a short, friendly check-in, an audit that is not an inspection, because you look at the home and never at the people, and a drift you catch this week is a two-minute fix while the same drift found in six months is a lost weekend) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. The check-in is framed as care and relief, never as inspection, surveillance, or shame about a shelf that slipped. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one tiny thing that stopped the slow slide in my house without a single big cleanup, and then I want to hear from you all.

We had done the whole 6S thing, sorted and cleaned and set up properly, and it was lovely. But slowly, quietly, little things drifted. A shelf here, a pile there. And I kept thinking the answer was another big reset.

Here is the idea that changed it for me, and it hangs on one word most of us flinch at: audit.

"A home audit is not an inspection."

That one line took all the dread off it for me. An inspection is the tax-man version, someone with a clipboard deciding whether you passed. This is the opposite thing.

"An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift."

So here is what I actually do now, and it takes about five minutes. Once a week I walk the rooms and ask, of the room and never of anyone in it, what has drifted since we set this up. That is the whole thing. And the reason it works is pure timing.

"A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend."

The big resets mostly stopped, because nothing ever got bad enough to need one. And the rule that keeps it kind is simple.

"An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

If you did a quick, friendly walk of your home once a week, looking only at the rooms and never at the people, which one small drift do you think you would catch early? And what has quietly crept back since you last set a space up properly?

I will go first in the comments. 😄

(If anyone wants it written out properly, this is from Chapter 27 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the idea that an audit can be a kindness you do for your home, not a judgment you pass on your family.)
