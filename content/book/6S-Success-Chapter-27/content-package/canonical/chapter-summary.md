## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 27 is the second chapter of Part Eight and lays the first stone on the keeping mindset Chapter 26 gave: the first concrete, repeatable thing you do to keep the gains, which is the humble practice of looking. A home holds its shape when someone, now and then, simply looks at it with clear eyes and notices what has begun to slip, while the slipping is still small. The trouble is the name for this. Audit is a word that makes almost everyone tense up, arriving with tax inspectors and clipboards and grades, and any keeping-practice that feels like being put on trial gets quietly abandoned within a month.

So the chapter takes the word apart. A home audit is not an inspection; it is a check-in: a quick, regular, warm look at the house, not the people, to see how the good things you built are holding up and to catch small drift before it becomes a large mess. An inspection points its attention at people and asks who failed; a check-in points it at the home and asks how the house is holding up. Four things turn one into the other: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working. Keep it about the shelf, never the shelf-slipper. Catch drift this week and it is a two-minute fix; catch it in six months and it is a lost weekend. The needle takes its first small Sustain step.
