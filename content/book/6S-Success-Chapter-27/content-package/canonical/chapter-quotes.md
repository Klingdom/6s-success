## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "A home holds its shape when someone, now and then, simply looks at it with clear eyes and notices what has begun to slip, while the slipping is still small and a two-minute matter."

2. "A home audit is not an inspection."

3. "Get that distinction right and the audit becomes something a whole household will happily do. Get it wrong and it dies on contact."

4. "A check-in points its attention at the home and asks how the house is holding up, so it produces small, cheerful fixes and a household that does not mind at all."

5. "A drifted shelf is a neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre, and you simply top it up."

6. "a home that catches its own drift early is a home that needs less minding to stay good."

7. "an audit is a kindness you do for your home, not a judgment you pass on your family."

## The rule (frozen, hero)

"An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame."

## The four things (frozen)

"What turns an inspection into a check-in: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped."

## The inspection-versus-check-in reframe (frozen, pull-quote)

"An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift."

## The no-blame reframe (frozen, pull-quote)

"The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected. Keep it about the shelf. A shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right."

## The catch-it-early reframe (frozen, pull-quote)

"The whole value of a regular check-in is timing. A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. You do not audit the home to judge it. You audit it so that it never again has to be rescued."

## The definition box (frozen)

"A 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up: what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small. It checks the home, not the people, and it is a health check, not a test."

## One Idea to Keep (frozen close)

"Keep the gains with a light, regular check-in that looks at the home and never at the people. A drift caught small is a small fix; a drift left to grow is an overhaul. Audit the house kindly and often, and it will never again need rescuing."

## Quick Win (canonical)

"Do one two-minute audit right now. Walk a single room and ask, of the room and not of anyone in it, "what has drifted since we set this up?" Fix the first small thing you find. That is a whole audit, and it was not frightening at all."

## Family Challenge (canonical)

"Let each person check their own zone rather than one person checking everyone's. A self-audit you own beats an inspection you resent, and people turn out to be honest about their own drift when nobody is grading them."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"Keep it short and regular. A five-minute check every week catches far more than a two-hour reckoning once a year, and only the short one actually keeps happening. You can reuse the simple snapshot from your baseline audit; you do not need a new instrument."

## Common Mistake (canonical)

"Turning the audit into a performance review of the people, with scores held over someone's head. The instant it feels like judgment, everyone starts hiding the mess before the audit instead of fixing it, and you have taught the household to fake a clean home for inspection day. Check the home, kindly, and never the person."
