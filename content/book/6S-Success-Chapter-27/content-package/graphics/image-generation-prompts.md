# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 27

**"6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain*

Four prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across
all four: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the
warm accent, the check-in side, the Sustain-step tag), honey `#DDA63A` (the single drift mark), slate blue
`#3C5A6B` (data, milestones, the catch, the greyed inspection word), soft green `#6E8B5B` (the holding ticks,
the small approving marks, the last-gap path, the goal), a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the
smallest marks only, the inspection band's red pen and the no-backslide bar, used barely if at all on these
calm images. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the finished home the reader has cleared, arranged, cleaned, made safe, and
standardized. This chapter, the SECOND of Part Eight, CONTINUES Sustain, the sixth and final S, and gives the
first concrete way to keep the gains that Chapter 26 promised: a regular check-in on the home that catches
drift while it is still small. Its whole work is a reframe, taking the dread off the word audit, because an
audit that feels like an inspection of the people will not survive, and only one that feels like care for the
home will actually get done. It names why the word makes everyone flinch (it arrives carrying tax inspectors,
surprise checks, someone official with a clipboard deciding whether you passed, but that is inspection, and
it is not what a home audit is) and delivers the inspection-versus-check-in reframe (an inspection is done to
people to catch them out and grade them; a check-in is done for a home to see how it is holding up and help
it early; it can be the same walk through the same rooms and feel like the opposite thing, because one is
looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift). It delivers the hero, a health check for the
house (a quick, regular, friendly look at the home, not the people, that catches drift early and notices what
is working), with the definition box and the four things that turn an inspection into a check-in (the home
not the people; light and quick; regular and expected; notices what works, not only what slipped), delivers
the crucial no-blame move (audit the home, never the household, because the moment it becomes about who let
the shelf slip it dies, and blame teaches people to hide the mess before audit day) with the frozen rule, and
delivers the catch-it-early timing logic (a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix, the same drift caught
in six months is a lost weekend, so look often and lightly and let each person check their own zone). Nothing
of the first five S's is re-taught, the one-time baseline audit from Chapter 5 is referenced in one light
line only and its radar and scoring are not re-explained, and none of the later Sustain mechanisms are built
(the family problem-solving of Chapter 28, the daily rhythm of Chapter 29, and the recurring event of Chapter
30 are named only as still to come). Keep the tone calm, warm, and de-frightening: drift is normal and
expected, which is the whole reason for a regular check-in, and finding drift is a success of the audit, not
a failure of the household, so nobody is ever scolded and nobody is on trial. The audit is a kindness you do
for your home, not a judgment you pass on your family; the register is relief, a frightening word handed back
meaning something completely different. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated house style: no
corporate or office look, no force, control, or surveillance framing (no crack down, no hold people
accountable in the punitive sense, no keep tabs on, no one standing over a shoulder), no cages, no hype (no
unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, audit-proof), no photoreal polish; the inspection side is stern but
never menacing, and the check-in is small, ordinary, warm, and human. Use inspection ONLY as the negated
thing the audit is not.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in
the scene, a hand-lettered card, a handwritten line on a real scrap, a hand-drawn mark on a real zone, or the
reading on a real gauge, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered
label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 27 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between
words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: check-in, home-audit, two-minute, five-minute, no-blame,
catch-it-early, light-and-quick, health-check, last-gap, three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide,
self-audit, audit-day, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, Shine-complete, Safety-complete,
Standardize-complete.

## 1. The same walk, two feelings (the opener)

A warm, wide editorial illustration of one short walk through the same three or four rooms of a home, drawn
twice in two horizontal bands, in soft daylight, to show that the identical act can feel like two opposite
things. On the top band, an inspection: a stern figure in a severe, dark outfit moves through the rooms
holding a clipboard and a red pen, marking ticks and crosses with a hard face, while the family stand stiffly
against the wall to attention, anxious, one child half-hiding a toy behind their back, the whole scene cold
and tribunal-like, with the red pen the only warm-red note in the band. On the bottom band, the very same
rooms walked by a warm, relaxed figure with an easy smile, giving small approving soft-green ticks to the
things that are holding up and quietly jotting one small honey-coloured drift on a scrap of paper, while the
family are loose and unbothered, one wandering along and pointing something out, a child cheerfully showing a
tidy shelf. The two bands are unmistakably the same walk through the same rooms, the same furniture, so the
only difference the eye reads is the orientation of the looking, at fault in the people or at drift in the
home. Warm paper cream, ink-brown line work, cool slate-charcoal for the inspection figure, warm terracotta
and soft green for the check-in, one honey accent on the single jotted drift, warm-red held to the red pen
alone. Editorial storybook illustration. The same walk, two feelings.

- Mood: nothing about the rooms changed, everything about the feeling did, and that difference is the whole
  of this chapter; an inspection is someone looking for fault and a family standing trial for the state of
  their house, a check-in is someone looking for drift, kindly, with the household relaxed and even helping;
  the register is warm and de-frightening, the top band stern but never menacing, the bottom band easy and
  affectionate.
- Avoid: a menacing or cruel inspector, a terrified or humiliated family, a corporate or office look, floating
  typeset text overlays, anyone being shamed, a cage or a raised voice, surveillance cameras or a one-way
  mirror, warm-red anywhere except the single red pen, brand names or recognizable products; keep the two
  bands plainly the same walk, the top cold and tribunal-like, the bottom warm with small green ticks and one
  honey drift, the household relaxed and joining in.

## 2. The check-in, not the inspection (the four things)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-made infographic titled the check-in, not the inspection, on a soft
cream panel, in soft daylight, showing the four small things that turn an inspection into a check-in as four
clean, warm cards, each with one plain hand-drawn icon and a short hand-lettered label. The first card shows
a small house, not a person, and reads the home, not the people. The second shows a little clock reading a
few minutes and reads light and quick. The third shows a calendar with a gentle repeating mark and no
surprise and reads regular and expected. The fourth shows a small soft-green tick beside a little note and
reads notices what works, not only what slipped. Across the foot, the word inspection sits greyed and cold in
slate on the left, a small arrow points right, and the word check-in sits warm in terracotta on the right, so
the eye travels from the frightening word to the friendly one. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line
work, slate for the icons and the greyed inspection word, soft green for the tick, one warm terracotta accent
held to the check-in word, no warm-red on this calm image. A picture of one plain idea: four small choices
turn a frightening word into an easy, welcome habit of care.

- Mood: each of the four defuses a different part of the dread, the home not the people so no one is on trial,
  light and quick so it never becomes a burden, regular and expected so it carries no ambush, notices what
  works so people leave encouraged rather than scolded; hold all four and a frightening word becomes an easy,
  welcome habit; the register is calm and reassuring.
- Avoid: a cold corporate slide, a scoreboard or a grade sheet, gridlines everywhere, neon color, numbers or
  scores, a scolding tone, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names; keep the four cards clean and
  equal in weight, one plain icon each, and make the foot turn from greyed inspection to warm check-in read
  clearly, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but not childish.

## 3. A light audit walk (the routine)

A warm, homely editorial illustration of a light audit walk in progress on a soft cream panel, in soft
daylight, drawn as a short, friendly dashed path winding through three or four small labelled home zones, for
example the launch pad, the kitchen counter, the kids' shelf, and the bathroom, each a small hand-drawn room
vignette. Most zones carry a small soft-green tick and a tiny hand-lettered word holding, showing what is
working; one zone carries a small honey-coloured mark and a short note reading drifted, quick fix, the single
thing to correct. A small clock in the top corner reads a few minutes, and a hand-lettered line across the
foot reads check the home, not the people, and keep it short. It is clearly a kind, quick look, warm and
unhurried, not a scoreboard or a grade sheet. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, slate for the
dashed path and the zone strokes, soft green for the holding ticks, one honey accent held to the single drift
mark, no warm-red anywhere. A picture of one plain idea: a whole audit is a few minutes, most of it holding,
one small drift noted for a quick fix.

- Mood: a whole audit, in a few minutes, a short walk through the zones, a green tick for everything holding
  up and one gentle note on the single thing that has drifted, ready for a quick fix, no scores, no grades,
  nobody on trial, just a kind, regular look at how the home is doing; the register is warm, homely, and
  light.
- Avoid: a clipboard-and-grade scoreboard, an out-of-thirty score, red crosses, a route march or a stern
  circuit, a cold clinical chart, neon color, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names; keep the walk
  short and easy, the zones warm and homely, the single drift held to one zone in honey and noted lightly as
  a quick fix, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but not childish.

- Mood: the needle moves for the first time since Standardize, but honestly only a little, because an audit

- Avoid: the live needle making a large forward jump or reaching the goal crosshair, drawing this as the

  and the goal closed, hype, neon color. The needle takes just its first small Sustain step, the Chapter 26
  ghost sits close behind so the smallness reads, the catch stays fully engaged and locked with a small
  padlock, the backslide is barred solidly, and the dashed remainder to the goal is shorter than it was last
  chapter but not gone.
