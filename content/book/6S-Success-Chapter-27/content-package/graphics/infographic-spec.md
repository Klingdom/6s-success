# Infographic Spec: 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections

**Chapter 27, "6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a home audit is
not an inspection, it is a check-in, and the identical walk through the same rooms can feel like two opposite
things depending only on whether the looking is aimed at fault in the people or at drift in the home. The
concept pairs the hero rule, **An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system
to fix, never a person to blame.**, with the picture at its heart: **the same walk, two feelings**, one short
walk through the same three or four rooms drawn twice in two horizontal bands, on the top an inspection with a
stern clipboard-and-red-pen figure and a family standing stiffly to attention, cold and tribunal-like, on the
bottom a check-in through the very same rooms with a warm, relaxed figure giving small green ticks and jotting
one small drift, the household loose and joining in. So the viewer sees the whole move at once, that a home
audit is a health check, not a test, and that nothing about the rooms needs to change, only the feeling.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_27_final.html`. The two bands are
unmistakably the same walk, the same rooms and furniture; cool slate-charcoal and a single spark-red pen
carry the stern inspection band, warm terracotta and soft green carry the check-in band, and one honey accent
is held to the single jotted drift so the eye reads the two feelings as the same walk; the single small warm
red is held back for the red pen and marks only, because the audit is a gift of care, not a live hazard.
"Inspection" is not the point, and neither is "surveillance"; a home checked by a kindly, regular walk is
warmer and lighter, a health check rather than a trial. Anti-shame throughout: drift is normal and expected,
which is the whole reason for a regular check-in, and finding drift is a success of the audit, not a failure
of the household, because a shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character, it is a small system
that needs a small adjustment. The register is warm and freeing, never corporate, never punitive; an audit is
a kindness you do for your home, not a judgment you pass on your family.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: an audit checks the
  home, not the household. The eye should read the rule, then see the same walk, two feelings make it real,
  the top band an inspection looking for fault in the people, the bottom band a check-in looking for drift in
  the home.
- The same walk, two feelings is the hero, so make the two bands unmistakably the same walk: the same rooms,
  the same furniture, only the feeling different. Keep the top band stern but never menacing, a tribunal and
  not a horror, and the bottom band warm and easy, the check-in figure open and relaxed, the household
  joining in. Set the rule, the definition, and the four things byte-for-byte as they read in the chapter;
  they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: cool slate and a single spark-red pen carry the inspection band, warm terracotta
  and green carry the check-in band, one honey accent is held to the single jotted drift so the two feelings
  read as one walk; the single small warm red is reserved for the red pen and marks and is barely used,
  because this image is a gift of care and not a live hazard.
- This image makes the concept, the reframe that opens the friendly audit: a home audit is a check-in, not an
  inspection. Do NOT re-teach the first five S's, do NOT re-teach the one-time baseline audit from Chapter 5
  (the 6S Snapshot radar, the 0 to 5 scoring, the out-of-30 baseline), which is referenced in one light line
  only, and do NOT build the later Sustain mechanisms, the family problem-solving (Chapter 28), the daily
  rhythm (Chapter 29), and the recurring event (Chapter 30), which are named only as still to come. And name
  no brand and draw no specific product; the rooms and their furniture are plain and generic, teaching the
  idea, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: drift is normal and expected, which is the whole reason for a regular check-in, and finding
  drift is a success of the audit, not a failure of the household, because a shelf that drifted is not a
  failure of anyone's character, it is a small system that needs a small adjustment. What you are building is
  not surveillance, it is a kindness you do for your home. Keep the register warm and freeing, a health check
  and not a trial; no force, control, or surveillance language (no crack down, no hold people accountable in
  the punitive sense, no keep tabs on, no hiding), no punitive judgment presented as virtue, no cage, no hype.
  Use inspection ONLY as the negated thing the audit is not.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the two bands drawn by hand as
  small warm rooms rather than clinical thumbnails, the inspection figure severe but not cruel and the
  check-in figure open and warm, the household drawn plain and human, the single honey drift noted lightly on
  a small scrap, the whole image the calm, warm register of a frightening word handed back meaning something
  completely different.
