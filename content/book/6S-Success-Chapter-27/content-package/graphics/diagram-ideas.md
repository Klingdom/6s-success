Scope fence: this chapter is the SECOND chapter of Part Eight, and it CONTINUES Sustain, the sixth and FINAL
of the six S's, with the first concrete way to keep the gains that Chapter 26 promised. Its whole work is a
reframe: it takes the dread off the word audit, because an audit that feels like an inspection of the people
will not survive, and only one that feels like care for the home will actually get done. It names why the
word makes everyone flinch (it arrives carrying tax inspectors, surprise checks, someone official with a
clipboard deciding whether you passed, but that is inspection, and it is not what a home audit is), delivers
the inspection-versus-check-in reframe (an inspection is done to people to catch them out and grade them; a
check-in is done for a home to see how it is holding up and help it early; it can be the same walk through the
same rooms and feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking
for drift), delivers the hero, a health check for the house (a quick, regular, friendly look at the home, not
the people, that catches drift early and notices what is working), with the definition box and the four
things that turn an inspection into a check-in (the home not the people; light and quick; regular and
expected; notices what works, not only what slipped), delivers the crucial no-blame move (audit the home,
never the household, because the moment it becomes about who let the shelf slip it dies, and blame teaches
people to hide the mess before audit day), with the frozen rule, and delivers the catch-it-early timing logic
(a drift caught this week is a two-minute fix, the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend, so look
often and lightly, keep it genuinely light so only the light version survives, and let each person check their
own zone). It USES the keeping mindset Chapter 26 built without re-teaching it, references the one-time
baseline audit from Chapter 5 in one light line only, and does NOT re-teach the 6S Snapshot radar, the 0 to 5
scoring, or the out-of-30 baseline, and does NOT build the family problem-solving method (Chapter 28), the
daily-times system (Chapter 29), or the recurring 6S event (Chapter 30), which are named only as still to
come. CRITICAL fence: this is the recurring, drift-catching CHECK-IN and its REFRAME (not an inspection),
NOT the one-time baseline audit, NOT the method for solving the bigger recurring problems (Chapter 28), and
NOT the daily rhythm or the recurring event. Because a home with a regular, kindly check-in needs a little
less minding, the friction needle takes its FIRST Sustain step, a small MOVE along the dashed last-gap path
revealed in Chapter 26, from the held position toward the goal; this is NOT a milestone, and honestly small,
because an audit clears nothing on its own, it scrubs no surface and removes no clutter, but it makes the home
a little more able to keep itself, because drift is now met while it is small instead of discovered when it is
large. Five of the six S's were complete going in; the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at
Chapter 30. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 27 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating
hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: check-in, home-audit,
two-minute, five-minute, no-blame, catch-it-early, light-and-quick, health-check, keeping-machinery,
last-gap, three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide, self-audit, audit-day, Sort-complete,
Straighten-complete, Shine-complete, Safety-complete, Standardize-complete. Anti-shame throughout: drift is
normal and expected, which is the whole reason for a regular check-in, and finding drift is a success of the
audit, not a failure of the household; a shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character, it is a
small system that needs a small adjustment. The audit is care, not inspection; use inspection ONLY as the
negated thing the audit is not. No force, control, or surveillance framing anywhere (no crack down, no hold
people accountable in the punitive sense, no keep tabs on, no one standing over a shoulder with a pen, no
hiding), no hype (no unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, audit-proof), no corporate register, no brand
names. The register is calm, warm, human, homely, and illustrated, the relief of a frightening word handed
back meaning something completely different: an audit is a kindness you do for your home, not a judgment you
pass on your family.

## 1. The Same Walk, Two Feelings (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole idea in one image. The identical short walk through the same three or
four rooms, drawn twice in two horizontal bands, so the only variable the eye can read is the orientation of
the looking, whether it is aimed at fault in the people or at drift in the home. On the top, an inspection: a
family standing trial for the state of their house. On the bottom, a check-in: the same rooms, the same walk,
the household relaxed and even helping. Nothing about the rooms changed; everything about the feeling did, and
that difference is the whole of this chapter. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** One short walk through the same three or four rooms, drawn twice in two horizontal bands.
The TOP band, `INSPECTION`, has a stern figure in a severe, dark outfit moving through the rooms holding a
clipboard and a red pen, marking ticks and crosses with a hard face, while the family stand stiffly against
the wall to attention, anxious, one child half-hiding a toy behind their back; the whole scene is cold and
tribunal-like. The BOTTOM band, `CHECK-IN`, walks the very same rooms with a warm, relaxed figure with an
easy smile, giving small approving green ticks to the things that are holding up and quietly jotting one
small honey-coloured drift on a scrap of paper, while the family are loose and unbothered, one wandering
along and pointing something out, a child cheerfully showing a tidy shelf.

**Rough layout:**
- A soft warm wash on paper cream `#F7F2E9` falling into panel `#FBF7EF`. A faint Newsreader italic line,
  muted `#6A625A`: `the same walk through the same rooms, twice`.
- TOP band eyebrow, cool slate sans caps: `INSPECTION`. The stern clipboard-and-red-pen figure, the family
  stiff against the wall, a child half-hiding a toy, the red pen the only spark `#CB4B36` in the band, the
  whole band cool and tribunal-like, with a Newsreader italic line beneath in slate: `someone looking for
  fault, and a family standing trial for the state of their house`.
- BOTTOM band eyebrow, warm terracotta sans caps: `CHECK-IN`. The very same rooms, the warm relaxed figure
  giving small green `#6E8B5B` ticks to what is holding and jotting one small honey `#DDA63A` drift on a
  scrap, the family loose and joining in, a child showing a tidy shelf, with a Newsreader italic line beneath
  in green: `someone looking for drift, kindly, with the household relaxed and even helping`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "The same walk through the same rooms, twice. On the top, an
  inspection: someone looking for fault, and a family standing trial for the state of their house. On the
  bottom, a check-in: someone looking for drift, kindly, with the household relaxed and even helping. Nothing
  about the rooms changed. Everything about the feeling did, and that difference is the whole of this
  chapter."

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is that the identical act can feel like two opposite things, so draw
the two bands unmistakably the same walk, the same rooms, the same furniture, only the feeling different.
Keep the top band stern but never menacing: the inspection figure is severe and cold, not cruel, and the
family is anxious, not terrified; it is a tribunal, not a horror. Keep the bottom band warm and easy, the
check-in figure open and relaxed, the family joining in without a trace of fear. Hold the color discipline:
cool slate and a single spark-red pen for the inspection band, warm terracotta and green for the check-in
band, one honey accent held to the single jotted drift; the red pen is the only spark in the whole image.
Warm line-and-flat-color style, the register of a before-and-after that turns entirely on where the attention
is pointed, at the people or at the home.

## 2. The Check-In, Not the Inspection (the four things, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's hero as one picture: the four small things that turn an inspection into a
check-in, each defusing a different part of the dread. Hold all four and a frightening word becomes an easy,
welcome habit of care; miss any one and the audit tips back toward inspection. The value is in the four clean
items and the greyed-inspection-to-warm-check-in turn at the foot. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** Four clean, warm items on a calm panel, each with a small icon and a short label. First,
the home, not the people, a small house rather than a person. Second, light and quick, a little clock reading
a few minutes. Third, regular and expected, a calendar with a gentle repeating mark and no surprise. Fourth,
notices what works, not only what slipped, a small green tick beside a note. Across the foot, the two words
are contrasted: `INSPECTION` greyed and cold on the left, an arrow pointing right, `CHECK-IN` warm on the
right.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft: `THE CHECK-IN, NOT
  THE INSPECTION`.
- Four clean cards in a row (or a two-by-two block), each with a small slate icon and a Fraunces label:
  - `THE HOME, NOT THE PEOPLE`, a small slate house, italic note `so no one is on trial`.
  - `LIGHT AND QUICK`, a little clock reading a few minutes, italic note `so it never becomes a burden`.
  - `REGULAR AND EXPECTED`, a calendar with a gentle repeating mark, italic note `so it carries no ambush`.
  - `NOTICES WHAT WORKS, NOT ONLY WHAT SLIPPED`, a small green `#6E8B5B` tick beside a note, italic note `so
    people leave encouraged`.
- Across the foot, `INSPECTION` in greyed cold slate small caps on the left, a small slate arrow pointing
  right, `CHECK-IN` in warm terracotta `#BC4B2A` small caps on the right.
- A caption strip, short rules either side, a Fraunces line in slate: `hold all four, and a frightening word
  becomes an easy, welcome habit`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "Four small things turn an inspection into a check-in. It looks
  at the home, not the people. It is light and quick. It is regular and expected, never a surprise. And it

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is that four small things carry the entire difference, so keep
the four items clean, warm, and equal in weight, each with one plain icon and a short label, none louder than
the others. Make the foot turn read clearly: INSPECTION greyed and cold, an arrow, CHECK-IN warm, so the eye
travels from the frightening word to the friendly one. Hold the color discipline: slate for the icons, the

## 4. Keep It About the Shelf (the no-blame move, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's crucial move as a picture: the whole practice lives or dies on keeping the
attention on the room and off the roommate, because the instant an audit assigns blame it wrecks itself two
ways. This diagram has no direct HTML figure twin; it renders the no-blame reframe as a calm side-by-side, so
the reader feels that blame is not merely unkind but self-defeating, and that blamelessness is the thing that
keeps the audit honest and therefore useful. A shelf that drifted is a neutral fact about a system, like a
low tyre, and you simply top it up.

**What it shows:** Two quiet panels, read left to right. The LEFT panel, blamed: the audit turned on the
people, the attention pointed at the roommate not the room, and the two ways it wrecks itself, a household
gone defensive and stopped being honest about drift, and a home tidied into a show of order before audit day,
the mess hidden rather than fixed. The RIGHT panel, kept about the shelf: the same drifted shelf treated as a
neutral fact about a system, like a low tyre, a small pair of warm hands simply topping it up, calm and
unhurried, the household relaxed. A gentle arrow crosses from blamed to kept-about-the-shelf. Beneath, a
small strip carries the rule, and to one side a dim, unlit panel names Family Problem Solving of Chapter 28 as
next, the shared way to solve the bigger recurring problems the audit keeps turning up.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft, `KEEP IT ABOUT THE SHELF, NOT THE SHELF-SLIPPER`, with a Fraunces ink line
  beneath carrying the chapter's spine: "An audit checks the home, not the household."
- Two cream `#FBF7EF` panels with rule `#E2D8C4` borders, joined by a gentle slate arrow:
  - `BLAMED` (left, cool and closed): the attention pointed at the roommate, two muted notes, `the household
    goes defensive, and stops being honest about drift` and `the mess is hidden before audit day, not
    fixed`, a single small spark `#CB4B36` mark only on the closed door of hiding.
  - `KEPT ABOUT THE SHELF` (right, warm and open): the drifted shelf as a neutral fact, a small low-tyre
    icon beside it, a small pair of warm hands topping it up, a small green sparkle, a note `a neutral fact
    about a system, simply topped up`.
- Beneath, a small slate-bordered strip carrying the frozen rule in ink: "An audit checks the home, not the
  household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame."
- To the right or below, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `FAMILY PROBLEM SOLVING · NEXT` in
  muted honey sans caps, a muted italic note beneath, `a shared way to solve the bigger, recurring problems
  the audit keeps turning up, together, instead of one person forever patching them alone`, held quiet and
  clearly one step ahead, not yet reached.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "The blamelessness is not a nicety; it is the thing that keeps the audit
  honest and therefore useful."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the chapter's crucial move, so keep it calm and plain, never alarming;
the point is not that anyone is bad but that blame is self-defeating, because a blamed household hides its
drift and the audit loses the very information it exists to gather. Draw the blamed panel closed and cool,
the kept-about-the-shelf panel warm and open, the drifted shelf met without strain, a low tyre simply topped
up. Hold the color discipline: slate for the arrow and the sub-strip, green for the topping-up sparkle, one
muted honey for the dim next-chapter panel, a single small spark-red held to the hidden door only; no cage,
no one standing over a shoulder, no scolding. Keep the next-chapter panel dim, unlit, and clearly one step
ahead; you may name Family Problem Solving of Chapter 28 as next, but teach nothing of it. The register is
warmth and respect, not worry: a drifted shelf is just a low tyre, and keeping it about the shelf is simply
what keeps the audit kind and useful. Warm line-and-flat-color style.
