## Identity strings (frozen; copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)
- **Title:** 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections
- **Subtitle:** The word audit makes everyone flinch, because it sounds like a test with an inspector and a grade. A home audit is the opposite: a quick, regular, friendly look at the house, not the people, to see what is still working and catch small drift before it grows. It checks the home, never the household, so a drift it finds is a system to fix, not a person to blame.
- **Masthead:** "Part Eight · Sustain" / "Chapter Twenty-Seven"
- **The rule (frozen):** An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame.
- **The four things (frozen):** What turns an inspection into a check-in: it looks at the home, not the people; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped.
- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up: what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small. It checks the home, not the people, and it is a health check, not a test.
- **The inspection-versus-check-in reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift.
- **The no-blame reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected. Keep it about the shelf. A shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right.
- **The catch-it-early reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The whole value of a regular check-in is timing. A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. You do not audit the home to judge it. You audit it so that it never again has to be rescued.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** the check-in, not the inspection / the home, not the household / catch it while it is small. Chapter 26 gave the mindset; Chapter 27 gives the first mechanism, a friendly audit.

A quick, regular, warm look at the home (never the people) that catches drift while it is still small and notices what is still working. A health check for the house, not a report card for the family. Four things turn an inspection into a check-in: home not people, light and quick, regular and expected, notices what works. ## Motifs
- **The word makes people flinch.** "Audit" carries the baggage of inspection; the chapter removes it.
- **Home, not household.** The audit looks at the room, never at the roommate; no blame.
- **Same walk, opposite feeling.** Inspection looks for fault; a check-in looks for drift.
- **Catch it small.** A drift caught early is a two-minute fix; left to grow it is an overhaul.
- **Light and regular.** A short weekly check beats a long yearly reckoning, and only the short one keeps happening.
- **A checked home minds itself.** The first piece of keeping-machinery; the needle takes its first Sustain step.

## Emotional purpose
Warm, kind, and de-frightening. The whole chapter exists to take the fear out of a scary word and make a regular check-in feel like affectionate care for the home, with nobody on trial. It must never scold or imply the reader's family is being judged; finding drift is a success, not a failure. The register is safe, light, and generous, and it explicitly protects the household from the audit ever becoming surveillance or a performance review. No shame, no policing, no force.
