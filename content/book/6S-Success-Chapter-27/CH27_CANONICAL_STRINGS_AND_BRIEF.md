## Hero device (frozen): the friendly audit (a check-in, not an inspection)
A quick, regular, warm look at the home (never the people) that catches drift while it is still small and notices what is still working. It is a health check for the house, not a report card for the family. Four things turn an inspection into a check-in: it looks at the home, not the household; it is light and quick; it is regular and expected, never a surprise; and it notices what is working, not only what has slipped. ## Definition box (frozen)
> A 6S audit at home is a quick, regular, friendly walk through the house to see how the gains are holding up: what is still working, and where small drift has crept in, so you can fix it while it is small. It checks the home, not the people, and it is a health check, not a test.

## The inspection-versus-check-in reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> An inspection is something done to people, to catch them out and grade them. A check-in is something done for a home, to see how it is holding up and help it early. It can be the same walk through the same rooms, and it will feel like the opposite thing, because one is looking for fault and the other is only looking for drift.

## The no-blame reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> The moment an audit becomes about who let the shelf slip, it dies, because nobody volunteers to be inspected. Keep it about the shelf. A shelf that drifted is not a failure of anyone's character; it is a small system that needs a small adjustment, and the audit's only job is to find it kindly and set it right.

## The catch-it-early reframe (frozen, pull-quote)
> The whole value of a regular check-in is timing. A drift caught this week is a two-minute fix; the same drift caught in six months is a lost weekend. You do not audit the home to judge it. You audit it so that it never again has to be rescued.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> Keep the gains with a light, regular check-in that looks at the home and never at the people. A drift caught small is a small fix; a drift left to grow is an overhaul. Audit the house kindly and often, and it will never again need rescuing.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Do one two-minute audit right now. Walk a single room and ask, of the room and not of anyone in it, "what has drifted since we set this up?" Fix the first small thing you find. That is a whole audit, and it was not frightening at all.
- **Family Challenge:** Let each person check their own zone rather than one person checking everyone's. A self-audit you own beats an inspection you resent, and people turn out to be honest about their own drift when nobody is grading them.
- **6S Tip:** Keep it short and regular. A five-minute check every week catches far more than a two-hour reckoning once a year, and only the short one actually keeps happening. You can reuse the simple snapshot from your baseline audit; you do not need a new instrument.
- **Common Mistake:** Turning the audit into a performance review of the people, with scores held over someone's head. The instant it feels like judgment, everyone starts hiding the mess before the audit instead of fixing it, and you have taught the household to fake a clean home for inspection day. Check the home, kindly, and never the person.

## Voice and anti-shame guardrails
- Warm, plain, concrete, quietly confident, and above all KIND, because this chapter's whole job is to remove fear from a frightening word.
