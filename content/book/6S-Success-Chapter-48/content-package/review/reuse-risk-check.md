# Chapter 48 Reuse Risk Check: The Hall Closet

## Source provenance
Every zone, its purpose, its passes, its hard call, its watch-for items, and its shine detail are folded from the Micro Zone Manual's Hall Closet entry, which is the project's own material. The five calls are the Manual's `the_call` entries. The room's intro and its three tips supplied the layer: the space nobody owns, the depth that cannot be sorted in place, the damp of a closed dark cupboard, and the instruction to ask which room each thing actually serves. Nothing here is drawn from an outside author, method, or publication.

## Third-party IP
- No quotations from any external book, article, or speaker.
- No named methodology other than 6S itself and the Toyota lineage the book already establishes.
- No brand names, product names, model numbers, or retailer names anywhere in the package.
- No logos, no QR codes, no readable label text called for in any image prompt.

## Claims that need to stay defensible
1. A closed, unventilated cupboard holds moisture, and stored linen and paper take on damp there. Stated qualitatively, as the reason for the waiting step.
2. Chlorine bleach and ammonia products release a hazardous gas if they combine. Stated as a storage-separation rule, with no chemistry beyond naming the outcome, consistent with Chapters 43, 45, 46, and 47.
3. A leaked alkaline cell deposits a crust and should not be handled casually. Stated as a flag-it observation.
4. A swallowed button cell is a serious hazard to a small child. Stated once, as the reason for storing nothing at floor level, with no treatment or first-aid content attached.
5. Loaded shelf brackets need fixing into studs rather than plasterboard alone. Stated as the reason for a check.
6. A tall stack of folded cotton can come down as one mass when the bottom item is pulled. Stated as the reason for a height limit.

No safety statistic was invented, and no claim is quantified.

## Liability posture
- **No medical content.** Medication is mentioned once, in the kit, as belonging in a lockable medicine box. It is never named, never dosed, never handled, and the b-roll notes forbid filming any medication close enough to read. The button cell hazard is a storage rule with no first-aid instruction.
- **No repair instruction.** The broken vacuum gets a dated decision and then recycling. The chapter never describes fixing a hose, a belt, or a brush roll.
- No electrical work. A cord is inspected for cracked insulation near the plug and flagged, never opened.
- No mixing, no decanting, no unlabelled containers, and no disposal advice beyond following the instructions printed on a product's own label.

## A note on the appendix
**This is the first Part 9 chapter that required no new product types.** The library stands unchanged at 119. One mapping fix was made instead: nitrile gloves already existed but were not mapped to `Hall Closet||Cleaning Supply Zone` despite that zone's `products_used` naming disposable gloves.

Recorded here because a reviewer comparing chapters might otherwise read the absence of additions as an oversight rather than a milestone. It means the additions made across Chapters 43 to 47 have caught up with the Manual's vocabulary.

Scissors, named in Zone 2's `products_used` for cutting hair from a beater bar, were deliberately left as an ordinary noun under the type-versus-noun line set in Chapter 45.

## Cross-chapter reuse
- Uses and credits by reference: Chapters 8 to 11, 14, 24, 25, and 31. None re-taught.
- **Kept distinct from Chapter 47, The Mudroom**, which is the nearest neighbour and the other room that accumulates homeless objects. The distinction is explicit in the layer: the mudroom catches things passing through, the hall closet absorbs things with nowhere to live, and its output is redistribution rather than interception.
- Does not borrow mudroom (Ch 47) or stair landing (Ch 49) material.
- The signature carries a variation documented in `chapter_48_signature.md`: a third photograph, and the only self-verifying image in the book.

## Verdict
Low risk. Own source material throughout, no external IP, no invented statistics, and the two traps available in a chapter that mentions medication and button cells are both closed by keeping every reference to a storage rule rather than a treatment.
