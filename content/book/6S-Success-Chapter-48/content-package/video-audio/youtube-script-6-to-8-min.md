# Chapter 48 YouTube Script: The Hall Closet

*Six to eight minutes.*

---

**[Cold open, on a closed closet door]**

Every space in your house belongs to somebody. Except this one. And that is why it looks like this.

**[Open the door on a loaded shelf. Title.]**

**[Section 1: the mechanism]**

A bedroom belongs to whoever sleeps in it. A workshop belongs to whoever builds there. Ownership is quiet, but it is real, and it does one specific thing: it makes a space push back when the wrong thing gets put in it.

The hall closet has no owner. And an unowned space accepts anything.

Which means your closet is not full because you have too much. It is full because nothing in it was ever going to object. Everything in the house that had to go somewhere, and had nowhere obvious to go, ended up behind that door.

**[Section 2: depth]**

There is a second problem, and it is physical. That closet is deeper than your arm.

A space deeper than your arm conceals its own contents. Well enough that you would describe what is in there confidently, right now, and be wrong.

The front eighteen inches is the part you have actually used since you moved in. Everything behind it is a rumour.

**[Section 3: the instruction]**

So here is the blunt part. You cannot sort this room in place.

Empty a shelf out onto the hallway floor, and keep going until you can see the back wall. Sorting in place means judging only what you can already see, and what you can already see was never the problem.

And yes, your hallway gets worse before it gets better. That is normal. A closet this deep gets sorted outside itself or not at all.

**[Section 4: the walking]**

Now the thing to expect once you start pulling. Most of what comes out serves another room entirely. The picture hooks belong with the tools. The spare charger belongs at the desk.

None of it arrived because this was the right home. It arrived because it had no home, and this was the nearest door.

So this is the only room in the book whose main output is not a pile going out of the house, but a series of small journeys back into it. Keep a basket beside you.

**[Section 5: two rules]**

Two practical rules. First, this is the least ventilated storage in the house, so wipe the boards and then wait. Let them dry completely before any linen goes back, because dry cotton on a damp board is how a whole stack takes on the smell.

Second, bleach and ammonia go on separate shelves. Not because you would mix them deliberately, but because one develops a weeping cap and a closed cupboard does the mixing for you.

**[Close]**

And then the proof. This room asks for a third photograph, and it is the only one it accepts: the back wall of the deepest shelf, visible.

A tidy front row can be produced in ten minutes and tells you nothing. A visible back wall cannot be faked, because the only way to photograph it is to have genuinely emptied the shelf.

Do one zone this week, and make it the shelf you cannot see the back of. Chapter 48. Next: the stair landing.
