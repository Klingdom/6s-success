# Chapter 48 Key Takeaways: The Hall Closet

- Every other space in this book belongs to a person or an activity. The hall closet belongs to nobody, and an unowned space accepts anything.
- It is not full because you have too much. It is full because nothing in it was ever going to object.
- A closet deeper than your arm conceals its own contents. The front eighteen inches is what you have used since you moved in; everything behind it is a rumour.
- So you cannot sort this room in place. Empty a shelf onto the hallway floor and keep going until you can see the back wall.
- Sorting in place means judging only what you can already see, and what you can already see was never the problem.
- This is the least ventilated storage in the house, so the cleaning rule has a waiting step: wipe the boards, then let them dry completely before linen or paper returns.
- Dry cotton on a damp board is how a whole stack takes on the smell. Leave the door open for an hour when you finish.
- Most of what comes out serves another room. This is the only chapter whose main output is journeys back into the house rather than a pile leaving it.
- Store nothing at floor level, so that anything on that floor is immediately obviously wrong. A dropped button cell is silent, it rolls, and a hallway floor is at a toddler's eye level.
- Bleach and ammonia are separated not because you would mix them but because a weeping cap in a closed cupboard does the mixing for you.
- The proof this room accepts is one photograph: the back wall of the deepest shelf, visible. A tidy front row can be produced in ten minutes and tells you nothing.
