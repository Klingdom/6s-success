# Chapter 48 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Hall Closet

**Subject:** The only storage in your house that nobody owns

Every space in this book so far has belonged to somebody. A bedroom belongs to whoever sleeps in it. A workshop belongs to whoever builds there. Ownership is quiet, but it is real, and it is what makes a space push back when the wrong thing gets put in it.

The hall closet is the exception. It belongs to nobody. And an unowned space accepts anything.

That single mechanism explains the state of your hall closet better than any amount of talk about clutter. Everything in the house that had to go somewhere, and had nowhere obvious to go, ended up behind that door. Not because it belonged there. Because nothing there was going to object.

There is a second problem, and it is physical. This closet is deeper than your arm, so it conceals its own contents well enough that you will confidently describe what is in there and be wrong. The front eighteen inches is what you have used since you moved in. Everything behind that is a rumour.

So the instruction is blunt: **you cannot sort this room in place.** Empty a shelf onto the hallway floor and keep going until you can see the back wall, because sorting in place means judging only the things you can already see, and the things you can already see were never the problem.

Two more rules. This is the least ventilated storage in the house, so wipe the boards and then wait: let them dry completely before any linen returns. And expect to walk. Most of what comes out serves another room, which makes this the only chapter whose main output is journeys back into the house rather than a pile leaving it.

Do one zone this week, and make it the shelf you cannot see the back of.
