# The Hall Closet: The Only Storage Nobody Owns

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 48 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

Every space in this book so far has belonged to somebody. A bedroom belongs to the person who sleeps in it. A workshop belongs to whoever builds there. Even the mudroom belongs to the household in a way everyone understands. Ownership is quiet, but it is what makes a space push back when the wrong thing gets put in it.

> The hall closet is the darkest and deepest storage in the house, and the only one nobody owns. Every other space in this book belongs to a person or to an activity: this is somebody's wardrobe, that is where the cooking happens. This belongs to nobody, and an unowned space accepts anything, which is exactly how everything that had to go somewhere ended up behind this door, stacked to the depth of your arm.

## Depth lies

> A closet deeper than your arm conceals its own contents, and it does it well enough that you will confidently describe what is in there and be wrong. The front eighteen inches is the part you have used since you moved in. Everything behind that is a rumour.

## So you cannot sort it in place

> Empty one shelf out onto the hallway floor before you touch anything else, and keep going until you can see the back wall. Sorting in place means judging only the things you can already see, and the things you can already see were never the problem.

## And most of it is simply homeless

> Ask of each thing in here which room it actually serves, and the honest answer for most of it is somewhere else. None of it arrived because this was the right home: it arrived because it had no home, and this was the nearest door.
