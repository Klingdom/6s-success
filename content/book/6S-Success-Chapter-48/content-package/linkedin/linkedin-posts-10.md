# Chapter 48 LinkedIn Posts: The Hall Closet

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The only space nobody owns
Every room in your house belongs to somebody. A bedroom belongs to whoever sleeps in it. A workshop belongs to whoever builds there. Ownership is quiet, but it is real, and it is what makes a space push back when the wrong thing gets put in it.

The hall closet is the exception. It belongs to nobody. And an unowned space accepts anything.

## 2. Not too much, just unowned
Your hall closet is not full because you own too much. It is full because nothing in it was ever going to object. Everything in the house that had to go somewhere, and had nowhere obvious to go, ended up behind that door.

## 3. The front eighteen inches
A closet deeper than your arm conceals its own contents, and it does it well enough that you will confidently describe what is in there and be wrong. The front eighteen inches is the part you have actually used since you moved in. Everything behind that is a rumour.

## 4. You cannot sort it in place
So the instruction is blunt and slightly inconvenient: empty a shelf onto the hallway floor, and keep going until you can see the back wall. Sorting in place means judging only the things you can already see, and the things you can already see were never the problem.

## 5. The output is journeys, not a pile
Most rooms in this book end with a pile leaving the house. This one ends with a series of small journeys back into it. The picture hooks belong with the tools. The spare charger belongs at the desk. None of it arrived because this was the right home. It arrived because it had no home.

## 6. Wipe, then wait
This is the least ventilated storage in the house: no window, a door that stays shut, usually the deepest point of the hallway. So the cleaning rule has a waiting step built in. Wipe the boards, then let them dry completely before any linen goes back, because dry cotton on a damp board is how a whole stack takes on the smell.

## 7. Why bleach and ammonia get separate shelves
It is not that you would ever mix them deliberately. It is that they sit side by side, one develops a weeping cap, and the leak does the mixing for you inside a closed cupboard where nobody is standing. Separate shelves costs nothing and removes the whole scenario.

## 8. Store nothing on the floor
A hall closet has a floor nobody looks at, in a hallway, at exactly the height a small child explores. A dropped button cell is silent, it rolls, and it is the one thing in there that can do harm quickly. Store nothing at floor level, so that anything on that floor is immediately obviously wrong.

## 9. A count, not a name
The shelf label reads "Queen, 3 sets", not "sheets". Anyone opening the door can then see the shelf is short before laundry day rather than at bedtime. That is the whole difference between a label and a control.

## 10. The only proof this room accepts
A tidy front row can be produced in ten minutes by anybody and tells you nothing. A visible back wall cannot be faked, because the only way to photograph it is to have genuinely emptied the shelf.
