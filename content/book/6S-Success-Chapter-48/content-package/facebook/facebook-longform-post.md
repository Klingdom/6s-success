# Chapter 48 Facebook Longform Post: The Hall Closet

---

Here is why your hall closet is like that, and it is not the reason you think.

Every other space in your house belongs to somebody. The bedroom belongs to whoever sleeps in it. The kitchen belongs to whoever cooks. Ownership is quiet, but it is real, and it is what makes a space push back when the wrong thing gets put in it.

The hall closet belongs to nobody.

And an unowned space accepts anything. That is the whole mechanism. Everything in your house that had to go somewhere, and had nowhere obvious to go, ended up behind that door. Not because it belonged there. Because nothing in there was ever going to object.

So it is not full because you own too much. It is full because it never said no.

There is a second thing working against you, and it is physical. That closet is deeper than your arm, which means it hides its own contents well enough that you would confidently describe what is in there and be wrong. The front eighteen inches is the part you have actually used since you moved in. Everything behind that is a rumour.

Which leads to the instruction that opens this chapter, and it is blunt: you cannot sort this room in place.

You have to empty a shelf out onto the hallway floor and keep going until you can see the back wall. Sorting in place means judging only the things you can already see, and the things you can already see were never the problem.

Yes, your hallway gets worse before it gets better. That is the method working.

One more thing to expect. Most of what comes out of that closet turns out to serve another room entirely. The picture hooks belong with the tools. The spare charger belongs at the desk. The birthday candles belong in the kitchen. So this is the only room where the main output is not a pile going out of the house, but a series of small journeys back into it. Keep a laundry basket beside you.

And one practical rule, because this closet holds damp better than anywhere else in the house: wipe the shelf boards, then wait. Let them dry completely before any linen or paper goes back, because dry cotton on a damp board is how a whole stack takes on the smell.
