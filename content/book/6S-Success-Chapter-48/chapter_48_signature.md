# Chapter 48 Signature: The Hall Closet

The Part 9 recurring close. The friction meter is retired (it reached GOAL at Chapter 30); each room playbook ends instead on a matched before-and-after room signature: the before kept per Chapter 6, the after held as the ideal-state standard per Chapter 25.

## The before-and-after room signature
Open the door and shoot straight in from the hallway, with the light on. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and do not straighten the front row first, because the front row looking reasonable while the back is unknown is the exact condition this chapter exists to fix. Then work the five zones and take the after from the same spot.

## The variation: a third photograph, and it is the only proof this room accepts
This is **the only chapter in Part 9 whose signature calls for three frames rather than two.** The third is the back wall of the deepest shelf, visible.

The reason is specific to this room. Everywhere else in the book, a before-and-after pair is sufficient evidence, because the change is visible from the doorway. Here it is not: a tidy front row can be produced in ten minutes by anybody, it photographs beautifully, and it tells you nothing at all, because the front row was never the problem.

A visible back wall cannot be faked. The only way to take that photograph is to have genuinely emptied the shelf, judged what came off it, and put back less than you took down. It is the one image in this book that is self-verifying.

Pin it inside the door. When the closet starts filling from the back again, that photograph is what you compare against.

Compare with the variations used so far: Ch 39 the child takes the after photo; Ch 40 the after is the nightly safe state; Ch 43 the after contains one detail that expires; Ch 44 the after is taped inside a drawer and matched nightly; Ch 45 the after keeps working as a diagnostic floor; Ch 46 the after is judged on time-to-first-cut; Ch 47 the after is judged on the floor strip. This one adds a frame rather than changing the criterion.

## The before-and-after caption (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> This is the hall closet the whole loop was for: the same closet you started with, three sets per bed nested in their own pillowcases with the count on the shelf edge, the vacuum inside its outline and every handle hanging clear of the floor, one caddy per room with the labels all readable, a minimum and a maximum on the paper bin, and the seasonal bins above each carrying the month they were last closed. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard. Then take the one photograph this room really needs, which is the back wall of the deepest shelf, visible.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> A hall closet is not full because you have too much. It is full because nobody owns it, and an unowned space accepts anything. So the fix is ownership: every shelf gets a job, every bin gets a label and a date, and everything that turned out to serve another room gets walked back to it. Do one zone this week, and make it the shelf you cannot see the back of, because that is the one the closet has been lying to you about.
