## Hero device (frozen): the working method (effective and easy)
For each target, the method that is BOTH effective (it actually cleans the surface and lets you read it) AND easy (quick enough that you will do it again without dreading it). A method that works on paper but never gets done does not work; a method that gets done but leaves the target dirty does not work either. The working method is the small overlap of the two.
> **The rule (frozen):** The best method is the one you will actually do.

## The five working methods (frozen taught set)
1. **Right tool for the surface.** Match the method to the material. The right cloth or brush beats effort and beats a shelf of products.
2. **Top to bottom, dry before wet.** Work in an order that never makes you redo a spot: dust and crumbs fall, so do the high, dry work first, then the low, wet work.
3. **Fewer, simpler products.** Most homes need a small handful, not a cupboard. Simpler is faster, safer, and you actually restock it.
4. **Keep the tool where the target is.** Supplies at the point of use, a cloth by the sink, so starting a clean takes no effort at all.
5. **Clean in passing.** Fold small cleans into moments you are already there, so a target rarely needs a big separate session.

## Definition box (frozen)
> A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target: effective enough that the surface actually comes clean and shows what it needs, and easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it.

## The reframe (frozen, distinct from Ch 16 "clean to see" and Ch 17 "a name on every target")
> You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> The best method is the one you will actually do. Match a quick, effective method to each target, keep it small enough to fold into an ordinary day, and Shine stops being a dreaded event and becomes a handful of easy passes that keep the whole home clean and read.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Take the one cleaning job you dread most and find a faster method for it: a better tool, fewer products, a different order. Do it once the new way and time it. Dread usually turns out to be a method problem.
- **Family Challenge:** Have each person show the others the quickest way they have found to clean their assigned target, and steal each other's best methods. The house gets a shared toolkit and nobody has to work it out alone.
- **6S Tip:** Keep the tool where the target is. A cloth kept by the sink gets used; a cloth kept in a cupboard down the hall does not. The easiest method is the one whose tools are already in reach.
- **Common Mistake:** Saving it all up for one big deep-clean, the whole-Saturday method, which is so large you keep putting it off. Small, frequent, easy passes beat a rare heroic scrub, because the small easy thing actually happens.
