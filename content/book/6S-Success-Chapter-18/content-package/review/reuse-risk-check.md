# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 18 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line or image hitting the same follower until it feels canned. This check flags where that can happen.

## High-frequency elements
| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The best method is the one you will actually do | Canonical, web, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics, slides (37 files) | A follower on several platforms sees the hero rule repeatedly | Calendar staggers it; vary the phrasing around it per platform |
| You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, FB, quote card, graphics (36 files) | Medium to High | The anti-shame reframe; the most shareable turn. Use across formats, not stacked on one day. |
| A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target... | Canonical, LinkedIn, X, quote card (28 files) | Medium | The woven definition; pair with a different worked example each time. |
| Small, frequent, easy passes beat a rare heroic scrub... | LinkedIn, X, quote card, graphics (16 files) | Medium | The small-and-often payoff; vary which concrete pass (shower wall / counter / sink) you lead with. |
| The friction-meter caption ("The needle follows the dashed arrow...") | Canonical, graphics, HTML (10 files) | Low to Medium | Long; it is the honest-MOVE beat, specific to this chapter. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording ("Read it free online", "Read the free chapter in the online book", "the online book") | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings; most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision
The calendar avoids two assets sharing a hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push one back a day so "You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem." does not land twice in one day.

## Cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same methods content. Post only one to LinkedIn (use the copy-ready slides file; keep the outline as planning).
- The LinkedIn newsletter and the email newsletter share structure; do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- Web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 18)
- Chapter 18 is the THIRD Shine chapter and the HOW chapter. Keep it distinct from Chapters 16 and 17 behind it: Chapter 16 reframed cleaning as inspection (why); Chapter 17 built the list and shared it (what and who); Chapter 18 gives each target a working method (how). Assets must not re-argue why we clean (Chapter 16, a callback only) or rebuild the Shine list / reassign owners (Chapter 17). Grep confirms the methods are applied to the list that already exists, not a new one.
- Do not let methods slide FORWARD into the next chapter. Ch 18 assets must not build the repeatable routine, the reset, or a schedule/cadence (that is Chapter 19). This is a CRITICAL fence and it is held: "routine," "reset," "schedule," and "rhythm" appear in reader-facing copy only inside named fences or as the Chapter 19 handoff. In particular, "clean in passing" is a method for a SINGLE target ("keeps a target from ever needing a big session"), NOT the whole-home routine; assets must not present it as the Chapter 19 reset.
- Do NOT drift into Standardize or Sustain: a working method is a practical technique, not a documented standard or a maintenance-over-time system (later S's, a critical fence, held).
- Do NOT re-open Sort or Straighten.
- The friction-meter MOVE is specific to this chapter (a genuine next Shine step to (73,153.5), following the dashed projection, with Chapter 16/17's (75,153) now the dashed ghost behind it and a SHINE · STEP 2 marker, NO holds/planning marker). Do not reuse Chapter 17's planning-HOLD framing or imply the needle held here; and keep the goal honestly ahead (Shine completes in Chapter 19; Safety, Standardize, and Sustain still follow).

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter is a draft authored in the book voice, grounded in the 6S source ("Principles of 6S: Shine Step," the Steps to Complete Shine step 2, "determine cleaning methods"), not supplied verbatim source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft; re-sync if the chapter is revised.
- Quote cards are verbatim: 10 cards plus 13 alternates (23 quoted pull-lines), each verified byte-identical to a quoted line in `canonical/chapter-quotes.md`. Nothing borrowed from outside the book; no brand-name cleaning products are named, and the Seiso / Toyota origin is referenced lightly, home-scaled, not overclaimed.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording (highest-value, lowest-effort fix; several posts close on "Read it free online").
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical.
4. Keep Chapter 18 from blurring into its neighbors: distinct from Chapters 16 and 17 (why, then what-and-who) behind it, and distinct from Chapter 19 (the reset) ahead of it and the later S's (Standardize, Sustain). Chapter 18 is the HOW: a working method for each target.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
