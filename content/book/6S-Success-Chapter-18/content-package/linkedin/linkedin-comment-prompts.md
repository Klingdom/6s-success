# Chapter 18 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Cleaning Methods That Actually Work

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Anti-shame voice, relief and permission not lecture: a job you dread is a slow method, not a character flaw, and the fix is a quicker method, not more willpower.

---

1. Be honest with me here. What is the one cleaning job in your home you dread most? Not because you are lazy, but because, done the way you know how, it is slow and horrible. Name it. Dread almost always turns out to be a method problem, and naming the job is where the faster method starts.

2. You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. Think of a job you have quietly avoided for months. If you swapped one thing, a better tool, fewer products, a different order, how much of the dread do you think would drain out of it? What would you swap first?

3. The best method is the one you will actually do. So which do you keep in your home: the thorough deep clean you run once a year and then avoid, or the quick good-enough pass you actually run every week? Be honest about which one keeps your home clean, versus which one you tell yourself you should do.

4. Keep the tool where the target is. A cloth kept by the sink gets used; the same cloth kept in a cupboard down the hall does not. Where in your home have you moved a tool right to the spot it is used, and did the cleaning suddenly start happening on its own? Or where could you?

5. Small, frequent, easy passes beat a rare heroic scrub, because the small easy thing actually happens. Have you ever swapped a big dreaded session for a wipe folded into a moment you were already spending, the shower wall while the water runs, the counter while the pasta boils? What changed once you did?
