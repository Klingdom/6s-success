# Chapter 18 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Cleaning Methods That Actually Work

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, relief and permission not exhortation, no force metaphors, dread is a method problem not a character flaw.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** The reason you never clean the oven is not laziness. It is a slow method.

**Body:** A job you dread is a target that waits. Swipe for the third chapter of Shine, where each target gets a method quick enough to actually do and good enough to actually work.

---

## Slide 2: The gap the list leaves

**Headline:** Every target has a name now. It still is not a clean house.

**Body:** Between a named target and a cleaned one sits a small, stubborn gap, and the gap is the how. A job can be perfectly assigned to a perfectly willing person and still not happen.

---

## Slide 3: The oldest reason there is

**Headline:** Nobody skips the bathroom because they forgot whose job it was.

**Body:** They skip it because, done the way they know how, it takes forty minutes and a cupboard of sprays and leaves them on their knees. So it slides to next weekend, and the weekend after that.

---

## Slide 4: Dread beats ownership

**Headline:** The target is owned. It is also dreaded.

**Body:** And dread, quietly, beats ownership almost every time. The list does not fail because it was unfair. It fails because the jobs on it were miserable, and miserable jobs do not get done.

---

## Slide 5: A method has to be both

**Headline:** A method must be effective AND easy.

**Body:** Effective, so the target comes clean and you can read it. Easy, so you will genuinely do it again without a fight. Most cleaning advice only ever gives you one of the two.

---

## Slide 6: The two common failures

**Headline:** Easy but useless. Or effective but never repeated.

**Body:** The spray-and-wipe that smears the grease around gets done and does nothing. The whole-hour scrub works once and never again. Neither is a working method.

---

## Slide 7: The definition

**Headline:** The working method is the small overlap of the two.

**Body:** A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target: effective enough that the surface actually comes clean and shows what it needs, and easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it.

---

## Slide 8: The rule

**Headline:** The best method is the one you will actually do.

**Body:** Thorough is worth nothing if it does not happen. A merely good method you run every week beats a perfect method you run once a year and then avoid.

---

## Slide 9: The five working methods

**Headline:** Five methods lift almost any target toward quick-and-good.

**Body:** Right tool for the surface. Top to bottom, dry before wet. Fewer, simpler products. Keep the tool where the target is. Clean in passing. None is a trick or a special product.

---

## Slide 10: The reframe

**Headline:** You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem.

**Body:** The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time. The fix is rarely to try harder.

---

## Slide 11: Still reads the surface

**Headline:** A working method still leaves you having looked.

**Body:** Do not spray a surface and walk away. The point of Shine was never only a clean surface but a read one, so the best method cleans the target and shows you its condition in the same easy pass.

---
