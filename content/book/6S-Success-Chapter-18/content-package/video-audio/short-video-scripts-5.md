## SHORT 1: A list you still dread
**Topic: the gap between a named target and a cleaned one, and dread beating ownership. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You put a name on every cleaning job. Half of them still fill you with dread.

**Script:**
Last time you built the Shine list: every target in the home, grouped by kind, with a name on every line. The what and the who, done. But a list with names on it is still not a clean house, because between a named target and a cleaned one sits a small, stubborn gap, and the gap is the how. A job can be perfectly assigned to a perfectly willing person and still not happen, for the oldest reason there is: it is a pain to do. Several lines on your list already fill you with a small sinking feeling, the oven, the shower screen, the floors. The target is owned. It is also dreaded. And dread, quietly, beats ownership almost every time. The list does not fail because it was unfair. It fails because the jobs on it were miserable. So the missing piece is not more willpower. It is a better way to do each job.

## SHORT 2: A method has to be both effective and easy
**Topic: the hero device, the working method. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Most cleaning advice gives you half a method. Here is the other half.

**Script:**
A method that works has to do two things at once, and most cleaning advice only ever gives you one. It has to be effective, so the target actually comes clean and you can read its condition. And it has to be easy, so it is quick enough that you will genuinely do it again without a fight. Miss either half and it fails. Easy but not effective is the spray-and-wipe that smears the grease around; it gets done and it does nothing. Effective but not easy is the hour of scrubbing you will not repeat until next year; it works once and never again. The one you want sits in the small overlap of the two, clean enough to matter and easy enough to repeat. Which points at the single most useful rule there is: the best method is the one you will actually do. Thorough is worth nothing if it does not happen. The home is kept by what you actually do, not by what you know you should.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A method has to be both: effective AND easy."
- 14s: "Easy but not effective: gets done, does nothing."
- 26s: "Effective but not easy: works once, never repeated."
- 40s: "The best method is the one you will actually do."

## SHORT 3: The five working methods
**Topic: the taught kit of five methods. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Five methods that lift almost any cleaning job toward quick and good.

**Script:**
Here is a small kit of methods that move almost any target toward quick and good. None is a trick or a special product. One, right tool for the surface. Most struggle at the sink is really the wrong tool applied with extra effort; the right cloth or brush does in one easy pass what a paper towel and determination do in ten. Two, top to bottom, dry before wet. Order is free speed; dust and crumbs fall, so do the high, dry work first. Three, fewer, simpler products. A small handful covers almost everything, costs less, and is simple enough that you actually restock it. Four, keep the tool where the target is. A cloth kept by the sink gets used; the same cloth in a cupboard down the hall does not. Five, clean in passing. A wipe of the shower wall while the water still runs, the counter while the pasta boils. Folded into a moment you were spending anyway, a small clean costs almost nothing.

- 12s: "Right tool. Top to bottom, dry before wet."
- 26s: "Fewer, simpler products."
- 38s: "Keep the tool where the target is. Clean in passing."

## SHORT 4: You do not have a cleaning problem
**Topic: the reframe, dread as a method problem, anti-shame. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You are not lazy. You were just handed a slow method.

**Script:**
Here is something kinder than the story most people tell themselves. You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small. If you have spent years feeling vaguely bad about some corner of your home, let this land: you are not lazy and you are not failing. You have been handed slow methods, by habit, by how you were taught, by adverts selling effort and products. The moment the method gets quick, the avoidance stops on its own, without any change in your character. Nobody has to become a more disciplined person to keep a clean home. They just have to stop using the forty-minute method for a two-minute job.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "You do not have a cleaning problem."
- 12s: "You have a method problem."
- 26s: "You are not lazy. You were handed a slow method."
- 40s: "Stop using the forty-minute method for a two-minute job."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A dreaded oven drawn large and heavy, a person looking at it and feeling vaguely bad. Then the method beside it changes, fewer products, the right tool, left to sit, and the oven shrinks to something small and ordinary, the sinking feeling lifting off the unchanged person. **Topic: the honest friction-meter MOVE, paying off Chapter 17's dashed projection. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Last chapter the needle held still. This time it earns the move.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, because the arrow the last chapter drew has come true. At the end of the last chapter the needle held still, and a dashed arrow projected forward along the dial to show where it would move once the plan was actually carried out. This is that chapter. The targets got their methods and, doing them, they got cleaned, really cleaned, across the home and not just on one launch pad. So the needle follows the dashed arrow and steps again toward calm, and the step is honest, because this time the room's condition genuinely changed. Grease that was on the hob is gone. But it is a real step and not the finish of Shine, because a home cleaned once is not yet a home that stays clean. The goal is still ahead. The needle stepped today. One more step finishes Shine.
