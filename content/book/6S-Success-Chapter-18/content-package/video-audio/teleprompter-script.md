# Teleprompter Script: Cleaning Methods That Actually Work

**Chapter 18, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** cleaning-methods-that-actually-work
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Last time you built the Shine list.

Every target, grouped by kind.

A name against every line.

The what and the who, both settled.

But a list with names is still not a clean house.

Between a named target and a cleaned one sits a gap.

The gap is the how.

A job can be perfectly assigned to a willing person.

And still not happen.

For the oldest reason there is: it is a pain to do.

Nobody skips the bathroom because they forgot whose job it was.

They skip it because the way they know is slow and horrible.

Several lines on your list already fill you with dread.

The oven. The shower screen. The floors.

The target is owned.

It is also dreaded.

And dread, quietly, beats ownership almost every time.

So the missing piece is not more willpower.

It is a better way to do each job.

A method that works has to do two things at once.

It has to be effective.

The target actually comes clean, and you can read it.

And it has to be easy.

Quick enough that you will do it again without a fight.

Easy but not effective gets done and does nothing.

Effective but not easy works once and never again.

The one you want sits in the overlap of the two.

Here is the definition. Keep it.

A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a target.

Effective enough that the surface comes clean and shows what it needs.

Easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it.

And the rule underneath all of it.

The best method is the one you will actually do.

Thorough is worth nothing if it does not happen.

Now the five working methods.

One. Right tool for the surface.

The right cloth beats effort and beats a shelf of products.

Two. Top to bottom, dry before wet.

Order is free speed.

Do the high, dry work first, then the low, wet work.

Three. Fewer, simpler products.

A small handful covers almost everything.

Simpler is faster, and you actually restock it.

Four. Keep the tool where the target is.

A cloth kept by the sink gets used.

The same cloth in a cupboard down the hall does not.

Five. Clean in passing.

Wipe the shower wall while the water still runs.

The counter while the pasta boils.

Folded into a moment you were spending anyway.

Now the kindest part.

You do not have a cleaning problem.

You have a method problem.

The jobs you dread are not dreadful in themselves.

They are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool.

You are not lazy. You were handed a slow method.

The moment the method gets quick, the avoidance stops.

Without any change in your character.

Nobody has to become a more disciplined person.

They just stop using the forty-minute method for a two-minute job.

One caution, so the speed does not cost you the reading.

A working method still leaves you having looked.

Do not spray a surface and walk away.

The point of Shine was never only a clean surface.

It was a read one.

A method that puts your hand and eye on the target inspects by its nature.

So clean the target and read it in the same easy pass.

Now put it to the list you already have.

The launch pad: a cloth kept in the entry drawer.

Wiped and checked in ten seconds, in passing.

The dreaded oven: a gentler method left to sit.

Fewer products, far less effort, the same result.

The door handles: the cloth already in your hand.

Wiped in a second, read in the same second.

Nothing on the list changed.

Each target just got a method quick enough to do.

The same list, suddenly doable.

Now go look at the friction meter.

The arrow the last chapter drew has come true.

This time the targets actually got cleaned.

So the needle follows the dashed arrow and steps again.

Grease gone from the hob. A dreaded oven, a done oven.

The step is honest, because the room really changed.

But it is a real step, not the finish of Shine.

A home cleaned once is not yet a home that stays clean.

The goal is still ahead.

So here is your job.

Take the one job you dread most.

Find a faster method for it. A better tool. Fewer products.

Do it once the new way and time it.

Dread usually turns out to be a method problem.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

The needle stepped today.

One more step finishes Shine.
