## You have a list, every line has a name, and the house still is not clean.

You have a list now, and every line on it has a name. That is further than most homes ever get, and it is still not a clean house, because between a named target and a cleaned one sits a small, stubborn gap, and the gap is the how. A job can be perfectly assigned to a perfectly willing person and still not happen, for the oldest reason there is: it is a pain to do.

Nobody skips the bathroom because they forgot whose job it was. They skip it because, done the way they know how, it takes forty minutes and a cupboard of sprays and leaves them on their knees, so it quietly slides to next weekend. "The reason a job does not get done is usually not that you are lazy, or busy, or disorganized. It is that the method you are using is slow and horrible, and a slow horrible job is one any sane person postpones."

You have a Shine list, grouped by kind, with a name against every line, and if you are honest, several lines on it already fill you with a small sinking feeling: the oven that means an hour and a caustic spray, the shower screen that never really comes clear, the floors that mean moving furniture. ## What makes a method actually work: it must be both.

A method that works has to do two things at once, and most cleaning advice only ever gives you one of them. It has to be effective, so the target comes clean and you can read its condition, and it has to be easy, so you will genuinely do it again without a fight. Easy but not effective is the spray-and-wipe that smears the grease around; it gets done and it does nothing. Effective but not easy is the hour of scrubbing you will not repeat until next year; it works once and never again. The one you want sits in the small overlap of the two.

> A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target: effective enough that the surface actually comes clean and shows what it needs, and easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it.

Which points at the single most useful rule in this part of the book.

> The best method is the one you will actually do.

Thorough is worth nothing if it does not happen, because the home is kept by what you actually do, not by what you know you should.

## Five working methods that make almost any target quick.

None of these is a trick or a special product. They are the handful of habits that separate people for whom cleaning is quick from people for whom it is a slog, and any of them lifts almost any target toward that corner.

- **Right tool for the surface** · most struggle at the sink is really the wrong tool applied with extra effort; match the tool to the material and the muscle disappears
- **Top to bottom, dry before wet** · order is free speed; clean the high before the low and the dry before the wet, or you dirty what you just cleaned
- **Fewer, simpler products** · a small handful covers almost everything, costs less, is safer around children, and is simple enough that you actually restock it
- **Keep the tool where the target is** · a cloth by the bathroom sink gets used on the bathroom sink; storing the tool at the target removes the largest hidden cost of cleaning, the effort of starting
- **Clean in passing** · a quick wipe of the shower wall while the water is still running, the counter while the pasta boils, folded into a moment you were spending anyway

## You do not have a cleaning problem.

Stop on what these methods quietly imply, because it is kinder than the story most people tell themselves.

> You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small.

You are not lazy and you are not failing. You have been handed slow methods, by habit, by how you were taught, and slow methods make jobs that any reasonable person avoids. "The moment the method gets quick, the avoidance stops on its own, without any change in your character." Nobody has to become a more disciplined person to keep a clean home. They just have to stop using the forty-minute method for a two-minute job.

## A good method still reads the surface.

One caution, so the speed does not cost you the thing Shine was for in the first place. It is possible to get so good at fast that you clean without looking, and a target cleaned blind is a target you did not inspect.

> A working method still leaves you having looked. Do not spray a surface and walk away; the point of Shine was never only a clean surface but a read one, so the best method cleans the target and shows you its condition in the same easy pass.

Keeping the reading in costs no time; it is a matter of attention, not effort. As your cloth crosses the surface, let your eyes cross it too. The quick methods actually help here, because a method that puts your hand and eye right on the target inspects by its nature.
