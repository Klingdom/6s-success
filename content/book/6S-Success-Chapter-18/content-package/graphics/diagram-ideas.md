# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 18

**"Cleaning Methods That Actually Work"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_18_final.html`: one
kitchen hob drawn twice, the dreaded way beside the working way, with the Shine list from Chapter 17
pinned between them and one line circled; a two-axis diagram plotting effective against easy, with the
two common failures falling short and the working method ringed green in the corner that is both; a calm
reference card of the five working methods, one small icon and a short label each; and the friction
meter, which MOVES this chapter, the live needle following the dashed projection Chapter 17 drew and
stepping again toward calm.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`, cream `#FCF8F0`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the THIRD chapter of Part Five and Shine continues. Chapter 16 reframed
cleaning as inspection and made one worked first pass on the launch pad. Chapter 17 turned that single
pass into a plan for the whole home, grouped by four kinds of target with a name against every line, and
HELD the needle with a dashed forward projection. Chapter 18 does one narrow thing: it gives each target
on that list a working method, a way to clean it that is quick enough to actually do and good enough to
actually work, and, doing them, the targets actually get cleaned across the home. It owns Shine step 2,
the HOW. It does NOT re-argue why we clean (Chapter 16, done, one-line callback only), it does NOT
rebuild the Shine list or reassign owners (Chapter 17, done and closed; the methods apply to the list
that already exists), and it does NOT build the repeatable routine, the reset, or any schedule or cadence
(Chapter 19). "Clean in passing" here is a method for a single target, not the whole-home routine, which
is Chapter 19's job. It does NOT write documented standard methods or maintenance-over-time systems (the
later Standardize and Sustain S's); a working method is a practical technique, not a documented standard.
It does NOT re-open Sort or Straighten. Because the targets genuinely got cleaned this chapter, the
friction needle MOVES: it follows the dashed projection Chapter 17 drew and takes its next Shine step,
honoring the book's rule that the meter moves only when the room does. Separator discipline: labels use
the middot, for example `CHAPTER 18 · SHINE`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words.
Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: point-of-use, dry-before-wet, two-minute, forty-minute,
whole-Saturday, line-and-flat-color, Shine-complete, first-Shine-step, forward-projection, calm-dot,
friction-spark, right-tool, small-and-often. "Spotless" does not appear here; it is a negated non-goal at
most, never the target. Anti-shame throughout: a job you dread is a method problem, not a character flaw;
the reader is not lazy, their method is just slow, and the avoidance stops on its own the moment the
method gets quick. No force or violence framing anywhere; no attacking grime, no war on dirt, no blitz,
no blast, no purge; the whole move is finding a quicker, kinder way to do a job you already have. No hype
words. Avoid brand names and specific product endorsements; teach principles, not a shopping list.

## 1. Same Target, Two Methods (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the whole chapter in one image. The same job is dreadful or easy depending only on the
method, and the difference is the reason the list sits undone. One kitchen hob is cleaned two ways side
by side, and the Shine list from the last chapter is pinned between them with one line circled, to say
this chapter is about doing the list, not remaking it. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** One target, a kitchen hob, drawn twice, side by side. On the left, THE DREADED WAY: a
bucket crowded with about ten bottles, a pair of hands scrubbing the hob hard, a clock showing a long
time gone, a small red friction-spark of wasted effort over the scene. On the right, THE WORKING WAY: the
same hob done with one right tool, two simple bottles, an easy one-handed wipe, a clock showing about two
minutes, a green calm-dot marking it clean and read. Pinned to the wall between the two scenes is the
Shine list from Chapter 17, one line on it, the hob, circled.

- A warm dawn wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint Newsreader

- Left panel in cream `#FCF8F0` with a `#E2D8C4` outline, headed in a small warm-red `#CB4B36` sans caps
  `THE DREADED WAY`. Inside: a bucket crowded with about ten simple bottles in wood fill `#E7C58B` with
  an ink outline, labeled `ten products` in Newsreader italic; a small clock showing a long time, its
  minute hand in warm red, labeled `40 min`; the hob at the base with two hands pressing a cloth hard,

friction-spark once, small, on the left only, to mark wasted effort; keep the right panel quiet and warm,
its one green calm-dot the only bright mark. The pinned list is load-bearing: it says the plan already
exists and this chapter is about carrying it out, so draw it as a real household's sheet, a little askew,
with one honest circled line, not a fresh form. Do not remake the list and do not reassign anyone; the
list here is a callback to Chapter 17, not a new artifact. No products named by brand; the bottles are
plain. Warm line-and-flat-color style, the register of relief, not exhortation.

## 2. A Working Method Is Both (the hero, effective and easy)

**Purpose:** Make the hero device visual and carry the frozen rule. A method has to be both effective (it
actually cleans the surface and lets you read it) and easy (quick enough that you will do it again without
dreading it), and the working method is the small overlap of the two. The two common failures, only easy
or only effective, both fall short. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML, and is the chapter's hero image.

**What it shows:** A simple two-axis diagram. The horizontal axis runs from `hard` on the left to `easy`
on the right; the vertical axis runs from `does not clean` at the bottom to `actually cleans and reads`
at the top. Three methods are plotted. A whole-Saturday scrub sits high on cleans but far into hard,
top-left, labeled `works once, never repeated`. A spray-and-walk-away sits far into easy but low on
cleans, bottom-right, labeled `gets done, does nothing`. The working method sits up in the corner that is
both easy and effective, ringed with a green calm-dot, labeled `clean enough to matter, easy enough to
repeat`.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label in soft gray: `A WORKING METHOD IS
  BOTH`.
- The both-corner, top-right, gently washed with a soft green field at low opacity `#6E8B5B` to mark the
  useful zone without shouting.
- Two slate axes `#3C5A6B` with small arrowheads. Horizontal axis labels: `hard` in slate on the left,
  a muted `to`, `easy` in slate on the right, with a small direction arrow. Vertical axis labels: `does
  not clean` in slate at the bottom, a muted `to`, `actually cleans and reads` in slate at the top.
- Whole-Saturday scrub: a solid slate dot top-left, a sans caps label `whole-Saturday scrub`, and a
  Newsreader italic note beneath, `works once, never repeated`.
- Spray and walk away: a solid slate dot bottom-right, a sans caps label `spray and walk away`, and a
  Newsreader italic note beneath, `gets done, does nothing`.
- The working method: a green dot top-right ringed with a dashed green calm-dot circle, a slate sans caps
  label `the working method`, and a two-line green Newsreader italic note, `clean enough to matter, /
  easy enough to repeat`.
- Beneath the diagram, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen rule set in Fraunces: "The best method
  is the one you will actually do." A faint Newsreader italic footer, soft gray: "Miss either half and
  the method fails, just in different ways."
- Caption (from the chapter): "A working method has to be both. Easy but ineffective gets done and
  changes nothing; effective but hard works once and never comes back. The method that keeps a home clean
  sits in the corner that is quick and good at once."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the hero, so give it room and make the both-corner unmistakable at
a glance. Keep the two failures calm and equal, neither dramatized: the whole-Saturday scrub is not a
villain, it simply never repeats, and the spray-and-walk-away is not lazy, it simply does nothing, so
draw both as honest near-misses rather than warnings. The green calm-dot ring on the working method is
the one bright accent; hold the color discipline and use no spark red here at all. Keep the axes plain and
the plotted points few, three and no more; the whole idea is that the useful method sits where two plain
qualities overlap. This is a picture of a principle, not a chart of data, so keep it warm and legible, the
register of a good idea. Do not add a fourth axis, a score, or a product; teach the overlap, nothing more.

## 3. The Five Working Methods (the reusable kit)

**Purpose:** Give the reader a compact, reusable kit of the five methods so "find a working method"
becomes concrete and portable to any target on the list. A calm labeled reference anyone can hold at a
glance, or print and keep by the door. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML, and matches the planned companion
resource, the Working Methods card.

**What it shows:** A calm labeled card of the five working methods, each with one small warm hand-drawn
icon and a short label. Right tool for the surface (a cloth and a brush). Top to bottom, dry before wet (a
downward arrow over a shelf and a floor). Fewer, simpler products (two bottles, not ten). Keep the tool
where the target is (a cloth hanging by a sink). Clean in passing (a hand wiping a shower wall under
running water). Arranged as a single tidy card, slate headings, sans labels.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Inside, one rounded card in quiet fill `#F3EEE2` with a `#E2D8C4`
  outline. Centered at the top, a slate sans caps title `THE FIVE WORKING METHODS`, with a faint rule
  beneath it.

  accents with an ink outline, then a sans bold ink heading and a Newsreader italic helper line. Row
  one, `Right tool for the surface.` helper `Match the method to the material.` (a folded cloth beside a
  small brush). Row two, `Top to bottom, dry before wet.` helper `Do the high, dry work before the low,

  products.` helper `A small handful, not a cupboard.` (two simple capped bottles). Row four, `Keep the

## 4. You Do Not Have a Cleaning Problem (the reframe, no HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's kindest idea as a picture: the jobs you dread are almost never dreadful
in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is
rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small. This diagram has no
direct HTML twin; it renders the "You Do Not Have a Cleaning Problem" section as a paired-panel figure,
carrying the frozen reframe beneath it, and it is the anti-shame heart of the chapter.

**What it shows:** Two facing panels of the same one person facing the same one dreaded job, read two
ways. The left is THE STORY YOU TELL: the person on their knees at a job that will not end, a cupboard of
sprays beside them, a long clock overhead, and a small quiet caption that reads it as a character verdict,
`I am just bad at keeping the house`. The right is WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE: the same person, the same job,
but with the wrong tool set down and a right one picked up, the cupboard closed to two simple bottles, the
long clock swapped for a short one, and a caption that reads it as a method fact, `the method was slow, not
me`. The two panels are the same person and the same job, told as a fault and then told as a method, side
by side.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE STORY YOU TELL · WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE`, with a Fraunces ink line
  beneath carrying the frozen reframe's opening: "You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method
  problem."
- Left panel, calm slate header bar `THE STORY YOU TELL`: the person kneeling at the endless job in warm
  cream with an ink outline, a crowded cupboard of sprays beside them, a long clock above, a small muted
  Newsreader italic note, `slow way, wrong tool, wrong time`. A soft gray sans caps line at the base:
  `FEELS LIKE A CHARACTER FLAW`. Hold a single small warm-red note `#CB4B36` at the long clock, the one
  tell of wasted time, used once and nowhere else.
- Right panel, calm green header bar `WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE`: the same person, standing, the wrong tool
  set down and a right one in hand, the cupboard closed to two simple bottles, a short clock above, a
  green calm-dot, a slate note, `right tool, fewer products, better order`. A soft green sans caps line at
  the base: `IS ACTUALLY A METHOD PROBLEM`.
- Between the panels, a faint slate arrow from the left face to the right face labeled `same person, same
  job`, so the eye reads the right panel as the truer telling of the identical scene.
- Beneath the two panels, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen reframe in full: "You do not have a
  cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves;
  they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try
  harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "The moment the method gets quick, the avoidance stops on its own,
  without any change in your character."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is relief, not a scolding, and it touches a live nerve, so keep both
panels warm and free of blame; the left is not the reader failing, it is the reader believing a false
story about themselves. Keep the person and the job identical across the panels, the same person at the
same job, so the eye reads one scene told two ways, a fault and then a method. The only thing that changes
between the panels is the method and the story, not the person; that is the entire argument. Use the
single small warm-red note only once, on the long clock in the left panel, and keep everything else calm.
Do not draw anyone as lazy, ashamed, or scolded, and do not draw the right panel as a triumphant makeover;
it is the same ordinary person, simply told the truer thing. No force framing, no hero cleaning montage.
Warm, plain, and kind, the register of a weight lifting.
