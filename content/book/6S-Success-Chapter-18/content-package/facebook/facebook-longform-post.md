# Facebook Longform Post: The Jobs I Dreaded Were a Method Problem, Not a Me Problem

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 18 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the third chapter of Shine, the third S. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes. Body hard-capped 300 to 450 words.

---

Last year there were three cleaning jobs in my home that I simply never did. The oven. The shower screen. The floors that meant moving furniture. I knew whose job they were. I knew they needed doing. And every single time, they slid to next weekend, and the weekend after that.

For a long time I thought that was a me problem. That I was a bit lazy, or too busy, or just not a naturally tidy person. I felt vaguely bad about it, the way a lot of us carry a low background guilt about some corner of the house we never get to.

Then I read a line that put the whole thing down in a different place.

"You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small."

That landed hard. The oven was not dreadful. My method for the oven was dreadful: an hour on my knees with a caustic spray and three cloths. No wonder I avoided it. Any sane person would.

So I stopped trying to be more disciplined and started making the jobs smaller instead. The oven got a gentler product left to sit while I did something else, so the work happened without me scrubbing. The shower screen got a quick wipe while the water was still running, folded into a shower I was already taking. The right tool, fewer products, a better moment.

Here is the rule the whole chapter turns on.

"The best method is the one you will actually do."

A merely good method you run every week beats a perfect deep clean you do once a year and then avoid, because the home is kept by what you actually do, not by what you know you should.

Nothing about me changed. I did not become a tidier person. The jobs just got small enough that the dread drained out of them, and once a job is a two-minute thing you barely notice, it quietly starts getting done on its own.
