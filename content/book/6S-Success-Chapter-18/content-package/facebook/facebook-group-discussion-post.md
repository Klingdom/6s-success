# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the job you dread is a method problem, not a you problem) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, the small idea that changed how I feel about the jobs I never get around to, and then I want to hear the one cleaning job you have put off the longest.

For years I had a short list of jobs in my home that simply never happened. The oven. The shower screen. The floors that meant moving furniture. I knew they needed doing. I knew whose job they were. And every single time, they quietly slid to next weekend, and the weekend after that.

For a long time I thought that was a me problem. That I was a bit lazy, or too busy, or just not a naturally tidy person. Then I read one line that put the whole thing down in a different place.

"You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small."

That landed hard. The oven was not dreadful. My method for the oven was dreadful: an hour on my knees with a caustic spray and three cloths. No wonder I avoided it. Any sane person would.

So instead of trying to become more disciplined, I made the jobs smaller. The oven got a gentler product left to sit while I did something else. The shower screen got a quick wipe while the water was still running, folded into a shower I was taking anyway. The right tool, fewer products, a better moment. The dread drained straight out of them, because there was nothing left to dread.

The line I keep coming back to is this one. The best method is the one you will actually do.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the one cleaning job you have dreaded for years, the one that keeps sliding to next weekend? And has anyone here ever found a faster way to do a job you used to hate, a better tool, fewer products, a different order?

I will go first in the comments with the oven I did not properly clean for about two years. 😄

(If anyone wants this written out properly, it is from Chapter 18 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the most freeing reframe of cleaning I have come across.)
