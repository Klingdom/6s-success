### Slide 1 (hook)

**You have the list. You have the names. And half of it still is not getting done.**

Last chapter you wrote a Shine list and put a name on every line. That is further than most homes ever get. And it is still not a clean house, because between a named target and a cleaned one sits a small, stubborn gap, and the gap is the how.

Swipe for the fix: give each target a method quick enough that you will actually do it and good enough that it actually works.

### Slide 2

**A job can be perfectly assigned to a perfectly willing person and still not happen.**

Nobody skips the bathroom because they forgot whose job it was. They skip it because, done the way they know how, it takes forty minutes and a cupboard of sprays and leaves them on their knees. So it slides to next weekend, and the weekend after.

The reason a job does not get done is usually not that you are lazy, or busy, or disorganized. It is that the method is slow and horrible, and a slow horrible job is one any sane person postpones.

### Slide 3

**The target is owned. It is also dreaded. And dread, quietly, beats ownership almost every time.**

Several lines on your list already fill you with a small sinking feeling, because you know how they go: the oven that means an hour and a caustic spray, the shower screen that never really comes clear.

A plan that dies of dread dies just as dead as a plan with no names at all. The list does not fail because it was unfair. It fails because the jobs on it were miserable, and miserable jobs do not get done no matter whose name is next to them.

### Slide 4

**A method that works has to do two things at once. Most cleaning advice gives you one.**

It has to be **effective**: the target actually comes clean and you can read its condition afterward. And it has to be **easy**: quick and painless enough that you will genuinely do it again without a fight.

Easy but not effective is the spray-and-wipe that smears the grease around; it gets done and it does nothing. Effective but not easy is the hour of scrubbing you will not repeat until next year; it works once and never again. Neither is a working method.

[VISUAL] A simple two-axis diagram in slate #3C5A6B: horizontal from "hard" to "easy," vertical from "does not really clean" to "actually cleans and reads." Two failures plotted low or to the side; the working method glows up in the both-corner with a green calm-dot #6E8B5B.

### Slide 5

**The working method is the small overlap of the two.**

"A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target: effective enough that the surface actually comes clean and shows what it needs, and easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it."

Clean enough to matter, easy enough to repeat. Finding that overlap for each target is the whole skill of this chapter.

### Slide 6

**The best method is the one you will actually do.**

Read that against everything you have been told about cleaning, most of which is about the most thorough method, the deep clean, the proper way.

Thorough is worth nothing if it does not happen. A merely good method you run every week beats a perfect method you run once a year and then avoid, because the home is kept by what you actually do, not by what you know you should.

### Slide 7

**Five methods lift almost any target toward quick-and-good.**

**Right tool for the surface.** Most struggle at the sink is really the wrong tool applied with extra effort.
**Top to bottom, dry before wet.** Order is free speed; work the other way and you do half of it twice.
**Fewer, simpler products.** A small handful covers almost everything, costs less, and you actually restock it.
**Keep the tool where the target is.** A cloth kept by the sink gets used; the same cloth down the hall does not.
**Clean in passing.** Fold a small clean into a moment you were already spending.

### Slide 8

**You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem.**

"The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small."

If you have spent years feeling vaguely bad about some corner of your home, let this land. You are not lazy and you are not failing. You were handed slow methods, by habit and by how you were taught.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel #FBF7EF, the frozen reframe set large in Fraunces, slate #3C5A6B rules. Beneath, a dreaded job in friction spark #CB4B36 relabelled from "a hard job" to "a slow method," the weight lifting off the person and onto the method.

### Slide 9

**Nobody has to become a more disciplined person to keep a clean home.**

The moment the method gets quick, the avoidance stops on its own, without any change in your character. They just have to stop using the forty-minute method for a two-minute job.

Quick Win: "Take the one cleaning job you dread most and find a faster method for it: a better tool, fewer products, a different order. Do it once the new way and time it. Dread usually turns out to be a method problem."

### Slide 10

**A working method still leaves you having looked.**

Do not spray a surface and walk away. The point of Shine was never only a clean surface but a read one.

"So the best method cleans the target and shows you its condition in the same easy pass." A method that puts your hand and eye right on the target inspects by its nature; only the lazy shortcuts, the spray you never touch, clean without reading. Choose methods that bring you into contact, and the inspection comes free.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel #FBF7EF, the frozen inspection line set large in Fraunces, slate #3C5A6B rules. Beneath, a hand and an eye crossing a surface together in one green #6E8B5B pass, a spray-and-walk-away shortcut faded in friction spark #CB4B36 beside it.

### Slide 11

**Same list, same owners, nothing reassigned. Suddenly doable.**

Give the launch pad a cloth kept in the entry drawer, so wiping the shelf and checking the hooks is a ten-second thing you do in passing. Give a dreaded oven a gentler method left to sit while you do something else. Give the door handles the cloth already in your hand.

"A rare heroic scrub loses to a small easy pass every time, because the small easy pass actually happens." A dreaded oven is a done oven.
