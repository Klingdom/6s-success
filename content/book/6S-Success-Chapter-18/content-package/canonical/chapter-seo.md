## Search intent
Turning a shared cleaning plan into a home that actually gets cleaned. Readers arriving here already know what to clean and who cleans it (Chapter 17), but several targets are dreaded and quietly wait. They want cleaning to be quick enough to actually do and good enough to actually work, without deep-cleaning marathons or a cupboard of products. The chapter answers with one hero idea and one rule: the working method, which is both effective (the surface comes clean and reads) and easy (quick enough you will do it again); and the best method is the one you will actually do. It teaches five working methods (right tool for the surface; top to bottom, dry before wet; fewer, simpler products; keep the tool where the target is; clean in passing), reframes dread as a method problem rather than a character flaw, and keeps the methods serving inspection so a target is cleaned and read in the same pass. Because the targets actually get cleaned, the friction meter follows Chapter 17's dashed projection and takes its next Shine step.

- Frames the problem as the method, not the person, so a dreaded job is a slow method rather than a character flaw, instead of the usual motivation-and-discipline advice
- Insists a method must be both effective and easy, and names the two common failures (easy but ineffective, effective but never repeated) that most cleaning tips ignore
- Gives a small, portable kit of five working methods that lift almost any target, rather than a room-by-room list of hacks or a product shopping list
- Keeps the methods serving inspection, so cleaning and reading a surface happen in the same easy pass, tying back to why we clean
- Keeps scope tight: this gives each target its how; it does not re-argue why we clean (Chapter 16), rebuild the list or reassign owners (Chapter 17), build the repeatable routine or reset (Chapter 19), or build documented standards (Standardize and Sustain)
- Moves the friction meter honestly, because the targets actually got cleaned, rather than claiming progress that did not happen
