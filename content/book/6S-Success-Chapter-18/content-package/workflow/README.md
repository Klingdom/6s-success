## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
You have a list and every line has a name, but between a named target and a cleaned one sits a small stubborn gap, and the gap is the how. A job can be perfectly assigned to a perfectly willing person and still not happen, for the oldest reason there is: done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, it is a pain to do, so it slides to next weekend and the weekend after. Chapter 18 closes that gap. It gives each target a working method, the one that is both effective (the surface actually comes clean and you can read it) and easy (quick and painless enough that you will do it again without dreading it), because the best method is the one you will actually do. It teaches a small kit of five: right tool for the surface; top to bottom, dry before wet; fewer, simpler products; keep the tool where the target is; and clean in passing. It reframes dread as a method problem rather than a character flaw, so the fix is a quicker method, not more willpower. It keeps the reading in, so a good method cleans the target and shows its condition in the same easy pass, exactly as on the launch pad. Then it works a pass across a couple of real list targets, the launch pad, a dreaded oven, the door handles, and nothing on the list changes; each just gets a way of being cleaned that is quick and good. Because real cleaning happened across the home this time, the friction meter MOVES: the needle follows the dashed forward projection Chapter 17 drew and takes its next Shine step toward calm, with Chapter 16 and 17's position now the dashed ghost just behind it. The goal stays ahead, because a home cleaned once is not yet a home that stays clean. Hands off to Chapter 19 · The 15-Minute Reset, which turns the whole thing into a light recurring rhythm and completes the third S.

## Frozen strings (copy verbatim; do not paraphrase)
- The rule: **The best method is the one you will actually do.**
- The definition box: **A working method is the quickest effective way to clean and read a given target: effective enough that the surface actually comes clean and shows what it needs, and easy enough that you will do it again without dreading it.**
- The reframe: **You do not have a cleaning problem. You have a method problem. The jobs you dread are almost never dreadful in themselves; they are jobs done the slow way, with the wrong tool, at the wrong time, so the fix is rarely to try harder and almost always to find the method that makes the job small.**
- One Idea to Keep: **The best method is the one you will actually do. Match a quick, effective method to each target, keep it small enough to fold into an ordinary day, and Shine stops being a dreaded event and becomes a handful of easy passes that keep the whole home clean and read.**
