## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The five zones (frozen, structural list):** The dining room is five small zones, not one big job: the dining table, the sideboard surface, the sideboard storage, the china or display cabinet, and the beverage or coffee station. Do them one at a time and the room the house gathers in comes back to you a zone at a time.

- **The finish rule (frozen, dining-room Shine principle):** A dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once so no water sits on the finish, the good china washed by hand and never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are not scrubbing a kitchen, you are caring for a room.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished dining room is one you could seat guests in on ten minutes' notice: bare wood and one centerpiece on the table, a clear serving run on the sideboard with three things displayed at the far end, linens rolled and platters on edge in the storage, the good china safe and visible behind clean glass in a cabinet strapped to the wall, and a drinks station that runs left to right on one liftable tray.

- **The lift-and-read reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Clean a dining room to read it. Lift every candlestick, vase, and photo frame rather than dusting around it, because the marks that tell the room's story, a pale ring a cold glass left, a white heat bloom from a hot dish, a fresh pinhole with a little powder beneath, only show on the bare wood underneath. The dust you can see is the least of what a proper clean finds.

- **The gentle-water reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** Water is the quiet enemy of every wooden surface in this room, and it does its damage slowly and out of sight: under a glass, along a leaf seam, beneath a drip tray. So the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit, because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.

- **The table-as-desk reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** If an activity lands on the table more than three evenings in a week, it is not clutter, it is an activity with nowhere else to be, and no table standard will ever survive it. The fix is not another polite reset, it is a home of its own for that activity, off the table, so the surface is free to do the one job a dining table is for.

- **The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure):** This is the dining room the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, cleared to bare wood, its finishes cared for and its good things safe and visible. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a dining room you could seat guests in on ten minutes' notice is one you will actually use, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A dining room is not clean because the table is wiped. It is clean because you lifted every object to reach the ring beneath it, matched a gentle touch to each finish, hand-washed the good china, and flagged the loose hinge and the heat bloom you found on the way. Do one zone this weekend, the table or the sideboard, and gather in a room that is ready for company.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / match the touch to the finish / lift and read, damp then dry. The nine-rule Shine method from Chapter 31 still governs; the dining room adds finish care, gentleness, and the good things.

## The four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win:** Do the table properly, not just the top where the plates sat. Clear it to bare wood, lift the centerpiece to get the ring beneath, then run a cloth wrapped over a fingernail down the leaf seam and along the apron rail where hands grip, wiping damp then drying at once. Finish by pushing the chairs out and vacuuming the footprint. Twenty minutes, and the room the house gathers in is ready for company.
- **Family Challenge:** Find the one activity that lands on the table more than three evenings a week, the homework, the laptop, or the craft, and give it a container and a home of its own off the table, because no table standard survives an activity with nowhere else to be. Then everyone agrees: whatever arrives on the table for a session leaves with it.
- **6S Tip:** Match the cleaner to the finish and never leave water on wood: a barely damp cloth dried at once on sealed wood, streak-free glass cleaner sprayed onto the cloth and not the glass so nothing runs behind a frame, and the good china washed by hand in a basin, never the dishwasher for a gilt rim or hand painting. When in doubt, go gentler and drier.
- **Common Mistake:** Wiping the tabletop where the plates sat and calling the room done, so the crumbs stay in the leaf seam, the tacky band stays under the apron rail, the ring stays under the centerpiece, and the good china waits behind clouded glass. And reaching for a wet cloth or a harsh spray on wood, which lifts the finish along with the mark. Lift everything, clean the surface itself, and keep it gentle and dry.

## The room: source facts (from the Micro Zone Manual, Dining Room, 5 zones)
Carry specific nouns. Each zone's full Inputs + numbered Steps + inspect-and-flag are authored in the manuscript from the Manual's shine_detail.

1. **Dining table.** Job: seat everyone for a shared meal without moving anything first. Done: one centerpiece on bare wood, chairs pushed in, placemats/trivets in the sideboard, no paper. Shine (clean it to read it): the tabletop and the centerpiece footprint (damp then dry, lift the centerpiece for the ring); the leaf seam or extension joint (a cloth over a fingernail; open an extension a hand's width and clean the slides); the underside lip and apron rail (goes tacky, dry cloth then damp); the pedestal, legs, casters (into carved detail and caster axles); the chair seats and back rails (wood dried at once, fabric vacuumed with the upholstery tool); the chair rungs and stretchers (into the joints); the floor under the table (chairs out, vacuum the footprint and the skirting behind). Call: the person who works at the table (an activity landing more than three evenings a week needs a home of its own). Hazards: candles at the ends of a runner (burn/fire); a worn drop-leaf or extension latch folding on a hand (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: lifting veneer or bubbling finish along the leaf seam (water crept under); a white heat ring or scorch bloom; a screw backing out of the apron; a leaf latch not sitting flush.
2. **Sideboard surface.** Job: hold serving dishes at a meal, a small display between them. Done: a taped bare serving run at the table end, three display objects at the lamp end, no paper, only the lamp cord in sight. Shine (lift every object, clean the wood itself): the display objects, vases, candlesticks (lift and wipe in your hands, detail brush the fluting and vase necks); the picture frames and glass (spray the cloth not the frame); the wood top, wax drips, water rings (damp then dry; a plastic card for wax; treat cold-glass rings); the back edge and wall seam (vacuum then detail brush behind the frame stands); the lamp, shade, cord run (dust the shade, wipe the whole cord length); the drawer fronts and pulls. Call: the clear run you actually need (measure the largest dish you serve; the mail always wins if the surface has three jobs). Hazards: a lamp, charger, and warming tray on one outlet (burn/fire); tall vases and heavy candlesticks at a child's head height on a narrow sideboard (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: a cracked/chafed cord or a browned outlet; a water ring with a soft patch under it; fresh pinholes with powder (woodworm) or a rockable leg; a new scorch bloom.
3. **Sideboard storage.** Job: the things that only come out when the table is set. Done: two tablecloths rolled with their napkins inside, serving bowls nested by size, platters on edge behind a divider, tapers of one length in a latched drawer, a count card inside the door. Shine (clean it empty, use your nose): the shelf boards (empty, vacuum corners, damp wipe, dry); the drawer interiors and dividers (vacuum grit, wipe, dry fully, or a rolled cloth soaks up damp); the drawer runners (wipe, slide to check flush); the linens (air and smell each, musty means rewash not restack); the nested bowls and platters on edge (rims and feet); the silver-plate (dust, look at all pieces for tarnish, polish a dulling piece with a soft cloth then buff); the door interior and count card. Call: the tablecloth for a table you do not own (a cloth that does not hang a hand's depth over your real table edge goes). Hazards: platters and the punch bowl above shoulder height (fall/cut/crush); taper candles and long matches loose in a low drawer (burn/fire). Inspect: a musty/damp smell or a water stain on a low shelf; tarnish spreading in the crevices; a bowing board or a runner pulling from its screws; a hairline crack or chip on a platter or punch-bowl rim.
4. **China or display cabinet.** Job: keep the good tableware safe, visible, and usable. Done: plates stacked no more than eight with felt between, cups single file, glasses rim up on a clean liner, tureens on the bottom shelf, the cabinet strapped to a wall stud, a shelf photo inside the door. Shine (the china stays usable only if the cabinet is sound): the top of the cabinet (thickest dust, high and unseen); the glass doors inside and out (spray the cloth, detail brush the glazing bars); the interior glass shelves and liners (edges and support pins); the china itself (hand-wash in a basin with a towel in the bottom, never the machine for gilt or hand painting, dry at once, set back rim up); the cabinet frame and hardware (hinges, lock, strap point, detail brush the hinge knuckles); the lower shelf and tureens (under each foot). Call: the twelve settings from your grandmother (keep the settings that fit your largest real meal in three years plus two; photograph and record the rest). Hazards: a tall glass-fronted cabinet loaded with heavy plates is the room's biggest tip-over risk (fall/cut/crush); antique/hand-painted pieces of unknown origin may carry lead glaze, keep off food use (poison/choke/strangle). Inspect: a cracked glass shelf or a bent/missing support pin; a door not closing flush or a sagging hinge; a frayed wall strap or a pulled fixing screw; condensation inside the glass or a bowing back panel.
5. **Beverage or coffee station.** Job: make a drink without crossing into the kitchen. Done: machine, grounds, mugs, spoons and sugar left to right on one liftable tray, mugs capped at daily drinkers plus two, a fill line on the bean jar, a hand's width of air behind the kettle. Shine (water and heat, so descale, clear the sticky rings, clean the hidden drip-tray underside where mold starts): the wall shelf and its underside above the kettle (steam warp); the counter and the liftable tray (lift it, degrease coffee splatter, dry); the machine, drip tray, and grate (barely damp on the body, lift the grate and wash the well where mold starts, descale on the manual's interval); the kettle body and element (unplug, look at scale, a descaler not a cloth for heavy scale); the bean jar, grinder, syrup bottles (fill line, brush the grinder throat off/unplugged, the sticky ring under the syrup); the mugs and shelf (the coffee ring under each base); the cords and the air gap behind the kettle. Call: the second machine (mark each machine every time it makes a drink someone drinks; keep the one that earns it). Hazards: machine, kettle, and an open jug sharing a surface with cords near the front (water/electricity); a kettle boiling under a wall shelf warping it (burn/fire); wine and spirits unlatched at child height (poison/choke/strangle). Inspect: a cracked/frayed/stiff cord or a browned plug or outlet; soft black mold in the drip-tray seam or under the grate; a swelling/warping shelf underside above the kettle; water pooling under the machine base (a failing seal).

## The kit for the dining room (product TYPES; no brand names)
- **Clean and shine (gentle inputs that do the work here):** a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner (safe on sealed wood); a streak-free glass and mirror cleaner (the cabinet glass, the picture frames); a colour-coded microfiber cloth set; a small detail brush set (carved detail, candlestick fluting, glazing bars, hinge knuckles); a compact vacuum with the upholstery tool and the floor tool (fabric seats, the table footprint); a plastic card for lifting wax; silver polish (the silver-plate serving pieces); mild dish soap and a plastic washing basin with a towel in the bottom (hand-washing the good china); a descaling solution or white vinegar and a food-area degreaser (the drinks station, descaling and coffee splatter). Nothing harsh, and never a wet surface left on wood.
- **Sort and routing:** a Keep, Relocate, Donate, Recycle, Trash container set.
- **Homes (Storage & Organization):** a drawer divider set; shelf dividers and shelf risers; a towel or linen shelf basket; clear lidded bins; a vertical file sorter (to get the daily paper off the surfaces and into a real home).
- **Standardize (Visual Controls):** a label maker; removable write-on and water-resistant permanent labels; shelf location labels; a count card taped inside the storage and cabinet doors; a cord label set; the ideal-state photo taped inside the cabinet.
- **Safety:** anti-tip anchors and hardware (the china cabinet is the room's biggest tip-over risk, strapped to a wall stud); a stable step stool (a high shelf or the top of the cabinet, never a chair); a moisture absorber or humidity monitor (damp in the linen storage or the cabinet).
