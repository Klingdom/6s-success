### Card 1 · The finish rule (verbatim)
> "A dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once so no water sits on the finish, the good china washed by hand and never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are not scrubbing a kitchen, you are caring for a room."

### Card 4 · The lift-and-read reframe (verbatim)
> "Clean a dining room to read it. Lift every candlestick, vase, and photo frame rather than dusting around it, because the marks that tell the room's story, a pale ring a cold glass left, a white heat bloom from a hot dish, a fresh pinhole with a little powder beneath, only show on the bare wood underneath. The dust you can see is the least of what a proper clean finds."

- Attribution: 6S Success: Home Edition · Chapter 34

### Card 5 · The gentle-water reframe (verbatim)
> "Water is the quiet enemy of every wooden surface in this room, and it does its damage slowly and out of sight: under a glass, along a leaf seam, beneath a drip tray. So the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit, because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year."

- Attribution: 6S Success: Home Edition · Chapter 34

### Card 7 · Quick Win (verbatim)
> "Do the table properly, not just the top where the plates sat. Clear it to bare wood, lift the centerpiece to get the ring beneath, then run a cloth wrapped over a fingernail down the leaf seam and along the apron rail where hands grip, wiping damp then drying at once. Finish by pushing the chairs out and vacuuming the footprint. Twenty minutes, and the room the house gathers in is ready for company."

- Attribution: 6S Success: Home Edition · Chapter 34

### Card 8 · 6S Tip (verbatim)
> "Match the cleaner to the finish and never leave water on wood: a barely damp cloth dried at once on sealed wood, streak-free glass cleaner sprayed onto the cloth and not the glass so nothing runs behind a frame, and the good china washed by hand in a basin, never the dishwasher for a gilt rim or hand painting. When in doubt, go gentler and drier."

### Card 16 · The nine Shine rules (verbatim frozen list, carried from Chapter 31)
> - work top to bottom
> - dry or clear before wet
> - back to front and clean to dirty
> - mist the cloth not the surface
> - two cloths one to clean and one to dry
> - colour-code your cloths
> - match the cleaner to the surface
> - give it dwell time
> - clean to inspect and flag rather than fix

- Attribution: 6S Success: Home Edition · Chapter 34
- Design: The method card, the nine rules carried from the door that govern every dining-room zone procedure, set

  cleaner to the surface`) given a small terracotta flag, because it is the one the dining room raises to a
  governing principle, match the touch to the finish. The list strings are byte-for-byte from the frozen nine
  Shine rules in `canonical/chapter-quotes.md`. The dining room keeps every one of these and adds the gentle
  touch, lift-and-read, damp-then-dry, and hand-washing the good china.
