## 5. How to Clean a Wood Table (the step ribbon, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the exact order of work for the dining room's first and highest-visible zone, the wood table,
so the reader sees the whole-piece sequence as one calm left-to-right ribbon and understands that you clean the
whole table top to bottom and back to front, not just the open top where the plates sat, and that water never sits
on the wood. This diagram has no direct HTML figure twin; it makes the Quick Win concrete, the properly-done table
that leaves the room ready for company.

**What it shows:** A gentle left-to-right ribbon of the moves the wood table runs in order, each a small step card
with a tiny type icon and a green-numbered node. `CLEAR TO BARE WOOD`, take everything off the table completely,
giving the homework and the laptop a home of their own off the table, so nothing is wiped around. `DAMP THEN DRY
THE TOP, LIFT THE CENTERPIECE`, wipe the whole surface with a cloth only just damp, working the full length in one
direction, and go back over it at once with a dry cloth so no moisture sits on the finish, lifting the centerpiece
to get the ring of dust beneath rather than wiping around it, drawn largest and greenest with the note that the
ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year. `THE LEAF SEAM`, run a fingernail wrapped in a thin
cloth down the seam where the leaves meet, because that gap holds crumbs a flat wipe skates over. `THE APRON
RAIL`, wipe the underside edge and the apron rail where hands grip to pull a chair in, a dry cloth first for
cobwebs then a barely damp one. `THE LEGS AND RUNGS`, work from the top of the pedestal down to the feet and wipe
every chair rung and low stretcher, right into the joints. `VACUUM THE FOOTPRINT`, push the chairs out and vacuum
the whole footprint under the table with the floor tool, then run it along the skirting behind. A faint note under
the ribbon reminds that on wood it is always damp then dry, never a wet surface left to sit.

**Rough layout:**

**What it shows:** A matched before-and-after pair of the SAME dining room, shot head-on from the doorway so the
table and the sideboard sit together in one frame, because that pairing is the room's public face and where the
difference reads most. On the LEFT, `before`: the table lost under mail, a laptop, and homework, coats off the
chairs, the sideboard a mail dump under chargers and a dead plant. On the RIGHT, `after`: the same frame, bare
wood and one centerpiece with the chairs pushed in, a clear serving run on the sideboard with three display
objects at the lamp end, the good china safe and visible behind clean glass in a strapped cabinet, the tidy
left-to-right tray. Between or beneath them, a small camera icon and two short notes: `the before, kept` under the
left image and `the after, kept as the standard` under the right, with a small note that a copy of the after is
taped inside the china cabinet door. This figure replaces the friction meter as the recurring close of every Part
9 room chapter. Secondary matched pairs a reader may also shoot: the open china cabinet (dusty and crammed versus
the good china safe and visible behind clean glass) and the drinks station (ringed with dried spills versus a tidy
left-to-right tray).

**Note for the illustrator:** keep it brief, matched, and honest, the Part 9 signature rather than a showpiece.
Draw both frames from the same chosen spot in the doorway, framing the table and the sideboard together, same
angle and same light, and do not tidy the "before", because the before is the valuable one, the proof of how far
the room came. Make the after clearly the standard, the ideal-state photo from Standardize, kept as the picture
the room has to match, with a copy taped inside the china cabinet door where the good things live. Draw the
secondary pairs (the open china cabinet, the drinks station) only if a small inset is wanted; the table-and-
sideboard frame from the doorway is the primary. Do NOT draw the friction meter; this pair replaces it for all of
Part 9. Hold the color discipline: spark for the "before" tag, the "before, kept" note in terracotta, green for
the "after" tag and the standard note, spark does not otherwise appear. Calm, warm, illustrated, not photoreal,
the register of a quiet, real transformation the reader is about to photograph for themselves.
