# Infographic Spec: The Dining-Room Shine Method

**Chapter 34, "The Dining Room"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE FOURTH PLAYBOOK**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter: the DINING-ROOM SHINE METHOD, the fine finishes and the gentle
touch that make a dining-room clean different from a kitchen or pantry clean, built on the one governing move that
you match the touch to the finish, gentle, and keep water off wood, and the exact inputs. It carries the chapter's
spine in one glance: you know the nine rules of Shine from the door, and they all hold here, and the dining room
adds one governing principle and two habits that follow from it. The concept pairs the hero finish rule, **A
dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth
only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once so no water sits on the finish, the good china washed by hand and
never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are not scrubbing a kitchen, you are caring
for a room.**, with the picture at its heart: the soft cloth chosen over the harsh spray, every object lifted so
the marks on the bare wood beneath can be read, the damp cloth followed at once by a dry one so no water sits on
the wood, the good china washed by hand in a towel-lined basin, and the dining kit, the exact inputs, laid out by
type. So the viewer sees the whole move at once: this is how you care for a dining room to a professional
standard, and every zone procedure in the chapter is just this method applied to a particular surface.

It stays consistent with the four inline figures in `chapter_34_final.html`, and in particular with Figure 3, the
two-panel `THE DINING ROOM SHINE METHOD AND THE KIT`, drawn in the accent greens of the Shine step. The Shine step
owns the green accent: the method path, the numbered nodes, and the gentle-touch move are green; slate carries the
labels; terracotta carries the warm accents. The single small warm red is held back entirely except for the two
true hazard marks in this room, the tall china cabinet tip-over and the frayed wall strap, with the heat bloom
carried in warm terracotta rather than red. This is not a lecture and not a scolding: it is a caring method laid
out plainly so anyone can follow it with their sleeves rolled up and a gentle hand. Types, never brands: every
cleaner, cloth, brush, tool, polish, basin, and strap is a plain generic type, never a brand name or a real logo,
and there is no degreaser for the room itself, because a dining room has no grease. The register is warm,
practical, expert, and a little tender about the good things that carry memory, capability and a little pride, no
shame about the before, the confidence of a method that cares for the room rather than scrubbing it. Clean to a
method, not until it looks better; care for the room, do not scrub it.

## Layout notes

- The finish rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole dining-room Shine pass compressed: a room
  of good finishes and things that matter, so match the touch to the surface, a cloth only just damp on sealed
  wood dried at once so no water sits, the good china by hand, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass, you
  are caring for a room, not scrubbing a kitchen. The eye should read the finish rule, then see the order of care
  (match the touch, lift and read, damp then dry, wash the good china by hand) and the four moves make it
  concrete.
- The order of care and the four moves are the hero, so make the gentle touch and the damp-then-dry motion
  unmistakable: the soft cloth chosen over the harsh spray at the head, drawn largest and greenest with the
  `gentle, never harsh` note, then the barely damp cloth and the dry cloth straight behind it as the single most
  important technique in the room, with the barred `never leave water on wood` warning and the note that the ring
  you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year. Keep lift and read plainly present, every object lifted
  so the marks on the bare wood beneath can be read. Set the finish rule byte-for-byte as it reads in the
  canonical quotes; it is the frozen string.
- The kit sits in support, not competing with the method: the exact inputs laid out by TYPE, a neutral pH
  multi-surface cleaner, a streak-free glass cleaner, mild dish soap, a colour-coded microfiber set, a small
  detail brush set, a compact vacuum with upholstery and floor tools, a plastic card for lifting wax, silver
  polish, a washing basin, a descaler for the drinks station, drawer dividers, a count card, and an anti-tip
  strap. Label every one by type, never by brand, draw no logo and no recognisable packaging, and draw no
  degreaser for the room itself, because a dining room has no grease.
- Hold the color discipline: the green Shine accent carries the method path, the numbered nodes, and the
  gentle-touch move; slate carries the labels; terracotta carries the warm accents and the heat bloom; the deeper
  label green carries the small labels; the single small warm red is held back entirely except for the two true
  hazard marks, the tall china cabinet tip-over and the frayed wall strap, because this is a calm caring card and
  the dining room's serious hazards are the tip-over and the strap.
- This image makes the concept, the thing that makes a dining-room clean different from a kitchen or pantry clean:
  the enemy is not grease or dust but fine finishes and water on wood, so you match the touch to the finish,
  gentle, and never leave water to sit. It also carries the two habits: lift and read the marks beneath every
  object, and damp then dry on wood, plus hand-wash the good china, so the pale cold-glass ring, the white heat
  bloom, the lifting veneer, and the loose hinge are found and flagged on the way. Do NOT re-teach the six S's or
  fully re-teach the nine-rule Shine method (recap only, it was taught at the door in Chapter 31), and do NOT
  teach the living room of Chapter 35, which is named only as where the reader points the loop next. Draw no
  friction meter, retired for Part 9. And name no brand and draw no specific product; the cleaners, cloths,
  brushes, vacuum, polish, basin, and strap are plain and generic, teaching the method, not a shopping list.
- Register: warm, practical, expert, and a little tender about the good things that carry memory, sleeves-rolled-up
  instruction rather than a lecture; the method exists because it cares for the room rather than harming it. No
  force or discipline-as-punishment language, no scolding, no hype, no corporate or boardroom look, no smugness,
  and no shame about the before. Give the damp-then-dry motion its due, because in a dining room keeping water off
  wood is the whole difference: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark and a dry cloth straight behind it, and the
  ring you leave tonight never becomes the ring you cannot lift next year.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the order of care and the four
  moves drawn by hand as a calm caring card rather than a clinical diagram, the green path a hand-drawn stroke
  over a dining-room vignette, the soft cloth and the towel-lined basin plain and friendly, the whole image the
  warm, capable, tender register of a caring method taught plainly: not a tidied room, a genuinely cared-for one,
  its finishes unmarked and its good things safe and visible.
