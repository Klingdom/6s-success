# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 34

**"The Dining Room"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE FOURTH PLAYBOOK*

Six prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across all
six: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the warm
accent, the eyebrows, the "before, kept" note), honey `#DDA63A` (the single accent, the family note), slate blue
`#3C5A6B` (data, labels, the plan), soft green `#6E8B5B` (the SHINE STEP accent, the method arrows, the numbered
nodes, the "after" standard), a deeper label green (a darker shade of the Shine green) for small labels on the
Shine figures, a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only and, in this room, for the two
true hazard marks, the tall china cabinet tip-over and the heat bloom, used barely. Natural soft daylight, shallow
but not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is one real dining room, the room the house gathers in, and it is different from the
two rooms before it in a way that changes how you clean it: the kitchen and the pantry are working rooms you clean
hard, and the dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, a wood table that marks if you look
at it wrong, a glass cabinet full of the good china, silver that tarnishes, linen that mildews. This is the FOURTH
of twenty room chapters, following the entryway of Chapter 31, the kitchen of Chapter 32, and the pantry of
Chapter 33 and handing off to the living room of Chapter 35: the six-S method is finished, and the nine-rule Shine
method was taught at the door in Chapter 31, so it is recapped here, not re-taught. The spine of this chapter is a
single overriding addition to the door's method: the dining room is not a working room you scrub but a room you
care for, so the governing principle is match the touch to the finish, gentle, and above all keep water off wood,
because a wet cloth left on a wooden tabletop leaves a ring you will not lift next year. It delivers the frame
(the dining room is five small zones, not one big job: the dining table, the sideboard surface, the sideboard
storage, the china or display cabinet, and the beverage or coffee station); the heart (the dining-room Shine
method: match the touch to the finish, lift every object and read the marks beneath, damp then dry on wood so no
water sits, hand-wash the good china); the five zone-by-zone procedures with their exact inputs and ordered steps;
the hard calls (the person who works at the table, the clear run you actually need, the tablecloth for a table you
do not own, the china settings that carry memory, the second coffee machine); and the Part 9 close (a matched
before-and-after pair of the table and sideboard from the doorway). The friction meter, which reached its goal at
Chapter 30, is RETIRED for Part 9 and replaced by the before-and-after room signature: do not draw a gauge. Keep
the tone warm, practical, expert, and a little tender about the good things that carry memory, caring for a room
rather than scrubbing it, capability and a little pride, no shame about the before. Crucially, describe only the
calm, warm illustrated house style: no corporate or boardroom look, no hype (no unlock, elevate, supercharge,
optimize), no scolding, no photoreal polish, and no brand names or real logos anywhere, every cleaner, cloth,
brush, tool, polish, basin, and strap is a plain generic TYPE, never a recognisable product.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in the
scene, a hand-lettered card, a hand-drawn label on a plan, a container, or a bottle, a mark on a cloth, or a note
under a photo, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered label: use the
middot, for example `CHAPTER 34 · THE DINING ROOM`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words.
Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: match-the-touch, finish-care, damp-then-dry, lift-and-read, hand-wash,
top-to-bottom, back-to-front, clean-to-dirty, mist-the-cloth, two-cloth, colour-coded, bare-wood, leaf-seam,
apron-rail, chair-rungs, cold-glass, heat-bloom, water-ring, gilt-rim, hand-painted, anti-tip, wall-stud,
count-card, ideal-state, before-and-after, matched-pair, zone-by-zone, six-S, step-by-step, dwell-time,
ten-minutes, child-height, drip-tray.

## 1. Before and after, one dining room (the opener)

A warm, wide editorial illustration of one dining room drawn in two states side by side in soft daylight, the same
furniture and layout, to show before a word is read that this is a playbook about transforming the room the house
gathers in, and that the transformation is gentle finish-care, safety for the good things, and the table given
back its one job, not just tidying. On the left, hand-lettered before, the everyday reality: the table lost under
a drift of mail, a laptop, and homework, a coat and bags hung off the chair backs, the sideboard a mail dump under

whether the loop has been pointed at it, the wood on the right bare and unmarked and the good things safe. Warm
paper cream, ink-brown line work, warm red for the before eyebrow, soft green for the after eyebrow, the gentle
care implied in the unmarked wood of the after side, warm-red otherwise held back. Editorial storybook
illustration. The same dining room, two states.

- Mood: on the left the room that has quietly become an office, a mailroom, and a dumping ground, its good things
  dusty and at risk; on the right the same room after five zones were each cleaned with a gentle, careful touch, a
  room you could seat guests in on ten minutes' notice; the register is warm, calm, quietly capable, and a little
  tender about the good things.
- Avoid: a filthy, squalid, or shameful before side (it is full and lived-in and half given up on, not a wreck), a
  corporate or boardroom look, floating typeset text overlays, a friction gauge or dial anywhere (retired for Part
  9), any brand names or recognisable products, warm-red except the smallest marks; keep the two sides plainly the
  same dining room, the same furniture and layout, only the state different.

## 5. How to clean a wood table (the step ribbon)
