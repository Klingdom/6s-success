2/
The kitchen you scrub. The dining room you do not. It is full of good finishes and things that matter: a wood table, a glass cabinet, silver, the china that is really a person. Here you care for the room, and the touch is gentle.

(232 chars)

---

3/
"The dining room is five small zones, not one big job: the dining table, the sideboard surface, the sideboard storage, the china or display cabinet, and the beverage or coffee station." Do them one at a time.

(211 chars)

---

4/
The governing rule: match the touch to the finish. A cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once. The good china by hand, never the machine. Nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are caring for a room, not scrubbing a worktop.

(243 chars)

---

5/
The single most important habit here, for the wood: "the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit."

(211 chars)

---

6/
Why it matters: "the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year." Water does its damage slowly and out of sight, under a glass, along a leaf seam, beneath a drip tray.

(188 chars)

---

7/
"Clean a dining room to read it." Lift every candlestick, vase, and frame rather than dusting around it. The marks worth finding, a cold-glass ring, a heat bloom, a woodworm pinhole, only show on the bare wood underneath.

(224 chars)

---

8/
The table, done properly: not just the top where the plates sat. Lift the centerpiece for the ring beneath, run a cloth over a fingernail down the leaf seam, wipe the tacky apron rail, then push the chairs out and vacuum the footprint.

(238 chars)

---

9/
The good china is a person. Wash it by hand in a towel-lined basin, dry it at once, set it rim up. Then read the cabinet itself: the cracked glass shelf, the bent pin, the sagging hinge, the frayed wall strap.

(212 chars)

---

10/
Safety, one big item: a tall glass cabinet loaded with heavy plates is the biggest tip-over risk in the house. Strap it to a wall stud, weight on the bottom shelf, no plate stack over eight with felt between.

(212 chars)

---

11/
The call that undoes every reset: "If an activity lands on the table more than three evenings in a week, it is not clutter, it is an activity with nowhere else to be, and no table standard will ever survive it." Give it a home off the table.

(245 chars)

---

12/
Even the drinks station: wipe the shelf underside above the kettle where steam warps it, lift the drip-tray grate where mold starts, descale the machine, and lift the syrup bottle for the sticky ring welded to the shelf.

(224 chars)

---

13/
The standard: "A finished dining room is one you could seat guests in on ten minutes' notice." Bare wood and one centerpiece, a clear serving run, the good china safe behind clean glass, a tidy drinks tray.
