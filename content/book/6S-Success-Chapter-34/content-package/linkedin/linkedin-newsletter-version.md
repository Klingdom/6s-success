# The Room You Clean Gently

*A 6S Success: Home Edition newsletter, from Chapter 34, The Dining Room.*

Hi friend,

We have done the two rooms that feed the house, the kitchen and the pantry, and cleaned them hard: degreased, soaked, dry-brushed. This week we walk into the room the house gathers in, and everything changes, because the dining room is not a working room. It is full of good finishes and things that matter, a wood table, a glass cabinet, the china that is really a person. Here you do not scrub. You care for the room, and the touch is gentle.

That is the whole shift, and it matters because the instinct you built in the kitchen will do quiet, expensive damage here.

**A dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once so no water sits on the finish, the good china washed by hand and never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are not scrubbing a kitchen, you are caring for a room.**

The room breaks into five zones: the dining table, the sideboard surface, the sideboard storage, the china or display cabinet, and the beverage or coffee station. In the chapter each gets its exact inputs and numbered steps. Two habits run through all of them.

The first is to lift everything and clean the surface itself, because the surface is telling you things. **Clean a dining room to read it.** The pale ring a cold glass left, the white heat bloom from a hot dish, the fresh woodworm pinhole with a little powder beneath, all of them only show on the bare wood underneath.

The second protects the wood: **the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit, because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.**

## To try this week

Do the table properly, not just the top where the plates sat. Clear it to bare wood, lift the centerpiece for the ring beneath, run a cloth wrapped over a fingernail down the leaf seam and along the tacky apron rail, then push the chairs out and vacuum the footprint. Damp then dry, every time. Twenty minutes, and the room the house gathers in is ready for company.

And if your table has quietly become a desk: **if an activity lands on the table more than three evenings in a week, it is not clutter, it is an activity with nowhere else to be, and no table standard will ever survive it.** Give that activity a home of its own, off the table.

Two printables go with this one: **The Dining Room Reset card** (the five zones and the gentle order of work) and **The Dining Room Kit list** (the gentle cleaners and care items, with the finish-care and heirloom notes).
