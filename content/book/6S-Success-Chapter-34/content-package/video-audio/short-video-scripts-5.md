## SHORT 1: A room you care for, not scrub, so match the touch to the finish
**Topic: the dining-room Shine method, the finish-care layer on top of the nine rules. Runtime ~60 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The reason your wooden table has a pale ring you cannot lift is one thing: you cleaned it like a kitchen. A dining room is a room you care for, not scrub.

**Script:**
You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, and clean to inspect every surface your hand passes over. The dining room keeps all of that and adds one governing rule, because the instinct you built in the kitchen will harm things in here. As the book puts it, a dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once so no water sits on the finish, the good china washed by hand and never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass. You are not scrubbing a kitchen, you are caring for a room. And two habits follow. Lift every object and clean the wood itself, because a pale ring or a heat bloom only shows on the bare wood underneath. And on wood, damp then dry, in one motion, because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.

- 12s: "A room of good finishes. Match the touch to the finish."
- 30s: "Lift every object. Read the marks beneath."

## SHORT 2: Five zones, not one room
**Topic: the hero device, the dining room as micro-zones. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A dining room you have half given up on looks like one big job. Faced as its zones, it is five short, finishable pieces.

**Script:**
Here is the trick that makes the room you keep apologising for doable. You do not clean the dining room. You clean one zone of it, then the next. The dining room is five small zones, not one big job: the dining table, the sideboard surface, the sideboard storage, the china or display cabinet, and the beverage or coffee station. Do them one at a time and the room the house gathers in comes back to you a zone at a time. Each one has a clear finish. You do not clean the dining room, you clean the table, which is done when it is bare wood with the seams and the apron rail and the chair rungs all wiped and the footprint vacuumed. And if you want the biggest visible win, start with the table, or start with the cabinet if the good china has been nagging at you. You never have to face the dining room. You face one zone, with a clear finish, and then you are done for the day.

- 0s: "A dining room you keep apologising for."
- 12s: "It is five small zones, not one big job."
- 28s: "Each zone has a clear finish."
- 40s: "Start with the table for the biggest visible win."

## SHORT 3: How to clean the dining table, step by step
**Topic: the showcase zone, Zone 1 inputs and numbered steps. Runtime ~75 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The biggest visible win in your dining room is the table, done properly, not just the top where the plates sat. Here is exactly how a professional cleans it, in order, and every step is gentle and dry.

**Script:**
First, gather the inputs into a caddy: a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, and a compact vacuum with the upholstery tool and the floor tool. Now the steps, in the order you actually do them. One, clear the table to bare wood and do the top, wipe the whole surface with a cloth just damp, working the full length in one direction, then go back over it at once with a dry cloth so no moisture sits on the finish, and lift the centerpiece to get the ring beneath it rather than wiping around it. Two, clean the leaf seam, run a fingernail wrapped in a thin cloth down the seam where the leaves meet, because that gap holds crumbs a flat wipe skates over. Three, do the underside lip and the apron rail where hands grip to pull a chair in, a dry cloth first for cobwebs, then a barely damp one. Four, dust the pedestal, legs, and casters, into the caster wheels where hair wraps the axle. Five, do the chair seats and back rails, drying a wood seat at once and vacuuming a fabric seat along the front edge. Six, clean the rungs and stretchers right into the joints. Seven, push the chairs out and vacuum the whole footprint last. That is the table that carries the room, cleaned gently to a professional standard.

- 0s: "How to clean the dining table, in order."
- 12s: "Clear to bare wood. Wipe damp, then a dry cloth at once."
- 35s: "Lift the centerpiece. Work the leaf seam and apron rail."
- 60s: "Rungs into the joints. Vacuum the footprint last."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The dining table cleaned in sequence, numbered 1 to 7: the caddy set down first, the table cleared to bare wood, the surface wiped damp then dried at once, the centerpiece lifted for the ring, a cloth over a fingernail run down the leaf seam, the apron rail wiped, the casters dusted, the rungs worked into the joints, the footprint vacuumed. ## SHORT 4: Hand-wash the good china, because it is really a person
**Topic: Zone 4, the china cabinet, the gentle-touch rule and the hand-wash discipline. Runtime ~55 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The fastest way to ruin a gilt rim or hand painting is to put it in the dishwasher. The good china is really a person, so in this zone you wash it by hand.

**Script:**
The china cabinet keeps the good tableware safe, visible, and usable, and it stays usable only if you treat it gently. Gather your inputs: a streak-free glass and mirror cleaner, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a small detail brush set, mild dish soap and a plastic washing basin with a towel in the bottom, and a compact vacuum with the brush attachment. Then, in order. One, dust the very top of the cabinet first, high and out of sight, where this room's dust settles thickest and nobody looks. Two, do the glass doors inside and out, spray the cleaner onto the cloth and not the glass, then run the detail brush along the glazing bars. Three, wipe the interior glass shelves and the support pins with the shelves bare. Four, the one people skip, hand-wash each piece of the good china in the basin with the towel in the bottom, never the dishwasher for a gilt rim or hand painting, and dry each piece straight away rather than leaving it to drain. Five, wipe the frame, the hinges, and the strap point where the case meets the wall. And read the cabinet while the shelves are bare: a cracked glass shelf, a bent pin, a sagging hinge, a strap pulling loose. Flag each.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Never put the good china in the dishwasher."
- 14s: "Dust the top of the cabinet first. It is thickest there."
- 32s: "Spray the cloth, not the glass. Both faces of every pane."
- 46s: "Hand-wash each piece. Towel-lined basin. Dry at once."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The china cabinet worked in order: the top of the case dusted first, both faces of the glass doors cleaned with the cleaner sprayed onto the cloth, the detail brush along the glazing bars, the interior glass shelves and support pins wiped bare, then the good china hand-washed in a towel-lined basin and dried at once, a dishwasher shown crossed out as the thing NOT to do, the frame and hinges and strap point wiped. ## SHORT 5: Take the before before you touch a thing
**Topic: the before-and-after pair, the Part 9 signature. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The most valuable photo of your dining room is the one you take before you clear a single thing, because within a week you will not believe the room was ever the other one.

**Script:**
Here is the ritual that closes every room from here on, and it is what makes the clean stick. Before you touch a thing, stand in the doorway and photograph the dining room exactly as it is, framing the table and the sideboard together, because that pairing is the room's public face and where the difference reads most. Then do the work, five zones cleaned with a careful, gentle touch, one at a time, every object lifted and the wood read beneath it, the good china hand-washed. And when it is done, stand in exactly the same spot and take the second photo. The two do different jobs. The before is the valuable one, the proof of how far the room came. The after is the standard, the picture the room has to match from now on, with a copy taped inside the china cabinet door. Here is the frozen line for it. This is the dining room the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, cleared to bare wood, its finishes cared for and its good things safe and visible. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a dining room you could seat guests in on ten minutes' notice is one you will actually use, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Take the before before you touch a thing."
- 12s: "Five zones, cleaned with a gentle touch."
- 28s: "Same spot. Same angle. Take the after."
- 40s: "The after is the standard the room has to match."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The matched before-and-after pair shot from the doorway, framing the table and the sideboard together.
