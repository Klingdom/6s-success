# Teleprompter Script: The Dining Room

**Chapter 34, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-dining-room
**Length:** about 950 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading. This is the FOURTH room of Part Nine, and its heart is HOW TO CLEAN each dining-room zone GENTLY, so read it warm, capable, and a little tender; you already own the method, now care for the room the house gathers in so it is ready for company. No hype, no shame about the before.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

You have done the pantry. Now step into the room the house gathers in.

The dining room.

And it is different from the two rooms before it, in a way that changes everything.

The kitchen and the pantry are working rooms. You clean them hard.

You degrease. You scrub. You soak.

The dining room is not a working room.

It is a room of good finishes and things that matter.

A wood table that marks if you look at it wrong.

A glass cabinet full of the good china. Silver that tarnishes.

Here you do not scrub. You care for the room, and the touch is gentle.

And that matters, because the kitchen instinct will harm things in here.

A wet cloth on a wooden top leaves a ring you will not lift next year.

A harsh spray strips a finish. The dishwasher ruins a gilt rim.

So the governing rule is the opposite of the kitchen's.

Match the touch to the surface. Keep it gentle.

And above all, keep water off wood.

Now, a whole dining room feels like too much.

Because you are looking at it as one job.

It is not.

The dining room is five small zones, not one big job.

The dining table. The sideboard surface. The sideboard storage.

The china or display cabinet. And the beverage or coffee station.

Do them one at a time, and the room comes back to you.

Before the clean, four passes are quick.

Sort each zone, giving the homework and the laptop a home of their own.

Straighten what survives by use.

Safety, Standardize, and Sustain come after the shine.

Now we slow right down, for Shine.

You already know the nine rules from the door.

Top to bottom. Dry or clear before wet. Back to front, clean to dirty.

Mist the cloth, not the surface. Two cloths, one to clean, one to dry.

Colour-code your cloths. Match the cleaner to the surface.

Give it dwell time. And clean to inspect and flag, do not fix.

The dining room adds one governing principle, and two habits.

The principle is the finish rule.

Match the touch to the surface. Gentle, and never harsh on wood.

A cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once.

The good china washed by hand, never in the machine.

The first habit is to lift and read.

Lift every candlestick, vase, and frame, rather than dusting around it.

Because the marks that tell the room's story only show on the bare wood.

A pale ring a cold glass left. A white heat bloom. A fresh pinhole.

The second habit protects the wood, and it matters most.

On wood, damp then dry, in one motion.

A barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it.

Never a wet surface left to sit.

Because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.

The kit is gentle and small, because you are caring, not scrubbing.

A neutral cleaner, a glass cleaner, soft cloths, a detail brush.

And a few care items: a plastic card for wax, silver polish, a basin.

Now watch that method clean the showcase zone, the dining table.

Gather the inputs into your caddy first.

A multi-surface cleaner. A colour-coded cloth set.

A vacuum with the upholstery tool and the floor tool.

Step one. Clear the table to bare wood.

Wipe damp in one direction, then a dry cloth at once, so no water sits.

And lift the centerpiece to get the ring beneath.

Step two. Clean the leaf seam.

Run a fingernail wrapped in a thin cloth down the gap where the leaves meet.

Step three. Do the underside lip and the apron rail where hands grip.

A dry cloth first for cobwebs, then a barely damp one.

Step four. Dust the pedestal, the legs, and the casters.

Step five. Do the chair seats and back rails.

Dry a wood seat at once. Vacuum a fabric seat along the front edge.

Step six. Clean the rungs and stretchers, right into the joints.

Step seven. Push the chairs out and vacuum the footprint, last.

That is the table that carries the room, cleaned gently.

The other four zones run the very same way.

The sideboard surface, where you lift every object and read the wood beneath.

And spray the frame glass onto the cloth, never the frame.

The storage, where you air and smell each linen.

A musty note means rewash, not restack.

The china cabinet, where you dust the top first.

And hand-wash the good china in a towel-lined basin, never the machine.

And the coffee station, where you clean the shelf underside above the kettle.

And lift the drip-tray grate to wash the hidden well where mold starts.

Then a few honest calls hold the room.

The person at the table: give the activity a home of its own, off the table.

The serving run: measure it to your largest dish, and tape it.

The good china: keep the settings that fit the largest meal you really host.

And read the room as you clean. The tall china cabinet is the biggest tip-over risk.

Strap it to a wall stud. That is the safety pass.

When the five zones are done, take the second photograph.

Stand in the doorway, framing the table and the sideboard together.

The before is the valuable one, the proof.

The after is the standard, the picture the room has to match.

A dining room is not clean because the table is wiped.

It is clean because you lifted every object to reach the ring beneath it.

Because you matched a gentle touch to each finish.

Because you hand-washed the good china.

And flagged the loose hinge and the heat bloom you found.

It is the room the house gathers in, and now it is ready to.
