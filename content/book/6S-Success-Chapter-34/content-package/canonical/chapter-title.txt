The dining room is where the house gathers, and its defining challenge is not grease or dust but fine finishes and things that matter (a wood table and veneer, silver-plate, the good china, a glass cabinet), so its governing principle is match the touch to the finish: gentle, and dry on wood. The CORE of the chapter is the "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It" section: for each of the five zones it gives the exact INPUTS to gather, the NUMBERED step-by-step clean-and-shine procedure, and an inspect-and-flag list, all governed by the universal nine-rule Shine method carried from Chapter 31 PLUS the dining-room layer: match the touch to the finish, lift and read the marks beneath every object, damp then dry on wood so water never sits on the finish, and hand-wash the good china. Shine finds the hazards, the safety pass fixes them. The friction meter, retired for Part 9, is replaced by a BEFORE-AND-AFTER room signature: a matched pair of the table and sideboard shot from the doorway, the room's public face, plus the two photos to take (the before to keep, the after as the standard). The dining room is worked as five small zones, not one big job.
