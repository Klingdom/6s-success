# Facebook Longform Post: The Room You Clean Gently

A story-driven post from Chapter 34 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the dining room. Body is warm and practical, no hype, product types only.

---

Here is a mistake almost everyone makes with the dining room: they clean it like a kitchen.

They reach for a wet cloth and a strong spray, wipe the tabletop where the plates sat, and call the room done. And slowly, quietly, they do damage. A wet cloth left on a wooden tabletop leaves a ring you will not lift next year. A harsh spray strips a finish. The dishwasher ruins the gilt rim on the good china.

The dining room is not a working room. It is the room the house gathers in, and it is full of good finishes and things that matter: a wood table, a glass cabinet, silver that tarnishes, the china that is really a person. Here you do not scrub. You care for the room, and the touch is gentle.

Two habits carry the whole thing.

The first: lift everything and clean the surface itself. Clean a dining room to read it. Lift every candlestick, vase, and photo frame rather than dusting around it, because the marks that tell the room's story, a pale ring a cold glass left, a white heat bloom from a hot dish, a fresh woodworm pinhole, only show on the bare wood underneath.

The second protects the wood, and it is the single most important thing in the room. Water is the quiet enemy of every wooden surface here, and it does its damage slowly and out of sight. So the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit. The ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.

And the good china? Wash it by hand in a basin with a towel in the bottom, never the dishwasher for a gilt rim, and dry it at once. Then, while the shelves are bare, read the cabinet itself and strap it to the wall, because a loaded glass cabinet is the biggest tip-over risk in the house.

Do one zone this weekend, the table or the sideboard, and gather in a room that is ready for company.

Which good thing in your dining room have you been afraid to clean properly?
