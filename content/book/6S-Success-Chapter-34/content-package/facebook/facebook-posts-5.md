# Chapter 34 Facebook Posts: The Dining Room

Five short standalone posts. Warm, practical, product types only.

## 1. Clean it gently

The dining room is not a working room. You do not scrub it, you care for it. A dining room is a room of good finishes and things that matter, so here you match the touch to the surface: a cloth only just damp on sealed wood, dried at once, the good china by hand and never in the machine, and nothing harsh on veneer, gilt, or glass.

## 2. The ring you leave tonight

Water is the quiet enemy of every wooden surface in this room. So the rule on wood is damp then dry, in one motion: a barely damp cloth to lift the mark, a dry one straight behind it, and never a wet surface left to sit, because the ring you leave tonight is the ring you cannot lift next year.

## 3. Clean the whole table, not just the top

The crumbs are not on the tabletop. They are in the leaf seam, under the apron rail where hands grip, and in the joints of the chair rungs. Lift the centerpiece for the ring beneath, run a cloth wrapped over a fingernail down the seam, wipe the tacky rail, then vacuum the footprint. Twenty minutes, and the room is ready for company.

## 4. The good china is a person

The china outlasts you only if the case does. Wash each piece by hand in a towel-lined basin, dry it at once, and set it back rim up. Then read the cabinet itself, the cracked glass shelf, the bent pin, the sagging hinge, the frayed strap, and strap it to a wall stud. A loaded glass cabinet is the biggest tip-over risk in the house.

## 5. Give the table back its job

If an activity lands on the table more than three evenings in a week, it is not clutter, it is an activity with nowhere else to be, and no table standard will ever survive it. Give it a home of its own, off the table. Then the table can be a table again, ready to seat guests on ten minutes' notice. Chapter 34. Next: the living room.
