## Identity strings (frozen; copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)
- **Title:** Shine Targets and Assignments
- **Subtitle:** One zone is clean, but the rest of the home is still unnamed, and Shine that belongs to no one lands on the same tired pair of hands. This chapter lists every target worth cleaning, groups it so nothing hides, and puts a name against each one, so the whole home gets read and the work gets shared.
- **Masthead:** "Part Five · Shine" / "Chapter Seventeen"
- **The rule for targets (frozen):** Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.
- **The rule for assignments (frozen):** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target.
- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A Shine list is one shared inventory of everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by kind of target, with a person's name against each one, so nothing is forgotten and no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands.
- **The invisible-target payoff (frozen, pull-quote):** The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads.
- **The assignments reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** When cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness, and about a home that stays cared for whether or not one person is watching.
- **Choosing, not assigning (frozen, pull-quote):** A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent. So let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers, and share out the jobs nobody wants instead of leaving them all with one person.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** the Shine list / the four kinds of target (surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use) / a name on every target. The room is already sorted and arranged and one zone is cleaned; Chapter 17 plans the whole-home clean and shares it.

## Hero device: The Shine list (targets by kind, a name on every line)
One whole-home inventory of everything that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by the four kinds of target, with a person's name written against each line. It makes the invisible targets visible so they get read, and makes ownership visible so the work is shared. Source: Seiso "determine targets and assignments" and the Traditional Shine Target Categories (Surface, Equipment, Stored Items, Point of Use), home-scaled. Referenced lightly, no overclaiming.

## Motifs
- **One zone clean, a whole house unnamed.** Instinct cleans the visible handful; the rest goes unread and unassigned.
- **Targets by kind, not by room.** Four categories catch what a room sweep skips.
- **The list.** One page turns a private pile of obligation into a finite, shareable set of lines.
- **Clean the whole thing.** The surface that faces away is the one that fails; the list includes the tops, backs, insides, and handles.
- **A name on every line.** No-owner defaults to the same person; names end the default and share the load.
- **Choosing, not assigning.** A job chosen gets done; a job handed gets resented.
- **The principled hold.** Planning is real work and not cleaning, so the needle holds, with a dashed arrow for the move to come.

## Emotional purpose
Fairness and relief, not lecture. This chapter touches the live nerve of who does the housework; the register is that the load falling on one person is a default to be fixed with a list and shared names, not a moral failing and not something to nag about. The payoff is a home kept by everyone, which asks less of anyone and stops depending on one person never getting tired.

## Practical purpose
Teach the reader to see targets by the four kinds, build one whole-home Shine list that surfaces the hidden targets, put a name against every line, and hand out the work by letting people choose (matched to ability, worst jobs shared or rotated). Do NOT re-open Sort or re-teach Straighten. Do NOT re-argue why we clean (Ch 16). Do NOT teach HOW to clean, products, tools, or technique (Ch 18). Do NOT build the repeatable routine/reset (Ch 19). Do NOT build documented standards or schedules (Standardize) or maintenance-over-time systems (Sustain): the Shine list is a target inventory plus owners, not a standard or a schedule.
