## Hero device (frozen): the Shine list (targets by kind, a name on every line)
One whole-home inventory of everything that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by the four kinds of target, with a person's name written against each line. It makes the invisible targets visible (so they get read) and makes the ownership visible (so the work is shared, not silently dumped).
> **The rule for targets (frozen):** Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.
> **The rule for assignments (frozen):** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target.

## The four target categories (frozen, home-scaled from source)
1. **Surfaces** — floors, walls, windows, counters, shelves, doors.
2. **Equipment and fixtures** — appliances, taps and sinks, furniture, tools.
3. **Stored things** — what lives inside cabinets, closets, drawers, and boxes (cleaned and checked, not re-sorted).
4. **Point of use** — the high-touch spots your hands hit most: handles, switches, remotes, the launch pad.

## Definition box (frozen)
> A Shine list is one shared inventory of everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by kind of target, with a person's name against each one, so nothing is forgotten and no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands.

## The "clean the whole thing" payoff (frozen, the invisible-target insight)
> The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads.

## The assignments reframe (frozen, distinct from Ch 16 "clean to see" and Ch 15 "your room to their room")
> When cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness, and about a home that stays cared for whether or not one person is watching.

## Choosing, not assigning (frozen)
> A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent. So let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers, and share out the jobs nobody wants instead of leaving them all with one person.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> List every target that needs cleaning, group it by kind so nothing hides, and put a name against each one. A Shine you have written down and shared is a Shine that actually happens, and it happens to the whole home, not just the parts that show and not just to the one person who always ends up doing it.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Walk one room and write down five things that need regular cleaning but that nobody has ever been assigned, the top of something, the inside of something, a handle everyone touches. You just started your Shine list.
- **Family Challenge:** Make the list together and let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers. A job you chose is a job you will actually do; a job you were handed is a job you will resent.
- **6S Tip:** Group targets by kind, surfaces, equipment, stored things, point of use, not by room. Grouping by kind catches the things a room-by-room sweep always skips, like the tops of the cupboards and the handles in every doorway.
- **Common Mistake:** Quietly assigning the whole list to yourself and calling it easier. It is easier today, and it is exactly why Shine collapses by next month. A list with a name on every line is not more work; it is the same work, finally shared.
