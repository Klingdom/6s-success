# Chapter 17 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Shine Targets and Assignments

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Anti-shame voice, fairness not lecture, no force metaphors, permission not exhortation.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** In most homes, all the cleaning quietly falls to one person.

**Body:** Not by decision. By default. Swipe for the second chapter of Shine, where a sheet of paper names what needs cleaning and shares who does it.

---

## Slide 2: One zone clean, a whole house unnamed

**Headline:** One corner is clean. The rest of the home is still unnamed.

**Body:** If Shine stops at the launch pad, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet. Two walls stand in the way, and they come in order.

---

## Slide 3: The two walls

**Headline:** First you do not know everything that needs cleaning. Then, even if you did, who does it?

**Body:** You know the surfaces you look at, not the top of the wardrobe or the coils behind the fridge. And in most homes the work falls on one person, the one who notices first and minds most.

---

## Slide 4: See targets by kind, not by room

**Headline:** A room-by-room sweep always misses the same spots.

**Body:** Your eye walks into a room and lands where it always lands. Sorting the world into a few kinds of target drags the overlooked ones into the light.

---

## Slide 5: The four kinds of target

**Headline:** Four kinds catch almost everything.

**Body:** Surfaces (floors, walls, windows, counters). Equipment and fixtures (appliances, taps, furniture). Stored things (inside cabinets, closets, drawers). Point of use (handles, switches, remotes), the one people forget most.

---

## Slide 6: The Shine list

**Headline:** A sheet of paper and about twenty minutes.

**Body:** A Shine list is one shared inventory of everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by kind of target, with a person's name against each one, so nothing is forgotten and no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands.

---

## Slide 7: Why writing it down is half the work

**Headline:** You cannot share a feeling of obligation. You can share a list.

**Body:** A target on a list is a target someone can see, question, and pick up. A target that lives only in your sense of unease gets cleaned by you, resentfully, or not at all.

---

## Slide 8: The rule for targets

**Headline:** Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.

**Body:** The front of the microwave wipes clean and tells you nothing; the vent underneath is what costs you the microwave. The surface that faces away is the one that fails.

---

## Slide 9: The rule for assignments

**Headline:** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target.

**Body:** A list of targets with no names is just a longer, more detailed version of the guilt you started with. Nobody chose to dump it on one person. The absence of names chose it.

---

## Slide 10: Fairness, not control

**Headline:** A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness.

**Body:** When cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. Names keep the home cared for whether or not one person is watching.

---

## Slide 11: Let people choose

**Headline:** A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent.

**Body:** Make the list together and let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers. Match jobs to ability. Share out or rotate the ones nobody wants.

---

**Headline:** You have the what and the who. The plan is real and it is shared.
