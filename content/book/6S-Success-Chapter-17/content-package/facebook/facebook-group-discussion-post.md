# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the dirt that matters is the dirt you never see) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, the small idea that changed how I think about cleaning my whole home, and then I want to hear the most overlooked spot you have ever finally noticed.

For years I cleaned by instinct. Whatever was in front of me, whatever was bothering me that day. Which means I wiped the same visible handful of surfaces over and over and never once touched the rest.

Then I read one line that reorganized the whole thing. Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.

The dirt that matters is almost never the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. The front of the microwave wipes clean in a second and tells you nothing. The greasy vent underneath is the part that will one day cost you the microwave. The surface that faces the room is the one you clean, and the surface that faces away is the one that fails.

So instead of cleaning on a whim, I made a list. One page, and I grouped it by kind of target rather than by room: surfaces, equipment, stored things, and the high-touch points like handles and switches. Grouping it that way dragged all the hidden spots into the open. My list was longer than I expected, and that was not a verdict on my home. It was just the first time all of it had ever been written in one place.

There is a second half to this, which is putting a name against each line so it is not all silently on one person, but I will save that for the comments because it is a whole conversation on its own.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the most overlooked thing in your home that you finally noticed once you actually looked? The top of a wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of a cabinet, a handle nobody had cleaned in years? What had been quietly hiding in plain sight?

I will go first in the comments with the extractor fan I had not looked at since we moved in. 😄

(If anyone wants this written out properly, it is from Chapter 17 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the most useful reframe of cleaning I have come across.)
