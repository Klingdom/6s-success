# Facebook Longform Post: The List That Stopped It All Landing on Me

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 17 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the second chapter of Shine, the third S. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA. No em dashes.

---

Last month I cleaned one small corner of my home properly and it caught two little problems before they turned into big ones. I was proud of it. Then I looked at the rest of the house and thought, fine, I will just clean the rest too. And I ran straight into two walls.

The first wall was that I did not actually know everything that needed cleaning. I knew the surfaces I look at every day. I had no real picture of the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of the medicine cabinet, the handle of every door. The stuff that never announces itself, because I never see it.

The second wall was quieter and heavier. Even if I listed all of it, who was going to do it? Because the honest answer in my home was one person. The same person. Me, mostly, absorbing it until it felt like my personality rather than a pile of unassigned jobs.

So I made a list, and it fixed both walls at once. I wrote four headings down a page: surfaces, equipment, stored things, and the high-touch points like handles and switches. Then I walked the house and listed the real targets under each. Grouping by kind, instead of by room, dragged all the hidden spots into the open.

Here is the line that carried the whole thing.

"Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows."

The surface that faces the room is the one you clean. The surface that faces away is the one that fails.

Then the harder half. I went down the list and put a name against every line, including the ones I would have silently kept for myself.

"A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target."

We made the list together and let people pick their targets first. A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent.

Nothing new got cleaned that day. It was just a plan. But it was a shared one, which is further than most homes ever get.
