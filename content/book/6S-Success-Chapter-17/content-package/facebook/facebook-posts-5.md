## Post 1 · One corner clean, a whole house unnamed

You clean one corner properly and it works. But a home is not one corner, and if the cleaning stops there, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet, exactly as before.

So the obvious next thought is, fine, I will clean the rest too. And that runs into two walls at once. First, you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning, because the targets that most need it are the ones you never see. Second, even if you listed it all, who is going to do it? In most homes the honest answer is one person, the same person, every time.

"This chapter fixes both problems with one artifact, and the artifact is almost comically simple: a list."

## Post 2 · Stop thinking room by room

A room-by-room sweep always misses the same things, because your eye walks into a room and lands where it always lands. The trick is to stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind.

There are four kinds, and between them they catch almost everything. Surfaces: floors, walls, windows, counters, shelves, doors. Equipment and fixtures: the appliances, the taps, the furniture, the working things that get dirty and can quietly fail. Stored things: whatever lives inside the cabinets, closets, and drawers, cleaned and checked, not re-sorted. And point of use, the one people forget most: the high-touch spots your hands hit a hundred times a day, the handles, the switches, the remotes.

Run those four through your home like a net and you will be surprised what surfaces.

## Post 3 · Clean the whole thing

Here is the single most useful idea in this chapter, and it is worth more than any product or technique.

"Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows."

The dirt that shows is almost never the dirt that matters. The front of the microwave wipes clean in a second and tells you nothing. The greasy vent underneath is the part that will eventually cost you the microwave. The fridge looks fine. The dusty coils behind it are why it runs hot and dies early.

"The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads."

Over and over, the surface that faces the room is the one you clean, and the surface that faces away is the one that fails.

## Post 4 · A name on every line

This is where most cleaning plans quietly die. A list of targets with no names attached is just a longer, more detailed version of the guilt you started with.

Think about why the whole load falls on one person. It is rarely a decision. It is a default. A target that is nobody's job in particular becomes the job of whoever notices it and cannot let it sit, and that is the same person every time. Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it.

"A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target."

So go down the list and assign every line, including the ones you would have silently kept for yourself. This is said with zero blame, because a home where the cleaning fell to one person is the common case, not a moral failing. The fix is not trying harder. It is a name on every line.

## Post 5 · Let people choose, and the needle holds

A name on every line is the goal, but how the names get there decides whether the plan lasts a week or a year.

"A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent. So let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers, and share out the jobs nobody wants instead of leaving them all with one person."

Make the list together. A young child can genuinely own the light switches and the low handles, and will do it proudly. An adult takes the oven and the fridge coils. And the jobs nobody wants, share them out or rotate them, so the worst of it moves around instead of always landing in the same place.

One honest note. Nothing new got cleaned today, so the friction meter holds exactly where the first Shine step left it. Making a list and putting names to it is real work, but it is not yet cleaning. A dashed arrow shows where the needle moves next, once the plan is carried out and the whole home, not just one corner, gets its cleaning. The goal is still ahead.
