Last chapter you cleaned one corner and let it show you what it was hiding. It worked, and it was meant to be small. But a home is not one corner, and if Shine stops there, the rest of the house wears out in the quiet. Chapter 17 of 6S Success: Home Edition.

2/
The next thought is obvious: fine, I will clean the rest too. It runs into two walls. First, you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning, only the surfaces you look at. Second, and heavier: even if you listed it all, who is going to do it?

(254 chars)

3/
That is the trap of cleaning by instinct. It cleans what is in front of you and what bothers you today, the same visible handful over and over, and never touches the rest. The counter gets wiped daily; the top of the cupboards gets wiped when you move house.

(261 chars)

---

4/
And underneath the what sits the harder question, the who. In most homes the cleaning is not shared so much as absorbed, by one person, invisibly, until it feels like their personality rather than a pile of unassigned jobs. This chapter fixes both with one list.

(265 chars)

---

5/
The trick, straight from the original method, is to stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind. A room-by-room sweep always misses the same spots. Sorting the world into a few kinds of target drags the overlooked ones into the light.

(252 chars)

---

6/
There are four kinds. Surfaces: floors, walls, windows, counters, doors. Equipment and fixtures: appliances, taps, furniture. Stored things: what lives inside cabinets and drawers. Point of use: the handles, switches, and remotes your hands hit all day.

(256 chars)

---

7/
Now make the list. It takes a sheet of paper and about twenty minutes. Write the four kinds as headings, walk your home category by category, and list the real targets under each. Not every object you own, the things that genuinely need regular cleaning and inspection.

(272 chars)

---

8/
"A Shine list is one shared inventory of everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by kind of target, with a person's name against each one, so nothing is forgotten and no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands."

(252 chars)

9/
Half the value is done just by writing it. You cannot share a feeling of obligation. You can share a list. The invisible pile of things you ought to get to becomes a finite set of lines that someone can see, question, and pick up.

(233 chars)

10/
Why the list beats instinct:

"Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows."

The front of the microwave wipes clean and tells you nothing. The greasy vent underneath is the part that costs you the microwave. The surface that faces away is the one that fails.

(271 chars)

---

11/
"The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads."

(255 chars)

---

12/
Then the part most plans skip:

"A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target."

A target that is nobody's job flows to whoever notices first, the same person every time. Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it. So name every line.

(276 chars)

---

13/
And how the names get there decides everything.

"A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent."

Let people pick their targets first, match jobs to who can do them, and share out or rotate the ones nobody wants.
