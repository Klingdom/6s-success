# Chapter 17 Calls to Action: Shine Targets and Assignments

This is the second chapter of Part Five, and Shine continues. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible first move toward the plan: walk one room and write down five things that need regular cleaning but that nobody has ever been assigned. The CTAs match the chapter's tone of fairness and relief, not exhortation: the load falling on one person is a default to be fixed, not a moral failing, and the fix is a list with shared names, not trying harder. The move costs about twenty minutes and a sheet of paper, because the value is in naming the targets and naming who, not in cleaning anything new today.

## Primary CTA
Start your Shine list. "Walk one room and write down five things that need regular cleaning but that nobody has ever been assigned, the top of something, the inside of something, a handle everyone touches. You just started your Shine list. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "One zone is clean, but the rest of the home is still unnamed, and Shine that belongs to no one lands on the same tired pair of hands. Chapter 17 lists every target worth cleaning, groups it by kind so nothing hides, and puts a name against each one, so the whole home gets read and the work gets shared. Read it free online, then Chapter 18 gives each target its how, the quick methods that make cleaning fast enough to actually happen."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Walk one room and write down five things that need regular cleaning but that nobody has ever been assigned, the top of something, the inside of something, a handle everyone touches. You just started your Shine list.
- **Family Challenge:** Make the list together and let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers. A job you chose is a job you will actually do; a job you were handed is a job you will resent.
- **6S Tip:** Group targets by kind, surfaces, equipment, stored things, point of use, not by room. Grouping by kind catches the things a room-by-room sweep always skips, like the tops of the cupboards and the handles in every doorway.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable first move toward the plan, not a burst of cleaning: walk one room and name five unassigned targets. Lead with "start your Shine list," which is concrete and doable in about twenty minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of fairness and relief. A home where the cleaning silently fell to one person is the common case, not a character flaw, and the fix is a list and shared names, not nagging or trying harder.
- The two rules (clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows; a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, put a name on every target), the invisible-target payoff, "you cannot share a feeling of obligation, you can share a list," and the choosing-not-assigning line are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is naming targets and sharing owners; nothing is attacked, blitzed, or purged. Use "spotless" only as a negated non-goal if at all.
- Scope note: this plans and shares; it does not re-argue why we clean (Chapter 16), teach how to clean, products, or tools (Chapter 18), build the repeatable routine or reset (Chapter 19), or build documented standards or schedules (Standardize and Sustain). The Shine list is a target inventory plus owners; the goal is still ahead.
