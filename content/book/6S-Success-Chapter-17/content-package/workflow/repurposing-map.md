# Chapter 17 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- One zone is clean, but the rest of the home is still unnamed, and there are two walls in order: you do not know everything that needs cleaning, and even if you did, the work falls on one person
- See targets by kind, not by room, so the overlooked ones surface; the four kinds are surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, and point of use
- The Shine list is the hero: one shared inventory grouped by kind, with a name against every line
- The rule for targets: clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows, because the surface that faces away is the one that fails
- The invisible-target payoff: the dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see; a target you never named is a surface no one ever reads
- The rule for assignments: a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one; put a name on every target
- The fairness reframe: names are not about control; when cleaning belongs to no one, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time
- Choosing beats assigning: a job you chose is a job you will actually do; a job you were handed is a job you will resent
- The friction meter HOLDS (the plan is real work but nothing new was cleaned), with a dashed forward projection toward the goal

## Cross-chapter / reuse cautions
- Keep the two Shine chapters distinct: Chapter 16 reframed cleaning as inspection and did one first pass on the launch pad; Chapter 17 turns that single pass into a whole-home plan, the list and the names. Do not re-argue why we clean beyond a one-line recap.
- Do not re-sort or re-arrange; the list cleans and checks stored things, it does not re-sort or re-arrange them (Chapters 12 to 15 stand).
- Hold the Chapter 18 idea (Cleaning Methods That Actually Work: how to clean each target, the quick methods) as a teaser only, so Chapter 17 and Chapter 18 do not blur. Chapter 17 decides what to clean and who owns it, not how.
- Do not build a repeatable routine, schedule, or reset (Chapter 19), and do not drift into the later Standardize S (documented standards, checklists) or Sustain (habits, backsliding, upkeep over time). The Shine list is a one-time target inventory plus owners, not a standard method or a maintenance system.
- Anti-shame register throughout: a home where the cleaning silently fell to one person is the common case, not a moral failing; the fix is a list and shared names, not nagging or trying harder. Keep it fairness and relief, not lecture.
- No hype words and no force or violence metaphors; the whole move is naming targets and sharing owners, and nothing is attacked, blitzed, purged, or conquered. Use "spotless" only as a negated non-goal if at all.
- The friction-meter HOLD is specific to Ch 17; it is a principled planning hold (the Shine parallel to Chapter 10's hold in Sort), not a stall. Be honest that naming and assigning is real, useful work and not yet cleaning, and that the goal is still ahead, with Safety, Standardize, and Sustain beyond.
- Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical; point syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium) back to it.
- Keep the framing plain: "target," "list," "clean," "name," "assign," "share," "kind." Group by kind, not by room.
