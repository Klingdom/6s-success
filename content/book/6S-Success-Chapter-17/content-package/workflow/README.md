## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
One zone is clean, but the rest of the home is still unnamed, and Shine that belongs to no one lands on the same tired pair of hands. Chapter 17 turns Chapter 16's single first pass into a plan that covers the whole home and shares the labor. It runs into two walls in order. First, you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning, because the targets that most need Shine are the ones you never see: the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of a cabinet, the handle every hand in the house touches. Second, even a complete list, in most homes, quietly falls to one person. The fix is one artifact, the Shine list: stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind, grouping every target into four categories, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, and point of use, so the overlooked ones surface. The rule for targets is to clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows, because the surface that faces away is the one that fails. Then the harder half, the names: a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, so put a name on every line, let people choose their targets rather than get handed them, match jobs to ability, and share or rotate the worst ones. Because the chapter plans but cleans nothing new, the friction meter holds exactly where the first Shine step left it, with a dashed forward projection showing the move to come once the targets get cleaned. Hands off to Chapter 18 · Cleaning Methods That Actually Work, which continues Part Five.
