# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: Shine Targets and Assignments

*Chapter 17, 6S Success: Home Edition. Use these for personal reflection or a group discussion. They are open-ended, with no single right answer.*

1. The chapter opens by admitting the last one was small on purpose: "But a home is not one corner, and if Shine stops at the launch pad, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet, exactly as before." Which one zone in your home has been getting all your attention, and which parts have been quietly wearing out while you looked after it?

2. The chapter names two walls that stand between you and a clean home, and takes them in order. The first is that you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning, the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of the medicine cabinet. Before reading on, what targets in your home do you suspect you have never once thought of because you never see them?

3. The second wall is quieter and heavier: even if you listed all of it, who is going to do it? In your home, be honest about who currently carries the cleaning. Is it shared, or is it absorbed by one person, "the one who notices first and minds most, carrying the whole invisible load alone"?

4. The chapter argues that cleaning by instinct "cleans what is in front of you and what is bothering you today, which means it cleans the same visible handful over and over and never once touches the rest." Which few surfaces do you clean constantly, and which have not been touched, in the chapter's phrase, since you moved house?

5. The useful trick is to stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind, because "a room-by-room sweep always misses the same things, because your eye walks into a room and lands where it always lands." Run the four kinds through your home in your mind, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use. Which kind surfaces the most targets you had been overlooking?

6. Point of use is named as the kind people forget most, the handles, switches, remotes, and the pull on the launch pad drawer, "the places that get the most human contact and almost none of the cleaning." Which high-touch spot in your home gets touched a hundred times a day and cleaned almost never?

7. The hero device is deliberately humble: "This chapter fixes both problems with one artifact, and the artifact is almost comically simple: a list." Why do you think a plain written list can succeed where good intentions and more effort have not? What does writing a target down actually change?

8. The chapter draws a sharp line: "You cannot share a feeling of obligation. You can share a list." Where have you been carrying a feeling of obligation about the home that you have never managed to hand to anyone else? What would it take to turn that feeling into lines on a page?

9. The rule for targets is "Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows," because "the surface that faces the room is the one you clean, and the surface that faces away is the one that fails." Where in your home has the visible face been kept clean while the back, the underside, or the inside was quietly failing?

10. The rule for assignments is "A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target." The chapter says the load falls on one person not by decision but by default: "Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it." Where in your household has an unnamed job silently become one person's job, just because they could not let it sit?

11. The reframe is meant to relieve, not to scold: "A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness, and about a home that stays cared for whether or not one person is watching." How does hearing assignments described as fairness, rather than as a chore chart imposed from above, change how the idea sits with you?

12. The chapter insists on how the names get there: "A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent." Think of a chore you were handed versus one you chose. How differently did you treat each, and what does that say about how your household should build the list?

13. The Common Mistake is "Quietly assigning the whole list to yourself and calling it easier." The chapter warns it is easier today and exactly why Shine collapses by next month. Which targets would you be tempted to silently keep for yourself, and what would it take to hand those out instead?

14. The friction meter holds this chapter, on purpose, because "nothing new was cleaned today," but a dashed arrow now projects forward toward the goal: "The plan is the loaded spring. The move is coming; it is simply honest to say it has not happened yet." Having made your list and shared the names, which target will get its first real cleaning this week, and who has agreed to read it, as you carry the plan into Chapter 18, where Shine gets its how?
