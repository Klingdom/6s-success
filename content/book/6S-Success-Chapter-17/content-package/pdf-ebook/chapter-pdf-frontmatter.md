# Shine Targets and Assignments

**Part Five, Shine**
Chapter 17 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

## Chapter descriptor

The second chapter of Part Five, and Shine continues. Chapter 16 reframed cleaning as inspection and did one first pass on the launch pad. But a home is not one corner, and if Shine stops there, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet. Today you turn that single pass into a plan that covers the whole home and shares the labor. First you learn to see targets by kind rather than by room, so nothing hides: surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, and point of use. Then you build one Shine list and put a name against every line, so the work is shared instead of silently landing on one person. Nothing is re-sorted or re-arranged, and nothing new is cleaned; today you make the list, and share it.

## How to read this chapter

This is the second chapter of Part Five, Shine, and it picks up exactly where the last one left off, with one zone cleaned and read and the rest of the home still unread and, in most households, quietly falling to one person. It is not about re-choosing whether a thing gets a home; you made those calls back in Sort. It is not about re-arranging anything; the layout stays where Straighten left it. It is about one job, done in two halves: make what needs cleaning visible, all of it, grouped so nothing hides, then put a name against every target.

Read it through once for the idea, then take a sheet of paper and about twenty minutes. Down the page, write the four kinds of target as headings, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use, then walk your home category by category and list the real targets under each. Grouping by kind is the trick, because a room-by-room sweep always misses the same things, the tops of the cupboards, the coils behind the fridge, the handles in every doorway. Keep one rule for the targets as you list them: clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows, because the surface that faces the room is the one you clean and the surface that faces away is the one that fails. Then do the harder half, the names, and keep the second rule: a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, put a name on every target.

Nothing here re-sorts or re-arranges; when the list touches stored things it means clean and check them, not decide again whether to keep them. Nothing here teaches how to clean, products, or tools; that is Chapter 18, and we only point to it. Nothing here builds the repeatable routine or reset; that is Chapter 19. Nothing here builds documented standards or schedules; those belong to Standardize and Sustain. Nothing re-opens Sort or Straighten. Today has one job: name every target, and name who. Because the chapter plans but cleans nothing new, the friction meter holds where the first Shine step left it, with a dashed arrow showing where it moves next.

If you only do one thing: walk one room and write down five things that need regular cleaning but that nobody has ever been assigned, the top of something, the inside of something, a handle everyone touches. You just started your Shine list.
