# Teleprompter Script: Shine Targets and Assignments

**Chapter 17, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** shine-targets-and-assignments
**Length:** about 720 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Last time you cleaned one corner.

The launch pad, by the door.

And you let the cleaning show you what it hid.

It worked. It was meant to be small.

But a home is not one corner.

If Shine stops at the launch pad,

the rest of the house wears out in the quiet.

So the next thought is, I will clean the rest too.

And that runs into two walls.

The first wall: you do not know all of what needs cleaning.

You know the surfaces you look at.

Not the top of the wardrobe.

Not the coils behind the fridge.

Not the underside of the bath mat.

The second wall is heavier.

Even if you listed it all, who does it?

In most homes, one person. The same person.

Carrying the whole invisible load alone.

This chapter takes both walls in order.

First, make what needs cleaning visible.

The trick is to stop thinking room by room.

Start thinking kind by kind.

There are four kinds of target.

Surfaces. Floors, walls, windows, counters, shelves, doors.

Equipment and fixtures. Appliances, taps, furniture, tools.

Stored things. What lives inside cabinets, drawers, and boxes.

Cleaned and checked. Not re-sorted.

And point of use. The one people forget most.

The handles, the switches, the remotes.

The spots your hands hit a hundred times a day.

Run those four kinds through your home like a net.

Now make the list.

A sheet of paper, about twenty minutes.

Write the four kinds as headings.

Walk your home and list the real targets under each.

Here is the definition. Keep it.

A Shine list is one shared inventory.

Everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection.

Grouped by kind of target.

With a person's name against each one.

So nothing is forgotten.

And no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands.

Half the value is done just by writing it.

You cannot share a feeling of obligation.

You can share a list.

Now the most useful idea in the chapter.

Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.

The dirt that shows is rarely the dirt that matters.

The front of the microwave wipes clean and tells you nothing.

The greasy vent underneath is what costs you the microwave.

The fridge looks fine.

The dusty coils behind it are why it dies early.

The surface that faces the room is the one you clean.

The surface that faces away is the one that fails.

A target you never named is a surface no one reads.

Now the part that keeps the plan alive.

A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one.

Put a name on every target.

The load falls on one person by default.

Not by decision.

An unassigned target flows to whoever cannot let it sit.

The same person, every time.

Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it.

A name against every target is not about control.

It is about fairness.

So assign every line. Not most lines. Every line.

Including the ones you would have kept for yourself.

But how the names get there matters.

A job you chose is a job you will actually do.

A job you were handed is a job you will resent.

So make the list together.

Let people pick before anyone gets the leftovers.

A child can own the light switches and the low handles.

An adult takes the oven and the fridge coils.

And the jobs nobody wants, share them or rotate them.

A home kept by everyone asks less of anyone.

Now go look at the friction meter.

And notice it does not move.

That is correct.

Last chapter you cleaned, and the needle stepped.

This chapter you made a list and put names to it.

Real work. But nothing new was cleaned today.

So the needle holds where the first Shine step left it.

The meter only moves when the room does.

But look just ahead of the needle.

A new dashed arrow, projecting toward the goal.

That is where it goes next.

Once the targets get cleaned.

And the whole home gets read on a rhythm.

The plan is the loaded spring.

The move is coming. It just has not happened yet.

So here is your job.

Walk one room.

Write down five things that need regular cleaning.

Things nobody has ever been assigned.

The top of something. The inside of something.

A handle everyone touches.

You just started your Shine list.

Now name who reads it.
