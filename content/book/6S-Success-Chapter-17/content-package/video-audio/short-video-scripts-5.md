## SHORT 1: One zone clean, a whole house unnamed
**Topic: the starting gap, one corner is done and the rest of the home is unnamed and unassigned. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You cleaned one corner and it worked. Then you realized a home is not one corner.

**Script:**
Last time you cleaned one corner, the launch pad, and let the cleaning show you what it was hiding. It worked. But a home is not one corner, and if Shine stops at the launch pad, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet, exactly as before. So the obvious next thought is, fine, I will clean the rest too. And that runs into two walls at once. First, you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning. You know the surfaces you look at, not the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the underside of the bath mat, the handle of every door. Second, and heavier: even if you listed it all, who is going to do it? In most homes the honest answer is one person, the same person, carrying the whole invisible load alone. This chapter takes both walls in order.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "One zone is clean. A whole house is still unnamed."
- 14s: "Wall one: you don't know all of what needs cleaning."
- 28s: "Wall two: who is going to do it?"
- 38s: "In most homes, one person carries it alone."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The cleaned launch pad drawn bright with a green calm-dot, and around it the rest of the home in faint outline, crowded with small unnamed targets, each with a tiny blank name-tag: the top of a cupboard, behind the fridge, inside a cabinet, a door handle, under a mat. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 2: See targets by kind, not by room
**Topic: the four kinds of target, the seeing tool. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A room-by-room clean misses the same spots every time. Here is what catches them.

**Script:**
Before you can list what needs cleaning, you have to see it, and the trick is to stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind. A room-by-room sweep always misses the same things, because your eye walks in and lands where it always lands. There are four kinds of target. Surfaces: floors, walls, windows, counters, shelves, doors. Equipment and fixtures: appliances, taps and sinks, furniture, tools. Stored things: whatever lives inside cabinets, closets, drawers, and boxes, cleaned and checked, not re-sorted. And point of use, the one people forget most: the high-touch spots your hands hit a hundred times a day, the handles, the switches, the remotes. Run those four kinds through your home like a net and you will be surprised what surfaces. Four angles of approach, so the targets that hide from one get caught by another.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Group by kind, not by room."
- 12s: "Surfaces. Equipment and fixtures."
- 26s: "Stored things. Point of use."
- 38s: "Four angles, so nothing hides."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A calm four-group reference. Surfaces (a floor, a window, a shelf). Equipment and fixtures (a kettle, a tap, a chair). Stored things (an open drawer, a cabinet interior, a box). Point of use (a door handle, a light switch, a remote). Each group headed in slate. Warm line-and-flat-color style.

## SHORT 3: Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows
**Topic: the invisible-target payoff. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The dirt that shows is almost never the dirt that matters.

**Script:**
Here is the single most useful idea in this chapter: clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows. The dirt that shows is almost never the dirt that matters. The front of the microwave wipes clean in a second and tells you nothing; the vent on its underside, thick with grease, is the part that will eventually cost you the microwave. The fridge looks fine; the coils behind it, furred with dust, are why it runs hot and dies early. The sink gleams; the overflow hole you never look into is where the smell is coming from. Over and over, the surface that faces the room is the one you clean, and the surface that faces away is the one that fails. The dirt that matters is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows."
- 14s: "The clean front tells you nothing. The greasy vent is the cost."
- 28s: "The surface that faces away is the one that fails."
- 40s: "A target you never named is a surface no one reads."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Paired face-and-hidden-side reveals: a clean microwave front over a greasy underside vent, a gleaming fridge front over dust-furred coils, a gleaming sink over its unlooked-into overflow. Warm light, small honey highlight on each hidden side.

## SHORT 4: Put a name on every target
**Topic: assignments, ending the one-person default. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one.

**Script:**
Most cleaning plans quietly die right here. A list of targets with no names attached is just a longer, more detailed version of the guilt you started with. A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target. Think about why the load falls on one person in the first place. It is rarely a decision, it is a default. A target that is nobody's job in particular becomes the job of whoever notices it and cannot let it sit, and that same person notices first every time, so every unassigned target flows toward them like water finding the low point. Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it. When cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness. So go down your list and assign every line. Not most lines. Every line, including the ones you would have silently kept for yourself.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one."
- 14s: "It's not a decision. It's a default."
- 28s: "Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it."
- 42s: "Assign every line. Not most. Every line."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The finished list, then every unassigned line drifting toward one pair of tired hands like water finding the low point. Then a pen goes down the page writing a different name against each line, an adult's, a child's, and the drift stops. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 5: The needle holds
**Topic: the honest planning-hold with a dashed forward projection. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** This time the needle does not move, and that is exactly right.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, and this time notice that it does not move, and that this is correct. Last chapter the needle took its first Shine step, because you actually cleaned something and caught a problem; the room's condition changed. This chapter you made a list and put names to it, which is real and necessary work, but nothing new got cleaned today. Not one target was scrubbed. So the needle holds exactly where the first Shine step left it, and it should, because the meter only moves when the room itself changes. But look just ahead of the live needle and you will see something new: a dashed arrow, projecting forward toward the goal. That is where the needle goes next, once the targets get their cleaning and the whole home gets read on a rhythm. The plan is the loaded spring. The move is coming; it is simply honest to say it has not happened yet.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Nothing new was cleaned today."
- 14s: "So the needle holds. That's correct."
- 28s: "But there's a new dashed arrow ahead of it."
- 40s: "The plan is the loaded spring. The move is coming."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The friction meter, holding. The live needle sits exactly where Chapter 16 left it, a short step past the Straighten-complete tick. A new dashed forward-projection arrow curves from the needle tip toward the GOAL crosshair. Chapter 15's Straighten-complete milestone sits just behind; earlier steps fainter; the GOAL crosshair stays ahead on the green calm side.
