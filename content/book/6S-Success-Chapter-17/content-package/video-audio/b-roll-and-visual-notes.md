# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Shine Targets and Assignments

**Chapter 17, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** shine-targets-and-assignments
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero device is the Shine list: one shared inventory of everything in the home that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by the four kinds of target, with a person's name written against every line. Show it as a single sheet with a slightly hand-made, pinned-to-the-fridge feel, four headings down it in slate (surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use), a few target lines under each, and a small written name to the right of every line, some in an adult's hand, some in a child's, so the whole sheet reads as shared.
- There are two rules and they carry the whole chapter. The rule for targets is: clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows. The rule for assignments is: a job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, put a name on every target. Show them as plain on-screen text, calm and legible.
- The starting point is the finished, cleaned, read launch pad from Chapter 16, the entryway zone, drawn bright and warm with a green calm-dot beside it, clearly cared for. Around it, the rest of the home is drawn in faint outline and quietly crowded with small targets nobody has named: the dusty top of a cupboard, the gap behind the refrigerator, the inside of a bathroom cabinet, a doorway handle, the underside of a doormat. Beside each of those sits a tiny blank name-tag, empty, waiting. One bright, finished spot in a house full of unclaimed work.
- The four kinds of target are the seeing tool: surfaces (floors, walls, windows, counters, shelves, doors); equipment and fixtures (appliances, taps and sinks, furniture, tools); stored things (what lives inside cabinets, closets, drawers, boxes, cleaned and checked, not re-sorted); point of use (the high-touch spots hands hit most, handles, switches, remotes, the launch pad drawer pull). Show them as a calm four-group reference, grouped by kind, not by room.
- The move this chapter is planning, not cleaning. Nothing new gets scrubbed on screen. Do NOT stage re-Sorting or re-arranging, and when the list touches stored things, it means clean and check them, not decide again whether to keep them. Do NOT teach cleaning methods, products, or tools (that is Chapter 18), and do NOT build a schedule, routine, or reset (that is Chapter 19). The only action is making one list and putting a name on every line.
- The invisible-target idea is the payoff: the dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see, it is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. Show it as the tops and backs and undersides and insides coming into view, the surface that faces away rather than the surface that faces the room.
- The assignments idea is the second payoff and the live nerve of the chapter: when cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. Show the unassigned targets flowing steadily toward one pair of hands like water finding the low point, then a name written against each line ending the default. This is about fairness, not control.
- The factory-floor provenance is referenced lightly and home-scaled: Seiso targets and assignments and the traditional target categories, surfaces, equipment, stored items, point of use, home-scaled from the Toyota Production System. Reference lightly, do not overclaim.
- The friction meter HOLDS this chapter. The live needle stays exactly where the first Shine step left it in Chapter 16, because nothing new was cleaned today. What is new is a dashed forward-projection arrow curving from the needle tip further toward the calm side, toward the GOAL crosshair, showing where the needle steps once the plan is carried out. Chapter 15's Straighten-complete milestone sits just behind the live needle; the earlier Straighten and Sort steps are fainter still. The GOAL crosshair stays out on the green calm side, clearly ahead. This must read as a principled hold, planning not stalling, not a finish line.
- Warm daylight, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look. "Spotless" is not the aim, on screen or in tone.
- Keep the whole thing calm and unhurried, and let it carry a note of relief and fairness, because this touches a chore people quietly resent or feel judged by. No force or violence framing. Nothing is attacked, blitzed, purged, or divided-and-conquered as combat. The tone is anti-shame: a home where the cleaning silently fell to one person is the common case, not a moral failing, and the fix is a list and shared names, not trying harder.

## CORE SHOT LIST

### Shot 1: One zone clean, a whole house unnamed (hero opener)
- The cleaned launch pad from Chapter 16 drawn bright and warm, a green calm-dot beside it, clearly read and cared for. Around it, the rest of the home in faint outline, quietly crowded with small unnamed targets: the dusty top of a cupboard, the gap behind the refrigerator, the inside of a bathroom cabinet, a doorway handle, the underside of a doormat. Beside each sits a tiny blank name-tag, empty, waiting.
- Critical detail: the first pass proved the idea on one zone, but the whole home is still full of targets that are both unread and unassigned. The blank name-tags say the real question is not just what to clean but who.
- Coverage: one wide of the whole home, the one bright corner against the faint crowd; one slow push toward a blank name-tag beside the top of a cupboard; one tilt across three or four more unnamed targets, each with its empty tag.
- **On-screen text moment:** "One zone is clean. The rest of the house is a crowd of targets no one has named."
- **Lower third:** Chapter 17 · Shine Targets and Assignments

### Shot 2: Two walls, the what and the who (the gap)
- The bright launch pad again, then two faint walls rise across the rest of the home. On the first wall, a scatter of targets you never see, the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of the medicine cabinet, the underside of the bath mat, every door handle. On the second wall, heavier and quieter, a single pair of tired hands, and every unassigned target sliding toward them.
- The point: the obvious next thought is "I will clean the rest too," and it runs into two walls at once. First, you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning. Second, even if you listed it all, in most homes the honest answer to who is one person, the same person, carrying the whole invisible load alone.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Two walls: you don't know all of what needs cleaning, and who is going to do it?"
- **Lower third:** The load falls on one person by default, not by choice

### Shot 3: What counts as a target (see by kind, not by room)
- A calm four-group reference. Surfaces (a floor, a window, a shelf). Equipment and fixtures (a kettle, a tap, a chair). Stored things (an open drawer, a cabinet interior, a box). Point of use (a door handle, a light switch, a TV remote). Each group headed in slate, examples labeled in sans. Then a faint room-by-room sweep walks in and lands only where the eye always lands, missing the same spots, while the by-kind net catches them.
- The point: stop thinking room by room and start thinking kind by kind. A room-by-room sweep always misses the same things; sorting the world into four kinds of target drags the overlooked ones into the light, especially the stored things behind closed doors and the handles everyone touches.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Four kinds of target: surfaces, equipment, stored things, point of use." then "Group by kind, not by room."
- **Lower third:** Four angles of approach, so nothing hides

### Shot 4: The Shine list (the hero device made literal)
- The list itself, drawn as a simple sheet pinned to the fridge. Four headings run down it in slate, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use, with two or three target lines under each. To the right of every line is a small written name, some in an adult's hand, some in a child's, so the whole sheet reads as shared. One line near the bottom still has a blank name space with a light circle around it, the last target waiting to be claimed.
- The point: it takes a sheet of paper and about twenty minutes. Down the page, write the four kinds as headings, then walk the home category by category and list the real targets under each, the things that genuinely need regular cleaning and inspection. Half the value is already done just by writing it: you cannot share a feeling of obligation, but you can share a list.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A Shine list: one page, four kinds, a name on every line." then "You cannot share a feeling of obligation. You can share a list."
- **Lower third:** The whole device is a list, built and shared a particular way

### Shot 5: Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows (the invisible targets)
- A quiet series of paired reveals, each showing the face and the hidden side of the same thing. The front of the microwave wipes clean and tells you nothing; its underside vent is thick with grease. The visible half of the fridge looks fine; the coils behind it are furred with dust. The bathroom sink gleams; the overflow hole you never look into is where the smell comes from. Over and over, the surface that faces the room comes up clean and the surface that faces away is the one that fails.
- The point: the dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. The list, built by kind, deliberately includes the tops and the backs and the undersides and the insides, the targets your eye slides past precisely because they do not show. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows." then "The surface that faces away is the one that fails."
- **Lower third:** The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see

### Shot 6: Put a name on every target (assignments)
- The finished list again, then every unassigned line drifting toward one pair of hands like water finding the low point, the same hands every time. Then a pen goes down the page writing a different name against each line, an adult's, a child's, a partner's, and the drift stops. Each line now has an owner. Include the lines someone would have silently kept for themselves.
- The point: a target that is nobody's job in particular becomes the job of whoever notices it and cannot let it sit, and that same person notices first every time. Nobody chose this; the absence of names chose it. Writing a name against each line ends the default. A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target." then "Not most lines. Every line."
- **Lower third:** A name against every target is about fairness, not control

### Shot 7: Choosing, not assigning (how the names get there)
- The household around the list together, hands reaching in to claim targets before anything is handed out. A young child proudly puts a name on the light switches and the low door handles; an adult takes the oven and the fridge coils. The jobs nobody wants, the bins, the bathroom, get shared out or a small rotation arrow set beside them, so the worst of it moves around instead of always landing in the same place.
- The point: a name on every line is the goal, but how the names get there decides whether the plan lasts a week or a year. A job you chose is a job you will actually do; a job you were handed is a job you will resent. Let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers, match targets to who can do them, and share out or rotate the jobs nobody wants.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A job you chose is a job you will actually do." then "Let people pick before anyone gets handed the leftovers."
- **Lower third:** A home kept by everyone asks less of anyone

The dashed arrow says exactly where it moves next, once the targets get their cleaning in the next chapter and the whole home starts getting read on a rhythm in the one after.
