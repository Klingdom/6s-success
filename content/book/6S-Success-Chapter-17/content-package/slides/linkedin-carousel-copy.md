## Shine: Targets and Assignments

### Slide 1 (hook)

**One zone is clean. The rest of the house is a crowd of targets no one has named.**

Last chapter you cleaned one corner and let the cleaning show you what it was hiding. It worked. But a home is not one corner, and if Shine stops at the launch pad, the rest of the house goes on wearing out in the quiet.

Swipe for the plan: list every target worth cleaning, group it so nothing hides, and put a name against each one.

The rest of the house is drawn in faint ghost #8A8073 outline and quietly crowded with small unnamed targets: the dusty top of a cupboard, the gap behind the fridge, the inside of a cabinet, a doorway handle, the underside of a mat. Beside each sits a tiny blank name-tag, empty, waiting. Terracotta chapter label.

### Slide 2

**Two walls stand between you and a clean home, and they come in order.**

The first wall: you do not actually know everything that needs cleaning. You know the surfaces you look at, not the top of the wardrobe, the coils behind the fridge, the inside of the medicine cabinet.

The second is quieter and heavier: even if you listed all of it, who is going to do it? In most homes the honest answer is one person, the same person, carrying the whole invisible load alone.

This chapter takes both walls in order. First the list. Then the names.

[VISUAL] Two faint ghost #8A8073 walls across the page. On the first, a scatter of unnamed targets (a fridge coil, a cabinet interior, a handle). On the second, a single figure carrying an armful of blank name-tags. Slate labels: "what needs cleaning" and "who does it." No verdict colours; nobody is failing here.

### Slide 3

**Instinct cleans the same visible handful over and over, and never once touches the rest.**

Cleaning by instinct cleans what is in front of you and what is bothering you today. The kitchen counter gets wiped daily; the top of the cupboards gets wiped when you move house. The bathroom sink is cleaned constantly; the extractor fan clogged with dust has not been looked at in years.

None of this is laziness. It is just what happens with no plan: the loud targets get all the attention and the quiet ones get none, until a quiet one fails.

[VISUAL] The same room drawn twice on paper ground. Left, "BY INSTINCT": a bright ring worn into the visible counter and sink, the tops and backs in ghost #8A8073 haze, untouched. Right, the quiet targets ringed lightly in friction spark #CB4B36. Slate tag: "the loud spots get all of it."

### Slide 4

**Stop thinking room by room. Start thinking kind by kind.**

A room-by-room sweep always misses the same things, because your eye walks into a room and lands where it always lands. Sorting the world into a few kinds of target drags the overlooked ones into the light.

There are four kinds, and between them they catch almost everything: **surfaces**, **equipment and fixtures**, **stored things**, and **point of use**, the high-touch handles and switches everyone touches and no one cleans.

[VISUAL] Four calm, slate-headed groups in a simple grid on paper ground, each with two or three warm home examples. Surfaces (a floor, a window, a shelf). Equipment and fixtures (a kettle, a tap, a chair). Stored things (an open drawer, a cabinet interior, a box). Point of use (a door handle, a light switch, a remote). Ink line-and-flat-color style.

### Slide 5

**A Shine list is one page, four kinds, a name on every line.**

"A Shine list is one shared inventory of everything in a space that needs regular cleaning and inspection, grouped by kind of target, with a person's name against each one, so nothing is forgotten and no job quietly lands on a single pair of hands."

It takes a sheet of paper and about twenty minutes. Write the four kinds as headings, walk your home category by category, and list the real targets under each.

### Slide 6

**You cannot share a feeling of obligation. You can share a list.**

Half the value is done just by writing it. A target on a list is a target someone can see, question, and pick up. A target that lives only in your sense of unease gets cleaned by you, resentfully, or not at all.

Do not aim for a perfect list. A first Shine list is usually longer than people expect, and that is not a verdict on how dirty your home is. It is just the first time all of this has been written in one place instead of carried in one head.

### Slide 7

**Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows.**

The dirt that shows is almost never the dirt that matters. The front of the microwave wipes clean in a second and tells you nothing; the vent on its underside, thick with grease, is the part that costs you the microwave. The visible half of the fridge looks fine; the coils behind it are why it runs hot and dies early.

"The dirt that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine, under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you never named is a surface no one ever reads."

### Slide 8

**A job that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Put a name on every target.**

A list of targets with no names attached is just a longer, more detailed version of the guilt you started with. The name is not a detail. It is the whole difference between a plan that happens and a plan that does not.

Go down your list and assign every line. Not most lines. Every line, including the ones you would have silently kept for yourself.

Beneath, a list where each blank name-tag is being filled in with a written name, one still blank near the bottom with a light circle around it, the last target waiting to be claimed. Green calm-dots beside the claimed lines.

### Slide 9

**Nobody chose this. The absence of names chose it.**

The load falls on one person by default, not by decision. A target that is nobody's job in particular becomes the job of whoever notices it and cannot let it sit, and that same person notices first every time. Every unassigned target flows toward them like water finding the low point.

"When cleaning belongs to no one in particular, it belongs to whoever cannot stand it any longer, which is the same person every time. A name against every target is not about control. It is about fairness, and about a home that stays cared for whether or not one person is watching."

[VISUAL] The frozen assignments reframe on panel in Fraunces, slate rules. Beside it, a page tilted so every unnamed target slides in ghost #8A8073 toward one low corner and one waiting figure. A slate note: "unassigned work finds the low point."

### Slide 10

**A job you chose is a job you will actually do. A job you were handed is a job you will resent.**

"So let people pick their targets before anyone gets handed the leftovers, and share out the jobs nobody wants instead of leaving them all with one person."

Make the list together. Match targets to who can do them: a young child can own the light switches and the low door handles, and will do it proudly if it is theirs. And the jobs nobody wants, share them out or rotate them, so the worst of it moves around instead of always landing in the same place.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the frozen choosing line set large in Fraunces, slate rules. Beneath, a household around one list, hands reaching to claim their own lines, a child's name against the low switches, an adult's against the oven. A small honey rotation-arrow on the jobs nobody wants.

### Slide 11

**A home kept by everyone asks less of anyone.**

You did two things today that instinct never does on its own: you made the whole of the cleaning visible, grouped so the hidden targets could not hide, and you shared it out with a name on every line. The launch pad is no longer a lucky exception. It is the first entry on a list that now covers everything, owned by everyone.

"List every target that needs cleaning, group it by kind so nothing hides, and put a name against each one. A Shine you have written down and shared is a Shine that actually happens, and it happens to the whole home, not just the parts that show and not just to the one person who always ends up doing it."
