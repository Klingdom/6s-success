# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 17

**"Shine Targets and Assignments"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to
hold across all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single warm
red `#CB4B36` reserved for rare small accents only. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy
depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the whole home, seen against the one settled launch pad by the door that
the reader built across Chapters 12 to 15 and cleaned for the first time in Chapter 16. That first pass
proved the idea on a single zone and caught two small problems; that work is done and stays. This
chapter, the second of Part Five, continues Shine and does one narrow thing: it turns that single pass
into a plan for the WHOLE home and shares the labor. It lists every target worth cleaning, grouped by
four kinds so nothing hides, and puts a name against each one, so the whole home gets read and the work
gets shared. Nothing new is cleaned here (that is Chapter 18), nothing is re-sorted or re-arranged
(Chapters 12 to 15, done and closed), no cleaning method, product, or tool is taught (Chapter 18), and
no repeatable routine or schedule is built (Chapter 19). Keep the tone warm and kind: a home where the
cleaning silently fell to one person is the common case, not a moral failing, and the fix is a list and
shared names, not trying harder or nagging more. This chapter touches a live nerve, who does the
housework, so the register is fairness and relief, never lecture, never guilt, never a scare tone. No
force or violence framing anywhere, no attacking grime, no war on dirt, no blitz, no purge.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label or name mentioned below is a physical object in
the scene, a hand-lettered name-tag or a handwritten name on a real sheet that a household would
actually write, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered
label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 17 · SHINE`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot
between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: name-tag, whole-home, room-by-room, high-
touch, point-of-use, pinned-to-the-fridge, first-Shine-step, forward-projection.

---

## 1. One zone read, a whole home unnamed (the opener)

A warm, wide cutaway illustration of a whole home in soft morning daylight, most of it drawn in faint,
quiet outline, as if the house is lightly sketched except for one finished corner. In the bottom-left
corner the launch pad from the last chapter glows warm and complete, cleaned and cared for: a small
hooked key rack, a hook holding a coiled dog leash, a woven basket for shopping bags, a slim umbrella
stand now dry and set right, and a shallow tray for outgoing mail, with a soft green calm-dot beside it
marking it as clean and read. The rest of the house, faint and calm, is quietly crowded with small
targets nobody has named: the dusty top of a cupboard, the gap behind the refrigerator, the inside of a
bathroom cabinet, a doorway handle, the underside of a doormat. Beside each of those sits a tiny blank
name-tag, a small cream card on a loop of string, empty and waiting, no name on it. Warm paper cream and
honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a soft slate blue for the faint walls, the one launch pad corner
warm and sharp against a house drawn light and unfinished. Editorial cookbook-style illustration. One
bright, finished spot in a home full of unclaimed work.

- Mood: the first pass proved the idea on one zone, but the whole home is still full of targets that are
  both unseen and unassigned, and the blank name-tags say the real question is not just what to clean
  but who; one corner read, a whole home still unnamed.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating typeset text overlays, any clutter or
  mess drama, any sense of a dirty or neglected home, any cleaning or scrubbing underway (nothing new
  is cleaned this chapter), a scare tone, price tags, hurry, or stress; the unnamed targets are small,
  ordinary, and half-hidden, and every name-tag is genuinely blank.

## 2. The four kinds of target (see by kind, not by room)

A warm editorial illustration of a calm reference of the four kinds of cleaning target, loose and hand-
made rather than a clinical chart, arranged as four gentle groups in a two-by-two block. The first
group, surfaces, shows a floor, a window, and a shelf, the flat faces that show dirt and take the
traffic. The second, equipment and fixtures, shows a kettle, a tap, and a chair, the working things
that get dirty and can quietly fail. The third, stored things, shows an open drawer, a cabinet
interior, and a box, what lives behind closed doors, cleaned and checked. The fourth, point of use,
shows a door handle, a light switch, and a TV remote, the high-touch spots your hands hit most. Each
group is headed with its own hand-lettered kind and its examples drawn as simple recognizable objects.
Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, soft slate and honey and green kept quiet, wood-fill
warmth on the objects, no single item louder than the rest. Documentary and hand-made, as if a
household had sketched the four angles they use to make sure nothing hides.

- Mood: a room-by-room sweep always misses the same spots, so sorting the home into four kinds of
  target gives your attention four different angles of approach, and the two people forget most, stored
  things behind closed doors and the point-of-use spots everyone touches, come into view.
- Avoid: a busy clinical chart, more than four groups, cold clinical light, floating typeset text, any
  scrubbing or cleaning method or product, tools or a repair scene, tying the examples to a single
  room; keep it to the four calm groups, legible at a glance, every kind drawn as an equal.

## 3. The Shine list, a name on every line (the hero)

A warm editorial photograph of a single hand-written sheet pinned to a fridge in soft kitchen daylight,
a real household's Shine list rather than a printed form. Four headings run down the page in a plain
hand, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point of use, with two or three target lines
under each: under surfaces the kitchen floor, the bathroom mirror, the tops of the cupboards; under
equipment the oven, the washing machine, the bathroom fan; under stored things the pantry shelves and
the medicine cabinet; under point of use the door handles, the light switches, the remote, the fridge
door. To the right of every line is a small written name, some in a confident adult's hand, some in a
child's larger, rounder letters, so the whole sheet plainly reads as shared across more than one
person. One line near the bottom still has an empty name space with a light hand-drawn circle around
it, the last target waiting to be claimed. The sheet is held by a single warm-red magnet and sits very
slightly askew, honest and lived-in. Warm paper cream and honey light, ink-brown handwriting, a soft
green for the waiting circle, real paper texture, a real fridge door. Documentary and unstaged, as if
someone had just finished writing it with the family.

- Mood: the Shine list is one page, grouped by the four kinds so the hidden targets come into view,
  with a name against every line so the work is shared instead of silently dumped; the grouping and the
  names are the two halves of the whole device, and the single blank line is the plan's live edge.
- Avoid: a clean corporate form or spreadsheet, typeset overlay text, a single perfect handwriting for
  the whole sheet (the names must come from more than one hand, at least one plainly a child's), any
  cleaning underway, cold light, glossy retouching, framing the sheet as a chore chart or a guilt list;
  keep it warm, shared, hand-made, and hopeful, a plan being made together.

## 4. Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows (the invisible-target payoff)

A calm, warm illustration of the same three ordinary things read at two faces, set as two facing
panels. On the left, the part that shows: the front of a microwave wiped clean, the visible face of a
fridge looking fine, a bathroom sink gleaming, each plainly clean and each telling you nothing. On the
right, the part that fails: the same microwave's underside vent thick with grease, the coils behind the
same fridge furred with dust, the overflow hole of the same sink you never look into, the quiet faces
that face away and decide how long the things last. Between them, the quiet sense that the surface you
clean is the one that shows and the surface that fails is the one that faces away. Warm paper cream
background, honey light, ink-brown shadows, one soft warm-red note only on the single tell that has
already begun to matter, everything else calm. The image reads as one useful idea: the dirt that shows
is almost never the dirt that matters.

- Mood: over and over, the surface that faces the room is the one you clean and the surface that faces
  away is the one that fails, so a good list, built by kind, deliberately reaches the tops and backs
  and undersides and insides your eye slides past precisely because they do not show; useful, not
  alarming.
- Avoid: a scare poster, guilt or shame cues, dramatic destruction or filth, framing anyone as
  careless, a warm-red accent sprayed across the failing panel, tools or a repair scene, harsh
  contrast, glossy retouching, floating typeset text; keep the three things identical across the
  panels, the same microwave, fridge, and sink read at two faces.

## 5. The needle holds (the friction meter, a principled planning hold)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge dialing
from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right. The dark live
needle sits exactly where the last chapter left it, a short, honest step onto the calm side past the
marked Straighten-complete tick, and it has not moved at all this chapter. What is new is a single
dashed forward-projection arrow curving from the needle tip further toward the calm side, toward the
goal crosshair, showing where the needle will step once the plan is carried out. Chapter 15's
Straighten-complete milestone is a small slate marker just behind the live needle; fainter dashed ghost
needles mark Chapters 14, 13, and 12 further back in order, and a slate marker for the earlier Sort-
complete milestone sits further back still. A slate marker flags the live needle's held position as a
planning hold, not a step. A slate crosshair labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag, still sits
further out on the green calm side, clearly ahead of the live needle, because three more disciplines
remain and Shine itself has three more chapters to run. Warm paper cream background, ink line work,
consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest planning hold, the Shine parallel to Sort's hold; nothing new was cleaned, so the
  needle does not move, but the list and its names are a loaded spring, so a dashed arrow projects the
  move that is coming once the targets are cleaned and the whole home gets read on a rhythm; a held
  needle and a loaded spring, both true at once.
- Avoid: the live needle nudged or moved this chapter, the needle swung toward calm, the needle
  reaching or passing the goal crosshair, the hold drawn as a stall or a failure, the dashed forward-
  projection arrow drawn solid or as bright as the live needle, the dashed ghosts drawn solid or
  bright, celebratory confetti, hype, neon color, any sense of the journey being finished. The needle
  holds on purpose, and the dashed arrow shows exactly where it moves next.
