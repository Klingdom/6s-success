# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 17

**"Shine Targets and Assignments"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_17_final.html`: one
bright, cleaned launch pad with a green calm-dot sitting in a faint whole-home cutaway that is quietly
crowded with small unnamed targets, each wearing a blank name-tag; a calm 2x2 reference of the four
kinds of target with two or three home examples each; the Shine list drawn as a sheet pinned to the
fridge, four headings, a written name against every line in more than one hand, one line still blank;
and the friction meter holding at Chapter 16's first-Shine-step position with a dashed forward
projection toward the goal, a principled planning hold rather than a step.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the SECOND chapter of Part Five and Shine continues. Chapter 16 already
reframed cleaning as inspection and made one worked first pass on the launch pad, catching two small
problems and taking the first Shine step. Chapter 17 does one narrow thing: it turns that single pass
into a plan that covers the WHOLE home and shares the labor. It makes what needs cleaning visible, all
of it, grouped by four kinds of target so nothing hides, and then it puts a name against every target
so the work is shared instead of silently dumped. It builds ONE artifact, the Shine list, and opens no
Shine tool beyond it. It does NOT re-argue why we clean (Chapter 16, done, one-line recap only), it
does NOT re-sort or re-arrange anything (Chapters 12 to 15, done and closed; the list cleans and checks
stored things, it does not re-decide whether to keep them), it does NOT teach HOW to clean, products,
tools, or technique (Chapter 18), it does NOT build the repeatable routine, schedule, or the reset
itself (Chapter 19), and it does NOT build documented standard methods or maintenance-over-time systems
(the later Standardize and Sustain S's). Critically, because Chapter 17 PLANS but cleans nothing new,
the friction needle HOLDS where the first Shine step left it, honoring the book's rule that the meter
moves only when the room does; a dashed forward projection shows where it resumes once the plan is
carried out. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 17 · SHINE`, never a
floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: name-tag,
room-by-room, kind-by-kind, high-touch, point-of-use, whole-home, launch-pad label glyphs, line-and-
flat-color, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, first-Shine-step, pinned-to-the-fridge, forward-
projection. "Spotless" does not appear here; it is not the target. Anti-shame throughout: a home where
the cleaning silently fell to one person is the common case, not a moral failing, and the fix is a list
and shared names, not trying harder or nagging more. This chapter touches a live nerve, who does the
housework, so keep the register fairness and relief, never lecture, never guilt. No force or violence
framing anywhere; no attacking grime, no war on dirt, no blitz, no purge; the whole move is a quiet act
of writing things down and sharing them out.

## 1. One Zone Read, a Whole Home Unnamed (the opener)

**Purpose:** Signal the chapter's starting point in one image. The launch pad from Chapter 16 is
cleaned and read, proof that the idea works on a single zone, but the rest of the home is still full of
targets that are both unseen and unassigned, and the blank name-tags say the real question is not just
what to clean but who. Mirrors Figure 1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A cutaway of a whole home drawn in faint outline. In one corner, bottom-left, the
launch pad from the last chapter is drawn bright, warm, and complete, with a small green calm-dot
beside it reading `clean · read`, clearly cared for. The rest of the house is faint and quietly crowded
with small targets nobody has named: the dusty top of a cupboard, the gap behind the refrigerator, the
inside of a bathroom cabinet, a doorway handle, the underside of a doormat. Beside each of those sits a
tiny blank name-tag, empty, waiting. One bright, finished spot in a house full of unclaimed work.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm dawn wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient), a faint whole-home cutaway drawn as
  outer walls and a few interior dividers in muted `#B0A794` at low opacity, a firm ink floor line
  along the base.
- Bottom-left, the launch pad drawn well and complete in warm cream `#FCF8F0` with an ink outline: a
  small shelf and tray, two tidy hooks, the umbrella stand now dry and set right. A muted Newsreader
  italic label beneath: `launch pad`. A green calm-dot `#6E8B5B` above it with a small sans line
  `clean · read`, so this one zone reads as cleaned and cared for.
- Five unnamed targets in faint outline `#B0A794`, each with a muted Newsreader label and a tiny blank
  name-tag beside it (a small cream card with a light punch-hole ring and two faint ruled lines, no
  writing): `top of the cupboard` (a dust line and a few specks along the top edge), `behind the
  fridge` (the appliance in outline with a faint coil in the gap), `inside the cabinet` (an open door
  and empty shelves), `doorway handle` (a door in outline with a small ring for the handle), `under the
  mat` (a lifted doormat corner).
- A faint Newsreader italic line floating along the top, muted: `one zone read · a whole home still
  unnamed`.
- A small sans caps note along the base in soft gray: `blank tags · no name, no owner`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "One zone is clean and read. The rest of the house is a crowd of targets
  no one has named, each waiting on a hand that has not been assigned. This chapter names them, and
  names who."

**Note for the illustrator:** the launch pad here is the one bright, finished exception; draw it warm
and complete, and keep the rest of the house faint, calm, and quietly crowded, not messy or dirty. The
point is not filth, it is that most of the home is unread and unassigned, so keep every unnamed target
small, ordinary, and half-hidden, the kind a room-by-room glance slides right past. The blank name-tags
are the whole tension: each is empty, waiting, so the eye reads them as targets with no owner rather
than as clutter. Do not write any name on any tag; the blankness is the message. Do not re-sort or re-
arrange anything, and do not show any cleaning underway; nothing new gets cleaned in this chapter, so
the scene is a plan waiting to be made, not work in progress. Keep it warm and matter-of-fact, no
scolding, no guilt about the dust, just a quiet noticing that most of the home has no name against it.

## 2. The Four Kinds of Target (see by kind, not by room)

**Purpose:** Teach the reader to see cleaning targets by kind rather than by room, so the overlooked
categories, especially the stored things behind closed doors and the point-of-use spots everyone
touches, come into view. A calm reference anyone can hold at a glance. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** Four calm, labeled groups arranged in a simple 2x2 grid, each headed in slate with
two or three warm home examples. Surfaces (a floor, a window, a shelf). Equipment and fixtures (a
kettle, a tap, a chair). Stored things (a drawer, a cabinet interior, a box). Point of use (a door
handle, a light switch, a TV remote). Warm line-and-flat-color style, each example labeled in sans.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label in soft gray: `THE FOUR KINDS OF
  TARGET`.
- Four rounded cards in quiet fill `#F3EEE2` with a `#E2D8C4` outline, arranged two by two. Each card
  carries a slate sans caps heading and a one-line Newsreader italic helper, then two or three simple
  glyph-and-label examples in ink and wood fill.
- Card A, top-left, `SURFACES`, helper `the flat faces that show dirt and take the traffic`: a floor
  strip, a window, a shelf, each labeled beneath.
- Card B, top-right, `EQUIPMENT AND FIXTURES`, helper `the working things that get dirty and can
  quietly fail`: a kettle, a tap, a chair, each labeled.
- Card C, bottom-left, `STORED THINGS`, helper `what lives behind closed doors, cleaned and checked`: a
  drawer, a cabinet, a box, each labeled.
- Card D, bottom-right, `POINT OF USE`, helper `the high-touch spots your hands hit most`: a handle, a
  switch, a remote, each labeled.
- Caption (from the chapter): "Four kinds of target, four angles of approach. A room-by-room sweep
  keeps missing the same spots; sorting by kind, especially the stored things behind closed doors and
  the handles everyone touches, is what pulls them into the open."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a reference, so keep it calm, plain, and legible, four groups and
no more; the whole point is that four different angles of approach catch what one angle misses. Keep the
glyphs simple and recognizable at a glance, and keep every heading in slate so the four kinds read as
equals. The two categories people forget, stored things and point of use, should read exactly as calmly
as the two they remember; do not dramatize them. This is planning, not cleaning: no scrubbing, no
products, no tools anywhere. Do not tie the examples to any one room; the grid teaches kind, not room.
Warm and unhurried.

## 3. The Shine List (the hero, a name on every line)

**Purpose:** Make the hero device literal. Show that the Shine list is one page, grouped by the four
kinds, with a name against every line, and that the names come from more than one person, so the reader
sees at once both halves of the device: the grouping that drags hidden targets into view, and the names
that keep the whole list from landing on one pair of hands. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The list itself, drawn as a simple sheet with a slightly hand-made, pinned-to-the-
fridge feel. Four headings run down it in slate, surfaces, equipment and fixtures, stored things, point
of use, with two or three target lines under each. To the right of every line is a small written name,
some in an adult's hand, some in a child's, so the whole sheet reads as shared. One line near the
bottom still has a blank name space with a light circle around it, the last target waiting to be
claimed.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`, a faint fridge panel behind the sheet in `#F1ECE0`. The sheet
  itself in warm cream `#FCF8F0` with an ink outline, a very slight tilt for a hand-pinned feel, held
  by a single warm-red magnet `#CB4B36` at the top.
- A Fraunces ink title on the sheet: `The Shine List`. A faint rule beneath it and a small sans caps
  column tag at the right edge: `TARGET · NAME`.
- Four slate sans caps headings running down the sheet, each with its target lines set in Newsreader.
  Under `SURFACES`: kitchen floor, bathroom mirror, tops of the cupboards. Under `EQUIPMENT AND
  FIXTURES`: oven, washing machine, bathroom fan. Under `STORED THINGS`: pantry shelves, medicine
  cabinet. Under `POINT OF USE`: door handles, light switches, the remote, the fridge door.
- To the right of each line, a written name in Newsreader italic terracotta `#BC4B2A`, joined by a
  faint dotted leader. Mix adult names and a child's clearly larger, rounder hand across the list (for
  example the light switches and low door handles owned in a child's hand), so the sheet plainly reads
  as shared across more than one person.
- One line near the bottom left blank, its name space marked with a light dashed green circle `#6E8B5B`
  and a small Newsreader italic note: `one line still waiting for a name`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "One page, four kinds, a name on every line. The grouping is what drags
  the hidden targets into view; the names are what keep the whole list from landing on one person. The
  single blank line is the one still waiting to be claimed."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the hero, so give it room and make both halves of the device
unmistakable at a glance: the four kinds grouped down the page, and a name against every single line.
The names must look hand-written and must come from more than one hand, at least one of them plainly a
child's, because the whole argument of the chapter is that the work is shared, not dumped on one person.
Keep the pinned-to-the-fridge feel, a real household's sheet, not a clean corporate form; a slight
tilt, a magnet, an honest hand. The one blank line with its light circle is deliberate and load-
bearing: it is the last target still waiting to be claimed, the live edge of a plan being made, so keep
it quietly hopeful, not a gap or a failure. Do not show any cleaning; the sheet is the plan, and the
plan is the whole chapter. Warm, shared, and legible.

## 4. Clean the Whole Thing, Not Just the Part That Shows (the invisible-target payoff)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's single most useful idea as a picture: the dirt that shows is almost
never the dirt that matters, so the list, built by kind, deliberately reaches the tops and backs and
undersides and insides your eye slides past precisely because they do not show. This diagram has no
direct HTML twin; it renders the "Clean the Whole Thing, Not Just the Part That Shows" section as a
paired-panel figure, with the frozen rule and the invisible-target payoff carried beneath it.

**What it shows:** Two facing panels of the same three ordinary things, read at two faces. The left is
THE PART THAT SHOWS: the front of the microwave wiped clean, the visible face of the fridge looking
fine, the bathroom sink gleaming, each with a calm note that it tells you nothing. The right is THE PART
THAT FAILS: the same microwave's underside vent thick with grease, the coils behind the same fridge
furred with dust, the overflow hole of the same sink you never look into, each tagged with the quiet
cost it carries. The two panels are the same three things, the face that shows and the face that fails,
side by side.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE PART THAT SHOWS · THE PART THAT FAILS`, with a Fraunces ink
  line beneath carrying the frozen rule: "Clean the whole thing, not just the part that shows."
- Left panel, calm slate header bar `THE PART THAT SHOWS`: the microwave front wiped clean, the fridge
  front looking fine, the sink gleaming, each in warm cream with an ink outline, each with a muted
  Newsreader italic note, `wipes clean in a second`, `looks fine`, `gleams`. A soft gray sans caps line
  at the base: `CLEAN, AND TELLS YOU NOTHING`.
- Right panel, muted terracotta header bar `THE PART THAT FAILS`: the same microwave's underside vent
  thick with grease, the same fridge's coils furred with dust, the same sink's overflow hole, each with
  a slate note, `the grease that will cost you the microwave`, `the dust that makes it run hot and die
  early`, `where the smell is coming from`. Hold a single small warm-red note `#CB4B36` at the one tell
  that has already begun to matter, used sparingly.
- Between the panels, a faint slate arrow from the showing face to the failing face labeled `the face
  that faces away`, so the eye reads the right panel as the surface the room never turns toward you.
- Beneath the two panels, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen invisible-target payoff: "The dirt
  that matters is rarely the dirt you can see. It is on the top of the cupboard, behind the machine,
  under the mat, inside the drawer, and on the handle every hand in the house touches. A target you
  never named is a surface no one ever reads."
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "The list, built by kind, includes the tops and the backs and the
  undersides and the insides, the targets your eye slides past precisely because they do not show."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is insight, not a warning poster, so keep both panels calm and
matter-of-fact; the left is not a failure and the right is not a disaster, they are simply the two faces
of the same ordinary object. Keep the three things identical across the panels, the same microwave, the
same fridge, the same sink, so the eye reads one object at two faces, the one that shows and the one
that fails. Use the single small warm-red note only once, on the one tell that has already begun to
matter, and keep the rest of the image quiet. This is planning, not cleaning: do not draw scrubbing,
products, tools, or a repair scene; the point is that a good list reaches the parts that were quietly
deciding how long your things last, not how to clean any of them today. Warm, plain, and quietly useful,
the register of a good idea, not an alarm.

## 5. The Needle Holds (the friction meter, a principled planning hold)

nothing new, so the live needle HOLDS exactly where the first Shine step left it, honoring the book's
rule that the meter moves only when the room does. What is new is a dashed forward-projection arrow
toward the goal, drawn because the list and its names are a loaded spring: the move is coming once the
targets get their cleaning in Chapter 18 and the whole home gets read on a rhythm in Chapter 19. Mirrors
Figure 4 in the HTML, and drives the live data-driven gauge.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with the live needle held exactly at Chapter 16's
first-Shine-step position, unmoved this chapter, flagged `HOLDS · PLANNING` in slate. What is new is a

`GOAL` crosshair, showing where the needle will step once the plan is carried out. Chapter 15's
Straighten-complete milestone sits just behind the live needle; Chapters 14, 13, and 12 are fainter
dashed ghosts further back, and the Sort-complete milestone sits further back still. The goal crosshair,
tagged purpose, remains ahead on the green calm side.

- The `STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE` milestone in slate, drawn clearly as the passed marker just behind the live

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** this is a HOLD, a principled planning hold, the Shine parallel to

than the live needle, curving from the needle tip toward the goal, so the reader reads a move that is
coming, not a move that has happened. The needle must NOT reach the goal crosshair; the goal stays
clearly ahead on the green side, because Shine has three chapters still to run and Safety, Standardize,
and Sustain still follow. A held needle and a loaded spring are both true at once. No confetti, no
completion glow, no swing toward calm this chapter.
