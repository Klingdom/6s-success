# Chapter 42 Infographic Spec: The Guest Bathroom Layer

## The single strongest image
The guest-bathroom layer plus the kit, because the layer (run the water, no demotion, stock for the shut door) is what makes this chapter different from the primary bathroom.

## Anchor string (byte-verbatim)
> The guest bathroom is the one room a visitor spends real time in alone, with the door shut and nothing to look at but your standards, and it is also the room nobody in the house uses, which is its own problem. So you clean it two ways at once. You clean it to be looked at closely, from the underside of the seat to the chalky ring at the foot of the tap, because a guest behind a shut door sees everything. And you fight the quiet problems of a room that sits unused: the drain traps that lose their water and let the smell in, the towels that go musty on the shelf, and the rejected products the household keeps trying to retire in here. Nothing gets demoted into the guest bathroom, and the water gets run whether anyone has used the room or not.

## Palette
paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green #6E8B5B (Shine). Fraunces headings, Inter labels.

## Layout, portrait (1080 x 1350)
- Title: The Guest Bathroom Layer.
- Band 1, "run the water": a mug poured down a drain, refilling the trap.
- Band 2, "no demotion": a cast-off bottle leaving the house, not moving in.
- Band 3, "stock for the shut door": a shut door with the roll and soap visible inside.
- Band 4, "clean up close": the tap collar, the seat underside, the pump base.
- Footer strip: run the water, no demotion, stock for the shut door.

## Layout, landscape (1200 x 675)
Two panels: the method on the left, the kit on the right.

## Rules
Types only, no brands or logos. Never show two cleaners being mixed, or the household's cast-offs stocked in the guest bathroom. No em/en/spaced-hyphen dashes in any label.
