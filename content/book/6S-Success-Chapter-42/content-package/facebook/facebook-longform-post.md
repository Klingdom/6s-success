# Facebook Longform Post: Run the Water, No Demotion, Ready for a Shut Door

A story-driven post from Chapter 42 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the guest bathroom.

---

Here is the mystery of the guest bathroom: it can smell faintly of sewer no matter how hard you clean it, and the cleaning is not the problem.

The guest bathroom has two opposite problems at once. It is the room a visitor spends the most concentrated time in of anywhere in your house, alone behind a shut door with nothing to look at but your standards. And it is the room nobody actually uses, which is a different problem entirely.

The smell is the disuse. A room nobody uses loses the water sitting in its drain traps, and an empty trap is an open pipe that lets the drain's smell straight up into the room. So once a month, used or not, pour a mug of water down the sink, the tub, and the floor drain to refill the seals, and give the bowl a treatment with full dwell time. You will fix a smell no amount of scrubbing ever could.

Then two rules that keep the room worthy of the word guest. First, nothing gets demoted in here. This room quietly becomes the retirement home for the conditioner nobody liked, the soap that smells wrong, the towels that went thin. But a guest is offered your standard, not your cast-offs, so if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough for a visitor, and it leaves the house rather than moving in.

Second, clean and stock it for a guest who never asks. A visitor behind a shut door will not open your cupboards, so the room has to answer every question before it is asked: a spare roll in plain sight, a wrapped soap, a fresh towel. And clean the surfaces they study up close, the tap collar, the underside of the seat, the base of the soap pump, because a guest looks at this room longer and harder than you ever do.

Do one zone this week, and give the next visitor a room they can be left alone in without a second thought.

What has been demoted into your guest bathroom that would not pass in your own?
