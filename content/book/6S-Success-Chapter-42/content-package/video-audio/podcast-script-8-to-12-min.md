# Chapter 42 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Guest Bathroom

Single host, warm and practical.

## Cold open
Here is the mystery of the guest bathroom: it can smell faintly of sewer no matter how hard you clean it, and the cleaning is not the problem. Today we solve that, and clean the second wet room the way it actually needs.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. Today, the guest bathroom, and it is a strange room, because it has two opposite problems at once. It is the room a visitor spends the most concentrated time in of anywhere in your house, alone behind a shut door with nothing to look at but your standards. And it is the room nobody actually uses. So we clean it two ways at once, and we keep the wet-room method from the last chapter and add three rules on top: run the water, no demotion, and stock it for a guest who never asks.

## Part 2: The five zones
The room is five zones: the vanity counter, the vanity storage, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the guest linen zone. Start at the counter, and put the stored towels in the wash before you touch anything, because the linen shelf needs a wash cycle to finish.

## Part 3: Run the water
The smell first, because it is the thing everyone blames on cleaning. A room nobody uses loses the water sitting in its drain traps, and an empty trap is an open pipe that lets the drain's smell straight into the room. So once a month, used or not, pour a mug of water down the sink, the tub, and the floor drain to refill the seals, and treat the bowl with full dwell time. You will fix a smell no scrubbing ever could.

## Part 4: No demotion
This room quietly becomes the retirement home for everything the household rejected: the conditioner nobody liked, the soap that smells wrong, the towels that went thin. But a guest is offered your standard, not your cast-offs. So nothing gets demoted in here. If it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough for a visitor, and it leaves the house rather than moving in.

## Part 5: Clean and stock for the shut door
A guest behind a shut door will not open your cupboards and will not ask you where anything is, so the room has to answer every question before it is asked. Stock it so a visitor finds the spare roll and a wrapped soap at a glance. And clean the surfaces they study up close, the tap collar, the underside of the seat, the base of the soap pump, because a guest looks at this room harder than you do.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
Safety here is about a stranger who does not know the room and the chemistry under the sink: latch it away, never mix bleach with an acid cleaner, fit a real grab bar, and put a non-slip mat in an unfamiliar tub. And the calls are all about the word guest: whose bathroom this is, the bag of miniatures, the grout you cannot clean, the brush nobody wants to touch, and the good towels.

## Close
A guest bathroom is not clean because you tidied it before people came. It is clean because you ran the water, refused the cast-offs, cleaned up close, and stocked it so no one asks. Do one zone this week. Next time: the laundry room.
