# Chapter 42 Key Takeaways: The Guest Bathroom

- The guest bathroom has two opposite problems: it is watched most closely (a visitor alone behind a shut door) and used least (nobody in the house uses it).
- It keeps the wet-room method from Chapter 41: spray first and let the chemistry work, treat drying as the real job, and keep a toilet-only cloth colour.
- Five zones, one at a time: the vanity counter, the vanity storage, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the guest linen zone. Start at the counter and wash the stored linen first.
- Run the water once a month, used or not: a dry drain trap, not dirt, is what that faint sewer smell actually is, so pour a mug of water down every drain and treat the bowl.
- Allow no demotion: nothing the household rejected retires here; if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough for a guest, and it leaves the house.
- Clean and stock for a guest who never asks: the spare roll and a wrapped soap are found at a glance, and the tap collar, seat underside, and soap-pump base are as clean as the surfaces on show.
- Safety is tuned to a stranger who does not know the room: latch the chemistry, fit a real grab bar, and put a non-slip mat in an unfamiliar tub.
- Close on a before-and-after photo the room matches before the next arrival.
