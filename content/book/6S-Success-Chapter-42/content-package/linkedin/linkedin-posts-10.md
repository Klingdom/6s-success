# Chapter 42 LinkedIn Posts: The Guest Bathroom

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The room watched most and used least
The guest bathroom has two opposite problems at once. It is the room a visitor spends the most concentrated time in of anywhere in your house, alone behind a shut door. And it is the room nobody actually uses.

"So you clean it two ways at once. You clean it to be looked at closely, from the underside of the seat to the chalky ring at the foot of the tap, because a guest behind a shut door sees everything."

## 2. The smell is a dry trap, not dirt
That faint sewer note in a guest bathroom is almost never dirt. A room nobody uses loses the water sitting in its drain traps, and an empty trap is an open pipe that lets the drain's smell straight up into the room. So once a month, used or not, pour a mug of water down the sink, the tub, and the floor drain to refill the seals. You will fix a smell no amount of scrubbing ever could.

## 3. Nothing gets demoted in here
The guest bathroom quietly becomes the retirement home for everything the household rejected: the conditioner nobody liked, the soap that smells wrong, the towels that went thin. But a guest is offered your standard, not your cast-offs. The rule is one line: if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough to offer a visitor, and it leaves the house rather than moving in here.

## 4. The good towels
Every house has a set of nice towels guests are not quite allowed to use and a set of worn ones guests actually get. But towels thin whether they are used or not, so holding the good ones back means they age untouched in a closet while your visitors dry off on something gray. The guest bundle is built from the best towels you own.

## 5. Clean the surfaces a guest studies
A guest behind a shut door has nothing to do but look, and they look at the surfaces you skip in your own bathroom: the collar at the foot of the tap, the underside of the seat, the base of the soap pump. Clean those as well as the ones on show. You are cleaning this room for the person who will look at it longer and harder than you ever do.

## 6. Stock it so no one has to ask
A guest will not open your cupboards and will not ask where anything is, so the room has to answer every question before it is asked. A spare roll in plain sight, a box of tissues, a wrapped bar of soap, a new toothbrush. Stock it so plainly that a visitor finds everything at a glance and never has to knock on your door.

## 7. Wash the stored towels first
The linen shelf is the one zone that needs a wash cycle to finish, so put the stored set in the machine before you touch anything else and let it run while you work the room. A towel that has sat folded all season carries a musty note the instant it gets wet, so it gets a hot wash even though nobody has touched it.

## 8. The grout you cannot clean
You have scrubbed one grout line three times and it stayed gray, and you have quietly started calling that clean. It is not dirt. It is mould grown into porous grout or a silicone bead that failed underneath, and no product will bring it back. Stop spending the weekly clean on a job that cannot succeed: one proper treatment, then re-grout or re-caulk it.

## 9. The brush is a consumable
The toilet brush in a guest bathroom is the object the household has silently agreed not to look at: splayed bristles, standing in an inch of gray water, spreading more than it removes. A brush is a consumable, not a fixture. Splayed bristles or standing water in the holder means it gets replaced.

## 10. Shown without a word of apology
"A guest bathroom is not clean because you tidied it before people came. It is clean because you ran the water so the traps do not smell, refused to let the household's cast-offs retire in here, cleaned the surfaces a guest studies up close, and stocked it so no one ever has to ask."
