## The room in one line
The guest bathroom keeps the wet-room method from the last chapter but adds the problems of a room nobody uses: it is cleaned to be judged up close by a visitor alone behind a shut door, the drain traps get their water run so they do not smell, and nothing the household rejected is allowed to retire in here.

## The guest-bathroom Shine layer (wet-room method from Ch 41 PLUS)
- **Judged up close**: a visitor spends real time here alone with the door shut, so the surfaces they study (the tap collar, the underside of the seat, the base of the soap pump) are cleaned as well as the ones on show.
- **Run the water**: a room nobody uses loses the water in its drain traps, and an empty trap lets the sewer smell in; once a month, used or not, run a mug of water down every drain and treat the bowl.
- **No demotion**: nothing the household rejected retires here; if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough for a guest, and it leaves the house.
- **Stocked for a guest who never asks**: the spare roll, the tissues, and a wrapped soap are found at a glance, so a guest behind a shut door never has to open a cupboard or ask.

## Five zones (start at the vanity counter, wash the linen first)
1. **Guest Vanity Counter** - somewhere to wash hands with nothing of yours in the way; the tap collar and drain lip a guest sees up close; mirror last.
2. **Guest Vanity Storage** - guest supplies and the cleaning kit; empty it to read the trap for a slow weep; wipe the bottle necks so the cabinet does not go tacky.
3. **Shower or Tub** - ready for tonight though unused since spring; spray the grout and liner first, work top down, the grout you cannot clean gets re-done not re-scrubbed.
4. **Toilet Area** - clean and stocked so obviously a guest never asks; treat the bowl and work outward; the floor behind the pedestal where the smell lives; the brush is a consumable.
5. **Guest Linen Zone** - one fresh bundle that smells of nothing; wash the stored set hot even unused; read the back wall for damp.

## FROZEN STRINGS (byte-verbatim in manuscript AND final.html)

### Five zones
The guest bathroom is five small zones, not one big job: the guest vanity counter, the guest vanity storage, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the guest linen zone. Do them one at a time, and start at the vanity counter, because it is the first thing anyone sees and the fastest thing to put right.

### The rule (judged closely; fight disuse; no demotion)
The guest bathroom is the one room a visitor spends real time in alone, with the door shut and nothing to look at but your standards, and it is also the room nobody in the house uses, which is its own problem. So you clean it two ways at once. You clean it to be looked at closely, from the underside of the seat to the chalky ring at the foot of the tap, because a guest behind a shut door sees everything. And you fight the quiet problems of a room that sits unused: the drain traps that lose their water and let the smell in, the towels that go musty on the shelf, and the rejected products the household keeps trying to retire in here. Nothing gets demoted into the guest bathroom, and the water gets run whether anyone has used the room or not.

### The definition (defbox)
A finished guest bathroom is one a visitor can be shown to without a word of apology and left alone in with the door shut: a clear counter with a half-full soap pump and one folded towel, a mirror and tap with no film or scale, a shower with no pink line where the wall meets the floor, a bowl clean under the rim with a spare roll in plain sight, storage holding a new toothbrush and a wrapped soap a guest can find without asking, and one fresh towel bundle that smells of nothing at all.

### Run-the-water reframe (the dry trap and the sewer smell)
A bathroom nobody uses has a problem no busy room has: the water sitting in its drain traps slowly evaporates, and an empty trap is an open pipe that lets the drain's smell straight up into the room. That faint sewer note in a guest bathroom is almost never dirt, it is a dry trap. So once a month, used or not, you pour a mug of water down the sink, the tub, and the floor drain to refill the seals, and you give the bowl a cleaner treatment with full dwell time even though no one has been near it, because a bowl grows its ring at the water line whether it is flushed twice a day or twice a year.

### No-demotion reframe (nothing rejected lands here)
This room quietly becomes the retirement home for everything the household rejected: the conditioner nobody liked, the soap that smells wrong, the towels that went thin, the shampoo left behind two summers ago. But a guest is offered your standard, not your cast-offs, so nothing gets demoted into the guest bathroom. The rule is one line: if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough to offer a visitor, and it leaves the house rather than moving in here. The guest towels are the best towels you own, and the guest supplies are new.

### Clean-and-stock-for-the-closed-door reframe (a guest who never asks)
A guest behind a shut door will not open your cupboards and will not ask you where anything is, so the room has to answer every question before it is asked. It is stocked so plainly that a visitor finds the spare roll, the tissues, and a wrapped bar of soap at a glance, and it is cleaned so closely that the surfaces a guest studies up close, the tap collar, the underside of the seat, the base of the soap pump, are as clean as the ones on show. You clean this room for the person who will look at it longer and harder than you ever do.

### Safety reframe (a guest who does not know the room)
The person who uses this room does not know it, and often it is where a visiting child is sent alone, so the safety here is about a stranger and the chemistry left in the cupboard. The bowl cleaner, the bleach, and the drain opener are latched away or taken out of the room, because a small guest gets sent in here on their own, and you never use an acid bowl cleaner after bleach in a small room with the door shut, because together they make a gas. A real grab bar goes in where someone might steady themselves, not a towel bar that lets go under load, and a non-slip mat sits in a tub with an unfamiliar slick floor.

### Before-and-after caption
This is the guest bathroom the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the counter clear and the tap scale-free, the shower with no pink line, the bowl clean under the rim with a roll in plain sight, and one fresh towel bundle that smells of nothing at all. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a guest bathroom you can show a visitor to without a word of apology is the one that stays good, and the photo is what it has to match before the next arrival.

### One Idea to Keep
A guest bathroom is not clean because you tidied it before people came. It is clean because you ran the water so the traps do not smell, refused to let the household's cast-offs retire in here, cleaned the surfaces a guest studies up close, and stocked it so no one ever has to ask. Do one zone this week, the counter or the shower, and give the next visitor a room they can be left alone in without a second thought.

## Four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win (win)**: Put the stored towels in the machine on a hot wash before you touch anything else, and let them run while you work the room, because a towel that has sat folded all season carries a musty note the instant it gets wet. Then do the counter first: it takes one trip, and clearing it changes how the whole room reads from the doorway.
- **6S Tip (tip)**: Run the water once a month, used or not. Pour a mug of water down the sink, the tub, and the floor drain to refill the drain traps that slowly dry out, and give the bowl a cleaner treatment with full dwell time. A dry trap, not dirt, is what that faint sewer smell in an unused bathroom actually is.
- **The No-Demotion Challenge (family)**: Nothing gets demoted into the guest bathroom. The disliked conditioner, the wrong-smelling soap, the towels gone thin: if it is not good enough for you, it is not good enough to offer a visitor, so it leaves the house rather than retiring in here. The guest towels are the best you own, and the guest supplies are new and unopened.
- **Common Mistake (mistake)**: Only cleaning the guest bathroom the hour before people arrive, so the traps have dried out and it smells however hard you scrub; letting it fill up with the products and towels the household rejected; and cleaning only the surfaces on show while the tap collar, the underside of the seat, and the base of the soap pump, the ones a guest studies up close, stay grimy.

## The calls that matter here
Whose bathroom is this really (if a housemate or child genuinely uses it, name it out loud and give that person a labelled caddy; otherwise hold the hotel-counter standard); the bag of hotel miniatures (a two-night guest uses one of each, so keep four unopened with the least battered labels and let the rest go); the grout you cannot clean (a gray line scrubbed three times is mould in porous grout or failed silicone that no product will bring back, so give it one proper treatment then re-grout or re-caulk, and stop spending the weekly clean on a job that cannot succeed); the brush nobody wants to touch (a brush is a consumable not a fixture; splayed bristles or standing water in the holder means replace it); the good towels (the guest bundle is built from the best towels you own, because towels thin whether they are used or not).

## Watch for in the guest bathroom (five safety questions)
- **Poison, choke, or strangle**: bowl cleaner, bleach, and drain opener under the sink of the room a visiting child is sent to alone (latch the door or take the chemistry out); an acid bowl or limescale cleaner used after bleach in a small closed room with no fan; a bowl cleaner bottle left at floor level behind the pedestal with the cap loose.
- **Fall**: an unfamiliar tub with a slick glazed floor used at night by someone who does not know the room (a non-slip mat, not a warning); a bath mat whose rubber backing has hardened and stopped gripping.
- **Fall, cut, or crush**: a towel bar or suction rail used as a grab handle (fit a real grab bar); glass tumblers and dishes on a wet counter in a small tiled room.
- **Water and electricity**: a corded tool left plugged in next to a basin a guest will fill.
- **Burn or fire**: textiles stacked against a water heater, a hot pipe, or its cable in a shared linen closet (keep a hand's width of clear air on every hot side).

## Kit (product TYPES only)
Key inputs, mostly shared with the primary bathroom: a bathroom grime and soap-film cleaner (the hero), a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a streak-free glass and mirror cleaner, a toilet bowl cleaner and a dedicated bowl brush, a dedicated mildew remover for grout the soap-film cleaner will not shift, a stiff grout or detail brush, a colour-coded microfiber cloth set (the toilet keeps its own colour), a compact vacuum with attachments, and a caddy. Storage and readiness: two labelled lift-out bins (one guest supplies, one cleaning kit), a shower squeegee and shower caddy, an over-door hook rack, a lockable medicine box and a chemical storage bin latched under the sink, a toilet-paper reserve holder, a moisture absorber or humidity monitor, water-resistant labels, and a washing machine on a hot wash for the stored linen. Never mix bowl cleaner and bleach.
