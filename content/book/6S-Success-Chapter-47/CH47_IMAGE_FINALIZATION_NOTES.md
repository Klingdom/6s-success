## The blocking issue: the whole batch is monochrome

Every one of the 27 plates is a black-and-white pencil illustration. This is not a judgement call, it
is measured. Mean per-pixel colour saturation:

| Batch | Saturation |
|---|---|
| **Ch 47, all 27 plates** | **0.2 to 6.1** |
| Ch 34 opener (wired) | 41.2 |
| Ch 37 opener (wired) | 42.4 |
| Ch 38 opener (wired) | 29.3 |

That is an order of magnitude, not a variation. A few plates carry small colour accents on their
labels, but every illustration itself is graphite.

**Why this blocks the pass.** Chapters 31 to 38 are wired with warm colour photography and colour
infographics, on the book's cream, terracotta, honey, slate and green palette. Chapter 47 would sit
nine chapters later in the same Part Nine sequence in an entirely different visual family. The House
Style Bible's whole purpose is that "every image must look like it came from the same designer as the
one before it."

**This is your call, and it is a large one.** Three options:

1. **Regenerate Ch 47 in the established colour style.** Keeps Part Nine coherent. Costs one batch.
2. **Restyle the book to monochrome line art.** The illustrations are genuinely handsome and would
   print beautifully in one colour, which materially reduces print cost. But it orphans the eight
   chapters already wired plus everything in Ch 1 to 30, so it is a whole-book decision, not a
   chapter one.
3. **Deliberate mixed style**, colour for Ch 1 to 38 and line art for the rest. I would advise
   against this. It reads as a production accident rather than a choice.

I did not wire anything, because wiring would quietly commit you to option 3 by default.

## Content findings from the seven plates I did open

Separately from the style question, the content has real problems and two of them are canon
violations. These are worth having whichever way the style decision goes, because they tell the
regeneration what to fix.

### Canon violations

**`image09` "Smart Storage Solutions for Every Item" — contradicts a frozen reframe outright.**
It presents a **"Lidded Basket · Hide clutter and reduce visual noise"** as a recommended solution.
The chapter's frozen lids-hide-the-drift reframe argues the exact opposite: *"the instinct when a room
fills up is to buy something with a lid on it, which is exactly the wrong move. A lid hides the
drift... this is the one room where the accumulation needs to stay embarrassing."* The plate
recommends the thing the chapter forbids, and praises it for the precise reason the chapter rejects it.

The same plate also **replaces the named person-columns with category hooks** (COATS / BAGS / SCARVES
/ HATS), while the shelf above still carries DAD / MOM / JACK / ELLA. The frozen definition is *one
named column per person at their own reach*, and the frozen One Idea to Keep turns on it. The plate is
internally inconsistent and contradicts the chapter's central device.

**`image13` and `image17` are off-scope and partly off-brand.** Both are six-panel "drop zone
solutions for different home types" spreads, and they are near-duplicates of each other. Problems:

- They call the room a **"drop zone"**. This chapter is the mudroom with six named zones; "drop zone"
  is not its vocabulary.
- Both include a **"MODERN MINIMALIST"** panel. The Project Instructions say plainly: do not make the
  book about minimalism.
- Both are furniture-buying guidance ("measure your space before buying furniture"), not zone cleaning
  instruction.
- Both switch the family cast to **EMMA / NOAH / LILY**, against DAD / MOM / JACK / ELLA everywhere
  else in the batch.

### Zone naming

`image02` carries all six zones in the correct order, which is good, but five of six are shortened:
Family Hook **Wall** for the family hook **zone**, Bench for the bench and transition surface, Shoe
Storage for the shoe and boot storage, Seasonal Gear for the seasonal outdoor gear, Cleaning Zone for
the cleaning and utility zone. Only Pet Station is exact. It also says "Micro-Zones" where the chapter
says zones.

### Continuity

`image06` shows the hook name labels differently in three of its own six panels: panel 4 reads DAD DAD
MOM JACK ELLA, panel 5 reads MOM JACK JACK ELLA, panel 6 reads DAD MOM MOM JACK ELLA. Duplicated and
inconsistent names, in a figure whose whole subject is the named-column system.

### Baked em dashes

Found in `image02` ("bowls—everything for the walk"), `image06` ("a damp cloth—but not dripping
wet"), and `image09` (twice: "put things back—every single day", "Involve kids—use names or icons").
Four in the seven plates I opened, which suggests the rate across the batch is similar to Ch 38's.

### What is genuinely good

Worth saying, because it should survive regeneration:

- **`image01`** is a strong before/after and states the frozen room-that-catches idea well: "A
  successful mudroom is not larger. It simply catches everything before it enters the house."
- **`image03`** states the frozen floor-is-a-symptom reframe almost exactly: "When hooks are shared,
  everything falls to the floor. Clutter on the wall creates clutter on the floor."
- **`image06`** is a correct six-step top-to-bottom cleaning sequence with a tools list.

The illustration craft itself is high. The problems are style-family, canon, and baked text, not
draughtsmanship.
