# X Thread: The Mudroom

Chapter 47 of *6S Success: Home Edition*, the seventeenth room playbook of Part Nine. One thread, 14 posts. Each at or under 280 characters, counting its number line. Plain "1/" numbering on its own line.

---

1/
The rooms get small again in Part Nine, and the job changes completely. This is the mudroom, and it is the first room in the book whose purpose is not to hold things at all.

(176 chars)

---

2/
The mudroom is where the weather stops. Wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October.

(137 chars)

---

3/
Its job is to intercept. Which means it is judged by what gets past it rather than by what it contains.

(106 chars)

---

4/
That inverts how most people assess the room. A mudroom holding a great deal, neatly, has usually failed. A nearly empty one that stops grit and water at the door has succeeded.

(180 chars)

---

5/
And it explains the order of work. Go and look at what is on your mudroom floor right now. The bags, the boots, the kit going back to the shop.

(146 chars)

---

6/
Almost every single item is there for one reason: the wall above it ran out of room. A bag on the ground is a hook that was full. A boot in the walking line is a rack with no space left.

(189 chars)

---

7/
So the floor is a symptom and the wall is the cause. Clean the floor first, which is what everyone does, and you will be cleaning it again inside a week.

(156 chars)

---

8/
Hooks first. Floor last.

(27 chars)

---

9/
And do the hooks with the household standing there. Not politeness: a coat is the most personal object in that room, and a coat removed by somebody else is a coat that comes straight back.

(191 chars)

---

10/
Then give every person a vertical strip of wall at their own reach. A child who can reach their own hook stops leaving their coat on the floor. Not because you asked. Because they can.

(188 chars)

---

11/
The practical heart is three pieces of kit. Grit does its damage underfoot, because grit ground into a floor sands the finish off it. So stop it at the threshold, in three layers.

(183 chars)

---

12/
A stiff bristle mat outside to scrape. An absorbent mat inside to soak. A lipped tray for anything still dripping. And maintain them: a mat already full of grit is a supply of grit at your door.

(198 chars)

---

13/
One that goes against instinct: do not buy storage with a lid for this room. A lid hides the drift. It lets a pile keep growing while it stops being visible.

(161 chars)

---

14/
Do one zone this week, and make it the hook wall, with everyone standing there. Full steps for all six zones in Chapter 47.
