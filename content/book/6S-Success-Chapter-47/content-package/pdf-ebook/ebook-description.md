# Chapter 47 Ebook Description: The Mudroom

The seventeenth room playbook of Part Nine, and the point where the rooms get small again.

The mudroom is where the weather stops. Everything the house wants to keep outside arrives here first: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. Its job is to intercept, which makes it the first room in this book judged by what gets past it rather than by what it contains. A mudroom holding a great deal, neatly, has usually failed.

The room breaks into six zones, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the family hook zone, the bench and transition surface, the shoe and boot storage, the pet station, the seasonal outdoor gear, and the cleaning and utility zone. The hook wall goes first, and it is the one zone in Part Nine done with the whole household standing there.

On top of the nine rules of Shine it adds four, including two that contradict instinct: the floor is a symptom and the wall is the cause, so the hooks are fixed before the floor is touched; and lids hide the drift, which makes this the one room where storage with a lid on it makes the problem worse rather than better.

It is also the first chapter to answer one of the five safety questions with an honest negative. This room carries no meaningful fire load, and rather than invent a hazard to fill the slot, it says so.
