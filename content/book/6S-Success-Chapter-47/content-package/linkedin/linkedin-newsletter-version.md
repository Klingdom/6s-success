# Chapter 47 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Mudroom

**Subject:** The floor is a symptom. The wall is the cause.

The rooms get small again now, and the job changes.

The mudroom is where the weather stops. Everything the house wants to keep outside arrives here first: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. Its job is to intercept, which makes it the first room in this book judged by what gets past it rather than by what it holds. A mudroom containing a great deal, neatly, has usually failed.

That single shift explains two things.

**Why the wall comes before the floor.** Look at what is lying on your mudroom floor and almost all of it is there because the hooks above ran out of room. A bag on the ground is a hook that was full. Clean the floor first and you will be cleaning it again within a week, because you treated the symptom.

**Why nothing here should have a lid.** This room is one door from the street, so it becomes the holding bay for the whole house. The instinct when a room fills up is to contain it, and containing it here is exactly wrong, because a lid lets a pile keep growing while it stops being visible.

Then the practical heart of it: beat grit at the threshold in three layers. A stiff bristle mat outside to scrape, an absorbent mat inside to soak, and a lipped tray for anything still dripping. Those three do more than any amount of wiping further in, and they only work maintained.

Do one zone this week, and make it the hook wall, with the household standing there.
