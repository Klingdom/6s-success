# The Mudroom: The Room That Catches Rather Than Stores

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 47 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

The rooms get small again now, and the job changes.

> The mudroom is where the weather stops. Everything the house wants to keep outside arrives here first: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, the dog towel that never quite dries, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. So this is the first room in the book whose job is not to store things but to catch them, and it is judged by what it stops rather than by what it holds.

## Why the wall comes before the floor

> Look at what is lying on your mudroom floor and almost all of it is there for one reason: the wall above it ran out of room. A bag on the floor is a hook that was full. A boot in the walking line is a rack with no space left. So the floor is a symptom and the wall is the cause, which is why the hooks come first and the floor last.

## Three layers at the threshold

> Grit is what wears this room out, and it does its damage underfoot rather than on a surface, because grit ground into a floor sands the finish off it. So you beat it at the threshold, in three layers, before it reaches the room.

## And the one thing not to buy

> A lid hides the drift. A closed box lets a pile grow while staying invisible, and this is the one room where the accumulation needs to stay embarrassing.
