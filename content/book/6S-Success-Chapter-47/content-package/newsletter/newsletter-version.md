# Chapter 47 Newsletter: The Mudroom

**Subject:** The floor is a symptom. The wall is the cause.
**Preview:** The first room in the book whose job is to catch things rather than store them.

---

The rooms get small again now, and the job changes.

For the last two chapters you have been in the biggest, heaviest rooms in the house, deciding about weight and chemistry and things stored above people's heads. The mudroom is none of that. It is often the smallest room in the book, and it is the first one whose purpose is not to hold things at all.

The mudroom is where the weather stops. Everything the house wants to keep outside arrives here first: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. Its job is to intercept, which means it is judged by what gets past it rather than by what it contains. A mudroom holding a great deal, neatly, has usually failed.

That single shift explains two things that would otherwise seem odd.

**Why you fix the wall before the floor.** Look at what is lying on a mudroom floor and almost all of it is there because the wall above it ran out of room. A bag on the ground is a hook that was full. A boot in the walking line is a rack with no space left. Clean the floor first and you will be cleaning it again inside a week, because you treated the symptom.

**Why nothing here should have a lid.** This room is one door from the street, so it quietly becomes the holding bay for the whole house: the returns, the charity bag, the kit for a season that finished in May. The instinct when a room fills up is to contain it, and that is exactly wrong here, because a lid lets a pile keep growing while it stops being visible.

Then the practical heart of the room, which is three pieces of kit rather than a technique. Beat grit at the threshold in three layers: a stiff bristle mat outside to scrape, an absorbent mat inside to soak, and a lipped tray for anything still dripping. They only work maintained, because a mat already full of grit has stopped being a mat and become a supply of grit sitting at your door.

One more thing. Do the hook wall with the household standing there, and give each person a vertical strip at their own reach. A coat removed by somebody else is a coat that comes back, and a child who can reach their own hook stops leaving their coat on the floor.

Do one zone this week, and make it the hooks.
