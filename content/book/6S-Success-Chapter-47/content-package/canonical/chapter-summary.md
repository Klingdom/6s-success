# Chapter 47 Summary: The Mudroom

The seventeenth room playbook of Part Nine, and the point where the rooms get small again and the job changes. The mudroom is where the weather stops, and it is the first room in the book whose purpose is not to store things but to catch them: wet boots, muddy paws, dripping umbrellas, and the coat wall that grows a layer every October. That makes it the only room judged by what gets past it rather than by what it holds. A mudroom containing a great deal, neatly, has usually failed.

The room breaks into six zones, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the family hook zone, the bench and transition surface, the shoe and boot storage, the pet station, the seasonal outdoor gear, and the cleaning and utility zone. The hook wall goes first, and it is done with the household standing there, because a coat is the most personal object in the room and a coat removed by someone else is a coat that comes back.

On top of the nine rules of Shine the room adds four: it catches rather than stores; grit is beaten at the threshold in three layers, a stiff bristle mat outside to scrape, an absorbent mat inside to soak, and a lipped tray for anything still dripping; the floor is a symptom and the wall is the cause, since almost everything on a mudroom floor is there because the hooks above ran out of room; and lids hide the drift, which makes this the one room in the book where you should not buy storage with a lid on it.

The chapter is also the first to answer one of the five safety questions with an honest negative: this room carries no meaningful fire load, and rather than invent a hazard to fill the slot, it says so. Previous: Chapter 46, The Workshop. Next: Chapter 48, The Hall Closet.
