# Chapter 47 Key Takeaways: The Mudroom

- The mudroom is where the weather stops, and it is the first room in the book whose job is to catch things rather than store them.
- It is judged by what gets past it, not by what it holds. A mudroom containing a great deal, neatly, has usually failed.
- Almost everything lying on a mudroom floor is there for one reason: the wall above it ran out of room.
- So the hooks come first and the floor comes last. Clean the floor first and you will be cleaning it again within a week.
- Beat grit at the threshold in three layers: a stiff bristle mat outside to scrape, an absorbent mat inside to soak, and a lipped tray for anything still dripping.
- A loaded mat has stopped being a mat and become a supply of grit sitting at your door.
- Do the hook wall with the household standing there, because a coat removed by someone else is a coat that comes back.
- Give each person a vertical strip of wall at their own reach, so a child never has to ask an adult for their own coat.
- This is the one room where you should not buy storage with a lid, because a lid lets a pile grow while it stops being visible.
- The tray sits exactly where boots actually come off, not two steps further in, because a wet sole past the tray leaves an invisible film where everyone turns towards the kitchen.
- Not every room answers all five safety questions. This one has no meaningful fire load, and the chapter says so rather than inventing one.
