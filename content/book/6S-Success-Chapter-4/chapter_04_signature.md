# Chapter 4 Signature Plan
## "How to Choose Your First Target Area"
### 6S Success: Home Edition

## Signature Overview

**Length:** 16 pages (8 spreads)
**Part:** Part 1, Discovering 6S (the closing chapter of Part 1; it hands off to Part 2, Prepare)
**Reading time:** 9 to 12 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 60% text, 40% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Turn a reader who now understands the method into a reader who has chosen one real first target. Pay off Chapter 3's promise ("the loop works on anything, which is exactly why the first real question is where to point it; we will pick a target you can win"). Give the reader a simple, honest way to choose a first target that is small enough to win and annoying enough to matter, steer them away from the classic doomed first project (the garage), and end with one space named, written down, and committed to. Do not start the work yet. That is Part 2, beginning with the audit in Chapter 5.

**Throughline space:** not a single fixed space this time, because the chapter is about choosing among several. Instead, a recurring "candidate shortlist" walks the reader past a handful of ordinary spaces (a kitchen catch-all drawer, the bathroom counter, the entryway drop zone, a linen closet, the garage) and scores them, landing on a sweet-spot winner. Keep the garage present as the tempting wrong answer.

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapters 1 to 3. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data. Green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns and is load-bearing here, because friction is one of the two axes. The friction meter appears at the close.

**New hero motif introduced:** **the First Target Map.** A two-by-two grid with friction on one axis (how much a space costs you, how often) and effort on the other (how big the job is). The high-friction, low-effort quadrant is the highlighted "start here" sweet spot, marked with green calm-dots. The high-effort quadrant holds the garage. This map is a reusable decision device the reader can apply to any room for the rest of the book.

**Friction meter call:** holds steady, but now aimed. The reader does not move objects in this chapter, so the needle does not earn a move (consistent with the book rule: the meter moves when objects move). Instead, a small crosshair or target reticle appears over the calm side of the dial, showing that the reader has now chosen where the needle is about to travel. Understanding became a decision. Movement starts in Part 2.

## Page-by-Page Layout

### PAGE 1 (recto) | Chapter Opener
- **Spread objective:** Catch the reader in the exact moment of motivated overreach, standing in front of the hardest space in the house, sleeves rolled up, about to make the classic mistake.
- **Layout:** Full-bleed image. Title lower third. Kicker "Part 1 · Discovering 6S."
- **Visual:** [VISUAL: Full-page photo] A person standing in an open garage doorway, hands on hips, surveying a wall of bins, bikes, and boxes. Morning light. The posture is determination. The space is overwhelming. Honest, not staged.
- **Caption:** "The day after you learn the method is the day you are most likely to aim it at the wrong thing. Where you start decides whether you finish."

### PAGE 2-3 (Spread A) | The Trap of the Big Brave Start
- **Spread objective:** Name the moment and the trap. Fresh off a successful lap in Chapter 3, the reader wants to go big. The chapter gently stops them and reframes the first project as a decision, not a feat of will.
- **Layout:** Left page narrative. Right page a small spot illustration of the garage with a single red friction-spark and a question mark, signaling "tempting, but wrong first."
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Opening" plus the top of "Why the First Target Decides Everything," roughly 480 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > Your first project is not really about a room. It is about proof. You are not trying to fix your whole house this week. You are trying to give yourself one undeniable win you can see.

### PAGE 4-5 (Spread B) | Momentum Is the Real Goal
- **Spread objective:** Make the psychology explicit and kind. A visible win makes the second project happen. A burnout defeat makes the reader quit and conclude, wrongly, that they are bad at this. Small and winnable beats big and brave.
- **Layout:** Text with a simple two-path spot illustration (one path: big start, stall, guilt; other path: small win, momentum, next win).
- **Body copy:** "Why the First Target Decides Everything," continued, roughly 420 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Choosing the space you feel most guilty about instead of the space that actually bites you every day. Guilt is a bad compass. Friction is a good one.

### PAGE 6-7 (Spread C) | The Two Questions
- **Spread objective:** Give the reader the whole decision in two questions: how much does this space cost me (friction), and how big is the job (effort). Introduce the map as the place where those two answers meet.
- **Layout:** Short intro text, then the hero infographic dominating the spread.
- **Body copy:** "The Two Questions That Pick a Target," roughly 420 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The First Target Map." A two-by-two. Horizontal axis: Effort, low to high. Vertical axis: Friction, low to high. Four quadrants labeled. Top-left (high friction, low effort) is the highlighted sweet spot, "Start Here," dense with green calm-dots. Top-right (high friction, high effort) is "Soon, Not First," holding the garage. Bottom-left (low friction, low effort) is "Fine, Not Now." Bottom-right (low friction, high effort) is "Avoid for Now."
- **Boxed definition:**
  > **Your first target in one line**
  > The best first target is the smallest space that will still feel like a real win. High friction, low effort. Big daily relief, small one-time cost.

### PAGE 8-9 (Spread D) | Why Not the Garage
- **Spread objective:** Take apart the most seductive wrong choice, with affection. The garage is high effort, often lower daily friction than it feels, usually shared, and frequently seasonal. It is a real target, just not a first one.
- **Layout:** Text left, a spot illustration of the garage sitting squarely in the "Soon, Not First" quadrant.
- **Body copy:** "Why Not the Garage (Yet)," roughly 380 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Starting with the garage, the basement, or the storage unit. They are high effort, often shared, and you do not stand in them every morning. A win you cannot see every day cannot keep you going.

### PAGE 10-11 (Spread E) | Win Where You Will See It
- **Spread objective:** Refine the sweet spot with three practical filters: pick a space you touch daily (so the win keeps paying you back), pick a space you control (avoid contested or shared spaces for a first solo win), and avoid emotional landmines (sentimental storage needs skills you have not built yet).
- **Layout:** Text with a small three-icon spot (an eye for daily-visible, a single hand for you-control, a heart for emotional-skip).
- **Body copy:** "Win Where You Will See It," roughly 460 words.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > A daily-touch space is a win that reintroduces itself every morning. Each time the drawer answers you instead of fighting you, it quietly recruits you for the next project.

### PAGE 12-13 (Spread F) | Make Your Shortlist
- **Spread objective:** Turn the idea into a list. Walk the house, name three to five spaces that genuinely annoy you, and score each one on friction and effort. The scorecard does the choosing so the reader does not have to agonize.
- **Layout:** Text with a compact "Target Scorecard" table illustration: candidate spaces down the side, two columns (friction 1 to 5, effort 1 to 5), a simple "friction minus effort" score.
- **Body copy:** "Make Your Shortlist," roughly 420 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The Target Scorecard." A small worked example: a kitchen catch-all drawer, the bathroom counter, the entryway drop zone, a linen closet, the garage, each scored, the entryway drop zone or the kitchen drawer winning.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Right now, without getting up, name the one space in your home that annoyed you most in the last week. Not the messiest. The most annoying. That feeling is friction talking, and it is usually pointing at your first target.

### PAGE 14-15 (Spread G) | Choose, and Write It Down
- **Spread objective:** Make the choice real and small. Pick the winner, write it on paper, set a rough time for it, and resist the urge to add three more. One target. The act of writing it down is the commitment.
- **Layout:** A short, encouraging section and a compact "Pick Your First Target" checklist.
- **Body copy:** "Choose, and Write It Down," roughly 340 words.
- **Checklist:**
  > **Pick your first target**
  > - List three to five spaces that genuinely annoy you.
  > - Score each on friction (how often it bites) and effort (how big the job).
  > - Circle the highest friction, lowest effort space. That is your target.
  > - Write it down where you will see it.
  > - Pick a rough day and a time box. One space. One session.
- **Callout:**
  > **Family Challenge**
  > Everyone nominates the one space that annoys them most and guesses how long it would take to fix. Shortest time plus loudest complaint wins, and that space goes first. Now the whole family has a stake in the result.

### PAGE 16 (verso) | Chapter Close and What's Next
- **Spread objective:** Land the choice warmly, mark the close of Part 1, and tee up Part 2 and the audit.
- **Layout:** Short close, "One Idea to Keep" box, friction-meter spot (holds, but now aimed), "Next" strip.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Close," roughly 240 words.
- **Boxed recap:**
  > **One Idea to Keep**
  > You do not need more motivation to start. You need a smaller, smarter first target. Win where you will see it, and the next project gets easier on its own.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Friction-meter spot] The meter holding where Chapter 3 left it, with a small crosshair now resting on the calm side, showing the reader has aimed but not yet fired. A note that the needle moves once the work begins in Part 2.
- **Next teaser:** "Next: Part 2 begins. Chapter 5 · The 6S Home Audit. Before you touch your target, you will spend a few honest minutes seeing it clearly, so the work goes where it actually helps."

## Production Notes / Review Flags
- This chapter chooses; it does not do. Keep the reader from starting the work here. The audit in Chapter 5 is the first real action of Part 2, and the chapter should hand off cleanly to it.
- The friction meter holds steady but gains a crosshair. Confirm this read is acceptable. If review prefers the meter to stay exactly as Chapter 3 left it with no crosshair, the close text and art swap easily.
- The First Target Map (friction vs effort) is a new decision device. Confirm the two axis labels (Friction and Effort) and the four quadrant names. If the book prefers different quadrant names, they are easy to relabel without changing the logic.
- Keep the garage affectionate, not mocked. Many readers love their garage project. The point is sequencing, not shame.
- Reuse Chapters 1 to 3's exact type, palette, and callout styling. The First Target Map should be drawn so the sweet-spot quadrant can be lifted out as a standalone graphic for social and companion use.
