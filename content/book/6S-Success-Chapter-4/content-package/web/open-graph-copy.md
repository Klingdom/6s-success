# Chapter 4 Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy

Two variants. Variant A leads with the decision tool and the promise of a winnable target. Variant B leads with the garage hook. Use the same image concept noted under each variant.

---

## Variant A: The winnable target

**og:title**
How to Choose Your First Target Area

**og:description**
The hardest room is the worst first target. Use a one-minute friction vs effort map to pick a smaller, smarter space you can actually win, then write it down.

**twitter:title**
How to Choose Your First Target Area

**twitter:description**
Skip the garage. Score your spaces on friction and effort, pick the high-friction, low-effort one, and let your first easy win carry the next.

**Suggested og:image concept**
The First Target Map: the clean friction vs effort two-by-two with the top-left Start Here quadrant highlighted in green dots, and the garage glyph sitting over in Soon, Not First. Warm paper background, terracotta and slate accents. This reads as a useful tool at a glance and rewards the click.

---

## Variant B: The garage trap

**og:title**
Should You Start With the Garage? Probably Not

**og:description**
Fresh off a burst of motivation, most people charge the hardest room and quit halfway. Here is where to start decluttering instead, and why a humble drawer wins.

**twitter:title**
Should You Start With the Garage? Probably Not

**twitter:description**
The garage is high effort, often shared, and quieter than its guilt. Choose a small, high-friction space first, win it, and keep going.

**Suggested og:image concept**
A person standing in an open garage doorway in warm morning light, hands on hips, facing a wall of bins, boxes, and a bicycle. The posture is determination, the space is overwhelming. Honest, not staged. Captures the exact moment of motivated overreach this chapter exists to rescue.
