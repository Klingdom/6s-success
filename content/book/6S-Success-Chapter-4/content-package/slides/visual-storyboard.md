# Visual Storyboard
## Chapter 4: How to Choose Your First Target Area
### 6S Success: Home Edition

This storyboard ties the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system. It is a build guide for whoever sets the slides, so the social assets read as the same book.

---

## The Design System (lifted from chapter_04_final.html)

**Palette (the :root variables):**
- Paper #F7F2E9 (slide background, warm and matte)
- Panel #FBF7EF (cards, infographic fields)
- Ink #2B2622 (body and headline text)
- Soft #6A625A (captions, axis sub-labels)
- Terracotta #BC4B2A (primary accent, "THE GARAGE" label)
- Honey #DDA63A (secondary accent, highlights, the Family Challenge callout rule)
- Slate #3C5A6B (data, axes, line-art weights)
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B (the sweet spot, the win, the calm side of the meter)
- Red friction-spark #CB4B36 (daily cost, the crosshair on the garage, the sigh)
- Rules #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8 (hairlines, muted quadrant borders)

**Type:** Fraunces for display and quadrant titles, Newsreader for body and captions, a sans (Inter) only for small all-caps axis and table labels.

**Style:** warm line-and-flat-color, roughly 3px stroke, rounded corners, consistent with Chapters 1 to 3.

**The two recurring motifs that carry meaning, not decoration:**
- Green calm-dot = where you should go, the win, calm earned. It clusters in the Start Here quadrant and sits on the calm side of the friction meter.
- Red friction-spark = the daily cost, the annoyance, the thing pulling at you. It marks the garage in the opener, sits beside the friction question, and becomes the crosshair at the close.

## Beat 1: The Overwhelming Garage (the tempting wrong start)

**Image:** Full-bleed photograph. A person in an open garage doorway, hands on hips, surveying a wall of bins, bikes, half-collapsed boxes, and a lawn mower. Morning light pours in behind them. Posture is determination. The space is genuinely overwhelming. Honest, not staged.

**Palette cues:** Let the natural warm morning light do the paper-tone work. Title plate in lower third on a translucent #2B2622 band, Fraunces in paper white.

**Motif:** A single red friction-spark with a small question mark floats over the garage, the chapter's only "tempting, but wrong first" mark. No green here yet. The reader has not chosen.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 1, Carousel slide 1.

## Beat 2: Motivation Is a Budget

**Diagram:** A battery or fuel gauge draining full to empty, set over a small slate garage silhouette. Line art, roughly 3px stroke.

**Palette cues:** Slate line work on paper, the draining portion bleeding from honey to terracotta to signal energy spent.

**Motif:** The red friction-spark hovers small over the garage, deliberately smaller than the garage looks, foreshadowing the later point that the garage's friction is lower than its guilt.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 2, Carousel slide 2.

## Beat 3: Choosing Better, Not Working Harder

**Diagram:** Two short labels. "Working harder" softly struck through, "Choosing better" circled.

**Palette cues:** Paper panel #FBF7EF, ink text, the circle drawn in honey. Calm, generous whitespace.

**Motif:** Quiet beat, no spark or dot. This is the hinge of the chapter and it should feel like a breath. Reassurance, not alarm.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 3, Carousel slide 3.

## Beat 4: Proof and Momentum

**Diagram:** A small drawer marked with one green calm-dot, an arrow leading to a second project, then a third, each slightly larger. Momentum drawn as a growing chain of small wins.

**Palette cues:** Green calm-dots on each completed step, ink arrows, honey emphasis on the word "proof" if a quote band is used.

**Motif:** First appearance of the green calm-dot as "the win." It will reappear, multiplied, inside the Start Here quadrant. Establish it here as a single dot so the cluster later reads as many wins.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 4, Carousel slide 4.

---

## Beat 5: Guilt vs Friction (the two compasses)

**Diagram:** A slate compass with two needles. One labeled "Guilt" swings toward a dramatic, heavy closet. One labeled "Friction" swings toward a humble junk drawer.

**Palette cues:** Slate dial and ring, ink needles. The Guilt needle points at a terracotta-heavy emotional space; the Friction needle carries the red spark and points at the plain drawer.

**Motif:** The red friction-spark rides the Friction needle, teaching that the spark is the compass you should trust. The dramatic space gets weight and warmth but no dot, because it is not the win.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 5, Carousel slide 5.

---

## Beat 6: The Two Questions

**Diagram:** Two cards side by side. Left card FRICTION (how much, how often), right card EFFORT (how big the job). A meeting-point arrow leads forward to the map.

**Palette cues:** Both cards on panel #FBF7EF. FRICTION card tagged with the red spark, EFFORT card tagged with a slate weight or block icon. All-caps labels in the small sans.

**Motif:** Spark and weight introduced as the map's two axes, so the next beat's grid reads instantly. Red is the vertical axis (friction), slate is the horizontal axis (effort).

**Used on:** Teaching slide 6, Carousel slide 6.

## Beat 7: The First Target Map (the hero)

**Diagram:** The hero two-by-two, rebuilt from the final HTML SVG. Panel field #FBF7EF. Vertical axis FRICTION (low at bottom to high at top), horizontal axis EFFORT (low at left to high at right), labels in slate small-caps.
- Top-left, Start Here: field fill #E8EFE0 with a #6E8B5B border, six green calm-dots scattered inside, title "Start Here" in Fraunces #4F6B3E, sub-line "Small to fix. Big daily relief." in #3F5A30.
- Top-right, Soon, Not First: muted #FAF0DA field, #E2D8C4 border, a small slate garage icon with a "THE GARAGE" label in terracotta, sub-line "Worth doing. Just not first."

- Bottom-right, Avoid for Now: #F4EEE2 field, muted border.

**Palette cues:** Only the Start Here quadrant carries saturated green. The other three sit deliberately muted so the eye lands on the sweet spot first.

**Motif:** The green calm-dot cluster is the visual payoff of the single dot from Beat 4. Many wins live here. The Start Here quadrant is drawn so it can be lifted out as a standalone graphic for social and for the companion worksheet.

## Beat 8: Why Not the Garage

**Diagram:** The garage icon parked squarely in the "Soon, Not First" quadrant, with three small tags: high effort, shared, seasonal.

**Palette cues:** Slate garage line art, terracotta "THE GARAGE" label, honey tag pills. Keep it affectionate, never mocked.

**Motif:** The red friction-spark beside the garage is drawn smaller than the garage's footprint, the literal picture of "its friction is lower than its guilt." No green dot, because it is not the first win.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 9, Carousel slide 8.

## Beat 9: Three Filters

**Diagram:** Three icons in a row: an eye (touch it daily), a single hand (you control it), a heart with a soft pause mark (skip emotional landmines for now).

**Palette cues:** Honey underline beneath each label, ink icons on paper, roughly 3px stroke.

**Motif:** A small green calm-dot can sit under the eye and the hand (filters that point toward the win). The heart stays neutral, signaling "later, not never."

## Beat 11: The Hand Writing the Target

**Diagram:** A hand writing a single space name on a paper card or sticky note. Beside it, a small calendar tag (a rough day) and a clock (a time box).

**Palette cues:** Honey highlight on the written line, ink handwriting, paper card on the warm background. Uncluttered, one card only, to echo "one target."

**Motif:** A single green calm-dot can sit on the written card, the chosen win named at last. The writing is the commitment.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 12, Carousel slide 10.

## Beat 12: The Aimed Friction Meter (the close)

**Diagram:** The recurring friction meter, holding exactly where Chapter 3 left it, a small step toward calm. A thin crosshair or target reticle now rests further along the green (calm) side of the dial. The needle has not moved. The aim has.

**Palette cues:** Slate dial face, green calm zone on one side, terracotta or red on the friction side. The crosshair drawn in the red friction-spark color, resting on the green, the two motifs meeting in one mark.

**Motif:** This is where the green calm-dot and the red friction-spark come together. The red crosshair sits on the green calm side: friction has chosen its destination. Caption: "The needle holds, because you have not moved anything yet. But look where it is aimed now." Honor the book rule, the meter moves only when objects move, which happens in Part 2.

**Used on:** Teaching slide 13, corner of Carousel slide 10.

## How the Motifs Recur (quick reference)

- **Green calm-dot (#6E8B5B):** single dot at "proof/momentum" (Beat 4), multiplied into the Start Here cluster (Beat 7), marks the winning scorecard rows (Beat 10), sits on the written target card (Beat 11), and finally fills the calm side of the meter the crosshair lands on (Beat 12).
- **Red friction-spark (#CB4B36):** the question-mark spark over the garage (Beats 1 and 2), rides the Friction compass needle (Beat 5), tags the Friction axis card (Beat 6), shrinks beside the garage to show guilt outsizing friction (Beat 8), and becomes the crosshair at the close (Beat 12).
- **The garage:** opens the chapter as the tempting wrong start, parks in "Soon, Not First" on the map, and closes out the scorecard at the bottom. Always affectionate, never mocked. It is a graduation project, not a first lap.
