### Slide 1: The Tempting Garage
- It is the Saturday after you learned the method. You have coffee and a dangerous amount of motivation.
- A bright, certain thought arrives: "I should do the garage."
- Stop right there. Where you start decides whether you finish.

### Slide 2: Motivation Is a Budget, Not a Renewable Resource
- The energy you feel right now is real, but it is a budget, and the garage spends all of it.
- You stall at the halfway point and end up with a garage that is somehow worse than when you started.
- Then a quiet voice says you are not an organized person. That voice is lying.

[VISUAL] A simple gas-gauge or battery icon draining from full to empty over a small garage silhouette. One red friction-spark hovering over the garage with a question mark. Slate and terracotta line art.

### Slide 3: You Did Not Fail at Organizing. You Failed at Choosing.
- The problem was never your discipline. It was your aim.
- You pointed a beginner's budget of energy at an expert's project.
- The single most important move in this book that does not involve moving an object: choosing well.

[VISUAL] Two side-by-side labels, "Working harder" crossed out softly, "Choosing better" circled in honey. Calm, reassuring layout.

---

### Slide 4: Your First Project Is About Proof
- The first project is not for the room. It is for you.
- A single organized drawer will not change your life. The win it gives you changes what you do next.
- A visible win is the most reliable fuel there is. It compounds where willpower runs out.

[VISUAL] A small drawer icon with a green calm-dot, and an arrow to a second project, then a third, growing. Momentum as a chain of small wins. Quote band: "People keep going because the last thing they tried worked."

---

### Slide 5: Guilt Is a Bad Compass. Friction Is a Good One.
- The spaces we feel guiltiest about are usually the worst places to begin.
- Guilt points at the hardest, heaviest, most emotional spaces, the ones most likely to defeat a beginner.
- Stop asking which space you feel worst about. Ask which space costs you the most, most often.

[VISUAL] A compass with two needles: one labeled "Guilt" pointing at a dramatic closet, one labeled "Friction" pointing at a humble junk drawer. Friction needle glows with a small red spark. Slate dial.

---

### Slide 6: The Two Questions That Pick a Target
- Question one, friction: how much does this space cost me, and how often?
- Question two, effort: how big is the job, honestly? Not how big it feels.
- Where the two answers meet is your target. You can answer both in under a minute.

[VISUAL] Two large question cards side by side. Left card headed FRICTION with a red spark, right card headed EFFORT with a slate weight icon. An equals sign or meeting-point arrow pointing forward to the next slide.

### Slide 7: The First Target Map

- Top-left, high friction and low effort, is the sweet spot. Start Here.
- Top-right, high friction and high effort, is Soon, Not First. That is where the garage lives.

### Slide 8: Read the Four Quadrants
- Start Here (high friction, low effort): a gift. A lot of daily relief for a small one-time cost.
- Soon, Not First (high friction, high effort): it matters, but it is not first.
- Fine, Not Now (low friction, low effort): easy, but it barely changes your day. Avoid for Now (low friction, high effort): a lot of work for almost no relief.

[VISUAL] The same 2x2, but with each quadrant called out one at a time, the Start Here quadrant pulsing in green calm-dots while the others sit muted. Definition band: "The best first target is the smallest space that will still feel like a real win. High friction, low effort."

### Slide 9: Why Not the Garage (Yet)
- It is far to the right: big, heavy, awkward, and it usually hides a project you did not know about.
- Its friction is often lower than its guilt. You do not stand in it every morning.
- It is usually shared (a negotiation) and often seasonal (homes that change). A graduation project, not a first lap.

[VISUAL] The garage icon sitting squarely in the "Soon, Not First" quadrant, with three small tags: "high effort," "shared," "seasonal." Affectionate tone, not mocking. One faint red spark, smaller than its visual size, to show friction lower than guilt.

---

### Slide 10: Three Filters to Find the Best Target
- Visibility: choose a space you touch every day, so the win reintroduces itself each morning.
- Control: choose a space that is mostly yours, so the first win does not depend on negotiation.
- Emotion: skip the emotional landmines for now. Some spaces are hard because they are heavy, not big.

[VISUAL] Three simple icons in a row: an eye (daily-visible), a single hand (you control), a heart with a soft pause symbol (emotional-skip). Honey accent under each label. Warm line-and-flat-color style.

---

### Slide 11: The Target Scorecard
- List three to five spaces that genuinely annoy you. Trust the sigh; the sigh is friction.
- Score each on friction (1 to 5) and effort (1 to 5). Then subtract effort from friction.
- The highest number wins. The humble drawer beats the dramatic garage on the only math that matters.

[VISUAL] The Target Scorecard table infographic. Rows: Kitchen catch-all drawer (F4 E1 = +3), Entryway drop zone (F5 E2 = +3), Bathroom counter (F3 E1 = +2), Linen closet (F2 E3 = -1), Garage (F3 E5 = -2). Winners highlighted with a green calm-dot, the garage row muted at the bottom. Slate column headers.

### Slide 12: Choose, and Write It Down
- Pick the winner. Then write the name of your first target where you will see it in the next day or two.
- A target in your head is a wish. A target written down with a rough day attached is a plan.
- Add a time box. One space. One session. And resist the urge to add a second and a third.

[VISUAL] A hand writing a single space name on a paper card or sticky note, with a small calendar tag and a clock (time box). Honey highlight on the written line. Calm, uncluttered.
