# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 4

**"How to Choose Your First Target Area" · 6S Success: Home Edition**

Five prompts for the chapter's photographic and illustrated moments. Each is specific about subject, composition, lighting, materials, palette, and mood, aimed at the book's warm, hand-made, editorial feel rather than glossy stock or generic AI art. The palette to hold throughout: warm paper #F7F2E9, soft cream #FBF7EF, dark ink brown #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey gold #DDA63A, slate blue #3C5A6B, muted sage green #6E8B5B. Each prompt ends with a short avoid note.

---

## 1. The tempting garage (chapter opener, hero photo)

A real person standing in an open garage doorway at the threshold, seen from slightly behind and to the side, hands on hips, surveying a wall of stacked plastic bins, half-collapsed cardboard boxes, a leaning bicycle, and a lawn mower. Early morning light pours in from behind them and rakes low across the concrete floor, throwing a long soft shadow inward. The posture reads as determination, the space reads as genuinely overwhelming. Lived-in and a little dusty, not styled or staged. Warm documentary photography, 35mm look, soft natural contrast, gentle film grain. Color grade leans into warm paper and honey highlights with cool slate-blue shadow in the depths of the garage. Mood: hopeful, slightly daunted, the calm before a bad decision.
**Avoid:** no spotless showroom garage, no model smiling at camera, no harsh HDR or heavy vignette, no floating motivational text, no glossy real-estate-listing perfection.

## 2. The humble first target (companion photo)

An ordinary kitchen catch-all drawer pulled open in warm afternoon kitchen light, shot from a high three-quarter angle close in. Inside, the honest everyday jumble: tangled charger cables, a tape measure, takeaway menus, spare keys, rubber bands, a couple of dead batteries, a pen with no cap. The wood of the drawer front is worn and real. Light is soft and warm from a window just out of frame, with a quiet slate-blue shadow on the far side. Editorial cookbook photography feel, shallow depth of field, natural materials, a touch of grain. Palette of warm paper, honey, and muted sage on the drawer edge. Mood: familiar, a little rueful, the small daily annoyance the chapter wants you to start with.
**Avoid:** no perfectly organized drawer dividers, no overhead flat-lay symmetry, no plastic studio sheen, no neon colors, no text overlay.

## 3. The First Target Map as a hand-drawn page (illustrated motif)

A two-by-two grid drawn as if inked by hand into a warm cream notebook page, slightly imperfect lines with a roughly 3px brush feel and rounded corners. Vertical axis hand-lettered "friction," horizontal axis "effort," small drawn arrows. The top-left square is gently washed in muted sage green and dotted with a scatter of small green dots, hand-labeled "Start Here." The top-right square holds a tiny simple line drawing of a garage with a peaked roof and a terracotta tag reading "the garage." The other two squares sit quiet on the cream paper. Soft paper texture, a faint pencil-guide ghost under the ink, warm directional light as if photographed on a wooden desk. Mood: friendly, clear, like a smart friend sketching the idea for you on the spot.
**Avoid:** no rigid vector flatness, no corporate infographic gloss, no drop shadows or 3D bevels, no rainbow palette, no stock clip-art icons.

## 4. The guilt versus friction compass (illustrated motif)

A single old brass-and-cream compass resting on a warm paper surface, drawn in a warm editorial illustration style with confident inked outlines and flat muted fills. Two needles share the dial: one faded and spark-red, swinging toward a dim, heavy corner suggested by a tiny haunted closet and a stack of boxes; the other solid slate-blue, swinging the opposite way toward small hand-drawn icons of a drawer, an entryway shelf, and a bathroom counter. Soft raking light from the left, gentle shadow on the right, faint paper grain. Palette of warm paper, terracotta, slate, and sage. Mood: a quiet correction, choosing the better direction.
**Avoid:** no photorealistic metal reflections, no busy ornate filigree, no glowing magical effects, no compass pointing at a literal treasure chest, no caption text baked in.

## 5. The friction meter, holding but aimed (closing motif)

A half-circle gauge illustrated like a warm hand-made dial, its arc running from muted sage green on the left through honey gold in the middle to spark red on the right, drawn with a roughly 3px inked stroke and rounded ends. A dark ink needle sits low on the calm green side, clearly at rest, not moving. A thin slate-blue crosshair, a small circle with cross-lines, hovers a little further along the green side to mark a chosen aim. Set on a warm cream panel with soft even light and a faint paper texture, small hand-lettered labels "calm" in green and "friction" in red at the base. Mood: poised, decided, the moment just before action.
**Avoid:** no glossy speedometer chrome, no glowing LED or sci-fi HUD look, no motion-blur on the needle, no harsh gradients, no dark background.

---

**Note on palette and tone:** keep every render in the warm book palette above, with natural soft light and a hand-made editorial finish. The book voice is cozy but clean, so favor real materials, honest mess where the chapter calls for it, and restraint over spectacle.
