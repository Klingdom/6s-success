# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 4

**"How to Choose Your First Target Area" · 6S Success: Home Edition**

Five diagrams drawn from the chapter's own devices and language. All use the book palette (paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green #6E8B5B, spark #CB4B36), Fraunces for display, Newsreader for body, and the warm line-and-flat-color style with about a 3px stroke and rounded corners.

---

## 1. The First Target Map (the 2x2)

**Purpose:** Give the reader the whole decision in one glance, and a reusable tool they can point at any room for the rest of the book.

**What it shows:** Friction on the vertical axis (low at the bottom to high at the top, "how often it costs you") against effort on the horizontal axis (low at the left to high at the right, "how big the job is"). Four quadrants: top-left Start Here (high friction, low effort, the green-lit sweet spot with calm-dots), top-right Soon, Not First (the garage lives here), bottom-left Fine, Not Now, bottom-right Avoid for Now.

**Rough layout:** Square grid centered on the canvas. Only the top-left quadrant is colored in (green tint, green border, six calm-dots). The other three sit in quiet panel tones with light borders so the eye goes straight to the start corner. Axis names in Inter caps, value words in Newsreader. Small line-art garage glyph plus a terracotta "THE GARAGE" tag in the top-right. This is the hero, matching the HTML version exactly.

---

## 2. The Target Scorecard (friction minus effort)

**Purpose:** Land the abstract map on real, humble spaces and show the math doing the choosing, so an ordinary drawer beats the dramatic garage.

**What it shows:** Five candidate spaces scored on friction (1 to 5), effort (1 to 5), and a final friction-minus-effort score. Kitchen catch-all drawer 4 / 1 / +3, entryway drop zone 5 / 2 / +3, bathroom counter 3 / 1 / +2, linen closet 2 / 3 / -1, garage 3 / 5 / -2.

**Rough layout:** A clean table on panel. Columns left to right: SPACE, FRICTION, EFFORT, SCORE, headers in Inter caps and soft gray. The two top rows (the tied +3 winners) get a green tint and a deep-green score. The garage row sits last in a faint terracotta tint with a spark-red -2. A one-line caption under it: "Friction minus effort, and the humble drawer wins." Numbers verbatim from the HTML.

---

## 3. The Guilt versus Friction Compass

**Purpose:** Make the chapter's central reframe visual: guilt is a bad compass, friction is a good one.

**What it shows:** One compass with two possible needles. The guilt needle (spark red) swings toward the heavy corner, the garage, the haunted closet, the room you apologize for, drawn small and dim. The friction needle (slate or green) swings the other way, toward the small daily spaces, the catch-all drawer, the entryway, the bathroom counter. A label pair: "Guilt points at the hardest, heaviest spaces" versus "Friction points at what costs you most, most often."

**Rough layout:** Circular compass face centered, line-art, about a 3px stroke. Cardinal marks replaced by tiny space icons. The guilt needle is faded and crossed with a thin red friction-spark. The friction needle is solid and confident. Short caption beneath in Newsreader: "Stop asking which space you feel worst about. Start asking which space costs you the most, most often." Warm paper ground.

---

## 4. Win Where You Will See It (daily-touch versus twice-a-year)

**Purpose:** Show why a daily-touch space is worth far more than a rarely-seen one for the same effort, which is the visibility filter from "Win Where You Will See It."

**What it shows:** A side-by-side contrast. Left: a kitchen catch-all drawer marked "every meal" with four green calm-dots (the win greets you four times a day). Right: a crawl space or seasonal closet marked "twice a year" with the win hidden behind a closed door, a single faint dot. A center label: "Same effort. Wildly different payoff."

**Rough layout:** Two equal panels split down the middle by a thin honey rule. Left panel green-leaning and active, with a small repeating clock or meal mark and a cluster of calm-dots. Right panel muted, with a closed-door glyph and the win tucked out of sight. Headline across the top in Fraunces: "A daily-touch space pays you back every morning." Footer line: "Fix the drawer you open at every meal, not the closet you see twice a year."

---

## 5. The Friction Meter, Holding but Aimed

**Purpose:** Honor the book rule that the meter moves only when objects move, while marking that this chapter changed something real: the reader now knows where the needle is about to travel.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-dial friction meter, green calm zone on the left running through honey to spark red on the right. The needle sits exactly where Chapter 3 left it, a small step toward calm, and has not moved. A thin slate crosshair now rests further along the calm side, showing the chosen aim.

**Rough layout:** Half-circle gauge centered, matching the HTML meter. CALM label in green at the lower left, FRICTION label in spark at the lower right. Needle in ink at its held position. Crosshair (a slate circle with cross-lines) placed on the green side where the reader has aimed. Caption in Newsreader: "The needle holds, because you have not moved anything yet. But look where it is aimed now." Small note: the needle moves once the work begins in Part 2.
