# X Short Posts: How to Choose Your First Target Area

10 standalone posts from Chapter 4 of "6S Success: Home Edition." Each reads on its own, stays within ~280 characters, and carries one real idea from the chapter. No em dashes. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
You did not fail at organizing. You failed at choosing.

You aimed a beginner's budget of energy at an expert's project, usually the garage. Pick a smaller, smarter first target and the whole thing gets easier.

(~213 chars)

---

2/
Motivation is a budget, not a fuel tank.

Spend the whole thing on the hardest room in the house and you stall at the halfway point. Spend a little on a space you can actually finish, and you bank a win.

(~205 chars)

---

3/
Your first organizing project is not about the room. It is about proof.

A single tidy drawer will not change your life. The win it gives you changes what you do next. That is the entire point of starting small.

(~213 chars)

---

4/
People do not keep going because they are disciplined. They keep going because the last thing they tried worked and they want that feeling again.

A win you can see does not run out the way willpower does. It compounds.

(~219 chars)

---

5/
Guilt is a terrible compass for decluttering.

It points at the hardest, heaviest, most emotional spaces, the exact ones most likely to defeat a beginner. Friction is the better compass: which space costs you the most, most often?

(~233 chars)

---

6/
Two questions pick your first target.

Friction: how much does this space cost me, and how often?
Effort: how big is the job, honestly?

The sweet spot is high friction, low effort. Big daily relief, small one-time cost. That is where you start.

(~248 chars)

---

7/
Tempted to start with the garage? A few honest reasons to wait.

High effort. Usually shared. Often seasonal. And its friction is often lower than its guilt. The garage is a graduation project, not a first lap.

Chapter 4 talks you out of it, kindly.

(~252 chars)

---

8/
Win where you will see it.

A daily-touch space is a win that reintroduces itself every morning. Fix the drawer you open at every meal and the win greets you four times a day. Fix a space you see twice a year and it vanishes the moment you close the door.

(~258 chars)

---

9/
Some spaces are not hard because they are big. They are hard because they are heavy.

The box of a parent's things. A child's outgrown clothes. Skip these for now. Earn the keep-or-release muscle on a junk drawer first.

(~221 chars)

---

10/
Pick your first target and write it down.

List 3 to 5 annoying spaces. Score friction and effort 1 to 5. Subtract effort from friction. Highest number wins.

A target in your head is a wish. Written down with a day attached, it is a plan. Free online, Chapter 4.

(~263 chars)
