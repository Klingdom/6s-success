# X Thread: How to Choose Your First Target Area

Chapter 4 of "6S Success: Home Edition." One thread, 12 posts. Each post stays within ~280 characters and reads on its own. No em dashes.

---

1/
You did not fail at organizing. You failed at choosing.

You aimed a beginner's budget of energy at an expert's project. Usually that project is the garage.

Here is how to pick a first target you can actually win.

(~205 chars)

---

2/
The day after you learn a method is the day you most want to point it at the hardest room in the house.

Fresh off a win, you stand in the garage doorway with one cup of coffee and big plans. Where you point that first project decides whether you finish it.

(~255 chars)

---

3/
Motivation is a budget, not a fuel tank.

The garage will spend the whole thing by lunch. You stall at the halfway point, end up with a worse mess than you started, and a quiet voice says "see, you are not really organized."

That voice is lying. You just chose wrong.

(~270 chars)

---

4/
Your first project is not really about a room.

A single organized drawer will not change your life. The win it gives you will change what you do next. The first project is for proof, not for the room.

(~205 chars)

---

5/
People do not keep going because they are disciplined.

They keep going because the last thing they tried worked and they want that feeling again.

A win you can see is the most reliable fuel there is. It does not run out the way willpower does. It compounds.

(~258 chars)

---

6/
The spaces we most want to fix are usually the worst place to begin.

We feel the most guilt about them, so we attack them first. But guilt is a terrible compass. It points at the hardest, heaviest, most emotional spaces, the ones most likely to defeat a beginner.

(~268 chars)

---

7/
There is a better compass, and you already carry it: friction.

Stop asking which space you feel worst about. Start asking which space costs you the most, most often.

Friction is not about how bad a space looks. It is about how often it taxes your actual life.

(~262 chars)

---

8/
Two questions pick a target.

1. Friction: how much does this space cost me, and how often?
2. Effort: how big is the job, honestly?

Where those two answers meet is your first target. That is the whole decision.

(~213 chars)

---

9/
Picture a simple map. Friction on one axis, effort on the other.

The sweet spot is top-left: high friction, low effort. Big daily relief, small one-time cost.

The best first target is the smallest space that will still feel like a real win.

(~243 chars)

---

10/
Why not the garage?

High effort. Usually shared. Often seasonal. And its friction is often lower than its guilt. You do not stand in it every morning.

A win you cannot see every day cannot keep you going. The garage is a graduation project, not a first lap.

(~258 chars)

---

11/
Win where you will see it.

Pick a space you touch daily, so the win greets you every morning. Pick a space you control, so no family meeting is needed. Skip the emotional landmines for now.

A kitchen catch-all drawer. The entryway drop zone. Humble, daily, yours.

(~265 chars)

---

12/
Make a shortlist. List 3 to 5 spaces that genuinely annoy you. Score each on friction and effort, 1 to 5. Subtract effort from friction. Highest number wins. Then write it down.

A target in your head is a wish. Written down, it is a plan.
