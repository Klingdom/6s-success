## 3. Why the First Target Decides Everything
The first project is for you, not the room. It exists to prove the method works. A visible win is fuel that compounds, where willpower runs out. Small and certain beats big and brave at the start. Guilt is a terrible compass (it points at the hardest, most emotional spaces). Friction is the better compass: ask which space costs you most, most often.
- Callout (6S Tip): Your first project is about proof, one undeniable win you can see.
- Callout (Common Mistake): Choosing the space you feel guiltiest about instead of the one that bites daily.

## 4. The Two Questions That Pick a Target
Question one, friction: how much does this space cost me, and how often? Question two, effort: how big is the job, honestly? Where the two answers meet is your target. High friction and low effort is the sweet spot.
- Hero infographic: The First Target Map (friction vs effort 2x2, sweet spot "Start Here," garage in "Soon, Not First")
- Definition box: The best first target is the smallest space that will still feel like a real win. High friction, low effort.

## 5. Why Not the Garage (Yet)
The garage is far right (high effort), big, hides projects, and its friction is often lower than its guilt (you do not stand in it every morning). It is usually shared (negotiation) and seasonal (changing homes). It is a graduation project, not a first lap.
- Callout (Common Mistake): Starting with the garage, basement, or storage unit. A win you cannot see daily cannot keep you going.

## 6. Win Where You Will See It (three filters)
Visibility: choose a daily-touch space so the win reintroduces itself each morning. Control: choose a space mostly yours, so the first win does not depend on negotiation. Emotion: skip the emotional landmines for now (sentimental storage needs a skill you build later). Ideal first target: small, daily, mostly-yours, no emotional weight, high daily annoyance.
- Callout (6S Tip): A daily-touch space is a win that reintroduces itself every morning.

## 7. Make Your Shortlist
Walk the home, name three to five genuinely annoying spaces (trust the sigh; it is friction). Score each on friction (1 to 5) and effort (1 to 5). Subtract effort from friction; the highest score is the target. The humble drawer beats the dramatic garage.
- Infographic: The Target Scorecard (kitchen drawer +3, entryway drop zone +3, bathroom counter +2, linen closet -1, garage -2)
- Callout (Quick Win): Name the one space that annoyed you most last week. That feeling is friction pointing at your target.

## 8. Choose, and Write It Down
Pick the winner and write it down where you will see it; a written target with a day attached is a plan, not a wish. Add a rough day and a time box. Resist adding a second and third target.
- Checklist (Pick your first target): list 3 to 5 annoying spaces, score friction and effort, subtract, write it down, pick a day and a time box.
- Callout (Family Challenge): Everyone nominates their most annoying space and guesses the time. Loudest complaint plus shortest time goes first.

## 9. Chapter Close: You Have a Target
You leave with a target chosen on purpose, small enough to win and annoying enough to matter. The skill was not working harder, it was choosing better. Momentum is built one well-chosen target at a time. Next, Part 2, we look hard at the target before changing it.
- Boxed recap (One Idea to Keep): You do not need more motivation to start. You need a smaller, smarter first target. Win where you will see it, and the next project gets easier on its own.
