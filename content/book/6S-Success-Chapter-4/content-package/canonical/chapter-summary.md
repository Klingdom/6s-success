# Chapter 4 Summary: How to Choose Your First Target Area

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part One, Discovering 6S*

## One-line summary

The best first target is not the biggest room or the one you feel worst about. It is the smallest space that will still feel like a real win: high friction, low effort.

## Short summary (about 100 words)

Chapter 4 catches the reader fresh off their Chapter 3 lap, motivated and about to charge the garage, and gently redirects them. The first project is not really about a room, it is about proof, and a visible win is the fuel that makes the next project happen. The chapter gives a one-minute decision tool, the First Target Map, which scores any space on two questions: how much does it cost me daily (friction) and how big is the job (effort). The sweet spot is high friction, low effort. It ends with the reader naming one target and writing it down.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 4 closes Part One by turning a reader who understands the method into a reader who has chosen one real first target. It opens on the dangerous Saturday morning after the Chapter 3 lap, when motivation is high and the reader is tempted to attack the garage. The chapter stops them, kindly, and reframes the choice.

The first project, it argues, is for the reader, not the room. It exists to prove that the method works, and a win you can see is the most reliable fuel there is, because it compounds where willpower runs out. The spaces we feel guiltiest about (the garage, the haunted closet) are usually the worst places to begin, because guilt points at the hardest, heaviest, most emotional spaces. Friction is the better compass.

The hero device is the First Target Map, a two-by-two of friction (how often a space costs you) against effort (how big the job is). The high-friction, low-effort quadrant is the "Start Here" sweet spot. The garage sits in "Soon, Not First." The chapter then dismantles the garage as a first target (high effort, often shared and seasonal, lower daily friction than its guilt), and adds three filters: pick a daily-touch space so the win keeps paying off, pick a space you control, and skip emotional landmines for now.

It closes with action: list three to five annoying spaces, score each on friction and effort, subtract effort from friction, pick the winner, and write it down. One target. The friction meter holds steady but is now aimed. Next is the audit in Chapter 5.
