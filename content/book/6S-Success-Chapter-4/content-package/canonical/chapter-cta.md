# Chapter 4 Calls to Action: How to Choose Your First Target Area

This chapter chooses a target; it does not do the work. Its strongest CTA is the decision itself: pick one first target and write it down. The CTAs match the chapter's encouraging, anti-overwhelm tone.

## Primary CTA
Choose your first target. "List three to five spaces that genuinely annoy you. Score each on friction (how often it bites) and effort (how big the job). Subtract effort from friction. The highest number is your first target. Write it down where you will see it. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "If you are tempted to start with the garage, read this first. Chapter 4 shows you how to pick a first target you can actually win. Read it free online, then Chapter 5 helps you see that target clearly before you touch it."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Name the one space that annoyed you most in the last week. Not the messiest. The most annoying. That is friction pointing at your first target.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone nominates their most annoying space and guesses the time to fix it. Loudest complaint plus shortest time goes first.

## Soft CTAs for social posts (rotate these)
- "This is from Chapter 4 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online."
- "Tempted to start with the garage? Chapter 4 talks you out of it, kindly."
- "Your first target in one line: high friction, low effort. The chapter shows you how to find it."
- "Grab the free First Target Map worksheet and pick your spot."
- "Next after you choose: Chapter 5, seeing your target clearly before you touch it."

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the signature)
- **The First Target Map worksheet:** a printable blank two-by-two so the reader can plot their own candidate spaces.
- **The Target Scorecard:** a single tear-off page to list and score three to five candidate spaces, with the winner circled.

## CTA writing notes
- The decision is the action. Lead with "pick one target and write it down," which is concrete and free.
- Keep the tone anti-overwhelm. The whole chapter exists to stop people from biting off too much, so the CTAs must not push them to start everything at once.
- The garage is the relatable hook. "Tempted to start with the garage?" is the single most clickable line in this chapter.
- Avoid hype and big promises. Small, smart, winnable. Keep claims honest.
