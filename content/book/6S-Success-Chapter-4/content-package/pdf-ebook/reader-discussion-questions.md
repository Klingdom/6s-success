# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions

*Chapter 4: How to Choose Your First Target Area. Ten open-ended questions grounded in the chapter.*

---

1. Think about the last time you tried to get organized. Where did you start, and how did it go? Looking back, was the problem the effort you put in, or the space you chose?

2. The chapter says the first project is for you, not the room, and that it exists to prove the method works. Has a small early win ever kept you going on something? What did that win do that willpower alone could not?

3. The author calls guilt a bad compass and friction a good one. Which space in your home do you feel most guilty about? Which one actually costs you the most, most often? Are they the same place or different?

4. The garage is the chapter's tempting wrong start. What is your version of the garage, the dramatic, high-effort project you keep wanting to charge at first? What keeps pulling you toward it?

5. The chapter rests on two questions: how much does a space cost me and how often (friction), and how big is the job, honestly (effort). When you score your own spaces, which question is harder for you to answer honestly, and why?

6. "Win where you will see it." How might choosing a daily-touch space change how long your motivation lasts, compared with fixing something you only see twice a year?

7. The second filter is to pick a space you mostly control. Where in your home would starting a project mean a negotiation with other people? How does sharing a space change the decision about whether to start there first?

8. The chapter gently says to skip the emotional landmines for now, the sentimental boxes and the museum closets. Which spaces in your home are hard because they are heavy rather than big? Why might those be the wrong place to begin?

9. The author insists that writing the target down turns a wish into a plan. Do you tend to keep your intentions in your head or on paper? What has that habit cost you or saved you?

10. The chapter warns motivated people against adding a second and third target before finishing the first. Where do you fall on the line between healthy ambition and overreach, and how would you hold yourself to just one target?
