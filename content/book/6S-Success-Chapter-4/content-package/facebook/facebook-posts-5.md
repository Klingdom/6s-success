## Post 1 · The tempting garage

It is Saturday. You have had a good cup of coffee and a dangerous amount of motivation. And a thought arrives, bright and certain: "I should do the garage."

Please don't. 🙂

I say that with love. The garage feels like the brave choice, but it is the one most likely to end your whole organizing streak before it starts. You pull everything out, you run out of daylight halfway through, and now the garage is somehow worse than when you began.

That is not you being bad at this. That is a beginner's budget of energy aimed at an expert's project.

Start somewhere smaller. The garage can wait.

## Post 2 · Guilt vs friction

Quick question. When you think about where to start organizing, do you pick the space you feel worst about?

Most of us do. And that is exactly the trap.

Guilt is a terrible compass. It points straight at the hardest, heaviest, most emotional spaces in your home. The ones most likely to defeat you on day one.

Here is a better compass: friction. Instead of asking "which space do I feel guiltiest about," ask "which space costs me the most, most often?"

It is rarely the same place. The drawer that fights you every single morning matters more than the closet you only feel bad about.

Friction is not about how a space looks. It is about how often it taxes your actual life.

## Post 3 · Win where you will see it

A small thing that makes a big difference: start somewhere you will actually see the win.

A daily-touch space, like a kitchen catch-all drawer or the bathroom counter, reintroduces the win to you every single morning. Each time the drawer answers you instead of fighting you, it quietly recruits you for the next project.

A space you see twice a year? The win hides the second you close the door.

So when you pick your first target, pick something that lives in your daily path. Bonus points if it is mostly yours to control, so your first win does not depend on negotiating with anyone else.

Small, daily, and yours. That is the sweet spot.

## Post 4 · The scorecard

Stuck on where to start? Here is a one-minute way to decide, no agonizing required.

1. Walk your home and name three to five spaces that genuinely annoy you. Trust the sigh. That sigh is friction talking.
2. Give each one a friction score from 1 to 5 (how often it bites you).
3. Give each one an effort score from 1 to 5 (how big the job honestly is).
4. Subtract effort from friction. Highest number wins.

That is your first target. Usually it is something humble, like the entryway drop zone or a kitchen drawer, not the dramatic garage.

The math does the choosing, so you don't have to.

## Post 5 · Write it down

One last step that people skip, and it matters more than you would think.

Write your target down.

A target that lives only in your head is a wish. A target written on paper, with a rough day attached to it, is a plan. That small act of writing it down is the commitment.

So pick one space. Just one. Resist the urge to add a second and a third. Give it a rough day and a time box, one space and one session, and put the note somewhere you will see it.

You do not need more motivation to start. You need a smaller, smarter first target.
