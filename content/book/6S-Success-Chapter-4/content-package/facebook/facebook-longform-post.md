# Facebook Longform Post: The Saturday-Morning Garage

*Chapter 4 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Conversational long-form, roughly 380 words.*

---

Let me tell you about a very specific Saturday morning.

You have read up on the method. Something finally clicked. You are standing in the kitchen with a second cup of coffee and a genuinely dangerous amount of motivation, and a thought arrives, bright and certain and completely wrong:

"I should do the garage."

I want to gently put a hand on your shoulder before you open that door. Because the garage is the project that feels like the brave choice and turns out to be the one that quietly ends your whole organizing streak before it really starts.

Here is the hard truth about motivation. It does not last long, and it does not come back on command. The burst of energy you feel right now is real, but it is also a budget. And you are about to spend the entire thing on the single most expensive room in your house.

You will pull everything out onto the driveway. You will run out of daylight and steam at roughly the halfway point. And you will be left with a garage that is somehow worse than when you started, a sore back, and a quiet little voice that says, "See, you are not really an organized person after all."

That voice is lying to you. You did not fail at organizing. You failed at choosing. You aimed a beginner's budget of energy at an expert's project.

So here is the reframe. Your first project is not really about the room at all. It is about proof. It exists to show the part of your brain that has watched you start and quit a dozen times that this time is different, because this time it actually worked.

And a win you can see is the most reliable fuel there is. It does not run out the way willpower does. It compounds.

That is why a small, certain win beats a big, brave attempt every single time at the start. The garage is a graduation project. It is not your first lap.

So this weekend, don't choose harder. Choose better.

Pick one small target. A drawer, the bathroom counter, the spot by the door where everything piles up. Something you will finish in one sitting and see every morning after.
