# B-Roll and Visual Notes: How to Choose Your First Target Area

**Source:** 6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 4
**Slug:** how-to-choose-your-first-target-area
**Purpose:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and teleprompter pieces. Honors the book's visual system: warm tones, green calm-dots, red friction-sparks, and the friction meter that holds but is now aimed.

---

## Visual system reminders (keep consistent with the book)

- Warm, lived-in lighting. Morning light where possible. Honest, not staged.
- Green calm-dots signal a solved, low-friction space. Red friction-sparks signal a space that costs you.
- The hero device is the First Target Map, a two-by-two of friction (vertical) vs effort (horizontal).
- The friction meter is a small dial. The book rule: it moves only when objects move. In this chapter it holds, but gains a thin crosshair on the calm green side, because the reader has aimed but not yet fired.
- Keep the garage affectionate, never mocked. It is a graduation project, not a villain.

## SHOT LIST

### 1. The overwhelming garage (hero / cold open)
[B-ROLL] A person stands in an open garage doorway, hands on hips, surveying a wall of bins, bikes, half-collapsed boxes, a lawn mower. Morning light pours in behind them. Posture is pure determination. The space is genuinely overwhelming.
- Coverage: wide establishing from inside looking out toward the silhouette; medium from behind the shoulder; slow push-in on the cluttered wall.
- Optional treatment: a single red friction-spark and a faint question mark float over the garage to mark it as tempting but wrong-first.
- Mood: this is the moment of motivated overreach the whole piece exists to rescue.

### 2. The doomed dig (motivation as a budget)
[B-ROLL] Hands pulling everything out of the packed garage onto the driveway. Time-lapse feel. The pile grows, not shrinks. Daylight shifting from bright to late-afternoon gold.
- End on a wide shot of a half-emptied garage that looks worse than the start, and a person sitting on a step, tired.
- This visualizes "you run out of daylight and steam at roughly the halfway point."

### 3. The humble high-friction drawer (the contrast / start-here)
[B-ROLL] A close, tactile look at an ordinary kitchen catch-all drawer. First version: jammed, tangled cords, dead batteries, takeout menus, a single red friction-spark. A hand fights it, digs, sighs.
- Then the resolved version: the same drawer calm and sorted, green calm-dots, a hand reaching in and grabbing exactly one thing instantly.
- Repeat the easy grab four quick times to sell "the win greets you four times a day."
- This is the deliberate David-vs-Goliath contrast against the garage. Humble, unimpressive, perfect.

### 4. Companion daily-touch spaces (the sweet-spot family)
[B-ROLL] Quick, warm cutaways of the other ideal first targets: an entryway drop zone (keys, mail, sunglasses fighting on a shelf), a bathroom counter, a nightstand. Each gets a before with a small red spark, an after with green calm-dots.
- Use for the "win where you will see it" and three-filters sections.

### 5. The First Target Map (hero graphic)
[GRAPHIC] Animated two-by-two grid, built in stages.
- Vertical axis draws in: Friction, low to high.
- Horizontal axis draws in: Effort, low to high.
- Quadrants fill: top-left lights up green with calm-dots, "Start Here." Top-right shows a small garage icon, "Soon, Not First." Bottom-left "Fine, Not Now." Bottom-right "Avoid for Now."
- Hold on the glowing top-left sweet spot for the "high friction, low effort" line.
- This graphic should be drawn so the sweet-spot quadrant can be lifted out as a standalone square for social.

### 6. The garage in its rightful quadrant (why not the garage, yet)
[GRAPHIC + B-ROLL] Push the garage icon across the map and drop it firmly into the top-right "Soon, Not First" quadrant.
- Intercut with a slow, quiet pan across a cluttered garage that nobody is standing in, to make the point that its friction is lower than its guilt. It looms in the mind, but it does not bite daily.

### 7. The three filters (icon spot)
[GRAPHIC] Three simple icons animate in one at a time, in the warm line-and-flat-color style, roughly 3px stroke, rounded corners.
- An eye (visibility, daily-touch).
- A single hand (control, mostly yours).
- A heart (skip emotional landmines).
- Each icon highlights as its filter is narrated.

### 8. The Target Scorecard (secondary graphic)
[GRAPHIC] A clean table animates row by row.
- Rows: Kitchen catch-all drawer, Bathroom counter, Entryway drop zone, Linen closet, Garage.
- Columns: Friction (1 to 5), Effort (1 to 5), Friction minus Effort.
- Reveal the math: Kitchen drawer 4 minus 1 equals 3. Bathroom counter 3 minus 1 equals 2. Entryway drop zone 5 minus 2 equals 3. Linen closet 2 minus 3 equals negative 1. Garage 3 minus 5 equals negative 2.
- The kitchen drawer and entryway drop zone glow green at the top. The garage sits at the bottom.

### 9. Writing the target down (the commitment)
[B-ROLL] Close on a hand writing a single space name on a small piece of paper or a sticky note, then placing it somewhere visible: a fridge, a bathroom mirror, a phone lock screen.
- Slow, deliberate, warm. This is the emotional payoff shot. A wish becoming a plan.
- Optional: a hand adding a rough day and a small time box next to the name.

### 10. The friction meter, holding but aimed (close)
[GRAPHIC] The recurring friction meter, a small dial holding exactly where Chapter 3 left it, a small step toward calm. A thin crosshair or target reticle settles over the calm green side of the dial.
- Animate the crosshair arriving and locking. The needle itself does NOT move.
- Caption beat: the needle holds because you have not moved anything yet, but look where it is aimed now.

## EDIT AND PACING NOTES

- Open cold on the garage, no logo, within the first second. The wrong thought lands fast.
- Let the humble drawer breathe. The contrast with the garage is the whole emotional argument.
- Build the First Target Map in time with the narration, axis by axis, so the viewer learns the tool, not just sees it.
- Save the friction meter crosshair for the final beat. It is the quiet, satisfying button on the piece.
- Keep claims honest and the tone anti-overwhelm. No hype, no big promises. Small, smart, winnable.
