# Teleprompter Script: How to Choose Your First Target Area

**Source:** 6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 4
**Slug:** how-to-choose-your-first-target-area
**Length:** approx. 700 words
**Read:** Narration only. Short lines. Natural pace. Warm, calm, unhurried.

---

There is a specific kind of Saturday morning
this is for.

You have learned a method.
You feel ready.
Second cup of coffee in your hand.

And a thought arrives.
Bright, certain, completely wrong.

I should do the garage.

I want to stop you right there.
Gently.
Before you lose your whole weekend
and most of your enthusiasm.

Here is the hard truth about motivation.
It does not last long.
And it does not come back on command.

The energy you feel right now is real.
But it is also a budget.
And the garage is the most expensive room
in your house.

You pull everything out.
You run out of daylight halfway through.
And you are left with a garage
that is somehow worse than when you started.

Plus a quiet little voice that says,
see, you are not really an organized person.

That voice is lying to you.

You did not fail at organizing.
You failed at choosing.
You aimed a beginner's budget of energy
at an expert's project.

So let us be honest about
what your first project is really for.

It is not for the space.
One organized drawer
will not change your life.

The first project is for you.
It exists to prove one quiet point.
This time is different.
This time it actually worked.

Because people do not keep going
because they are disciplined.

They keep going
because the last thing they tried worked,
and they want that feeling again.

A win you can see
is the most reliable fuel there is.
It does not run out the way willpower does.
It compounds.

So here is how you choose.
Two questions.
Under a minute, for any space.

Question one is friction.
How much does this space cost you,
and how often?

The drawer you fight every evening
is expensive.
The crawl space you open twice a year
is cheap.

Friction is not about how bad a space looks.
It is about how often
it taxes your actual life.

Question two is effort.
How big is the job, honestly?

A drawer is fifteen minutes.
A closet is an afternoon.
The garage is a series of weekends.

Now put them together.
High friction, low effort.
A space that costs you a lot every day,
and very little to fix once.

That is the sweet spot.
That is where you start.

And this is why guilt leads you wrong.
Guilt points at the garage.
The haunted closet.
The room you apologize for.

Guilt points at the hardest,
heaviest, most emotional spaces.
Exactly the ones most likely
to defeat a beginner.

Friction is the better compass.
Stop asking which space you feel worst about.
Start asking which space costs you the most,
most often.

Once you are in the sweet spot,
three quick filters.

One.
Choose a space you touch every day,
so the win greets you every morning.

Two.
Choose a space that is mostly yours,
so your first win
does not depend on a negotiation.

Three.
Skip the emotional landmines for now.
Some spaces are not hard because they are big.
They are hard because they are heavy.
Start somewhere light.

So here is what to do.
Name three to five spaces
that genuinely annoy you.
Not the messiest.
The most annoying.

Trust the sigh.
The sigh is friction talking.

Score each one.
Friction, one to five.
Effort, one to five.
Subtract effort from friction.

The highest number is your target.
The humble drawer beats the dramatic garage.
And that is exactly right.

Then do the most powerful little thing left.
Write it down.

A target that lives only in your head
is a wish.
A target written down,
with a day attached,
is a plan.

One target.
One session.
A rough day, a time box.
And resist the urge to add a second.

You do not need more motivation to start.
You need a smaller, smarter first target.

Win where you will see it,
and the next project gets easier on its own.

Momentum is not a personality trait.
It is a thing you build,
one well-chosen target at a time.

You have not moved a single object yet.
And that is exactly right.

But look where you are aimed now.

So before you do anything else,
pick your one target,
and write it down.

That is the whole job today.

---

*End. Narration only.*
