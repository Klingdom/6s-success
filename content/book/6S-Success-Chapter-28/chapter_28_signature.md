- **The rule (frozen):** When the same problem keeps coming back, stop fixing it and start solving it: ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again.
- **The method (frozen):** Solve a recurring problem together in four small steps: name the problem that keeps drifting back; ask why, a few times, until you reach the real cause; change the small system so the cause is gone; and let the people who live with it help decide the fix, so they keep it.
- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** Family problem solving is how a household settles the recurring problems a home throws up, together: when the same thing keeps drifting back, you stop patching the symptom, ask why it keeps happening rather than who is to blame, and change the small system so it cannot easily happen again. It fixes the cause once, instead of the symptom forever.
- **The fixing-versus-solving reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix. It is a problem you are failing to solve. Fixing puts the thing back; solving changes why it fell in the first place, so that you never have to put it back again.
- **The why-not-who reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** When something keeps going wrong, the useless question is who keeps doing it, and the useful one is why it keeps happening. Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place.
- **The solve-together reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The person who lives with a problem is the one who knows why it really happens, and the one who has to keep any fix alive, so they are exactly who should help design it. A solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** fix versus solve / ask why, not who / change the system, not the symptom / solve it together. Chapter 27 finds the recurring problems; Chapter 28 solves them, together.

## Motifs
- **Fixing versus solving.** A problem fixed more than twice is one you are failing to solve; fixing puts it back, solving changes why it fell.
- **Why, not who.** The question that turns blame into design; a person to blame changes nothing, a system to fix changes everything.
- **A design flaw, not a character flaw.** The real cause is almost always the missing or awkward part of a system, never a person.
- **Solve it together.** The people who live with a problem know its cause and keep the fix; handed-down solutions are dropped.
- **Fix the cause once.** Solving ends the endless loop of patching the same thing forever.
- **A home that solves itself.** The second piece of keeping-machinery; the needle takes its second Sustain step.

## Emotional purpose
Warm, collaborative, and above all relieving, the relief of stopping an exhausting cycle of patching the same annoyance forever. The chapter must be neither a cold corporate framework nor a tense blaming family meeting; it is light, human, genuinely shared, and children are included. It carries the no-blame spirit of the audit chapter all the way through: nobody is ever the problem, because the method turns "who is at fault" into "why does the system fail". No shame, no blame, no scolding of any family member, real or imagined.
