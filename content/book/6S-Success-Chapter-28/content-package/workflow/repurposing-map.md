# Chapter 28 · Repurposing Map

How the one chapter fans out into every channel, and which idea leads on each.

## The spine (reused across channels)
- You have the keeping mindset now, from Chapter 26, and the friendly check-in from Chapter 27 that catches drift while it is small; here is how to solve the more stubborn thing the check-in keeps turning up, not the one-off slips, which are a two-minute fix, but the same coats on the same chair, the same dishes by the sink, the same shoes in the same doorway, week after week, that you keep fixing and that keep coming back, until a small, tired frustration begins to build. Here is the reframe that dissolves it, and it is the whole of this chapter: a problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct, it is a signal that a small system is wrong
- The fixing-versus-solving distinction (frozen reframe): a problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix, it is a problem you are failing to solve, fixing puts the thing back and solving changes why it fell in the first place so you never have to put it back again; a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often, any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping the floor more diligently, it has to be solved at the cause, once; fixing is mopping the floor, solving is finding the leak, and if you find yourself mopping the same square of floor every single day, some part of you already knows that more mopping is not the answer
- The definition box (frozen): family problem solving is how a household settles the recurring problems a home throws up, together, when the same thing keeps drifting back you stop patching the symptom, ask why it keeps happening rather than who is to blame, and change the small system so it cannot easily happen again, it fixes the cause once, instead of the symptom forever
- Ask why, not who (frozen reframe), the heart of the method: when something keeps going wrong, the useless question is who keeps doing it and the useful one is why it keeps happening, who finds a person to blame and changes nothing while why finds a system to fix and changes everything, the coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy, they are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place; this carries the no-blame spirit of the audit straight into problem-solving, you get from symptom to cause by gently asking why a few times the way a curious child would, and the real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw, which is good news because you cannot easily change a person but can nearly always change a system
- Solve it together (frozen reframe): the person who lives with a problem is the one who knows why it really happens and the one who has to keep any fix alive, so they are exactly who should help design it, a solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped while a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps; it is doubly true for two reasons, knowledge and ownership, so you bring the recurring problem to the household as a shared puzzle rather than a verdict and let everyone, including the children, who are often startlingly good at this, propose the fix
- The four-step method (frozen), run on the coats: solve a recurring problem together in four small steps, name the problem that keeps drifting back, ask why a few times until you reach the real cause, change the small system so the cause is gone, and let the people who live with it help decide the fix so they keep it; the household sits down for five minutes, names it plainly as a problem and not a person, asks why together until they reach a cause everyone recognizes, adds a row of hooks by the door so the shortest path and the right path are the same path, and because a child picked where the hooks went and hammered one in, it is theirs, and the problem that returned every week for a year quietly stops returning at all
- The rule (frozen, hero): when the same problem keeps coming back, stop fixing it and start solving it, ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again; and the One Idea to Keep (frozen): when something keeps going wrong, do not look for someone to blame, look, together, for the small system to fix, fix the cause once, and you will never fix the symptom again
- The friction meter takes its SECOND Sustain step, a small MOVE further along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from Chapter 27's position toward the goal; this is NOT a milestone and honestly small, because you solved one recurring problem and not all of them at once, but it is real and permanent, because a source of friction that used to come back every single week is simply gone now, and a home with one fewer returning problem is a home that keeps itself that little bit more on its own; Chapter 27's position is a faint ghost just behind the needle and Chapter 26's fainter still, the dashed path continues shorter now to the goal crosshair, the anti-backslide catch that Standardize locked stays fully engaged, five of six S's are done, and the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30
