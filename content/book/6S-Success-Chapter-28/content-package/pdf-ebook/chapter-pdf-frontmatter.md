# Family Problem Solving

**Part Eight, Sustain**
Chapter 28 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

Subtitle · Some drift is not a slip to correct but a signal that a small system is wrong, and quick fixes will never settle it, because you keep patching the symptom while the cause sits untouched. When the same problem keeps coming back, the household stops patching and solves it together: by asking why it happens, never who is to blame, and changing the little system so it cannot easily happen again.

---

## Chapter descriptor

The third chapter of Part Eight, and it adds the second piece of keeping-machinery on top of Chapter 27's friendly audit. Chapter 26 gave the mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, and Chapter 27 gave the first concrete practice, a light, regular check-in that catches drift while it is small. That check-in is good at what it does, but it keeps turning up a more stubborn kind of finding: not a one-off slip, corrected in two minutes and forgotten, but the same coats on the same chair, the same dishes by the sink, the same shoes in the same doorway, week after week. You keep fixing them, and they keep coming back, and a small, tired frustration builds, because it feels like the home, or the family, or you, keeps failing at something simple. This chapter is the reframe that dissolves that frustration, and it is the whole of the chapter: a problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct, it is a signal that a small system is wrong, and a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often, any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping the floor more diligently, so it has to be solved, at the cause, once. It draws the distinction the chapter turns on, between fixing and solving: a problem you have to fix more than twice is not one you are failing to fix but one you are failing to solve, because fixing puts the thing back while solving changes why it fell, so you never put it back again, which is to say fixing is mopping the floor and solving is finding the leak. It fits the hero in a plain definition box, that family problem solving is how a household settles the recurring problems a home throws up, together, patching the symptom no longer and instead asking why it keeps happening rather than who is to blame, and changing the small system so it cannot easily happen again. Then it delivers the two things that make solving work, more than any technique. The first is the heart of the method, ask why and never who, carrying the no-blame spirit of the audit straight into problem-solving, because who finds a person to blame and changes nothing while why finds a system to fix and changes everything, so you gently ask why a few times, the way a curious child would, until you reach the real cause, which is almost always a design flaw and not a character flaw, a missing hook or an awkward distance rather than a lazy person, and that is genuinely good news, because you cannot easily change a person but you can nearly always change a system. The second is the discipline of solving together, because the person who lives with the problem knows why it really happens and has to keep any fix alive, so they are exactly who should help design it, and because a solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped while a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps. It walks the four-step method end to end on the coats that always land on the chair, naming the problem plainly, asking why together until the cause everyone recognizes is said aloud, changing the small system so the shortest path and the right path are the same path, a row of hooks by the door, and deciding the fix together so that, because a child picked the spot and hammered a hook in, it is theirs and the problem that returned every week for a year quietly stops returning at all. Then it reads the friction meter as a small second Sustain step, not a milestone: the needle moves a little further along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from Chapter 27's position toward the goal, because a home that solves its recurring problems, instead of patching them forever, has removed a source of returning friction and become a little more able to keep itself, though the move is honestly small, because you solved one recurring problem and not all of them at once. It hands off to the rest of Part Eight before Chapter 28 is done. Five of the six S's are complete, Sustain is under way, and the goal, its dashed path a little shorter again, is reached only at the very end of the book, when all six S's are done.

## How to read this chapter

This is the third chapter of Part Eight, Sustain, and it gives the household a shared way to solve the recurring problems that keep coming back. It picks up exactly where the last two chapters left off: you have the keeping mindset from Chapter 26, that a home is kept and not finished, and the friendly check-in from Chapter 27, that catches drift while it is still small. What you did not yet have was what to do about the findings the check-in keeps turning up that will not go away, and this chapter hands you that, the ability to take one of those recurring problems, understand why it really keeps happening, and change the little system so that it stops, for good. Read it through once for the reframe, that a problem which keeps coming back is a signal that a small system is wrong and has to be solved rather than fixed, then do the one small, concrete thing it asks: take the one thing in your home you are tired of fixing, the pile that always returns to the same spot, and ask out loud, "why does this keep happening here?", three or four times, until you reach something you can actually change.

The chapter rests on a plain reframe and two disciplines. The reframe is that fixing is not solving: fixing corrects the symptom and is necessary, quick, and completely temporary, while solving looks past the symptom to the cause and changes that, so the symptom stops being produced. The two disciplines are what make solving work, more than any technique. The first is to ask why, not who, because the useless question is who keeps doing it and the useful one is why it keeps happening, and who only ever finds a person to blame and changes nothing while why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The second is to solve it together, because the person who lives with the problem knows why it happens and has to keep the fix alive, so the household works it out as a shared puzzle rather than a verdict handed down. The register throughout is warm, collaborative, and above all blameless: nobody is ever the problem, a recurring problem is a design flaw and not a character flaw, the coats are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place and not because anyone is lazy, and the chapter never once scolds a family member, real or imagined. The emotional heart is relief, the relief of ending the exhausting loop of patching the same thing forever.

A word on the scope fence, so the chapter stays the shared way of solving the recurring problems, and the reframe that makes it blameless, rather than drifting into the mechanisms that follow or back into the earlier S's. It uses the audit's findings from Chapter 27 without re-teaching the audit, and it uses the keeping mindset Chapter 26 built without re-teaching it. It keeps the problem-solving occasional, something you run when a recurring problem surfaces, and it is careful not to be the daily rhythm, which is Chapter 29, Daily 6S Times, that folds keeping into the ordinary shape of a day; nor the recurring event that renews the whole loop and completes the book, which is Chapter 30, Your Next 6S Event. It does not re-open Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, or Standardize; a one-line callback is fine, but the earlier S's are not re-taught here. And the practice of gently asking why a few times over is referenced lightly, the way a curious child would, and is deliberately not presented as a rigid formula to be followed to the letter. Because a home that solves its recurring problems, instead of patching them forever, has removed a source of returning friction, the friction meter takes its second Sustain step, a small move further along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26, from Chapter 27's position toward the goal. This is a small move, not a milestone, and honestly so, because you solved one recurring problem and not all of them at once; but it is real, because solving a recurring problem changes something permanent, and a source of friction that used to come back every single week is simply gone now. The goal is reached not in this chapter but at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30, when all six S's are done.

If you only do one thing: take the one thing you are most tired of fixing, the pile that always returns to the same spot, and ask out loud, of the problem and not of anyone in the room, "why does this keep happening here?", three or four times, until you reach something you can actually change. You just started solving it instead of fixing it, and it was not a confrontation with anyone at all. Not who keeps doing this, but why does this keep happening, together, until you reach the small system to fix. That is the smallest possible proof of the whole chapter, and it is how the endless patching quietly ends.
