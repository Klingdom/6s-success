# Back Cover Copy: Family Problem Solving

## Back-cover text

You have the keeping mindset and the friendly check-in now. Chapter 26 gave the mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, held good over the years by small, shared habits. Chapter 27 gave the first concrete practice, a light, regular check-in that catches drift while it is still small. What neither gave you was what to do about the findings that will not go away: not the one-off slip, corrected in two minutes and forgotten, but the same coats on the same chair, the same dishes by the sink, the same shoes in the same doorway, week after week. You keep fixing them, and they keep coming back, and a small, tired frustration builds, because it feels like the home, or the family, or you, keeps failing at something simple. Here is the reframe that dissolves that frustration, and it is the whole of this chapter: "A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct. It is a signal that a small system is wrong." A wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often, any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping the floor more diligently. It has to be solved, at the cause, once, and together.

Chapter 28 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the third chapter of Part Eight, and it gives the household a shared way to solve the recurring problems the audit keeps turning up. Its whole work is a reframe, and it draws the core line first, between the two things we confuse to our exhaustion: "A problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix. It is a problem you are failing to solve. Fixing puts the thing back; solving changes why it fell in the first place, so that you never have to put it back again." Fixing is mopping the floor; solving is finding the leak. It fits the hero in a plain box: "Family problem solving is how a household settles the recurring problems a home throws up, together: when the same thing keeps drifting back, you stop patching the symptom, ask why it keeps happening rather than who is to blame, and change the small system so it cannot easily happen again. It fixes the cause once, instead of the symptom forever." Two things make it work, more than any technique. First, you ask why, not who, carrying the no-blame spirit of the audit straight into solving: "Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place." The real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw, which is good news, because you cannot easily change a person but you can nearly always change a system. Second, you solve it together, because "a solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps." The chapter runs the whole four-step method once on the coats, end to end: name it, ask why together until you reach the cause, add a row of hooks by the door so the shortest path and the right path are the same path, and let the child who picked the spot hammer one in, so it is theirs and they keep it. The problem that returned every week for a year quietly stops returning at all. Run a recurring problem to ground in a few minutes with **The Ask Why, Not Who card**, and gather the household for a short, warm session with **The Family Huddle guide**. Then look at the friction meter, which moves again, a little further along the dashed last-gap path: "The needle takes its second Sustain step, a little further along the path to the goal. A home that solves its recurring problems, instead of patching them forever, has quietly removed a source of friction that used to return every week." It is honestly small, one recurring problem solved and not all of them, but it is real and permanent. Next, Chapter 28 hands off to Chapter 29 · Daily 6S Times, where all of this keeping folds into the ordinary shape of a day, so the small acts of upkeep happen at set, natural moments rather than depending on anyone remembering.
