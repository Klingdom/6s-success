## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Take the one thing in your home you are tired of fixing, the pile that always returns to the same spot, and ask out loud, "why does this keep happening here?", three or four times, until you reach something you can actually change. You just started solving it instead of fixing it.
- **Family Challenge:** Bring one recurring annoyance to the whole household, phrase it as "why does this keep happening?" and never "who keeps doing this?", and let everyone, including the children, propose a fix. The best solution often comes from the very person you were about to blame.
- **6S Tip:** The real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw: the bin is too far, the hook is too high, the shelf has no label, the thing has no home. When a problem keeps returning, look for the missing or awkward part of the system before you look at the people.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: take the one thing you are tired of fixing and ask "why does this keep happening here?" three or four times until you reach something you can actually change. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in a few minutes.
- Keep it warm, collaborative, and blameless. Nobody is ever the problem: a recurring problem is a design flaw, not a character flaw, so the question is always why it happens and never who is to blame. The emotional heart is relief, the relief of stopping an exhausting cycle of patching the same thing forever. Never scold a family member, real or imagined.
- The rule (stop fixing it and start solving it: ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again), the fixing-versus-solving reframe, and the why-not-who reframe are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid corporate framing, hype, and any force, blame, or control language (no "hold accountable" in the punitive sense, "crack down," "whose fault," "lay down the law," unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, "problem-solving machine," "root out"). Do not present the ask-why practice as a rigid five-step formula. Avoid brand names.
- Scope note: this gives the household a shared way to solve the recurring problems the audit keeps turning up, and its work is the REFRAME (fixing is not solving; ask why, not who; solve together) and the four-step method. It USES the audit's findings from Chapter 27 without re-teaching the audit, and USES the keeping mindset from Chapter 26 without re-teaching it; it keeps the problem-solving occasional (run when a recurring problem surfaces), NOT the daily rhythm, which is Chapter 29; and it does not build the recurring 6S event and the book's close, which are Chapter 30. It does not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, or Standardize. Because a home that solves its recurring problems, instead of patching them forever, has removed a source of returning friction, the friction meter takes its SECOND Sustain step, a small move further along the dashed last-gap path revealed in Chapter 26; the goal is reached only at Chapter 30, when all six S's are done.
