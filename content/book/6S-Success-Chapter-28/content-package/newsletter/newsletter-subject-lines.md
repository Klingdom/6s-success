# Newsletter Subject Lines: Family Problem Solving

Ten options. Mix of curiosity, plain clarity, and benefit angles. Honest, no clickbait, no hype, no force or blame language. This is the third issue of Part Eight and gives the household a shared way to solve the recurring problems the friendly audit keeps turning up, so the register stays warm, collaborative, and blameless: nobody is ever the problem. A recurring problem is a design flaw, not a character flaw, so you ask why it happens and never who is to blame, and you solve it together. The emotional heart is relief, the relief of ending an exhausting cycle of patching the same thing forever. The audit of Chapter 27 finds the recurring problem; this chapter gives the household a shared way to solve it.

1. When the same problem keeps coming back, stop fixing it and start solving it: ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again. (the rule, hero)
2. A problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix. It is a problem you are failing to solve. (fixing versus solving)
3. Fixing is mopping the floor; solving is finding the leak. (the core image)
4. Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. (why, not who)
5. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place. (the worked example)
6. The real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw. (good news, blameless)
7. You cannot easily change a person, but you can nearly always change a system. (why this is good news)
8. A solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps. (solve together)
9. Bring one recurring annoyance to the household as "why does this keep happening?" and never "who keeps doing this?" (family challenge)
10. When something keeps going wrong, do not look for someone to blame. Look, together, for the small system to fix. (One Idea to Keep)
