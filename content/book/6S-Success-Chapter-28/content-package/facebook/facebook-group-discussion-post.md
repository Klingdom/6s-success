# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the thing you are tired of picking up is not a person who keeps failing but a small system that is wrong, and you end it not by fixing it a two-hundredth time but by solving it once, together, asking why it keeps happening and never who is to blame) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. The whole thing is warm, collaborative, and blameless: nobody is ever the problem, a recurring annoyance is a design flaw and not a character flaw. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small shift that ended a thing I had been quietly picking up for a year, and then I want to hear from you all.

We had the whole 6S thing set up, and a weekly check-in was catching most of the little drift while it was small. But there was one stubborn thing it kept turning up: the same coats on the same chair, week after week. I kept hanging them up, and they kept coming back.

Here is the idea that finally ended it, and it hangs on one word: solving.

"A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct. It is a signal that a small system is wrong."

That took all the frustration out of it for me. I had been treating it as a discipline problem, someone not trying hard enough. It was never that.

"Fixing is mopping the floor; solving is finding the leak."

So I stopped asking who kept dumping their coat and started asking why the coats kept landing there. That one swap changes everything.

"When something keeps going wrong, the useless question is who keeps doing it, and the useful one is why it keeps happening. Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place."

The real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw. And the fix that actually sticks is the one the household works out together, not the clever one you hand down.

"A solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps."

We added hooks by the door, the kid picked the spot and hammered one in, and the coats just stopped landing on the chair. The rule underneath all of it is simple.

"When the same problem keeps coming back, stop fixing it and start solving it: ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the one thing in your home you are tired of picking up, the pile that always returns to the same spot? And if you asked "why does this keep happening here?" a few times instead of who keeps doing it, where do you think it would land?

I will go first in the comments. 😄
