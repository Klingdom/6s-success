## Post 1 · Stop fixing it, start solving it

If there is one thing in your home you keep picking up and it keeps coming back, this is for you. You are not failing to fix it. You are fixing a thing that needs to be solved.

"A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct. It is a signal that a small system is wrong."

And you cannot fix a wrong system by correcting it more often.

"A problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix. It is a problem you are failing to solve. Fixing puts the thing back; solving changes why it fell in the first place, so that you never have to put it back again."

Fixing is mopping the floor; solving is finding the leak. This is from Chapter 28 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online. 🙂

## Post 2 · Ask why, not who

Here is the swap the whole thing lives or dies on: when something keeps going wrong, stop asking who and start asking why. The question you pick decides where you end up.

"When something keeps going wrong, the useless question is who keeps doing it, and the useful one is why it keeps happening. Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place."

Ask who keeps dumping their coat and you get a defensive family member, a small argument, and a coat that is back tomorrow. Ask why the coats keep landing there and you are suddenly looking at the real thing. Same problem, opposite direction. Read the free chapter.

## Post 3 · A design flaw, not a character flaw

The reason ask-why works is that it takes you somewhere you can actually change something. You get there by asking why a few times, the way a curious child would.

Why are the coats on the chair? Because it is the easiest place. Why is it the easiest place? Because the hooks are far away. A few whys in, you have walked from a symptom to a cause.

"the real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw."

The bin is too far, the hook is too high, the shelf has no label, the thing has no home.

"That is genuinely good news, because you cannot easily change a person, but you can nearly always change a system."

Nobody is failing. The system is just asking people to walk further than a tired human will walk. More in Chapter 28 of 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online.

## Post 4 · Solve it together, or do not bother

Once you know the cause, there is a real temptation to fix it yourself with one clever move. Resist it. Whether a fix survives depends less on how clever it is than on who came up with it.

"The person who lives with a problem is the one who knows why it really happens, and the one who has to keep any fix alive, so they are exactly who should help design it. A solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps."

So bring the problem to the whole household as a shared puzzle, not a verdict, and let everyone, including the children, propose a fix.

"people keep what they helped build and abandon what was dropped on them from above."

The best solution often comes from the very person you were about to blame. This is from Chapter 28 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online.
