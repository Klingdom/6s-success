# Teleprompter Script: Family Problem Solving

**Chapter 28, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** family-problem-solving
**Length:** about 780 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the third chapter of Sustain.

You have the keeping mindset.

And the friendly check-in that catches drift while it is small.

Most of what the check-in turns up is easy.

A small drift. A two-minute fix.

But some findings are more stubborn.

The same coats on the same chair.

The same dishes stacking by the sink.

Week after week.

You keep fixing them, and they keep coming back.

And a small, tired frustration builds.

Here is the reframe that dissolves it.

A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip.

It is a signal that a small system is wrong.

The coats are not there because anyone lacks discipline.

Something about the setup makes the chair the easiest place.

And a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often.

Any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping harder.

It has to be solved, at the cause, once.

So start with the distinction the chapter turns on.

Fixing is not solving.

Fixing is correcting the symptom.

The coats are on the chair, so you hang them up.

Necessary, quick, and completely temporary.

Solving looks past the symptom to the cause.

And changes that, so the symptom stops.

Fixing is mopping the floor.

Solving is finding the leak.

A problem you have to fix more than twice.

Is not a problem you are failing to fix.

It is a problem you are failing to solve.

Fixing puts the thing back.

Solving changes why it fell in the first place.

So you never have to put it back again.

Now, how do you find the cause.

It hinges on asking the right question.

When something keeps going wrong.

The useless question is who keeps doing it.

The useful one is why it keeps happening.

Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing.

Why finds a system to fix and changes everything.

Ask who keeps dumping their coat.

And you get a defensive family member.

And a coat that is back tomorrow.

Ask why the coats keep landing there.

And you see the real thing.

The chair is by the door. The hooks are down the hall.

And tired people take the shortest path.

Nobody is failing.

The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy.

They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place.

You get there by gently asking why a few times.

The way a curious child would.

And you notice something reassuring.

The real cause is almost always a design flaw.

Not a character flaw.

A missing hook. An awkward distance. A thing with no home.

That is good news.

You cannot easily change a person.

But you can nearly always change a system.

Now, one more thing decides if a fix survives.

Not how clever it is. Who came up with it.

The person who lives with a problem.

Knows why it really happens.

And has to keep any fix alive.

So they are exactly who should help design it.

A solution handed down is quietly dropped.

A solution the household worked out together is kept.

People keep what they helped build.

And abandon what was dropped on them from above.

So bring the problem to the household.

As a shared puzzle, not a verdict.

And let everyone, including the children, propose the fix.

Here is the whole method on the coats.

Four small steps, done together.

First, name the problem plainly.

As a problem, not a person.

The coats keep ending up on the chair.

Second, ask why, together, a few times.

Until you reach the cause everyone recognizes.

Tired people take the shortest path.

Third, change the small system.

A row of hooks goes up right by the door.

Now the shortest path and the right path are the same.

And fourth, a kid picked where the hooks went.

And got to hammer one in.

So it is theirs, and they use it.

The problem that returned every week for a year.

Quietly stops returning at all.

Now go and look at the friction meter.

It moves again, a little further.

Last chapter, the audit caught drift faster.

This chapter, you stopped it returning at all.

So the needle takes its second Sustain step.

It is small, honestly so.

You solved one recurring problem, not all of them.

But it is real, because it is permanent.

A source of friction that came back every week is gone.

A home with one fewer returning problem.

Keeps itself that little bit more on its own.

So here is the rule that holds it all.

When the same problem keeps coming back.

Stop fixing it and start solving it.

Ask why it happens, not who is to blame.

And change the system so it cannot easily happen again.

So here is your job. One of the smallest in the book.

Take the one thing you are tired of fixing.

The pile that always returns to the same spot.

And ask out loud, why does this keep happening here.

Three or four times, until you reach something you can change.

You just started solving it instead of fixing it.

When something keeps going wrong.

Do not look for someone to blame.

Look, together, for the small system to fix.
