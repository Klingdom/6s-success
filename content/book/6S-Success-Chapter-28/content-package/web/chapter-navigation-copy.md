## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 28, the third chapter of Part Eight, Sustain, which continues the sixth and final S. Chapter 26 gave the keeping mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, on small shared habits, until keeping becomes second nature. Chapter 27 gave the first concrete way to do that keeping, a regular, friendly check-in that catches drift while it is small and feels like care rather than an inspection. This chapter adds the second piece of keeping-machinery, and it solves what the check-in finds. The check-in is good at catching drift while it is small, but it keeps surfacing the same stubborn thing: the same coats on the same chair, the same dishes by the sink, the same shoes in the same doorway, week after week, no matter how often they are fixed, and a small, tired frustration builds. This chapter is the reframe that dissolves it. A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct; it is a signal that a small system is wrong, and a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often, any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping the floor more diligently. It has to be solved, at the cause, once. Fixing puts the thing back; solving changes why it fell in the first place, so that you never have to put it back again; fixing is mopping the floor, solving is finding the leak. Two things make solving work, more than any technique. First, you ask why it keeps happening and never who is to blame, because who finds a person to blame and changes nothing, while why finds a system to fix and changes everything; you ask why a few times, the way a curious child would, until you reach the real cause, which is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw, a missing hook or an awkward distance, not a lazy family member, and that is good news, because you cannot easily change a person but you can nearly always change a system. Second, you solve it together, because the person who lives with a problem knows why it really happens and has to keep any fix alive, so they are exactly who should help design it; a solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped, a solution the household worked out together is one it actually keeps. The chapter walks the four small steps end to end on the coats that always land on the chair, name it, ask why together, change the small system, decide together, until the problem that returned every week for a year quietly stops returning at all. The friction meter takes its second Sustain step. The needle moves a little further along the dashed last-gap path that appeared in Chapter 26, because a home that solves its recurring problems, instead of patching them forever, has removed a source of returning friction that used to come back every single week. It is honestly small, because you solved one recurring problem, not all of them at once, but it is real and permanent, so this is a small move and not a milestone; the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. The rule to carry: when the same problem keeps coming back, stop fixing it and start solving it, ask why it happens, not who is to blame, and change the system so it cannot easily happen again.
