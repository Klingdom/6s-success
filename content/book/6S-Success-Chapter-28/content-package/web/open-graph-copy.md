## Variant A (stop fixing it and start solving it)

- og:title: Stop Fixing It and Start Solving It.
- og:description: You have the keeping mindset from Chapter 26 and the friendly check-in from Chapter 27, but the check-in keeps turning up the same problem, month after month, and you keep fixing it and it keeps coming back. A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct; it is a signal that a small system is wrong, and a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often. So you stop patching the symptom and solve the cause, once, together: you ask why it keeps happening, not who is to blame, until you reach the real cause, which is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw, and you change the small system so it cannot easily happen again, with the people who live with it helping decide the fix, so they keep it. Fix the cause once, and you will never fix the symptom again.

## Variant B (ask why, not who)

- og:title: Ask Why, Not Who.
- og:description: When something keeps going wrong, the useless question is who keeps doing it, and the useful one is why it keeps happening. Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy; they are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place. You get from symptom to cause by gently asking why a few times, the way a curious child would, and the real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw, a missing hook or an awkward distance. That is good news, because you cannot easily change a person, but you can nearly always change a system. So do not look for someone to blame; look, together, for the small system to fix.
