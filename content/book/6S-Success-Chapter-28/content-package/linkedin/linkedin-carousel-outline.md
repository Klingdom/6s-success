# Chapter 28 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: Stop Fixing the Same Thing, Start Solving It

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, collaborative, blameless voice; a recurring problem is a design flaw, not a character flaw; nobody is ever the problem; the household solves it together, children included; no force, blame, or control words, no hype, no brand names.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** The same coats land on the same chair every week, and you keep hanging them up. That is not the problem. That is the clue.

**Body:** A problem you keep fixing is a problem you have not yet solved. Swipe for the shared, blameless way a household ends the endless-patching loop, together.

---

## Slide 2: The stubborn finding

**Headline:** Your friendly check-in keeps turning up the same thing.

**Body:** Not a one-off slip, but the same coats on the same chair, the same dishes by the sink, week after week. You keep fixing them, and they keep coming back, and a small, tired frustration builds.

---

## Slide 3: The reframe that dissolves it

**Headline:** A problem that keeps coming back is not a slip anyone is failing to correct. It is a signal that a small system is wrong.

**Body:** And a wrong system cannot be fixed by correcting it more often, any more than a leaking pipe can be fixed by mopping the floor more diligently. It has to be solved, at the cause, once.

---

## Slide 4: Fixing is not solving

**Headline:** A problem you have to fix more than twice is not a problem you are failing to fix. It is a problem you are failing to solve.

**Body:** Fixing puts the thing back; solving changes why it fell in the first place, so that you never have to put it back again. Fixing is mopping the floor; solving is finding the leak.

---

## Slide 5: The definition

**Headline:** What family problem solving actually is.

**Body:** How a household settles the recurring problems a home throws up, together: when the same thing keeps drifting back, you stop patching the symptom, ask why it keeps happening rather than who is to blame, and change the small system so it cannot easily happen again.

---

## Slide 6: Ask why, not who

**Headline:** The useless question is who keeps doing it. The useful one is why it keeps happening.

**Body:** Who finds a person to blame and changes nothing; why finds a system to fix and changes everything. The coats are not on the chair because someone is lazy. They are on the chair because the hooks are in the wrong place.

---

## Slide 7: The ladder of whys

**Headline:** Ask why a few times, the way a curious child would.

**Body:** Why are the coats on the chair? It is the easiest place. Why? The hooks are far away. Why does that matter? Tired people take the shortest path. A few whys in, you have walked from a symptom to a cause you can change.

---

## Slide 8: A design flaw, not a character flaw

**Headline:** The real cause is almost always a design flaw, not a character flaw.

**Body:** A missing hook, an awkward distance, a bin too far, a shelf with no label, a thing with no home. That is genuinely good news, because you cannot easily change a person, but you can nearly always change a system.

---

## Slide 9: Solve it together

**Headline:** Whether a solution survives depends not on how clever it is, but on who came up with it.

**Body:** The person who lives with a problem knows why it really happens and has to keep the fix alive, so they should help design it. A solution handed down is resented and quietly dropped; a solution worked out together is one it actually keeps.

---

## Slide 10: The four small steps

**Headline:** Solve a recurring problem together in four small steps.

**Body:** Name the problem that keeps drifting back; ask why, a few times, until you reach the real cause; change the small system so the cause is gone; and let the people who live with it help decide the fix, so they keep it.

---

## Slide 11: The coats, solved

**Headline:** The problem that returned every week for a year quietly stops returning at all.

**Body:** The household names it, asks why together to the cause everyone recognizes, adds a row of hooks by the door so the shortest path and the right path are the same path, and a child picks the spot, so it is theirs, and they keep it.
