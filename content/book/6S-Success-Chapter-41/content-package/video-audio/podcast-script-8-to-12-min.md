# Chapter 41 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Primary Bathroom

Single host, warm and practical.

## Cold open
Here is why your bathroom films over again by Friday no matter how hard you scrub: you are fighting water, and water does not wait. Today we clean the first wet room the way it actually needs it.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. We cross from the dry rooms into the wet ones today, starting with the smallest room you use the most. And the whole method changes, because this room fights three problems at once: standing moisture that breeds mould, open chemistry that has to be kept safe, and dates that quietly pass. So we do three things differently: we spray first and let the chemistry work, we treat drying as the real job, and we read the date on everything.

## Part 2: The seven zones
The room is seven zones: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage. Start at the counter, and spray the wet zones the moment you walk in so they dwell while you do the dry ones.

## Part 3: Spray first
The wet surfaces are cleaned with time, not muscle. So spray the tub, the shower walls, and the bowl before you touch anything, and then leave them. Clean the counter and the cabinets while the cleaner breaks down the soap film and the scale on its own. When you come back it wipes off in one pass instead of ten. Work top down, squeegee the glass last, and leave the fan running.

## Part 4: Dry beats mould
Mould and the pink film are a water problem, not a dirt problem, so the winning move is drying. A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould, and one that stays wet grows it back faster every week. So squeegee the glass, stand the bath mat on edge to drain, hang the towels where air reaches them, and run the fan until the room dries out.

## Part 5: Read the date
Every product in here has a date, and a date that has passed is not stock, it is a failure on your shelf. Read the date on every Sort pass, on the creams and the cosmetics and the medicines. What has turned goes, and the medicines go to a pharmacy take-back, never down the toilet. The leftover half-course of antibiotics goes first.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
This is the room where chemistry, water, and electricity share a few square feet, so keep one product per bowl and never mix them, store the chemistry and medicines locked or high, keep nothing plugged in over a full basin, and keep the plumbing under the sink visible. And the calls are all about what you keep versus what you use: the tool that lives out, the expensive shade, the pipe you cannot see, the good towels you never use.

## Close
A bathroom is not clean because you scrubbed it hard. It is clean because you sprayed first, dried the surfaces so the mould cannot return, read the date, and locked the chemistry away. Do one zone this weekend. Next time: the guest bathroom.
