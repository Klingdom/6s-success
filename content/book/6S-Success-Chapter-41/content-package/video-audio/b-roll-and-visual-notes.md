# Chapter 41 B-Roll and Visual Notes: The Primary Bathroom

Warm, natural light. Product types only, no brand labels on screen.

## Visual vocabulary
- The two-state opener: a crowded, steamy before and a clear, dry after.
- The recurring "spray and dwell" motif: a wall misted, a hand walking away, the wall wiping clean in one pass.

## Core shot list (seven shots)
1. Spraying the shower walls, tray, and caulk seam, then stepping out to clean the counter while it dwells.
2. The counter: the mirror wiped with cleaner sprayed onto the cloth, the faucet collar and switch plate wiped, the stone dried off.
3. Coming back to the shower: wiping top down, squeegeeing the glass, and standing the bath mat on edge to drain.
4. The under-sink cabinet: a dry finger run around each pipe joint, and a pale drip tray lifted and rinsed.
5. The medicine cabinet: shelves out, sticky rings washed off, and a date read on a tube before it goes back or into the bin.
6. The toilet: the bowl cleaner applied and left to dwell, then the outside wiped in order with a toilet-only cloth.
7. The safety beat: one bottle set per bowl with a crossed-out mixing shot, a lock on a cabinet, and a heat tool moved away from a full basin.

## On-screen text bank (use sparingly)
- Spray first, then walk away.
- Dry beats mould.
- Read the date on everything.
- One product per bowl, never mixed.
- The toilet cloth touches nothing else.

## Lower thirds (rotate)
- Zone 1 to 7, one per segment.
- Do one zone this weekend.

## Editing notes
- Close each cut on the before-and-after room signature from the doorway (the clear counter, the filmless glass, the dry shower).
- No presenter required; hands and surfaces only.
