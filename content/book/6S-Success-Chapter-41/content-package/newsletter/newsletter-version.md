Hi friend,

We cross from the dry rooms into the wet ones this week, and the whole method changes, because the enemy is no longer dust that waits for you, it is water that does not.

> The primary bathroom is the smallest room you use the most, and it is the first wet room in this book, which means it fights three problems no other room faces all at once: standing moisture that breeds mould, open chemistry that has to be kept safe, and dates that quietly pass while you sleep. So the method changes here. You spray the tub, the shower, and the bowl first and let the cleaner do the standing about while you work the dry surfaces, because chemistry needs time, not scrubbing. You treat drying as the real job, not wiping, because mould is a water problem. And you read the date on everything as you go, because in this room an expired thing is not stock, it is a failure already sitting on your shelf.

The room is seven zones, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage.

The move that keeps it clean between deep cleans:

> Mould and the soft pink film are not a dirt problem, they are a water problem, so the winning move in this room is not cleaning, it is drying. A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould, and a surface that stays wet grows it back faster every week however hard you scrub. So you squeegee the glass, you stand the bath mat on edge to drain rather than leaving it flat and wet, you hang the towels where air actually reaches them, you buff the metal dry so it does not spot, and you run the fan and leave the door open until the room has dried out. The cloth cleans what is there; the drying stops it coming back.

## The shower, done properly (the worked example)

**Inputs:** a bathroom grime and soap-film cleaner, a floor cleaner for the tray and mat, a microfiber set, a detail brush, a shower squeegee, your caddy.

**Steps:**

1. Spray the walls, screen, caulk seam, and grout, and step out to clean the dry zones while it dwells.
2. Work the showerhead scale with the detail brush, then run the water hot to clear the jets.
3. Come back, wipe top down, rinse, and squeegee the glass and tile.
4. Scrub the taps and trim, rinse, and buff the metal dry.
5. Lift the mat, wash both faces and the tray, and stand the mat on edge to drain.
6. Clear the drain hair, rinse the squeegee, and hang it inside to dry.

## To try this week

Do the shower to the method above, and leave the door open and the fan running when you walk out.

> A bathroom is not clean because you scrubbed it hard. It is clean because you sprayed first and let the chemistry work, dried the surfaces so the mould cannot come back, read the date on everything and let the failures go, and locked the chemistry safely away. Do one zone this weekend, the counter or the shower, and give yourself the smallest room you use the most, actually clean and actually safe.
