# Chapter 41 Key Takeaways: The Primary Bathroom

- The primary bathroom is the first wet room, so it fights three problems at once: standing moisture, open chemistry, and dates that quietly pass.
- Seven zones, one at a time: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage. Start at the counter.
- Spray first and let the chemistry work: spray the tub, shower, and bowl before anything else and clean the dry surfaces while it dwells, because chemistry needs time, not scrubbing.
- Dry beats mould: mould and the pink film are a water problem, so squeegee the glass, drain the mat on edge, buff the metal dry, and run the fan; a surface that dries between uses does not grow mould.
- Read the date on everything: an expired cream, cosmetic, or medicine is a failure on your shelf, not stock; medicines go to a pharmacy take-back, never the toilet.
- Colour-code the cloths hardest here: the cloth that cleans the toilet never touches anything else.
- Keep the chemistry safe: one product per bowl and never mixed, cleaners and medicines locked or high above a child's climbing reach, nothing plugged in over a full basin, and the plumbing kept visible.
- Close on a before-and-after photo the room resets to after every clean.
