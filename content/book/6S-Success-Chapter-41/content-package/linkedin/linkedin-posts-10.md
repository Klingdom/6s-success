# Chapter 41 LinkedIn Posts: The Primary Bathroom

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The first wet room cleans differently
The primary bathroom is the smallest room you use the most, and it is the first wet room in this book. That changes the whole job, because the enemy is no longer dust and clutter, it is water.

"It fights three problems no other room faces all at once: standing moisture that breeds mould, open chemistry that has to be kept safe, and dates that quietly pass while you sleep."

## 2. Spray first, then walk away
The wet-room surfaces are cleaned with time, not muscle. So spray the tub, the shower walls, and the bowl before you touch anything else, and then leave them. Clean the counter and the cabinets while the cleaner breaks down the soap film on its own. When you come back, it wipes off in one pass instead of ten. Chemistry needs time, not scrubbing.

## 3. Drying beats scrubbing
Mould and the soft pink film are not a dirt problem, they are a water problem. So the winning move in a bathroom is not cleaning, it is drying. A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould; one that stays wet grows it back faster every week however hard you scrub. Squeegee the glass, drain the mat on edge, buff the metal dry, and run the fan.

## 4. Read the date on everything
Every product in a bathroom has a date, and a date that has passed is not a supply you are saving, it is a failure already on your shelf: the separated sunscreen, the crumbling tablet, the mascara from two summers ago. Read the date on every Sort pass, and let what has turned go. Medicines go to a pharmacy take-back, never down the toilet.

## 5. The toilet cloth touches nothing else
Colour-coding your cloths matters more in this room than anywhere in the house. The cloth that cleans the toilet gets one colour, that job only, and never touches another surface. It is the simplest way to keep the dirtiest patch of the room from contaminating the cleanest.

## 6. One product per bowl, never mixed
The most dangerous mistake in a bathroom is chemistry. You keep one product per bowl and never mix them, because bowl cleaner and bleach together make a gas. One cleaner does the job alone. And you store the bleach, the drain opener, and every medicine locked or high, above the height a child reaches by standing on the closed toilet lid.

## 7. Clean the under-sink cabinet to read the plumbing
The cabinet under the sink is the one you clean partly to read the pipes. Keep a pale drip tray on the floor and the trap and shut-off valves in plain sight, because a joint that weeps unseen for months turns a cheap washer into a floor that has to come up. Run a dry finger around each joint; it tells you about a seep before a puddle ever will.

## 8. The leftover prescription
The single most common thing in a medicine cabinet that should not be there is the leftover half-course of antibiotics, or the strong painkillers from a procedure two years ago. A medicine belongs to a condition someone in the house has now. The rest goes to a pharmacy take-back, and it goes first.

## 9. Nothing that touches your face within splash range
Every flush throws a fine mist across the cistern lid, the most tempting shelf in the room. Settle it with one line: nothing that touches your face, your mouth, or your food goes within arm's reach of the flush. The toothbrush lives in a holder on the counter, not on the tank.

## 10. Actually clean and actually safe
"A bathroom is not clean because you scrubbed it hard. It is clean because you sprayed first and let the chemistry work, dried the surfaces so the mould cannot come back, read the date on everything and let the failures go, and locked the chemistry safely away."
