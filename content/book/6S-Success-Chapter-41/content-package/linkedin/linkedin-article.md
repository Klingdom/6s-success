# The Primary Bathroom: Spray First, Dry Beats Mould, Read the Date

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 41 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

The primary bathroom is the smallest room in the house and the one you use the most, and it is where this book crosses from the dry rooms into the wet ones. Everything you have cleaned so far fought dust and clutter, problems that sit still and wait for you. This room fights water, which does not wait.

> The primary bathroom is the smallest room you use the most, and it is the first wet room in this book, which means it fights three problems no other room faces all at once: standing moisture that breeds mould, open chemistry that has to be kept safe, and dates that quietly pass while you sleep. So the method changes here. You spray the tub, the shower, and the bowl first and let the cleaner do the standing about while you work the dry surfaces, because chemistry needs time, not scrubbing. You treat drying as the real job, not wiping, because mould is a water problem. And you read the date on everything as you go, because in this room an expired thing is not stock, it is a failure already sitting on your shelf.

## Seven zones, not one room

> The primary bathroom is seven small zones, not one big job: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage. Do them one at a time, and start at the vanity counter, because it is the surface you look at twice a day and clearing it changes what the whole room looks like from the doorway.

## Spray first, let the chemistry work

> The wet-room surfaces are cleaned with time, not muscle, so the order of work is the opposite of scrubbing. You spray the tub, the shower walls, and the bowl before you touch anything else, and then you leave them: you work the counter, the drawers, and the cabinets while the cleaner does the standing about, breaking down the soap film and the scale and the ring on its own.

## Dry beats mould

> Mould and the soft pink film are not a dirt problem, they are a water problem, so the winning move in this room is not cleaning, it is drying. A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould, and a surface that stays wet grows it back faster every week however hard you scrub.

## Read the date on everything

> Every product in this room has a date, and in here a date that has passed is not a supply you are saving, it is a failure already sitting on your shelf: the separated sunscreen, the crumbling tablet, the mascara from two summers ago.
