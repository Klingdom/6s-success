# Facebook Longform Post: Spray First, Dry Beats Mould, Read the Date

A story-driven post from Chapter 41 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the primary bathroom.

---

Here is why your bathroom films over again by Friday no matter how hard you scrub: you are fighting water, and water does not wait.

The primary bathroom is the smallest room you use the most, and it is the first wet room. That changes the whole job, because the enemy is no longer dust and clutter, it is standing moisture, open chemistry, and dates that quietly pass. So three rules run through the room.

Spray first, and let the chemistry work. The wet surfaces are cleaned with time, not muscle, so spray the tub, the shower walls, and the bowl before you touch anything else, and then leave them. Clean the counter and the cabinets while the cleaner breaks down the soap film on its own. When you come back, it wipes off in one pass instead of ten.

Drying beats scrubbing. Mould and the soft pink film are a water problem, not a dirt problem, so the winning move is drying, not cleaning. A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould. So squeegee the glass, stand the bath mat on edge to drain, buff the metal dry, and run the fan until the room has dried out.

And read the date on everything. A cream or a medicine or a mascara that has passed its date is not a supply you are saving, it is a failure already on your shelf. Read the date on every Sort pass and let what has turned go. Medicines go to a pharmacy take-back, never down the toilet.

Around all of that, keep the chemistry safe: one product per bowl and never mixed, and every cleaner and medicine locked or high above the height a child reaches by standing on the closed toilet lid.

Do one zone this weekend, the counter or the shower, and give yourself the smallest room you use the most, actually clean and actually safe.

Do you spray and wait, or scrub and lose the afternoon?
