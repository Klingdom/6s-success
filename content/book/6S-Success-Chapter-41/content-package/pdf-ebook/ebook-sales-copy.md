# Chapter 41 Ebook Sales Copy: The Primary Bathroom

## Headline
The smallest room you use the most, cleaned the wet-room way. Spray first, let drying beat mould, and read the date on everything.

## The promise
In the first wet room, scrubbing harder is the wrong move, because the enemy is water and chemistry, and both work on their own schedule. This chapter gives you the exact inputs and the numbered steps for each of the room's seven zones, plus the three rules that make a bathroom stay clean: spray first and let the chemistry work, treat drying as the real job, and read the date on everything.

## What you get in this chapter
- The wet-room Shine method: spray first and let the chemistry work, dry beats mould, read the date, and keep the chemistry safe.
- The seven per-zone clean procedures, each with its inputs and numbered steps: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage.
- The spray-and-dwell order that cleans a shower in one pass instead of ten.
- The drying habits that stop mould coming back, and why reading the date matters more here than anywhere.
- The safety layer, one product per bowl, chemistry locked high, and the plumbing kept visible, plus the before-and-after signature.

## The common mistake it fixes
> Scrubbing a dry surface hard instead of spraying it and giving the cleaner time, wiping the mould off again and again instead of drying the surface so it cannot return, keeping expired creams and medicines as if they were stock, and storing the bleach and the pills at a height a child reaches by standing on the closed toilet lid.
