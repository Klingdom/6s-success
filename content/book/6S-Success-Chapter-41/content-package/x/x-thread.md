# X Thread: The Primary Bathroom

1/
Part Nine crosses from the dry rooms into the wet ones: the primary bathroom. Like the rooms before it, this chapter gives the exact inputs and steps for each zone. But here the enemy is no longer clutter, it is water, and water does not wait.

(246 chars)

---

2/
The first wet room cleans differently. It fights three problems no other room faces at once: standing moisture that breeds mould, open chemistry that has to be kept safe, and dates that quietly pass while you sleep.

(218 chars)

---

3/
So the method changes: you spray first and let the chemistry work, you treat drying as the real job, and you read the date on everything.

(140 chars)

---

4/
"The primary bathroom is seven small zones, not one big job: the vanity counter, the vanity drawers, the under-sink cabinet, the medicine cabinet, the shower or tub, the toilet area, and the linen and towel storage."

(219 chars)

---

5/
Start at the counter, and spray the wet zones the moment you walk in so they dwell while you do the dry ones.

(112 chars)

---

6/
"The wet-room surfaces are cleaned with time, not muscle." Spray the tub, the walls, and the bowl, then leave them and clean the counter while the cleaner breaks down the soap film on its own.

(195 chars)

---

7/
When you come back it wipes off in one pass instead of ten. Work top down, squeegee the glass last so nothing dries into a film, and leave the fan running after you walk out.

(177 chars)

---

8/
"Mould and the soft pink film are not a dirt problem, they are a water problem, so the winning move in this room is not cleaning, it is drying." A surface that dries out between uses does not grow mould.

(206 chars)

---

9/
So squeegee the glass, stand the bath mat on edge to drain, hang the towels where air reaches them, buff the metal dry, and run the fan until the room has dried out.

(168 chars)

---

10/
"Every product in this room has a date, and in here a date that has passed is not a supply you are saving, it is a failure already sitting on your shelf." Read the date on every Sort pass.

(192 chars)

---

11/
Medicines go to a pharmacy take-back, never down the toilet, and the leftover half-course of antibiotics is the single most common thing in the cabinet that should not be there.

(181 chars)

---

12/
"You keep one product per bowl and you never mix them, because bowl cleaner and bleach together make a gas." Store the chemistry and medicines locked or high, above what a child can climb to.

(195 chars)

---

13/
Clean the under-sink cabinet partly to read the plumbing: keep the trap and valves visible, because a joint that weeps unseen for months turns a cheap washer into a floor that has to come up.

(195 chars)

---

14/
Do one zone this weekend, the counter or the shower, and give yourself the smallest room you use the most, actually clean and safe. Full steps for all seven zones in Chapter 41.
