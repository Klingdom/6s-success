# Newsletter Subject Lines: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

Ten options. Mix of curiosity, plain clarity, and benefit angles. Honest, no clickbait, no hype, no force language. This is the first issue of Part Eight and opens the sixth and final S, so the register stays warm, human, and freeing, not corporate: keeping a home is lighter than fixing it, a shared choice rather than one person's burden, and relief rather than obligation, and a home that slid back after a big push is the normal case, never a failure. You do not finish 6S. You keep it.

1. You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit of keeping it. (the rule, hero)
2. The first five S's are things you do. Sustain is the one you keep doing. (practice-not-project line)
3. Held is not the same as kept, because the world is relentless and nothing stays good on its own. (held is not kept)
4. A home is not a project you complete. It is a thing you keep. (the "done" trap)
5. Sustain runs on habit, not willpower: small enough to survive the weeks nobody has energy to spare. (habit-not-willpower reframe)
6. Lower the bar until the habit is unbreakable. Small and relentless beats heroic and brief. (the craft, quick win)
7. Kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do and becomes simply how your home is. (second-nature reframe)
8. Keeping is lighter than fixing: the household that sustains has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work. (relief, not obligation)
9. The needle holds, and for the first time the last gap to the goal appears. (the friction meter)
10. A home stays good the way a garden stays alive, by a little ongoing care. (plain, One Idea to Keep)
