## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 26, the first chapter of Part Eight, Sustain, and it opens the sixth and final S. Five of the six S's are done: the last part built standards, and Chapter 25 completed Standardize by adding the visual standard, so your home is now written down and shown in pictures and holds its shape without leaning on your memory. Held against sliding back, as firmly as a home can be. And here is the last thing the book has to tell you. Held is not the same as kept. Standardize stopped the passive slide, the quiet decay of people forgetting the best way, but it cannot stop the world, and the world is relentless: new things arrive, seasons change, people come and go, and no standard survives a busy or sad or chaotic season untended. A home is not a machine you can set and leave running; it is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive. That keeping is Sustain, and it is different in kind from the five before it. The first five S's are things you do and, eventually, finish; Sustain is the one you never finish, because it is not a task with an end. So you do not finish 6S, you keep it: the first five S's build a home worth keeping, and the sixth is the habit of keeping it. Read that as relief, not burden, because it quietly rewrites the job. You are no longer trying to reach some final, perfect state and then defend it forever, which is exhausting and impossible. You are just living in your home and keeping it as you go, which is ordinary and human and light. And keeping runs on habit, not willpower, because willpower runs out first, precisely in the busy, tired stretches when a home most wants to fall apart. So the craft is to lower the bar until the habit is almost impossible to fail: a one-minute reset of a single surface, done every day, keeps more of your home over a year than a two-hour blitz you manage twice and then dread. Keeping is lighter than fixing, because a home tended a little every day never gets bad enough to need the exhausting overhaul, so the household that sustains is not the one doing more work, it is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all. And kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do to your home and becomes simply how your home is: second nature, and nearly free. The friction meter holds. The needle stays exactly where Standardize left it, at the Standardize-complete mark, because a chapter about a mindset does not change a single thing in your rooms. But for the first time the dial shows the last gap plainly: a faint dashed path runs from the needle to the goal crosshair, and that path is Sustain, the small remaining distance closed not by clearing new friction but by letting the small habits run, month after month, until the effort of keeping fades toward nothing. This is not a move and not a milestone; the goal is reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. The rule to carry: you do not finish 6S, you keep it, the way a garden stays alive, by a little ongoing care from the people who love it.
