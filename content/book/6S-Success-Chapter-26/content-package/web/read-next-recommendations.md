# Read Next Recommendations: Chapter 26, Sustain: Keeping the Gains

1. Chapter 27, 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections
   The next step, and the first concrete way to keep the gains. Chapter 26 gave you the mindset, that a home is kept and not finished, that keeping runs on small shared habits rather than willpower, and that kept long enough it becomes second nature. Chapter 27 turns that mindset into a practice: a light, regular way to check in on the home and catch drift while it is still small, one that looks nothing like an inspection and feels like care for the home rather than judgement of the people in it. Chapter 26 gives the why; Chapter 27 gives the first how.

2. Chapter 25, Pictures, Storyboards, and Visual Standards
   The setup, and the chapter just behind this one. Chapter 25 added the last kind of standard, the one a picture carries better than any list, a photo of the ideal state and a short storyboard of the steps, and completed Standardize and the fifth S, so your home is now good and, for the first time, held against sliding back. But held is not the same as kept. Read how the fifth S locked the gains, then see how Chapter 26 opens the sixth and final S, the work of keeping a held home held over all the years to come.

3. Start one keeping-habit small enough to do forever (the chapter action)
   Pick the smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, resetting a single surface, and start it today. Sustain is built from habits so small they survive your worst weeks, not from grand resolutions that die inside a month. It costs about a minute, and it is the smallest possible proof of the whole idea: a home stays good not by one heroic effort but by a little ongoing care that never quite stops.

4. The Keeping Habit card (free resource)
   A printable and phone wallpaper to help you pick one keeping-habit small enough to do forever, with the small-and-relentless principle on it, so a household can start sustaining with something unbreakable. Lower the bar until the habit is almost impossible to fail, because a one-minute reset done every day keeps more of a home over a year than a two-hour blitz done twice and then dreaded.

5. The How Do We Want to Live conversation guide (free resource)
   A printable short prompt sheet for the one household conversation that makes keeping a shared choice rather than one person's burden. It asks a bigger question than "can we keep this up?" It asks "how do we want to live now?" A home is kept by the people who live in it choosing to, together, not by one person quietly carrying it.
