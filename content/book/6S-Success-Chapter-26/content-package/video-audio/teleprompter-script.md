# Teleprompter Script: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

**Chapter 26, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** sustain-keeping-the-gains
**Length:** about 760 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

This is the first chapter of Sustain.

The one that opens the sixth and final S.

Look at what you have made.

A home cleared, arranged, clean, and safe.

And now written down and shown in pictures.

It holds its shape without leaning on your memory.

Five of the six S's are done.

And here is the last thing this book has to tell you.

Held is not the same as kept.

Standardize stopped your home drifting back on its own.

But it cannot stop the world.

And the world is relentless.

New things arrive. Seasons change. People come and go.

No standard survives a hard season untended.

A home is not a machine you can set and leave running.

It is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive.

That keeping is the sixth S. Sustain.

And it is different in kind from the five before it.

The others are things you do and finish.

Sustain is the one you never finish.

Because it is not a task with an end.

So here is the rule that holds the whole chapter.

You do not finish 6S. You keep it.

The first five S's build a home worth keeping.

The sixth is the habit of keeping it.

For twenty-five chapters you did projects.

Work with a beginning, a middle, and an end.

Sort the drawer, and the drawer is sorted.

Sustain is not like that.

But read that not as a burden. Read it as a relief.

You are not chasing some final, perfect state.

And then defending it forever.

You are just living in your home and keeping it as you go.

The first five S's are things you do.

Sustain is the one you keep doing.

Now, the obvious question is how.

And the obvious wrong answer is, try harder.

So let me close that door.

You will not keep your home by trying harder.

Any more than you kept it tidy by resolving to be neater.

Sustain runs on habit, not willpower.

Willpower runs out first.

And it runs out precisely when you need it most.

In the busy, tired, difficult stretches.

A habit is something you do without deciding to.

So it keeps running when your motivation is gone.

And those are the days that matter.

So lower the bar. Deliberately.

Until the habit is almost impossible to fail.

A one-minute reset of a single surface, done every day.

That keeps more of your home than a blitz you dread.

Small and relentless beats heroic and brief.

And keeping is genuinely lighter than fixing.

A home tended a little every day.

Never gets bad enough to need the overhaul.

So the household that sustains is not doing more work.

It has quietly stopped doing the big work at all.

Here is where all of this is heading.

There is a point, if you keep a home long enough.

Where the keeping stops feeling like a thing you do.

The keys on the hook are just what your hand does.

The after-dinner reset is simply part of dinner.

The home is held not by effort, but by identity.

Kept long enough, 6S becomes simply how your home is.

Not a home you are forever fixing.

But one that mostly keeps itself.

It gets easier. Genuinely, and a great deal.

Not because you finish.

But because keeping becomes automatic, and automatic is nearly free.

Now go and look at the friction meter.

It does not move this chapter.

Because a concept does not change your rooms.

The needle holds where Standardize left it.

But it shows you something new.

Look at the small gap left to the goal.

For the first time, the dial names it.

A faint dashed path from the needle to the goal.

And that path is Sustain.

You do not close it by pushing harder.

You close it by letting the small habits run.

Month after month, until keeping is second nature.

So here is your job. It is the smallest in the book.

Pick the smallest keeping-habit you could do forever.

One minute, once a day. And start it today.

You are not trying to finish.

You are learning to keep.

A home stays good the way a garden stays alive.

By a little ongoing care from the people who love it.
