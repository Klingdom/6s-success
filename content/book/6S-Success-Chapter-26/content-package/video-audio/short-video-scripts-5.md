## SHORT 1: Held is not the same as kept
**Topic: the honest distinction, Standardize stopped the passive slide but the world is relentless. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You built a home that holds its shape without leaning on your memory. And it will still slide back. Not because your standards are weak. Because the world is relentless.

**Script:**
Five of the six S's are done. Your home is cleared, arranged, clean, safe, and now written down and shown in pictures, so it holds its shape without leaning on your memory. That is real. But here is the last thing this book has to tell you: held is not the same as kept. Standardize stopped the passive slide, the quiet decay of people forgetting the best way. But it cannot stop the world, and the world is relentless. New things come through the door, seasons turn, children grow, people move in and out. Nothing you have built will stay built on its own, and not because your standards are weak. It is because life is relentless. A home is not a machine you can set and leave running. It is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive.

## SHORT 2: You do not finish 6S. You keep it.
**Topic: the hero reframe, Sustain is a practice, not a project, and it is a relief. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** For twenty-five chapters you have been finishing things. This is the one you never finish. And that is the good news.

**Script:**
Here is the whole sixth S in one line. You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit of keeping it. For twenty-five chapters you have been doing projects, bounded work with a beginning, a middle, and a satisfying end. Sort the drawer, and the drawer is sorted. Sustain is not like that, and trying to treat it like that is why so much hard-won order collapses a few months after the big push. But read that not as a burden, read it as a relief. You are no longer trying to reach some final, perfect state and then defend it forever, which is exhausting and impossible. You are just living in your home and keeping it as you go. The first five S's are things you do. Sustain is the one you keep doing, because it is not a step with an end but the ongoing life of the home.

## SHORT 3: Habit, not willpower
**Topic: willpower runs out first, lower the bar until the habit is unbreakable. Runtime ~50 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You will not keep your home by trying harder. You already know that. So stop trying harder, and do this instead.

**Script:**
The obvious answer to keeping a home is "try harder", and it is the wrong one, so let me close that door. You will not keep your home by resolving to try harder, any more than you kept it tidy by resolving to be neater. Sustain runs on habit, not willpower: small, normal, shared, and low enough in effort that it survives the weeks when nobody has any energy to spare. Willpower is the wrong fuel because it runs out first, and it runs out precisely when you need it most, in the busy, tired stretches when a home most wants to fall apart. A habit is something you do without deciding to, so it keeps running on the days your motivation is gone, and those are the days that matter. So lower the bar, deliberately, until the habit is almost impossible to fail. A one-minute reset of a single surface, done every day, keeps more of your home over a year than a two-hour blitz you manage twice and then dread. Small and relentless beats heroic and brief.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "You will not keep your home by trying harder."
- 14s: "Sustain runs on habit, not willpower."
- 30s: "Lower the bar until the habit cannot fail."
- 44s: "Small and relentless beats heroic and brief."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The willpower spike rising high and crashing within weeks beside the modest habit line running flat and unbroken, the low dashed keeping line cleared every day by the habit. Then a one-minute reset of a single surface. Warm, calm, honey accent on "habit".

## SHORT 4: Keeping is lighter than fixing
**Topic: a little care every day prevents the dreaded overhaul, so the household relaxes. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The household with the tidiest home is not the one working hardest. It is the one that quietly stopped doing the big scary clean-up at all.

**Script:**
Here is the part that should take some weight off you. Keeping is genuinely lighter than fixing. A home tended a little every day never gets bad enough to need the exhausting overhaul, the dreaded weekend where you finally deal with the whole thing. So the household that sustains is not the one doing more work. It is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all. This is not more discipline, and it is not more effort. It is a smaller effort that prevents the big one. A one-minute reset today is the overhaul you never have to do in three months. So the household that keeps its home is not grinding away at it. It is the one that gets to relax in it. Kept long enough, all of this becomes automatic, and automatic is nearly free. A home you keep on habit is not a burden you carry. It is a place that, quietly and permanently, holds you up.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Keeping is lighter than fixing."
- 12s: "A little care every day. No dreaded overhaul."
- 28s: "The household that sustains stopped doing the big work at all."
- 40s: "Not a burden you carry. A place that holds you up."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The one-minute act of daily care, then the dreaded weekend overhaul fading out because it is never needed, then the household relaxing in the good home. Warm, human, slate and honey.

## SHORT 5: The needle holds, and the last gap appears

**Hook line:** The needle did not move this time. And for the first time in the whole book, that is the most important thing on the dial.

**Script:**
Go and look at the friction meter, because although it does not move this chapter, it shows you something it has never shown before. The needle holds exactly where Standardize left it, at the mark, because a chapter about a mindset does not change a single thing in your rooms. So no, it did not move, and that is honest. But look at the small distance still remaining between the needle and the goal, the gap that has sat there, unexplained, since the very first chapter. For the first time, the dial names it: a faint dashed path runs from where the needle rests to the goal crosshair, and that path is Sustain. That last gap was never more clutter to clear. It is the difference between a home that stays good because you are working at it and a home that stays good on its own. You close it not by pushing harder, but by letting the small habits run, month after month, until keeping becomes second nature. The goal is reached at the very end of the book.
