## Search intent
Keeping a good home good over the years, after the reader has built it and, with Standardize, held it against sliding back. Readers arriving here have a home that is cleared, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized, and they have felt the familiar fear that it will drift back the way every past effort did. They are tired of big pushes that collapse a few months later, and of one person carrying the whole load. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: Sustain is a practice, not a project, so you do not finish 6S, you keep it. It names why held is not the same as kept (Standardize stopped the passive slide, but life is relentless and no standard survives untended); it delivers the definition (Sustain is the sixth S, the ongoing habit of keeping the first five alive, the one S you never finish, a way of living); it delivers the habit-not-willpower reframe (keeping runs on habit, made small, normal, shared, and low-effort so it survives the weeks nobody has energy to spare, and lowering the bar until the habit is unbreakable); and it delivers the second-nature reframe (kept long enough, 6S becomes simply how the home is, and keeping it becomes nearly free). It reads the friction meter as a HOLD, not a milestone: the needle holds at the Standardize-complete position because a concept does not move the room, but for the first time a faint dashed path appears from the needle to the goal, the last gap Sustain closes over the years. It opens Part Eight and sets up the mechanisms without building them (a gentle check-in in Chapter 27, family problem-solving in Chapter 28, a daily rhythm in Chapter 29, a recurring event in Chapter 30). Five of six S's are done; the goal is reached only at the end of the book.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Names the real reason organized homes slide back, and it is not weak willpower or laziness: the world is relentless, new things arrive and seasons change and no standard survives untended, so the fix is a small ongoing habit, not more discipline
- Draws the exact line between held and kept: Standardize stops the passive slide of people forgetting the best way, but Sustain is the ongoing human tending that keeps it alive over years and handles what no standard anticipated
- Names the "done" trap plainly: the moment you decide the home is finished is the moment you stop tending it and the drift begins, so a home is a thing you keep, never a project you complete
- Reframes keeping as lighter than fixing, not a life sentence of chores: a home tended a little every day never needs the dreaded overhaul, so the household that sustains does less total work, not more
- Gives the counterintuitive craft plainly: lower the bar until the habit is almost impossible to fail, because a one-minute reset done every day keeps more of a home over a year than a two-hour blitz done twice and dreaded
- Makes keeping a shared choice rather than one person's burden, with the household conversation that asks not "can we keep this up?" but "how do we want to live now?"
- Promises that it genuinely gets easier, and explains why honestly: not because you finish, but because keeping becomes automatic, and automatic is nearly free
- Reads the friction meter honestly as a hold, not a milestone: a mindset does not move the room, but for the first time the dial shows the last gap to the goal, so the reader is not sold a false forward leap
- Keeps the register warm, freeing, and anti-shame, never punitive: no discipline in the harsh sense, no "stay on top of it" or "hold the line," no hype, no brand names, and never a scolding for homes that slid back before
