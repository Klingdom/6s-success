# Chapter 26 Calls to Action: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

This is the first chapter of Part Eight, and it opens the sixth and final S, Sustain. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible start of the whole idea: pick the smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, resetting a single surface, and start it today, because Sustain is built from habits so small they survive your worst weeks, not from grand resolutions that die inside a month. The CTAs match the chapter's tone, warm and human and freeing, never corporate and never punitive: keeping a home is lighter than fixing it, a little ongoing care that prevents the big dreaded overhauls, so the household that keeps its home is the one that gets to relax in it. The move costs about a minute a day, because the whole point is that keeping is small and shared and low-effort enough to last for years, until it becomes second nature.

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick the smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, resetting a single surface, and start it today. Sustain is built from habits so small they survive your worst weeks, not from grand resolutions that die inside a month.
- **Family Challenge:** Talk about it as a household, once, and ask a bigger question than "can we keep this up?" Ask "how do we want to live now?" A home is kept by the people who live in it choosing to, together, not by one person quietly carrying it.
- **6S Tip:** Lower the bar until the habit is unbreakable. A keeping-routine you do badly every day beats a perfect one you do for two weeks and abandon. Sustain rewards the small and relentless, never the heroic and brief.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small, provable move that demonstrates the whole chapter: pick one keeping-habit small enough to survive your worst weeks and start it today. Lead with it, which is concrete and doable in about a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame, in a register of warmth and relief. A home that slid back after a big push is the normal case, not a failure; the fix is smaller and gentler habits, not more discipline or guilt. Never scold the reader for past lapses.
- The rule (you do not finish 6S, you keep it), the practice-not-project line (the first five S's are things you do, Sustain is the one you keep doing), and the relentlessness and second-nature reframes are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid corporate framing, hype, and any force or control language (no discipline in the punitive sense, stay on top of it, no excuses, hold the line, unlock, leverage, elevate, transform your life). Sustain is relief and freedom, not obligation; keeping is lighter than fixing. Avoid brand names.
- Scope note: this opens Sustain with the concept (you keep 6S, you never finish it) and the mindset (habit not willpower, shared not one person's burden, small enough to be second nature); it does not build the audit (Chapter 27), the family problem-solving method (Chapter 28), the daily 6S rhythm (Chapter 29, "a little, often" as a principle only), or the recurring 6S event (Chapter 30). It uses the standards Standardize built without re-teaching them, and does not re-teach Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, or Standardize. Because this opens the sixth and final S, the friction meter holds at the Standardize-complete position and, for the first time, shows the last gap Sustain closes; the goal is reached only at Chapter 30, when all six S's are done.
