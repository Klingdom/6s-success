# Infographic Spec: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

**Chapter 26, "Sustain: Keeping the Gains"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a home is not a
project you finish but a thing you keep, and the difference between a home that lasts and one that drifts
back is not a greater effort but a smaller one that never quite stops. The concept pairs the hero rule,
**You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit
of keeping it.**, with the picture at its heart: **the same room, one year, two futures**, the same living
room drawn across a single year in two parallel rows, on the top a home finished once and then left to
itself drifting back season by season toward where it began, on the bottom the very same room kept by a
small pair of hands doing one tiny ordinary act each season so it stays good all year. So the viewer sees
the whole move at once, that Sustain is a practice you keep, not a project you finish, and that keeping is
small, ordinary, and light.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_26_final.html`. The two rows are
unmistakably parallel, the same room and furniture; terracotta carries the drift arrows of the top row that
is left to itself, soft green carries the keeping arrows and the small sparkle of the bottom row that is
kept, and one warm honey accent is held to a small plant that appears in both rows so the eye reads the two
futures as the same room; the single small warm red is held back for marks only and is barely used, if at
all, on this calm image, because Sustain is a gift of care, not a live hazard. "Discipline" is not the
point, and neither is "policing"; a home kept by small shared habits is warmer and lighter, a garden kept
alive rather than a project defended. Anti-shame throughout: a home that slid back after a big push is the
normal case, not a failing, because nothing stays built on its own, and not because anyone's standards are
weak, but because life is relentless and pushes on them every single day. The register is warm and freeing,
never corporate, never punitive; Sustain is a little ongoing care from the people who love the home.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole chapter compressed: you do not finish 6S,
  you keep it. The eye should read the rule, then see the same room, two futures make it real, the top row
  drifting back because it was left, the bottom row staying good because it was kept.
- The same room, two futures is the hero, so make the two rows unmistakably parallel: the same room, the
  same furniture, only the keeping different. Keep the top row's drift gentle and sympathetic, never a
  scolding, and the bottom row warm and easy, the keeping-hands doing something tiny and ordinary in each
  season. Set the rule, the definition, and the practice-not-project line byte-for-byte as they read in the
  chapter; they are the frozen strings.
- Hold the color discipline: terracotta carries the drift arrows and eyebrows, green carries the keeping
  arrows, the sparkle, and the goal, one warm honey accent is held to the small plant that appears in both
  rows so the two futures read as one room; the single small warm red is reserved for marks and is barely
  used, if at all, because this image is a gift of care and not a live hazard. This image is calm, so no
  fill-line red anywhere.
- This image makes the concept, the mindset that opens Sustain: a home is kept, not finished. Do NOT
  re-teach the first five S's, and do NOT build the concrete Sustain mechanisms, the gentle check-in
  (Chapter 27), the family problem-solving (Chapter 28), the daily rhythm (Chapter 29), and the recurring
  event (Chapter 30), which are named only as still to come. And name no brand and draw no specific
  product; the room and its furniture are plain and generic, teaching the idea, not a shopping list.
- Anti-shame: a home that slid back after a big push is the normal case, not a failing, because nothing
  stays built on its own, and not because anyone's standards are weak, but because life is relentless and
  pushes on them every day. What you are building is not discipline, it is a little ongoing care from the
  people who love the home. Keep the register warm and freeing, keeping is lighter than fixing, never a
  rule to enforce; no force or control language (no stay on top of it, no hold the line, no enforce or
  inspect), no punitive discipline presented as virtue, no cage, no hype.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the eight seasonal panels
  drawn by hand as small warm rooms rather than clinical thumbnails, the keeping-hands and the small
  sparkle plain and human, the whole image the calm, warm register of a home kept the way a garden stays
  alive rather than a project finished and then left.
