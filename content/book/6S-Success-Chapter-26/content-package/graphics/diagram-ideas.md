# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 26

**"Sustain: Keeping the Gains"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter fonts
(Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not finished art.
They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_26_final.html`: the opener, the same
living room shown across one year in two parallel rows, on the top a home finished once and then left to
itself slowly re-cluttering season by season back toward messy, on the bottom the very same room kept by a
small pair of hands doing one tiny ordinary act each season so the room stays good all year; the
habit-not-willpower graph, a two-line time graph where the willpower line spikes very high then crashes
flat within weeks while the habit line runs low and nearly flat and unbroken across the whole span,
clearing a low dashed keeping line every day; the project-to-practice graph, a two-line time graph where
the project line climbs to a peak then slopes down as the gains decay while the practice line climbs then
levels off flat and high across the years, held there by small regular tick-marks of keeping; and the
friction meter, which this chapter reads as a HOLD, the live needle staying exactly where Standardize left
it at the fifth slate milestone marked Standardize complete, the three-tooth catch behind it still fully
engaged and locked with its small padlock, and for the first time a faint dashed path running from the
needle tip across the small remaining distance to the GOAL crosshair, the last gap Sustain closes over
time.

`#F3D9A6` with edge `#C6923F`, drift tone `#9C9284`, gauge track `#EDE3CE`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the FIRST chapter of Part Eight, and it OPENS Sustain, the sixth and FINAL of
the six S's, with the concept and the mindset rather than the mechanisms. It does one turn: it names the
distinction the whole part rests on (Standardize stopped the passive slide back, the quiet decay of people
forgetting the best way, but held is not the same as kept, because life is relentless and no standard
survives untended over the years), and it delivers the hero, that Sustain is a practice, not a project.
It delivers the practice-not-project line and the definition box (Sustain is the sixth S, the ongoing
habit of keeping the first five alive, so the home stays sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized
not for a week or a season but over the years, the one S you never finish, a way of living), delivers the
relentlessness reframe (nothing stays built on its own, not because standards are weak but because life is
relentless and pushes on them every day), delivers the habit-not-willpower reframe (keeping runs on habit,
not willpower, made small, normal, shared, and low enough in effort to survive the weeks nobody has any
energy to spare, lowering the bar until the habit is unbreakable, because keeping is lighter than fixing),
delivers the second-nature reframe (kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do to your home and
becomes simply how your home is, the home that mostly keeps itself), and reads the friction meter as a
HOLD, not a milestone. It USES the standards Standardize built without re-teaching them, and does NOT
build the mechanisms of Sustain: the gentle check-in (Chapter 27), the family problem-solving (Chapter
28), the daily rhythm (Chapter 29), and the recurring event (Chapter 30) are named as still to come, not
taught here. CRITICAL fence: this is the concept and the mindset of keeping the gains (you keep 6S, you
never finish it; habit not willpower; small and shared; second nature), NOT the concrete practices that do
the keeping (the rest of Part Eight, only named as ahead), and NOT any re-teaching of the first five S's.
Because this chapter changes a mindset rather than a room, the friction needle does NOT move: it HOLDS at
the Standardize-complete position, and the honest new thing this chapter shows is the last gap itself, a
faint dashed path from the needle to the goal that Sustain closes slowly over the years, reached only at
the very end of the book. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 26 · SUSTAIN`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
keeping-habit, keeping-routine, keeping-act, keeping-line, second-nature, habit-not-willpower,
practice-not-project, project-to-practice, drift-catch, low-effort, one-minute, after-dinner, tick-marks,
three-tooth, anti-backslide, no-backslide, Sort-complete, Straighten-complete, Shine-complete,
Safety-complete, Standardize-complete, worst-weeks. Anti-shame throughout: a home that slid back after a
big push is the normal case, not a failing; nothing stays built on its own, and not because anyone's
standards are weak, but because life is relentless and pushes on them every single day. Sustain is meant
to make keeping light and shared, not to police anyone; it is a little ongoing care from the people who
love the home, a gift and not a rule. No force or control framing anywhere (no discipline in the punitive
sense, no stay on top of it, no hold the line, no enforce or inspect, no one standing over a shoulder), no
hype (no unlock, leverage, elevate, transform), no corporate register, no brand names. The register is
calm, warm, human, and illustrated, the relief of a home kept the way a garden stays alive, by a little
care rather than one heroic effort and then neglect.

## 1. The Same Room, One Year, Two Futures (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the chapter's whole idea in one image. The same living room across a single year, drawn
as two parallel rows, so the only variable the eye can read is the small ongoing care. On the top, a home
finished once and then left to itself, drifting back season by season toward where it began. On the
bottom, the same home kept by a little ordinary care in every season, staying good all year. The
difference between them is not a greater effort; it is a smaller one that never quite stops. Mirrors Figure
1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** One living room drawn eight times, in two rows of four seasonal panels (spring, summer,
autumn, winter). The TOP row, `FINISHED, THEN LEFT`, opens on a gleaming perfect room and, across the
seasons, slowly fills again: a floor item, then a leaning pile, then scattered clutter and a crooked
picture, until the winter panel looks worryingly like where the book began; small terracotta drift arrows
tip gently downward between the panels. The BOTTOM row, `KEPT`, shows the same room in every season, but in
each panel a small pair of warm hands does one tiny ordinary thing, straightening a plant, wiping a
surface, and a small green sparkle shows the room staying good and warm across all four seasons; small
green arrows run flat between the panels.

**Rough layout:**
- A soft seasonal wash, a honey `#FBE9C8` gradient falling into panel cream `#FBF7EF`. A faint Newsreader
  italic line, muted `#9C9284`: `the same room, one year, two futures`.
- Season labels across the top in soft sans caps: `SPRING`, `SUMMER`, `AUTUMN`, `WINTER`.
- TOP row eyebrow, terracotta sans caps: `FINISHED, THEN LEFT`. Four panels with `#8B7E6A` room strokes on
  cream `#FCF8F0` faces, the clutter growing panel by panel, terracotta drift arrows tipping downward
  between them, and a Newsreader italic line beneath in terracotta: `left to itself, the room drifts back
  toward where it began`.
- BOTTOM row eyebrow, green sans caps: `KEPT`. The same four panels, each staying good, each with a small
  pair of keeping-hands `#F3D9A6` doing one act and a small green `#6E8B5B` sparkle, flat green arrows
  between them, and a Newsreader italic line beneath in green: `kept by a small act each season, the room
  stays good all year`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter): "The same room, one year, two futures. On the top, a home finished
  once and then left to itself, drifting back season by season toward where it started. On the bottom, the
  same home kept by a little ordinary care, staying good across the whole year. The difference between them
  is not a greater effort. It is a smaller one that never quite stops."

**Note for the illustrator:** the point is that the difference between a home that lasts and one that
drifts is not effort but a small care that never quite stops, so draw the two rows unmistakably parallel,
the very same room, the same furniture, only the keeping different. Keep the top row's drift gentle and
sympathetic, never a scolding: the room is not failing because anyone is careless, it is drifting because
that is what living does when nobody is keeping. Keep the bottom row warm and easy, the keeping-hands doing
something tiny and ordinary in each panel, the green sparkle small. Hold the color discipline: terracotta
for the drift arrows and the top eyebrow, green for the keeping arrows, the sparkle, and the KEPT eyebrow,
one honey accent held to the small plant that appears in both rows; spark-red does not appear. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, the register of a quiet before-and-after that turns entirely on one small,
repeated act.

## 2. Habit, Not Willpower (the fuel, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's answer to "how" as one picture: keeping runs on habit, not willpower,
because willpower spikes high and then crashes, and it fails exactly when life gets hard, while a small
habit never spikes but never stops, so it clears the low bar of keeping every single day. The value is in
the shapes of the two lines, not in any numbers. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A two-line time graph on a warm panel, time along the bottom (week after week) and the
effort you can keep up along the side. The willpower line, in slate, starts low, spikes up very sharply to
a tall early peak, wobbles, and then crashes down within a few weeks to run flat and low along the bottom.
The habit line, in warm terracotta, starts low and modest and simply continues, a nearly flat, unbroken
line holding steady across the whole span, never spiking but never crashing. A low dashed keeping line
sits near the bottom; the habit line stays above it the whole time, clearing it every day, while the
willpower line clears it only briefly during its spike.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft: `HABIT, NOT
  WILLPOWER`.
- Two hairline `#D9CDB8` axes; a rotated soft sans axis label `effort you can keep up` up the side and
  `time, week after week` along the foot.
- A low dashed slate keeping line near the bottom, with a small Newsreader italic tag in slate: `the
  keeping line: clear it every day`.
- The `willpower` line in slate `#3C5A6B`, spiking to a tall early peak then crashing flat, a small slate
  dot on the peak, a sans label `willpower` above it and a Newsreader italic note `spikes, then crashes`.
- The `habit` line in warm terracotta `#BC4B2A`, low, steady, and unbroken across the whole span, a sans
  label `habit` and a Newsreader italic note `never spikes, never stops`.
- A caption strip, short rules either side, a Fraunces line in slate: `small and relentless beats heroic
  and brief`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "Why keeping runs on habit, not willpower. Willpower spikes
  high and then crashes, usually within weeks, and it fails exactly when life gets hard. A small habit
  never spikes, but it never stops, so it clears the low bar of keeping every single day. Small and
  relentless beats heroic and brief."

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is the contrast of the two shapes, so make the willpower
spike genuinely dramatic and its crash genuinely flat, and make the habit line calm, low, and clearly
unbroken, always above the keeping line. Set the caption `small and relentless beats heroic and brief`
exactly as it reads; it is frozen wording and must not be trimmed or reworded. Hold the color discipline:
slate for the willpower line and the axes, one warm terracotta accent held to the habit line, slate again
for the low keeping line; green and spark-red do not appear. This is not a scolding of willpower: the
point is simply that it is the wrong fuel because it runs out first, exactly when you need it most. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, a hand-made graph, friendly but not childish, the register of relief that
keeping does not depend on trying harder.

## 3. Project to Practice (the destination, an infographic)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's promise as one picture: a project peaks and then decays, while a
practice climbs and then levels off flat and high across the years, held there by small ongoing care. The
home you built is the peak; whether it slides down the project line or holds along the practice line is
decided by one thing only, whether the small keeping continues. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A two-line time graph on a warm panel, the gains up the side and the years along the
bottom. The project line, in slate, climbs steeply to a high peak, the big one-time effort of building the
home, and then, once the project ends, slopes steadily downward as the gains decay season by season,
ending low. The practice line, in warm terracotta, climbs the same way at first but, instead of peaking
and falling, levels off and runs flat and high across all the years, held there by small regular
tick-marks showing the little acts of keeping. The shared peak is marked as the home you built.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft: `PROJECT TO
  PRACTICE`.
- Two hairline `#D9CDB8` axes; a rotated soft sans axis label `the gains` up the side and `the years, one
  after another` along the foot.
- The `a project` line in slate `#3C5A6B`, climbing to a high peak then sloping down as the gains decay, a
  sans label `a project` and a Newsreader italic note `peaks, then decays`.
- The shared peak marked with a small ink dot and a Newsreader italic tag `the home you built`.
- The `a practice` line in warm terracotta `#BC4B2A`, climbing then levelling off flat and high across the
  years, a row of small terracotta tick-marks along it for the little acts of keeping, a sans label `a
  practice`.
- A note strip, short rules either side, a Fraunces line in slate: `the practice, not the peak, is what
  lasts`.
- Caption (frozen, from the chapter figure): "A project peaks and then decays; a practice levels off and
  lasts. The home you built is the peak on the left. Whether it slides down the project line or holds along
  the practice line is decided by one thing only: whether the small keeping continues. The practice, not
  the peak, is what lasts."

**Note for the illustrator:** the message is that the peak is not the point, the keeping is, so let the two
lines share the same climb and the same peak, then diverge cleanly, the project sliding down and the
practice holding flat and high. Draw the practice tick-marks small and regular, the visible little acts of
keeping that hold the line up. Set the note `the practice, not the peak, is what lasts` exactly as it
reads; it is frozen wording and must not be trimmed or reworded. Hold the color discipline: slate for the
project line and the axes, ink for the shared peak dot, one warm terracotta accent held to the practice
line and its tick-marks; spark-red does not appear. No celebration of the peak itself, no confetti at the
top: the quiet truth of the image is that the flat, unglamorous practice line is the one worth having.
Warm line-and-flat-color style, a hand-made graph, the register of a promise kept slowly.

## 4. Held Is Not Kept (the distinction, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's opening turn as a picture: Standardize held the home against the passive
slide back, the quiet decay of people forgetting the best way, but held is not the same as kept, because
there is a second force no standard can hold back, the active, ceaseless arrival of life. This diagram has
no direct HTML figure twin; it renders the held-is-not-kept distinction as a calm side-by-side, so the
reader feels that the gap between held and kept is exactly where homes fall apart, and that closing it is
not distrust of the standards but respect for how hard the world pushes on them.

**What it shows:** Two quiet panels, read left to right. The LEFT panel, held: the home standing steady on
its written, tool-carried, visible standards, a small slate catch behind it barring the passive slide of
forgetting, the drift of the old chapters held firmly at bay. The RIGHT panel, kept: the same home, but now
life is arriving at the door, new things coming in, a season turning, people coming and going, and a small
pair of warm hands doing one ordinary keeping-act that meets it, calm and unhurried. A gentle arrow crosses
from held to kept. Beneath, a small strip carries the definition, and to one side a dim, unlit panel names
the gentle check-in of Chapter 27 as next, the first concrete way to keep the gains.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft, `STANDARDIZE HELD IT · SUSTAIN KEEPS IT`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath
  carrying the section's spine: "Held is not the same as kept."
- Two cream `#FCF8F0` panels with rule `#E2D8C4` borders, joined by a gentle slate arrow:
  - `HELD` (left): the steady home on its standards, a small slate three-tooth catch behind it barring the
    dashed slide of forgetting, a muted note `the passive slide, stopped`.
  - `KEPT` (right, warm and lit): the same home with life arriving at the door, a small pair of
    keeping-hands meeting it, a small green sparkle, a note `the relentless world, met daily`.
- Beneath, a small slate-bordered strip carrying the frozen definition in ink: "Sustain is the sixth S:
  the ongoing habit of keeping the first five alive, so the home stays sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and
  standardized not for a week or a season but over the years. It is the one S you never finish, because it
  is not a task you complete. It is a way of living."
- To the right or below, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `6S AUDITS THAT DO NOT FEEL LIKE
  INSPECTIONS · NEXT` in muted honey sans caps, a muted italic note beneath, `the first concrete way to
  keep the gains, a gentle check-in that catches drift early`, held quiet and clearly one step ahead, not
  yet reached.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "A home is not a project you complete. It is a thing you keep."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the chapter's opening turn, so keep it calm and plain, never
alarming; the point is not that the standards failed but that they were built to hold one force, the
passive slide of forgetting, and there is a second force they were never meant to hold, the active arrival
of life. Draw the held panel steady and reassuring and the kept panel warm and living, life arriving and
being met without strain. Hold the color discipline: slate for the catch, the arrow, and the sub-strip,
green for the keeping-sparkle, one muted honey for the dim next-chapter panel; no spark-red, no cage, no
one standing over a shoulder. Keep the next-chapter panel dim, unlit, and clearly one step ahead; you may
name the gentle check-in of Chapter 27 as next, but teach nothing of it. The register is warmth and
respect, not worry: the world pushes hard, and keeping is simply how a home pushes gently back, every day.
Warm line-and-flat-color style.

## 5. The Needle Holds, and the Last Gap Appears (the friction meter)

Standardize left it, at the fifth slate milestone marked STANDARDIZE COMPLETE. What is new is the last gap

HOLD with a newly visible last-gap path, NOT a move and NOT a milestone; the goal is reached only at the
