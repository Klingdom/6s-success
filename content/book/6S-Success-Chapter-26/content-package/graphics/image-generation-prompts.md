# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 26

**"Sustain: Keeping the Gains"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Eight · Sustain*

Four prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold
across all four: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta
`#BC4B2A` (the warm accent, and in the two graphs the line that lasts, habit and practice), honey
`#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B` (data lines, milestones, the catch, and in the two graphs the line that

goal), a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only, the no-backslide bar, used barely
if at all on these calm images. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real textures over
polish.

The named space throughout is the finished home the reader has cleared, arranged, cleaned, made safe, and
standardized in words, tools, and pictures. This chapter, the FIRST of Part Eight, OPENS Sustain, the
sixth and final S, with the concept and the mindset rather than the mechanisms, and does one turn: it names
the distinction the whole part rests on (Standardize stopped the passive slide back, the quiet decay of
people forgetting the best way, but held is not the same as kept, because life is relentless and no
standard survives untended over the years) and delivers the hero, that Sustain is a practice, not a
project. It delivers the practice-not-project line and the definition box (Sustain is the sixth S, the
ongoing habit of keeping the first five alive, the one S you never finish, a way of living), delivers the
relentlessness reframe (nothing stays built on its own, not because standards are weak but because life
pushes on them every day), delivers the habit-not-willpower reframe (keeping runs on habit, not willpower,
made small, normal, shared, and low in effort so it survives the weeks nobody has energy to spare, lowering
the bar until the habit is unbreakable, because keeping is lighter than fixing), delivers the second-nature
reframe (kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do to your home and becomes simply how your home
is), and reads the friction meter as a HOLD, not a milestone, with the last gap to the goal shown for the
first time. Nothing of the first five S's is re-taught, the standards Standardize built are used and not
re-explained, and none of the concrete Sustain mechanisms are built (the gentle check-in of Chapter 27,
the family problem-solving of Chapter 28, the daily rhythm of Chapter 29, and the recurring event of
Chapter 30 are named only as still to come). Keep the tone calm, warm, and freeing: a home that slid back
after a big push is the normal case, not a failing, because life is relentless and nothing stays good on
its own, so the fix is a small ongoing habit and not more discipline. Sustain is a little care from the
people who love the home, a gift and not a rule; the register is relief, a home kept the way a garden stays
alive. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated house style: no corporate office look, no force
or control framing (no discipline in the punitive sense, no stay on top of it, no hold the line, no enforce
or inspect), no one standing over a shoulder, no cages, no hype (no unlock, leverage, elevate, transform),
no photoreal polish; the keeping is small, ordinary, and human, a pair of warm hands doing one tiny thing.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object
in the scene, a hand-lettered card, a handwritten line on a real sheet, a hand-drawn line on a real graph,
or the reading on a real gauge, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental
hand-lettered label: use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 26 · SUSTAIN`, never a floating hyphen or a
floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: keeping-habit, keeping-routine,
keeping-act, second-nature, habit-not-willpower, practice-not-project, project-to-practice, low-effort,

## 1. The same room, one year, two futures (the opener)

A warm, wide editorial illustration of the same living room shown across a single year, drawn as two
parallel rows of four seasonal panels each, spring through winter, in soft daylight, to make a single
point. On the top row, a home finished once and then left to itself: it opens on a gleaming, perfect room
and, across the seasons, slowly fills again, a floor item, then a leaning pile, then scattered clutter and
a crooked picture, until the winter panel looks worryingly like where the whole story began, with small
warm-terracotta drift arrows tipping gently downward between the panels. On the bottom row, the very same
room in every season, but in each panel a small pair of warm hands does one tiny ordinary thing,
straightening a plant, wiping a surface, and a small soft-green sparkle shows the room staying good and
warm all year, with small green arrows running flat between the panels. The two rows are unmistakably
parallel, the same room and furniture, so the only difference the eye reads is the small ongoing care.
Warm paper cream and a soft honey seasonal wash, ink-brown line work, terracotta for the drift arrows,
soft green for the keeping arrows and the sparkle, one honey accent on a small plant that appears in both
rows, no warm-red anywhere. Editorial storybook illustration. The same room, one year, two futures.

- Mood: the difference between a home that lasts and one that drifts back is not a greater effort but a
  smaller one that never quite stops; left to itself a home drifts back toward where it began, not because
  anyone is careless but because that is simply what living does; kept by a little ordinary care each
  season, the same room stays good all year; the register is warm and sympathetic, never a scolding.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, a corporate or office look, floating typeset text
  overlays, anyone being scolded or shamed for the drifting room, a cage or lock, any warm-red accent,
  brand names or recognizable products; keep the two rows plainly parallel, the top row drifting gently and
  sympathetically toward clutter, the bottom row staying good with one small keeping-act and a small green
  sparkle in each season.

## 2. Habit, not willpower (the fuel)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-drawn two-line time graph on a soft cream panel, with time along
the bottom, week after week, and the effort you can keep up along the side, in soft daylight. The willpower
line, in slate blue, starts low, spikes up very sharply to a tall early peak, wobbles, and then crashes
down within a few weeks to run flat and low along the bottom. The habit line, in warm terracotta, starts
low and modest and simply continues, a nearly flat, unbroken line holding steady across the whole span,
never spiking but never crashing. A low dashed keeping line sits near the bottom; the habit line stays
above it the whole way, clearing it every day, while the willpower line clears it only briefly during its
spike. A small hand-lettered line reads small and relentless beats heroic and brief. Warm paper cream
background, ink-brown line work, slate for the willpower line and the axes, one warm terracotta accent held
to the habit line, no soft-green and no warm-red on this graph. A picture of one plain idea: willpower runs
out first, exactly when you need it, and a small habit that never stops beats it every time.

- Mood: willpower is the wrong fuel because it is the thing that runs out first, and it runs out precisely
  when you need it most, in the busy, tired stretches when a home most wants to fall apart; a habit keeps
  running on the days your motivation is gone, and those are the days that matter; small and relentless
  beats heroic and brief; the register is relief, that keeping does not depend on trying harder.
- Avoid: a cold clinical chart, gridlines everywhere, a corporate slide, neon color, numbers or data
  labels, a scolding of willpower as a moral failing, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names;
  keep the willpower spike genuinely dramatic and its crash genuinely flat, the habit line calm and low and
  clearly unbroken always above the keeping line, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but not childish.

## 3. Project to practice (the destination)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-drawn two-line time graph on a soft cream panel, with the gains up
the side and the years along the bottom, one after another, in soft daylight. The project line, in slate
blue, climbs steeply to a high peak, the big one-time effort of building the home, and then, once the
project ends, slopes steadily downward as the gains decay season by season, ending low. The practice line,
in warm terracotta, climbs the same way at first but, instead of peaking and falling, levels off and runs
flat and high across all the years, held there by a row of small regular tick-marks along it, the little
acts of keeping. The shared peak is marked with a small note, the home you built. A hand-lettered line
reads the practice, not the peak, is what lasts. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, slate for
the project line and the axes, ink for the shared peak, one warm terracotta accent held to the practice
line and its tick-marks, no warm-red anywhere. A picture of one plain idea: a project peaks and then
decays, a practice levels off and lasts, and only the small ongoing keeping decides which line your home
follows.

- Mood: the home you built is the peak, but the peak is not the point; whether the gains slide down the
  project line or hold along the practice line is decided by one thing only, whether the small keeping
  continues; a project peaks and then decays, a practice levels off and lasts; the flat, unglamorous
  practice line is the one worth having; the register is a quiet promise kept slowly.
- Avoid: a cold clinical chart, gridlines everywhere, a corporate slide, neon color, a celebration or glow
  or confetti at the peak, floating typeset text, any warm-red, brand names; keep the two lines sharing the
  same climb and the same peak then diverging cleanly, the project sliding down and the practice holding
  flat and high with small regular tick-marks, the whole thing hand-made and friendly but not childish.

## 4. The needle holds, and the last gap appears (the friction meter)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge dialing from
calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right. This chapter the dark
live needle does not move at all: it holds exactly where Standardize left it, at a clearly marked slate
milestone tick on the dial, the fifth, after Sort complete, Straighten complete, Shine complete, and Safety

checklist, the schedule, and the name, a list, a time, and a name, stays fully engaged and locked, its

friction side is barred solidly, hand-marked no backslide. What is new this chapter is a faint soft-green

background, ink line work, consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: the needle does not move this chapter, because a chapter about a mindset does not change a single
  thing in the rooms, so any forward tick would be dishonest; the honest new thing is the last gap itself,
  shown for the first time as a faint dashed path from the needle to the goal, and that path is Sustain,

  second nature; the catch behind the needle stays fully engaged and locked, the gains held firmly; the
  goal is reached only at the very end of the book.
- Avoid: the live needle moving forward or reaching the goal crosshair at all, drawing this as any forward
  step or the whole journey finished, a completion glow or celebratory confetti, the passed markers or the
  catch or the last-gap path drawn so faint they cannot be read, the gap between the needle and the goal
  closed, hype, neon color. The needle holds exactly where it was, the catch stays fully engaged and locked
  with a small padlock, the backslide is barred solidly, and for the first time a faint dashed path shows
  the last gap between the needle and the goal.
