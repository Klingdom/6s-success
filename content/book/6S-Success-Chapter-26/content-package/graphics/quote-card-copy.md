**Strong alternates if you want to swap any of the above (all verbatim):**

- (verbatim, a living thing has to be kept alive)
  "A home is not a machine you can set and leave running. It is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive."

- (verbatim, why the small version wins)
  "The small, dull, relentless version wins, every time, over the heroic version, because the small one actually keeps happening."

- (verbatim, keeping is lighter than fixing)
  "the household that sustains is not the one doing more work. It is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all."

- (verbatim, it gets easier because it becomes automatic)
  "It gets easier because keeping becomes automatic, and automatic is nearly free."

- (verbatim, the home that holds you up)
  "A home you keep on habit is not a burden you carry. It is a place that, quietly and permanently, holds you up."

- (verbatim, the one idea the whole sixth S rests on)
  "you are not trying to finish. You are learning to keep."

- (verbatim, the Quick Win)
  "Pick the smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, resetting a single surface, and start it today. Sustain is built from habits so small they survive your worst weeks, not from grand resolutions that die inside a month."

- (verbatim, the Family Challenge)
  "Talk about it as a household, once, and ask a bigger question than "can we keep this up?" Ask "how do we want to live now?" A home is kept by the people who live in it choosing to, together, not by one person quietly carrying it."

- (verbatim, the 6S Tip)
  "Lower the bar until the habit is unbreakable. A keeping-routine you do badly every day beats a perfect one you do for two weeks and abandon. Sustain rewards the small and relentless, never the heroic and brief."

- (verbatim, the Common Mistake)
  "Treating 6S as a project you finished, so you stop, and then watching the whole thing quietly come undone over the following months. The moment you decide you are done is the moment the drift begins. You are never done. You are keeping."

- (verbatim, the friction-meter caption)
  "The needle holds at the Standardize-complete mark, because a concept does not move the room. But for the first time the dial shows the last gap plainly: a faint dashed path from the needle to the goal. Sustain is what closes that gap, not by clearing new friction but by making the keeping of the home so habitual and so shared that it becomes second nature. The goal is reached at the very end, when 6S has become simply how the household lives."
