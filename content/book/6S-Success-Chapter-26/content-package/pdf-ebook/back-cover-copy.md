# Back Cover Copy: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

## Back-cover text

By now you have built something real: a home that is sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and, with the fifth S, standardized, written down and shown in pictures so it holds its shape without leaning on your memory. Five of the six S's are done, and the fifth locked the gains so they cannot quietly slide back through forgetting. But you already know the fear, because you have felt it after every past effort: that in a few months the home will drift back the way it always has. Here is the last thing the book has to tell you, the thing the sixth S exists for. Held is not the same as kept. Standardize stopped your home drifting back on its own, but it cannot stop the world, and the world is relentless: new things arrive, seasons turn, people come and go, and no standard survives a busy or sad or chaotic season untended. A home is not a machine you can set and leave running. It is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive. "You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit of keeping it."

Chapter 26 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the first chapter of Part Eight, and it opens Sustain, the sixth and final S, with its concept and its mindset rather than its mechanisms. It names the "done" trap, the quiet, fatal moment of looking at your good home and thinking "there, done", because the moment you believe a home is finished is the moment you stop tending it and the relentless world begins carrying it back. And it delivers the hero, that Sustain is a practice, not a project: "The first five S's are things you do. Sustain is the one you keep doing, because it is not a step with an end but the ongoing life of the home." Then the plain definition of the sixth S: "Sustain is the sixth S: the ongoing habit of keeping the first five alive, so the home stays sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized not for a week or a season but over the years. It is the one S you never finish, because it is not a task you complete. It is a way of living." The chapter's gift is relief, not another chore: keeping is genuinely lighter than fixing, because a home tended a little every day never gets bad enough to need the exhausting overhaul, so "the household that sustains is not the one doing more work. It is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all." It runs on habit, not willpower, made small and shared and low enough in effort to survive the weeks nobody has energy to spare, until "Kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do to your home and becomes simply how your home is." Pick a keeping-habit small enough to do forever with **The Keeping Habit card**, and make it a shared choice rather than one person's burden with **The How Do We Want to Live conversation guide**. Then go and look at the friction meter, which does not move this chapter, because a concept does not move the room, but for the first time shows the last gap plainly: a faint dashed path from the needle to the goal, the small distance Sustain closes over the years. Next, Chapter 26 hands off to Chapter 27 · 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections, the first concrete way to keep the gains, a light, regular check-in that catches drift while it is still small and feels like care rather than an inspection.
