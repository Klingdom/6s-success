# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: Sustain: Keeping the Gains

*Chapter 26, 6S Success: Home Edition. Use these for personal reflection or a group discussion. They are open-ended, with no single right answer.*

1. The chapter opens on the distinction the whole sixth S turns on: "Held is not the same as kept." After all the work of the first five S's, where do you feel the difference between a home that is held against sliding back and one that is actively kept?

2. The chapter names why nothing stays built on its own: "It is because life is relentless: new things arrive, seasons change, people come and go, and the pull back toward chaos never takes a single day off." Which relentless part of your own life pushes hardest on the order you have built, and does naming it as the world's fault rather than yours change how you feel about it?

3. The chapter says a home cannot simply be switched on and left: "A home is not a machine you can set and leave running. It is a living thing, and living things have to be kept alive." Where have you treated a part of your home like a machine you finished, only to watch it quietly come undone?

4. The "done" trap is the moment the sixth S exists to help you avoid: "A home is not a project you complete. It is a thing you keep." Can you remember a past effort that collapsed a few months after a big push? What might it have taken to keep it instead of finish it?

5. The rule of the whole part is short: "You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit of keeping it." Read as relief rather than burden, how does it feel to learn there is no finish line to defend, only a home to keep?

6. The practice-not-project line draws the difference: "The first five S's are things you do. Sustain is the one you keep doing, because it is not a step with an end but the ongoing life of the home." Which past attempts of yours failed because you treated keeping like a project with an end?

7. The definition box calls Sustain "the one S you never finish, because it is not a task you complete. It is a way of living." What would it mean, concretely, for keeping your home to become a way of living rather than a task on a list?

8. The chapter closes the door on trying harder: "You will not keep your home by resolving to try harder, any more than you kept it tidy by resolving to be neater." Sustain runs on habit, not willpower. Where have you leaned on willpower to keep your home, and where did it run out?

9. The chapter argues willpower fails exactly when you need it: it runs out "in the busy, tired, difficult stretches that are also when a home most wants to fall apart." Think of your hardest recent week. What kept happening on its own, and what only happened when you had energy to spare?

10. The 6S Tip is counterintuitive: "Lower the bar until the habit is unbreakable. A keeping-routine you do badly every day beats a perfect one you do for two weeks and abandon." What is one keeping-habit you could make so small it survives your worst weeks, and does making it smaller feel like a relief or a compromise?

11. The chapter promises less work, not more: "the household that sustains is not the one doing more work. It is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all." Which big, dreaded overhaul in your home might simply disappear if a little care happened every day?

12. The second-nature reframe is where it is all heading: "Kept long enough, 6S stops being something you do to your home and becomes simply how your home is." Which small habit in your home already runs on its own, without a decision, and what would it be like if keeping the whole home felt like that?

13. The chapter gives an honest answer to a question you may have carried since the beginning: "It gets easier because keeping becomes automatic, and automatic is nearly free." Where in your home has something once effortful already become nearly free, and what did the change feel like?

14. The Quick Win is the smallest possible start: "Pick the smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, resetting a single surface, and start it today." What single surface would you pick, and could you start it before you finish this discussion?

15. The Family Challenge points at a shared choice rather than one person's load: "Talk about it as a household, once, and ask a bigger question than "can we keep this up?" Ask "how do we want to live now?"" Who in your home has quietly carried the keeping alone, and what might change if the question became how you all want to live?

16. The chapter reads the friction meter as a hold with a new thing revealed: "The needle holds at the Standardize-complete mark, because a concept does not move the room. But for the first time the dial shows the last gap plainly: a faint dashed path from the needle to the goal." With five of the six S's done and Sustain only just begun, what would it take, in your home, to let the small habits run until that last gap closes on its own?
