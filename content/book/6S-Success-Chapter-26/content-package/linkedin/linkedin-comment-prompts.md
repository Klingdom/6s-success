# Chapter 26 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: Sustain, Keeping the Gains

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, human, freeing voice, relief and permission not compliance: a home that slid back after a big push is the normal case, not a failure, and keeping is lighter than fixing, a shared choice about how to live, not a discipline anyone imposes. No corporate framing, no force or control language, no scolding for past lapses, no brand names.

---

1. Be honest with me: how many times have you built the good home, felt that lovely "there, done", and then watched it quietly come undone over the following months? That is not weak willpower and it is not laziness. It is because held is not the same as kept, and the world is relentless. What was the moment you first noticed the drift starting again, and what, if anything, had changed in the house that no standard could have stopped?

2. You do not finish 6S. You keep it. The first five S's build a home worth keeping; the sixth is the habit of keeping it. Has that reframe landed for you, or does it feel like bad news? Because it is meant as a relief: you are no longer chasing a final, perfect state and then defending it forever, you are just living in your home and keeping it as you go. Which reading is yours right now, the burden or the relief, and why?

3. Sustain runs on habit, not willpower: small, normal, shared, and low enough in effort that it survives the weeks when nobody has any energy to spare. Think about the keeping-routines you have tried before. Were they built for your good weeks or your worst ones? If you had to name the single smallest keeping-habit you could imagine actually doing forever, one minute, once a day, what would it be, and what has stopped you from starting it today?

4. Lower the bar until the habit is almost impossible to fail. A one-minute reset of a single surface, done every day, keeps more of your home over a year than a two-hour blitz you manage twice and then dread. This is the counterintuitive part, so I want to test it against your life: has a small, dull, relentless routine ever quietly kept something good in your home better than a big heroic push did? What was it, and did you notice at the time that the small one was doing the work?

5. Here is the warmer half of the question. A home is kept by the people who live in it choosing to, together, not by one person quietly carrying it. So many homes have one person holding the whole load. If you sat down as a household, once, and asked not "can we keep this up?" but "how do we want to live now?", what do you think would change? For those of you who have had that conversation, did sharing the keeping actually make it lighter, or did it stay on one pair of shoulders anyway?
