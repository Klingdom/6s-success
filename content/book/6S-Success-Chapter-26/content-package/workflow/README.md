## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
You have built a home that is sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized, and with Standardize it is written down and shown in pictures so it holds its shape without leaning on memory, and here is the last thing the book has to say: held is not the same as kept. Standardize stopped the passive slide back, the quiet decay of people forgetting the best way, but it cannot stop the world, and the world is relentless, new things arrive, seasons turn, people come and go, and no standard survives untended over the years, because a home is not a machine you set and leave running, it is a living thing that has to be kept alive. That keeping is Sustain, and it is different in kind (the hero, the rule, and the practice-not-project line): the first five S's are things you do and finish, Sustain is the one you never finish, because it is not a task with an end, so you do not finish 6S, you keep it, the first five S's build a home worth keeping and the sixth is the habit of keeping it. The quiet, fatal moment is the "done" trap, looking at your good home and thinking "there, done", because nothing in a home is ever done and the moment you believe it is you stop tending it and the relentless world begins carrying it back, so a home is not a project you complete but a thing you keep. The relentlessness reframe names why (nothing you have built will stay built on its own, and not because your standards are weak, it is because life is relentless and the pull back toward chaos never takes a single day off, so Sustain is not distrust of your standards, it is respect for how hard the world pushes on them), and the definition box names the S itself (Sustain is the sixth S, the ongoing habit of keeping the first five alive so the home stays sorted, arranged, clean, safe, and standardized not for a week or a season but over the years, the one S you never finish because it is not a task you complete, a way of living). The engine is the habit-not-willpower reframe: you will not keep your home by resolving to try harder, any more than you kept it tidy by resolving to be neater, Sustain runs on habit, not willpower, small, normal, shared, and low enough in effort that it survives the weeks nobody has any energy to spare, because willpower runs out first and runs out precisely when you need it most, so you lower the bar until the habit is almost impossible to fail, a one-minute reset of a single surface done every day keeps more of your home over a year than a two-hour blitz you manage twice and then dread, the small, dull, relentless version wins every time because it actually keeps happening, and keeping is lighter than fixing, so the household that sustains is not the one doing more work, it is the one that has quietly stopped doing the big, dreaded work at all. Kept long enough, the second-nature reframe arrives: 6S stops being something you do to your home and becomes simply how your home is, the destination the whole book has been walking toward, not a home you are forever fixing but one that mostly keeps itself, because keeping it has quietly become second nature, and it gets easier not because you finish but because keeping becomes automatic and automatic is nearly free, so a home you keep on habit is not a burden you carry, it is a place that, quietly and permanently, holds you up. The friction meter HOLDS at the STANDARDIZE COMPLETE position, because a chapter about a mindset does not change a single thing in your rooms, so this is NOT a milestone and NOT a move, but for the first time the dial names the small distance still remaining, a faint dashed path runs from the needle to the goal crosshair, and that path is Sustain, closed not by clearing new friction but by letting the small habits run month after month until the effort of keeping fades toward nothing, the goal reached only at the very end of the book, at Chapter 30. Hands off to Chapter 27 · 6S Audits That Do Not Feel Like Inspections, the first concrete way to keep the gains, a gentle, regular check-in that catches drift early and feels like care rather than an inspection.
