## Hero device: Sustain is a practice, not a project (you keep 6S, you never finish it)
The first five S's are things you do and complete; Sustain is the one you keep. It is not a task with an end but the ongoing habit of keeping the other five alive, made small enough and shared enough to last for years, until keeping the home is simply how the household lives. Source: "Sustain the gains", "develop a sense of responsibility and self-discipline", Shitsuke / continuous improvement. Referenced lightly and accurately; do not overclaim.

## Motifs
- **Held is not kept.** Standardize prevents the passive slide; Sustain answers the active, relentless push of life over time.
- **You never finish.** The "done" trap; a home is kept, not completed; the one S with no end.
- **A practice, not a project.** The turn from doing 6S to living it.
- **Habit, not willpower.** Keeping runs on small, shared, low-effort habits, not resolve.
- **Keeping is lighter than fixing.** A little ongoing care prevents the big dreaded overhauls; Sustain is relief, not obligation.
- **Second nature.** Kept long enough, 6S becomes simply how the home is; the effortless home the whole book aimed at.

## Emotional purpose
Warm, encouraging, and freeing. The chapter must defuse the natural fear that "you never finish" means a life sentence of chores, and instead make keeping feel lighter than fixing: the household that tends its home with a little regular care is the one that gets to relax in it and never faces the dreaded overhaul. Sustain is framed as relief and freedom, and as a shared choice about how to live, not a burden on one person or a discipline imposed. No shame for past lapses (a home that slid back is normal), no punitive-discipline language.

## Practical purpose
Give the reader the Sustain mindset: understand that held is not kept and the world is relentless; accept that 6S is kept, not finished; build keeping on small, shared, low-effort habits rather than willpower; and aim for second nature.
