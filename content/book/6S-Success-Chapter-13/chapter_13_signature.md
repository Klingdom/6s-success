## Hero device: The Prime Zone
There is a small band of space that is genuinely easy to reach, roughly between your hips and your shoulders, within arm's reach, at the front of the shelf, and measured from where you actually use the thing. Call it the prime zone. It is limited, and it is the most valuable storage you own. What earns a spot there is not size, category, or tidiness; it is how often your hand goes to the thing. This is the home-scaled version of the Straighten (seiton) placement principle: store by frequency of use, at the point of use. Reference lightly, do not overclaim.

## Motifs
- **Prime space is expensive.** The easy-reach band is small and precious; spend it on what you use most.
- **Match the reach to the use.** Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off. The more you use it, the closer and easier it goes.
- **The point of use, not the category.** The prime spot is measured from your hand at the moment you need the thing, not from a tidy list.
- **Arrangement, not failing.** A wasted best spot is a fixable placement, not a personal flaw. Move the home, not yourself.
- **The needle steps without anything leaving.** Nothing was added or removed; the homes moved to match frequency, and the busiest things became the easiest to reach, so the meter steps again.

## Callouts (inventory; frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** open one drawer or cabinet you use every day; find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to; one swap, and the space is faster.
- **Family Challenge:** everyone names the one thing they use every single day that lives in an annoying spot, and together move it to the easiest reach in that space; the most-used things should be the least trouble to get.
- **6S Tip:** store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list; coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods, because the prime spot is measured from your hand, not from a category.
- **Common Mistake:** storing by category instead of by use; putting all the baking gear together on one high shelf looks tidy, but if you reach for the measuring cup every day and the cake tin once a year, they should not share a hard-to-reach spot; sort the shelf by frequency, not by kind.
- **Definition box:** "Storing by frequency of use."
