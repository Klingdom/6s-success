## Hero device (frozen): The Prime Zone
There is a small band of space that is genuinely easy to reach, roughly between your hips and your shoulders, within arm's reach, at the front of the shelf, and measured from where you actually use the thing. Call it the prime zone. It is limited, and it is the most valuable storage you own.
> **The rule (frozen):** The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.
> **The principle (frozen):** Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off.

## The audit reframe (frozen, set as a pull-quote)
> Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn.

## Match reach to frequency (frozen framing)
Sort each home into a rough tier and place it to match:
- **Daily** (you reach for it most): the prime zone, front and center, no bending, no stretching, no moving anything aside.
- **Weekly** (regular but not constant): near and still easy, a shelf up or over, one small reach.
- **Rare or seasonal** (a few times a year): a step further off, high, low, at the back, in another room, or in storage.
The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be.

## Point of use (supporting, frozen tip)
> Store it where you use it, not where it "belongs." The prime spot is measured from your hand at the moment you need the thing, not from a tidy category on a list.

## Outcome vocabulary (frozen)
- **the prime zone** = the small band of easiest-to-reach space, measured from where you use the thing.
- **frequency of use** = how often you actually reach for a thing (daily / weekly / rare).
- Chapter 13 does NOT re-assign homes (that was Chapter 12) and does NOT add labels or visual cues (Chapter 14) or map the whole room (Chapter 15).

## Worked example (the entryway keepers, then scaling up)
Every entryway keeper has a home now; Chapter 13 places each by how often it is used:
- **Keys** (touched every time you leave and return, many times a day): THE prime spot, the hook or tray closest to the door at hand height, the first thing your hand meets.
- **Leash** (daily): also prime, right by the door.
- **Reusable bag** (a few times a week): near but not the prime-est, a hook just to the side.
- **Umbrella** (only on rainy days): a step further off, a stand in the corner or a lower spot, because it does not earn the best reach.
- **Outgoing mail** (brief, but daily on the way out): in the exit path, easy to grab as you go.
Note that the entryway is a small space with mostly frequent things, so its tiering is subtle; the principle earns its keep in fuller spaces. Scale it up in one paragraph: the kitchen cabinet, where the daily mugs and plates go to the front at eye-to-waist height and the once-a-year roasting pan goes up high; the coat closet, where everyday jackets sit at the front and out-of-season coats get pushed to the ends.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> Match the reach to the use. The things your hand wants a dozen times a day should be the easiest to reach; the things you want once a year can afford to be a stretch. Spend your best space on your busiest things, and the room quietly speeds up.

## Definition box (frozen)
> Storing by frequency of use means placing each home by how often you reach for it: daily things in the prime, easy-reach space, weekly things near, and rare or seasonal things a step further off. The more you use it, the closer and easier it goes.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Open one drawer or cabinet you use every day. Find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone names the one thing they use every single day that lives in an annoying spot, and together move it to the easiest reach in that space. The most-used things should be the least trouble to get.
- **6S Tip:** Store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list. Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods, because the prime spot is measured from your hand, not from a category.
- **Common Mistake:** Storing by category instead of by use. Putting all the baking gear together on one high shelf looks tidy, but if you reach for the measuring cup every day and the cake tin once a year, they should not share a hard-to-reach spot. Sort the shelf by frequency, not by kind.
