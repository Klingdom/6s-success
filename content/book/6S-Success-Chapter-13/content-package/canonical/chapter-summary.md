## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 13 is the second chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten. Chapter 12 gave every keeper a home; now the four entryway keepers hang on four identical hooks at the same prime height, each home well built but every one treated the same. Giving a thing a home and giving it the right home are two separate acts, and only the first is done. So the work turns from assigning homes to placing them well. The chapter names its fence plainly: it does not re-assign homes, does not add labels or visual cues, does not map the room, and does not re-open Sort.

The idea the chapter rests on is that not all storage is equal. A small band is genuinely pleasant to use, and a great deal is a nuisance, so the good storage is scarce and, like anything scarce, valuable. Prime space is expensive, so spend it on what you use every day and send the once-a-year things a step further off. That easy-reach band gets a name, the prime zone, measured from where you actually stand, and one rule decides who gets in: the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.

Placing is then a rough sort into three tiers, daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off. Audit the best spots for things that never earned them, and store each thing at its point of use. The keys take the prime spot, the umbrella steps off, and the friction meter takes its second Straighten step.
