# Chapter 13 Key Takeaways: Store by Frequency of Use

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **This chapter continues Straighten, the second S.** It is the second chapter of Part Four. Chapter 12 gave every keeper a home; Chapter 13 keeps the same keepers and the same room and decides where each home should sit, by how often you reach for it.

2. **A good home can still be in the wrong place.** Every entryway keeper has a home now, but they all hang at the same easy height, treated the same, when the keys go out a dozen times a day and the umbrella maybe twice a month. Giving a thing a home and giving it the right home are two separate acts, and only the first was done.

3. **Nothing has to be added or removed.** The whole chapter happens without buying a system or carrying anything to the curb. The room holds exactly what it held this morning; all that changes is that the homes slide to match how often each thing is used.

4. **Not all storage is equal, and the good part is scarce.** A small band is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height; a great deal is a nuisance, up high or at the back. The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce it is valuable and can be spent well or badly.

5. **Prime space is expensive real estate.** The front of the shelf at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street; the high shelf and the deep back are the storage unit across town. Neither is good or bad on its own. The only mistake is putting the wrong tenant in each.

6. **Read the principle as a budget, not a rule of neatness.** Prime space is expensive, so spend it on what you use every day and send the once-a-year things a step further off. You have a fixed amount of good, easy reach, and frequency tells you how to allocate it.

7. **The prime zone is the hero device.** It is the small band of genuinely effortless reach, roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front. It is the most valuable storage you own, and there is never very much of it.

8. **The prime zone is measured from where you stand.** The prime zone for your coffee is at the coffee maker; for your reading glasses, at the chair where you read. It is not a tidy category on a chart, it is the reach measured from the point where you actually use the thing.

9. **The prime zone is small on purpose.** If you try to make everything prime, nothing is, because stuffing the band full just buries the everyday things behind the rare ones. Scarcity is the whole point; it forces a choice, and the choice is what makes the room work.

10. **One rule decides who gets the best spot: the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.** That is the entire rule. When two items want the same good spot, you do not weigh size or tidiness; you ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

11. **Placing is a rough sort into three tiers.** Daily things (the ones your hand goes to most) go in the prime zone, front and center. Weekly things (regular but not constant) go near and still handy, one small reach away. Rare and seasonal things go a step further off, up high, down low, at the back, or in another room. The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be.

12. **Frequency placement is a first draft, not a test you can fail.** Do not agonize over borderline cases. A busy thing stuck in a far spot will nag at you within a day or two, and a rare thing hogging a prime spot just looks silly, so the room tells you where you guessed wrong, and sliding a thing one shelf over to fix it costs nothing.

13. **Audit the best spots you already own.** In most homes the prime zone is quietly occupied by things that never earned it, the waffle iron at the front, the wedding china at eye level. Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now, and if you touch it twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn.

14. **Say the audit without blame.** Nobody placed the waffle iron there as a character flaw. It landed in the good spot and never moved, the way water sits where the ground is lowest. Finding it there is not a verdict on you; it is just useful information about which expensive spot is about to come free.

15. **Store it where you use it, not where it belongs.** We tend to store by where a thing seems to belong, its proper category, but belonging on a list is not the same as being useful to your hand. The point of use beats the tidy category every time, because you live at the point of use and only visit the category.

16. **The entryway keepers get placed by frequency.** Keys take THE prime spot closest to the door; the leash stays prime beside them; the reusable bag moves near, a hook to the side; the umbrella steps a little off the main run into a corner stand; tomorrow's mail rides the exit path. The entryway is small and mostly frequent, so the tiering is gentle, the differences are inches.

17. **The principle earns its keep in fuller rooms.** Scale it up: in the kitchen cabinet the daily mugs and plates come to the front at eye-to-waist height and the once-a-year roasting pan goes up top; in the coat closet this week's jackets hang at the front and the out-of-season coats get slid to the ends. Same principle, more room to breathe.

18. **The friction meter takes its second Straighten step.** Nothing left the house, yet the needle steps a little further toward calm, because reaching got cheaper for the things you reach for most. The keys now sit where your hand already goes, so their toll drops to nearly nothing twelve times over, while the umbrella pays its higher toll only on the rare rainy day.

19. **The step is real, and the goal is still ahead.** Chapter 12's position is now the faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle, and the Sort-complete milestone sits further back still. The goal crosshair on the calm side is still a fair way off, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain.

20. **A room arranged by frequency pays you back on a schedule.** You did not add storage; you let how often your hand moves spend your scarce, valuable reach on your behalf. Every time you grab the keys without thinking, you collect a little of what you saved, again tomorrow and the day after. Next, Chapter 14 makes each home obvious at a glance, because the placement is right but still silent.
