# Chapter 13 Calls to Action: Store by Frequency of Use

This is the second chapter of Part Four, and it continues Straighten, the second S. Its strongest CTA is the smallest possible placement move: open one everyday drawer and trade the wasted best spot for a daily thing that is currently awkward to reach. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: wrong placement is a fixable first draft, not a personal failing, and the fix costs nothing but a swap.

## Primary CTA
Make one swap. "Open one drawer or cabinet you use every day, find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Every keeper has a home now, but not every home is in the best spot. Chapter 13 places each home by how often you reach for it: the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most. Read it free online, then Chapter 14 makes each home clear at a glance with a label, an outline, or a simple visual cue."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Open one drawer or cabinet you use every day. Find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone names the one thing they use every single day that lives in an annoying spot, and together move it to the easiest reach in that space. The most-used things should be the least trouble to get.
- **6S Tip:** Store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list. Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods, because the prime spot is measured from your hand, not from a category.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is a small placement move, not a burst of effort: one swap, and let the room speed up on its own. Lead with "make one swap," which is concrete and provable in a minute.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and anti-shame. Wrong placement is a fixable first draft, not a character flaw; nobody put the waffle iron in the good spot on purpose, it just landed and never moved.
- The rule (the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most), the principle (prime space is expensive), and the audit reframe (paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn) are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is matching each home to how often your hand goes to it.
- Scope note: this places homes by frequency; it does not re-assign homes (Chapter 12), add labels or visual cues (Chapter 14), or map the whole room (Chapter 15). The homes are settled; Sort is not re-opened.
