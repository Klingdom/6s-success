# Chapter 13 Quotes: Store by Frequency of Use

Pull quotes lifted directly from the manuscript and final HTML, ready for quote cards, social posts, and graphics. Every line below was grep-verified as present in chapter_13_manuscript.md and chapter_13_final.html. All are verbatim or lightly trimmed. Nothing here is invented.

## Verbatim lines (book voice)

1. "But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place."

2. "The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable, and like anything valuable, it can be spent well or spent badly."

3. "Scarcity is the whole point; it forces a choice, and the choice is what makes the room work."

4. "That is the entire rule, and it is worth committing to memory, because it quietly settles a hundred small arguments about where things go."

5. "the more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off."

6. "Frequency placement is not a test you can fail; it is a first draft that your own hand keeps editing, week after week, until each thing lands where it belongs."

7. "The point of use beats the tidy category every time, because you live at the point of use and only visit the category."

8. "A room arranged by frequency of use pays you back on a schedule, quietly, in seconds you never notice losing anymore."

## The principle (frozen, hero)

"Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

## The rule (frozen)

"The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most."

## The audit reframe (frozen pull-quote)

"Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."

## Point of use (frozen tip)

"Store it where you use it, not where it \"belongs.\" The prime spot is measured from your hand at the moment you need the thing, not from a tidy category on a list."

## Definition box (canonical)

"Storing by frequency of use means placing each home by how often you reach for it: daily things in the prime, easy-reach space, weekly things near, and rare or seasonal things a step further off. The more you use it, the closer and easier it goes."

## One Idea to Keep (canonical close)

"Match the reach to the use. The things your hand wants a dozen times a day should be the easiest to reach; the things you want once a year can afford to be a stretch. Spend your best space on your busiest things, and the room quietly speeds up."

## Quick Win (canonical)

"Open one drawer or cabinet you use every day. Find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster."

## 6S Tip (canonical)

"Store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list. Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods, because the prime spot is measured from your hand, not from a category."

## Common Mistake (canonical)

"Storing by category instead of by use. Putting all the baking gear together on one high shelf looks tidy, but if you reach for the measuring cup every day and the cake tin once a year, they should not share a hard-to-reach spot. Sort the shelf by frequency, not by kind."

## Family Challenge (canonical)

"Everyone names the one thing they use every single day that lives in an annoying spot, and together move it to the easiest reach in that space. The most-used things should be the least trouble to get."

## The friction-meter caption (for graphics)

"The needle steps again, the second step of Straighten. Nothing new was added or removed; the homes just moved to match how often you use them, so the busiest things became the easiest to reach. The goal is still ahead, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain."
