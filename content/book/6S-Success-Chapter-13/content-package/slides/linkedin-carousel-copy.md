# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## Store by Frequency of Use

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 13. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36, ghost needle #8A8073. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader. Footer strip: DAILY · PRIME  WEEKLY · NEAR  RARE · FAR.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**Every keeper has a home. So why is the room still slow?**

Because a good home can still be in the wrong place.

Swipe for the second Straighten step, and the one rule that decides where each thing goes.

[VISUAL] The settled door: every keeper on its hook, the keys, leash, reusable bag, and umbrella all at the same easy arm's height, the mail in a tray below. A faint ghost note reads "same hook · same height · same reach." A quiet question floats above: "which of these do you actually reach for most?" Warm paper, slate chapter label.

---

### Slide 2

**Keys a dozen times a day. Umbrella twice a month. Same hook.**

Every home at the door gets the same easy arm's reach, as though you needed everything the same amount.

You do not.

[VISUAL] The four keepers in a row at one height. A green note by the keys, "used a dozen times a day"; a honey note by the umbrella, "used twice a month." Both hang at the same reach. No spark; nothing here is a bad home, only a mismatched one.

---

### Slide 3

**Your best storage is scarce.**

A small band is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height. A great deal is a nuisance, up high or at the back.

The good part is small. That is the fact that changes everything.

[VISUAL] A shelf in cross-section: a slim green band at the front at hand height, glowing; the high shelf and the deep back drawn larger and greyed in soft. A tag: "the pleasant part is small."

---

### Slide 4

**Prime space is expensive.**

Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off.

Read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the principle set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A single green calm-dot beside a small tag: "read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness." No sparks.

---

### Slide 5

**The corner shop, or the storage unit across town.**

The front of the shelf at hand height is expensive, and worth it. The high shelf and the deep back are cheap, and a bother to visit.

Neither is good or bad. The only mistake is the wrong tenant in each.

[VISUAL] Two property tags: a green storefront, "THE CORNER SHOP · front, hand height, expensive"; a soft crate, "THE STORAGE UNIT · high, deep back, cheap." A swap arrow moves the everyday mug forward and a novelty teapot back. Slate rule: "the only mistake is the wrong tenant in each."

---

### Slide 6

**Name the good, easy reach: the prime zone.**

Hips to shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front. Measured from where you stand.

The most valuable storage you own, and there is never very much of it.

[VISUAL] A standing figure beside a shelf in cross-section. A green band spans hip to shoulder at the front, "THE PRIME ZONE · easy reach." Above, "a stretch"; below, "a bend"; behind the front row, in soft and ghost tones, "deep at the back · hard to reach." An arm's-length arc from the body.

---

### Slide 7

**The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.**

That is the entire rule.

When two items want the same good spot, you ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the rule set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. A single green calm-dot beside a small tag: "the busier hand wins." No sparks.

---

### Slide 8

**Daily to the prime zone. Weekly near. Rare a step further off.**

A rough sort, not a spreadsheet. Most things fall into one pile at a glance.

The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be.

[VISUAL] A kitchen cabinet in cross-section, three shelves. The front middle shelf is a green prime band, "DAILY · PRIME," holding the mugs and plates. A honey shelf to the side, "WEEKLY · NEAR," holds the mixing bowl. The high shelf in soft, "RARE · FAR," holds the once-a-year roasting pan.

---

### Slide 9

**A busy thing in a far spot will nag at you within a day.**

And a rare thing hogging a prime spot just looks silly. The room tells you where you guessed wrong.

Frequency placement is a first draft, not a test you can fail. Sliding a thing one shelf over costs nothing.

[VISUAL] A single shelf with one item mid-slide: a green arrow moves a daily mug from a far corner into the front prime band; a soft arrow slides a rare platter one shelf up. A gentle tag: "the room edits your first draft for you." Patient, never scolding.

---

### Slide 10

**Premium rent on a spot it did not earn.**

Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn.

No blame owed. It just landed there and never moved, the way water sits where the ground is lowest.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel, the audit reframe set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Beneath, the waffle iron at the front of a cabinet with a small honey "premium rent" tag. No verdict colours; this is information, not blame.

---

### Slide 11

**Store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list.**

Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods.

The point of use beats the tidy category every time, because you live at the point of use and only visit the category.

[VISUAL] A coffee mug drawn twice: greyed with the pantry goods "where it belongs," and green and lit by the coffee maker "where you use it." A green arrow moves it to the point of use. The 6S Tip on a slate-bordered callout beneath.

---

**The busiest things became the easiest to reach.**

Nothing left the house. The homes just moved to match how often you use them, so the room quietly speeds up. The friction meter takes its second Straighten step, and the goal is still ahead.
