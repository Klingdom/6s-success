## 1. Every home, the same prime reach

Every keeper at the door has a home now. Keys, leash, bag, umbrella, and tomorrow's mail, each on its own hook or tray. Nothing is loose.

And every place is treated exactly the same.

You grab the keys a dozen times a day. You reach for the umbrella maybe twice a month. Yet both hang at the same height, at the same easy arm's length, as though you needed them the same amount.

Giving each keeper a home was the right first move. But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place. That is the whole job of this chapter, and it adds nothing and removes nothing.

## 2. Your best storage is scarce

Most of us have never thought of our own shelves this way.

In any room, a small amount of storage is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height. A great deal of it is a nuisance, up high or at the back. The pleasant part is small.

That is the fact that changes everything. The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable, and like anything valuable, it can be spent well or spent badly.

Think of it as real estate. The front of the shelf at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street. The high shelf is the storage unit across town.

The only mistake is putting the wrong tenant in each.

## 3. Read it as a budget

Here is the line the whole chapter rests on.

Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off.

Read that as a budget, not a rule of neatness. You have a fixed amount of good, easy reach, and far more of the awkward kind. Frequency tells you how to allocate it.

The things your hand wants many times a day belong in the expensive spots, earning their keep every time you reach. The things you want twice a year can sit in the cheap seats, because the cost of the occasional stretch is tiny, paid once in a while.

## 4. The prime zone

Let me give the good, easy reach a name, because it is easier to protect something once you can point at it.

There is a small band of space in front of you that is genuinely effortless to use. Roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front rather than the back. Reach into it and you take a thing without bending, stretching, or moving anything aside.

Call it the prime zone. It is the most valuable storage you own, and there is never very much of it.

It is measured from where you stand, not from a chart. The prime zone for your coffee is at the coffee maker.

## 5. One rule for the best spot

If the prime zone is scarce, what earns a place in it? Not size, not category, not how nice a thing looks lined up with its cousins. Only one thing earns it.

The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.

That is the entire rule, and it quietly settles a hundred small arguments about where things go. When two items want the same good spot, you do not weigh their size or their tidiness. You ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

Store a thing by how often you use it, and store it where you use it.

## 6. Three loose piles

Placing things is refreshingly rough. You do not need a spreadsheet. You need three loose piles, and most things fall into one at a glance.

Everyday things · the coffee, the pans, the coat you actually wear. These go in the prime zone, front and center.

Regular but not constant · the good scissors, the mixing bowl, the second jacket. These go near and still handy, one small reach away.

Rare and seasonal · the roasting pan, the holiday box, the good umbrella in a dry month. These step a little further off, up high, at the back, in another room.

The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be.

## 7. Not a test you can fail

Do not agonize over the borderline cases.

If a thing sits somewhere between weekly and rare, put it near and watch what happens. The items you place wrong tend to announce themselves quickly. A busy thing stuck in a far spot will nag at you within a day or two. A rare thing hogging a prime spot just sits there looking increasingly silly.

The room tells you where you guessed wrong, and sliding a thing one shelf over to fix it costs nothing.

Frequency placement is not a test you can fail. It is a first draft that your own hand keeps editing, week after week, until each thing lands where it belongs.

## 8. Premium rent on a spot it did not earn

Before you place anything, look at what is already sitting in your best spots. In most homes the prime zone is quietly occupied by things that never earned it. The waffle iron at the front. The wedding china at eye level. The gadget opened twice.

Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn.

Say that without any blame. Nobody placed the waffle iron there as a character flaw. It landed there and never moved, the way water sits where the ground is lowest. Finding it is not a verdict, just useful information about which spot is about to come free.

## 9. Store it where you use it

We tend to store by where a thing seems to belong, its proper category, its natural shelf with its own kind. But belonging on a list is not the same as being useful to your hand.

Store it where you use it, not where it belongs on a list.

Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen with the other pantry goods, because the prime spot is measured from your hand, not from a category. When the tidy list and the point of use disagree, let the hand win. You live at the point of use and only visit the category.

## 10. Nothing left, and the room sped up

Nothing left the house today, and the room still got easier to use.

The keys and the umbrella used to cost you the same small effort, and the keys made you pay it a dozen times a day. Now the keys sit exactly where your hand already goes, so that toll drops to nearly nothing, twelve times over, while the umbrella pays its slightly higher toll only on the rare rainy day.

You did not add storage or buy a system. You let how often your hand moves spend your scarce, valuable reach on your behalf.
