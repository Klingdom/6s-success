# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (the prime zone and the one rule), then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, the small idea that finally made my kitchen cabinet make sense, and then I want to hear about the thing hogging the best spot in your house.

For a long time I stored things by category. All the baking gear on one shelf, all the mugs together, everything with its own kind. It looked tidy. It also meant I reached past a novelty teapot I have poured from once to get to the mug I drink from every single morning.

Here is what actually changed it. There is a small band of space that is genuinely easy to reach, roughly hips to shoulders, at the front, within an arm's length. It is the best storage you own and there is never much of it. So one rule decides who gets in. The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.

That flipped it for me. You stop sorting by kind and start sorting by how often your hand actually goes to a thing. Daily things come to the front at hand height. Weekly things go near, one small reach up or over. Rare and seasonal things step a little further off, up high or at the back, where the once-in-a-while cost of reaching them barely counts.

And the audit reframe is the part I keep repeating to myself. Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn. Said without blame, because nobody put the waffle iron front and center on purpose. It just landed there and never moved.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

What is the thing currently sitting in your easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never actually use? And what daily thing are you reaching past it to get? If you swapped the two right now, what would move to the front?

I will go first in the comments with my teapot, which spent years in the good seat doing precisely nothing. 😄
