# Editorial Review: Chapter 13 Content Package

Reviewed against the checklist: repetition, AI-sounding phrasing, unsupported claims, hooks, CTAs, source alignment, overlong social posts, tone, formatting. This review reports only checks actually run; all counts below were measured, and every cited noun/quote was grep-verified in the manuscript or final HTML before citing.

## Overall verdict
Publish-ready after author sign-off, with one standing caveat: the chapter is a draft (authored in the book voice, no source file supplied). The package holds the warm, anti-shame voice across all channels, the facts trace cleanly to the manuscript and HTML, and the hooks are concrete (the daily keys and the twice-a-month umbrella on identical hooks, the mug behind a novelty teapot, the audit reframe). Findings below are minor.

## Repetition
- Cross-asset repetition of the spine (the best-spot rule, prime space is expensive, the audit reframe, daily/weekly/rare) is by design.
- The hero vocabulary runs high in the manuscript ("prime," "reach," "space," "spot," "frequency"), because it is the subject of the chapter, not a tic. When excerpting, vary the surrounding language so a single post does not stack the same three words.

## AI-sounding phrasing
- None found. The writing leans on concrete images (the corner shop, the storage unit across town, the mug and the teapot) rather than generic filler.
- Widened dash scan clean (em, en, and spaced-hyphen " - "). No hype words.

## Unsupported claims
- No invented statistics, names, or dates. The Straighten origin (store by frequency, at the point of use) is referenced lightly, not overclaimed.
- Testimonials in pdf-ebook/back-cover-copy.md are clearly marked placeholders.

## Hooks
- Strong and specific: identical hooks for wildly different things ("which of these do you actually reach for most?"), the mug parked behind the novelty teapot, and "paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."

## CTAs
- Every social post carries a soft CTA to the chapter or the online book. The hero action (swap one rarely-used thing out of an easy-reach spot for a daily thing that is currently awkward) is concrete and free. Rotate wording before publishing; insert the live URL for "the online book."

## Source alignment
- Strong. The best-spot rule, the prime zone, the daily/weekly/rare tiers, the entryway pass, and the second-Straighten-step friction move all match the manuscript and HTML.

## Overlong social posts (measured)
- X: a 16-post thread whose header states 16, all posts at or under 280 characters INCLUDING the "N/" number line (longest 272); plus 10 short posts (longest 273). Pass.
- LinkedIn: 10 posts, each under 150 words (longest 136). Pass.
- Facebook longform: 391 words in the post body (within the 300 to 450 target). Not a flag.

## Tone
- Correct platform-fit; the light, anti-shame, arranging-not-judging register holds everywhere.

## Formatting
- Clean Markdown. Both JSON files parse; the publishable HTML JSON-LD validates. CSV has 7 columns and 56 data rows. The final HTML has 4 balanced SVG figures.

## Recommended edits before publishing
1. Author approval of the authored chapter first.
2. Insert the real chapter URL for "the online book."
3. Replace author / publisher / URL / date / og:image placeholders in the schema JSON, PDF/ebook, and chapter-13-publishable.html.
4. Rotate the LinkedIn and X CTA wording.
5. Replace or remove the back-cover testimonial placeholders.
6. Lead the visual production with the Prime Zone reach diagram and the kitchen-cabinet tiering graphic.
