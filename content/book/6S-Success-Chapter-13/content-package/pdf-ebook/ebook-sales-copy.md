# 6S Success: Home Edition (Sales Page Copy)

## Every keeper has a home now, and every home gets the same prime reach.

Stand at the door one more time. Every keeper from the last chapter has a home. The keys hang on a hook. The leash hangs beside them, the reusable bag on the next hook over, the umbrella on the one after that, and tomorrow's mail waits in a small tray below. Nothing is loose. Every thing has a place. And every place is treated exactly the same.

That last part is the whole of this chapter. You grab the keys a dozen times a day, every coming and going. You reach for the umbrella maybe twice a month, and only when the sky turns. Yet both hang at the same height, at the same easy arm's length, as though you needed them the same amount. You do not. "But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place."

## Welcome to the second step of Straighten.

Chapter 13 is the second chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten. Chapter 12 gave every keeper a home; this chapter keeps the same keepers and the same room and decides where each home should sit, by how often you reach for it. Giving a thing a home and giving it the right home are two separate acts, and only the first is done. So the work turns from assigning homes to placing them well.

Nothing has to be added or removed. The room holds exactly what it held this morning. All that changes is that the homes slide to match how often each thing is used, and somehow the room gets easier.

## Your best storage is scarce, so spend it well.

Here is the idea the rest of the chapter rests on. Not all storage is equal. A small band is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height, and a great deal is a nuisance, up high or down at the back. "The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable, and like anything valuable, it can be spent well or spent badly."

Think of it as real estate. The front of the shelf at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street, expensive and worth it. The high shelf and the deep back are the storage unit across town, cheap and a bother to visit. Neither is good or bad on its own. The only mistake is putting the wrong tenant in each. Read the principle as a budget, not a rule of neatness: "Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

## Name the prime zone, and one rule sorts the room.

Give the good, easy reach a name, because it is easier to protect something once you can point at it. There is a small band of space in front of you that is genuinely effortless to use, roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front of whatever holds it. Call it the prime zone. It is the most valuable storage you own, and there is never very much of it. It is measured from where you stand when you use the thing, and it is small on purpose. "Scarcity is the whole point; it forces a choice, and the choice is what makes the room work."

Only one thing earns a place in that scarce band: "The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most." When two items want the same good spot, you do not weigh their size or their tidiness. You ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

## What you get in this chapter

- The prime zone, named and made concrete · the small band of genuinely easy reach, measured from where you actually use a thing
- The reframe that changes how you see your own shelves · easy-reach space is scarce, valuable real estate, spent well or badly
- The one rule that settles a hundred small arguments about where things go · the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most
- A rough sort into three tiers · "the more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off."
- The anti-shame audit of your best spots, and the point-of-use fix · "The point of use beats the tidy category every time, because you live at the point of use and only visit the category."
- The friction meter's second Straighten step, marked honestly and never oversold

## No test you can fail, just a first draft your hand keeps editing.

Do not agonize over the borderline cases. Put a thing near and watch what happens. A busy thing stuck in a far spot will nag at you within a day or two, and a rare thing hogging a prime spot just sits there looking increasingly silly. The room tells you where you guessed wrong, and sliding a thing one shelf over to fix it costs nothing. "Frequency placement is not a test you can fail; it is a first draft that your own hand keeps editing, week after week, until each thing lands where it belongs."

And say the audit without any blame in it, because none is owed. Nobody placed the waffle iron in the good spot as a character flaw. It landed there the day it arrived and simply never moved, the way water sits wherever the ground is lowest. Finding it there is not a verdict on you; it is just information, and useful information, because it tells you exactly which expensive spot is about to come free.

## The needle steps again.

Nothing leaves the house in this chapter. Not one thing is carried to the curb. And still the friction meter steps again, a little further toward the calm side, because reaching got cheaper for the things you reach for most. The keys now sit exactly where your hand already goes, so that toll drops to nearly nothing, twelve times over, while the umbrella pays its slightly higher toll only on the rare day it rains. That saved effort is real friction removed, and this is the second step of Straighten. The step is real, and the goal is still ahead, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain.

"A room arranged by frequency of use pays you back on a schedule, quietly, in seconds you never notice losing anymore." Open one drawer or cabinet you use every day, find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster.
