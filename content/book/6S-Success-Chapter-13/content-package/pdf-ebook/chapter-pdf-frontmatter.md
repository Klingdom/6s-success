# Store by Frequency of Use

**Part Four, Straighten**
Chapter 13 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

Subtitle · Every keeper has a home; now place the homes by how often you reach for them. Prime space is expensive, so spend it on what you use most and send the rare things a step further off.

## Chapter descriptor

The second chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten, the second S. Chapter 12 gave every keeper a home; now four keepers hang on four identical hooks at the same easy height, each home well built but every one treated the same, when the keys go out a dozen times a day and the umbrella maybe twice a month. The work turns from assigning homes to placing them well, and nothing has to leave the house.

## How to read this chapter

This is the second chapter of Part Four, Straighten, and it picks up exactly where the last one left off, with every keeper in a home but every home treated the same. It is not about re-deciding whether a thing gets a home; you made those calls in Chapter 12, and they are done. It is about deciding where each home should sit, by a single question we have not asked yet, how often does your hand actually go to this thing?

Read it through once for the idea, then go to your own entryway, cabinet, or closet and place the homes by frequency. Give the good, easy reach a name, the prime zone, the small band from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front, measured from where you stand when you use the thing. Then sort each home into a rough tier and place it to match: daily things to the prime zone, front and center; weekly things near, one small reach away; rare and seasonal things a step further off, up high, down low, at the back. The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Do not agonize over the borderline cases; put a thing near and let the room tell you where you guessed wrong, because sliding it one shelf over costs nothing.

Nothing here re-assigns homes; the keepers are settled and stay. Nothing here adds labels, outlines, or visual cues; that is the next chapter, Chapter 14, and we do not touch it today. Nothing here maps how a whole room fits together; that is Chapter 15. Nothing re-opens Sort. Today has one narrow job: put each home where the reaching is cheapest for the things you reach for most.

If you only do one thing: open one drawer or cabinet you use every day, find the thing in the easiest-to-reach spot that you almost never use, and trade it with a daily thing that is currently awkward to get to. One swap, and the space is faster.
