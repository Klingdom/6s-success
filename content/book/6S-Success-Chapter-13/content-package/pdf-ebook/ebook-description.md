# 6S Success: Home Edition (Ebook Description)

You finished the last chapter with every keeper in a home, and the room looked settled. Then you noticed something. At the door, four keepers hang on four identical hooks at the same easy height, with tomorrow's mail in a tray below, and every one is treated the same, when the keys go out a dozen times a day and the umbrella maybe twice a month. Giving a thing a home and giving it the right home are two separate acts, and only the first was done. Chapter 13 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is that quiet second half.

"Store by Frequency of Use" is the second chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten, the second of the six S's. Chapter 12 assigned the homes; this chapter keeps the same keepers and the same room and decides where each home should sit, by how often you reach for it. So the work turns from assigning homes to placing them well. The chapter names its fence plainly: it does not re-assign homes, does not add labels or visual cues, does not map the whole room, and does not re-open Sort. Today has one narrow task, put each home where the reaching is cheapest for the things you reach for most. "But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place."

The idea the rest of the chapter rests on is that not all storage is equal. A small band is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height, and a great deal is a nuisance, up high or at the back. "The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable, and like anything valuable, it can be spent well or spent badly." Think of it as real estate: the front of the shelf at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street, expensive and worth it; the high shelf and the deep back are the storage unit across town, cheap and a bother to visit. The only mistake is putting the wrong tenant in each. Read the principle as a budget, not a rule of neatness: "Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

Then the chapter gives the good, easy reach a name, the prime zone. It is a small band of space in front of you, roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front of whatever holds it, and it is the most valuable storage you own. Two things make it easy to misjudge. It is measured from where you stand when you use the thing, not from a tidy category on a chart, so the prime zone for your coffee is at the coffee maker. And it is small on purpose, because if you try to make everything prime, nothing is. "Scarcity is the whole point; it forces a choice, and the choice is what makes the room work." Only one thing earns a place in that scarce band: "The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most."

Placing is then a rough sort into three loose tiers. The everyday things your hand goes to most go in the prime zone, front and center. The regular but not constant things go near and still handy, one small reach away. The rare and the seasonal go a step further off, up high, down low, at the back, in another room. "the more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off." Do not agonize over the borderline cases: "Frequency placement is not a test you can fail; it is a first draft that your own hand keeps editing, week after week, until each thing lands where it belongs."

Before placing anything, the chapter runs an audit that takes all the blame off. In most homes the prime zone is quietly occupied by things that never earned it, the waffle iron at the front, the wedding china at eye level. "Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn." Say it without blame, because none is owed; nobody placed the waffle iron there as a character flaw, it just landed and never moved, the way water sits where the ground is lowest. And the other half of the audit corrects a common habit: "Store it where you use it, not where it \"belongs.\" The prime spot is measured from your hand at the moment you need the thing, not from a tidy category on a list." When the two disagree, let the hand win, because "The point of use beats the tidy category every time, because you live at the point of use and only visit the category."

Then the chapter walks the entryway keepers into placements by frequency. The keys take THE prime spot, the hook or tray closest to the door at hand height, the first thing your hand meets, because nothing here is touched more. The leash stays right up front beside the keys, out and back with the dog morning and evening. The reusable bag moves near rather than prime, a hook just to the side, because you take it a few times a week. The umbrella steps the furthest off, into a stand in the corner or onto a lower hook, because it works hard on a rainy day and does nothing on the many dry ones. Tomorrow's mail wants the exit path, brief but constant, so it rides out with you. The entryway is small and mostly frequent, so the tiering is gentle and the differences are inches, so the chapter scales the principle up to the kitchen cabinet, where the daily mugs come to the front at eye-to-waist height and the once-a-year roasting pan goes up top, and the coat closet, where this week's jackets hang at the front and the out-of-season coats get slid to the ends.

Nothing leaves the house in this chapter, yet the friction meter takes its second Straighten step, a little further toward calm, because reaching got cheaper for the things you reach for most. Chapter 12's position becomes the faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle, the Sort-complete milestone sits further back, and the goal crosshair on the calm side is still ahead, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain. "A room arranged by frequency of use pays you back on a schedule, quietly, in seconds you never notice losing anymore." The homes are assigned and now well placed, but a good spot is obvious only to the person who set it up, and that is where Chapter 14 goes next.
