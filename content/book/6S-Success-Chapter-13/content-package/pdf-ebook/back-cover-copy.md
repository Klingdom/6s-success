# Back Cover Copy: Store by Frequency of Use

## Back-cover text

Every keeper has a home now. At the door, the keys, the leash, the reusable bag, and the umbrella hang on four identical hooks at the same easy height, with tomorrow's mail in a tray below. Every one is treated the same, when the keys go out a dozen times a day and the umbrella maybe twice a month. "But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place."

Chapter 13 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the second chapter of Part Four, and it keeps working inside Straighten, the second of the six S's. Chapter 12 assigned the homes; this chapter decides where each one should sit, by how often you reach for it. Nothing has to be added or removed. Not all storage is equal: "The best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable, and like anything valuable, it can be spent well or spent badly." So read the principle as a budget: "Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

The chapter names the good, easy reach the prime zone, the small band from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front, measured from where you stand. One rule decides who gets in: "The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most." Placing is a rough sort into three tiers, daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off, and it is forgiving: "Frequency placement is not a test you can fail; it is a first draft that your own hand keeps editing, week after week, until each thing lands where it belongs." Audit your best spots without blame: "Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."

Nothing leaves the house, yet the friction meter takes its second Straighten step toward calm, because reaching got cheaper for the things you reach for most. The step is real, and the goal is still ahead, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain. "A room arranged by frequency of use pays you back on a schedule, quietly, in seconds you never notice losing anymore." Open one drawer you use every day, trade the wasted best spot for a daily thing that is currently awkward to reach, and watch the space get faster.
