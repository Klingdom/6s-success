# X Thread: Store by Frequency of Use

Chapter 13 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. One thread, 16 posts. Each post is at or under 280 characters, counting its number line, and stands on its own. Plain "1/" numbering on its own line.

---

1/
Stand at the door again. Every keeper has a home now. Keys, leash, bag, umbrella, all on identical hooks at the same easy height. But the keys go out a dozen times a day and the umbrella twice a month. Chapter 13 of 6S Success: Home Edition places each home by use.

(approx. 260 chars)

---

2/
Giving each thing a home was the right first move, and it holds. But the umbrella and the keys are paying the same rent for very different work.

"But a home can be well built and still be in the wrong place."

(approx. 205 chars)

---

3/
Not all storage is equal. A small band of it is genuinely pleasant to reach, at hand height, front of the shelf, no bending. Most of it is a nuisance: high up, deep at the back. The pleasant part is scarce, and scarce things are valuable.

(approx. 235 chars)

---

4/
The idea the whole chapter rests on:

"Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

Read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness.

(approx. 200 chars)

---

5/
Name the good reach so you can protect it. There is a small band in front of you, roughly hip to shoulder, within arm's length, at the front, that you use without bending, stretching, or moving anything aside. Call it the prime zone. There is never much of it.

(approx. 258 chars)

---

6/
Two things about the prime zone. It is measured from where you stand when you use the thing, not from a category on a chart. And it is small on purpose. Try to make everything prime and nothing is, because the everyday things get buried behind the rare ones again.

(approx. 262 chars)

---

7/
So what earns a place in that scarce band? Not size, not category, not how nice it looks lined up with its cousins. Only one thing:

"The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most."

That is the entire rule.

(approx. 210 chars)

---

8/
Placing things is then just rough sorting into three piles. Daily: coffee, the pans you cook with, the coat you actually wear. Weekly: good scissors, the mixing bowl. Rare or seasonal: the roasting pan, the holiday box, the umbrella in a dry month.

(approx. 245 chars)

---

9/
The whole method fits in a line:

"the more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off."

Notice how often your hand moves, and let that decide the address.

(approx. 238 chars)

---

10/
Before placing anything, look at what already sits in your best spots. In most homes the prime zone is quietly occupied by things that never earned it: the waffle iron at the front, the wedding china at eye level, the gadget you have opened twice.

(approx. 245 chars)

---

11/
The audit is one honest question, asked without any blame:

"Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."

(approx. 218 chars)

---

12/
And a small correction to how we store. We tend to put a thing where it seems to belong, its proper shelf with its own kind.

"Store it where you use it, not where it "belongs.""

Coffee lives by the coffee maker, not across the kitchen.

(approx. 232 chars)

---

13/
Back to the door. Keys are touched most, so they take THE prime spot, the hook closest to the door at hand height, the first thing your hand meets. The leash goes out just as often, so it stays right beside them. Two everyday things, two of the best spots.

(approx. 253 chars)

---

14/
The bag you take a few times a week, so it moves near rather than prime, a hook just to the side. The umbrella works hard on a rainy day and does nothing on the dry ones, so it steps off to a stand in the corner. The mail rides the exit path in your hand.

(approx. 251 chars)

---

15/
The friction meter moves again, and nothing left the house for it to move. The homes just slid to match how often each thing is used, so the busiest things became the easiest to reach. The needle steps once more, the second step of Straighten. The goal is still ahead.

(approx. 265 chars)
