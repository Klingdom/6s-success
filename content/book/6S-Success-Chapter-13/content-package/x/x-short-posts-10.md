# X Short Posts: Store by Frequency of Use

Chapter 13 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters, counting its number line, and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Chapter 13 of 6S Success: Home Edition continues Straighten. Every keeper has a home now, but not every home is in the best spot. This chapter places each one by how often you reach for it: daily things close, rare things a step further off. Free online.

(approx. 249 chars)

---

2/
"The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most."

That is the whole rule of storing by frequency of use. When two things want the same good spot, you do not weigh their size or their tidiness. You ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

(approx. 262 chars)

---

3/
"Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off."

Not all storage is equal. A small band of it is genuinely easy to reach, and it is scarce. Read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness.

(approx. 253 chars)

---

4/
There is a small band of space, roughly hip to shoulder, within arm's length, at the front, that you use without bending or stretching. Call it the prime zone. It is the most valuable storage you own, and there is never much of it, so the rule decides who gets in.

(approx. 262 chars)

---

5/
"Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."

Say it without blame. Nobody put the waffle iron there on purpose. It just landed and never moved.

(approx. 257 chars)

---

6/
Three rough piles, and most things fall into one at a glance:

"the more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off."

That is the whole method.

(approx. 227 chars)

---

7/
"Store it where you use it, not where it "belongs.""

We tend to store a thing by its proper category, its natural shelf with its own kind. But belonging on a list is not the same as being useful to your hand. When the two disagree, let the hand win.

(approx. 247 chars)

---

8/
Storing by category instead of by use is the common miss. All the baking gear on one high shelf looks tidy, but if you reach for the measuring cup every day and the cake tin once a year, they should not share a hard-to-reach spot. Sort by frequency, not by kind.

(approx. 259 chars)

---

9/
Frequency placement is not a test you can fail. Put a borderline thing near and watch what happens. A busy thing stuck far off will nag within a day; a rare thing hogging a prime spot just sits there looking silly. The room tells you where you guessed wrong.

(approx. 254 chars)

---

10/
"Spend your best space on your busiest things, and the room quietly speeds up."

Match the reach to the use. You did not add storage or buy a system; you let how often your hand moves spend your scarce reach for you. Next · Chapter 14 makes each home clear at a glance.

(approx. 267 chars)
