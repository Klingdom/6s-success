# Infographic Spec: The Prime Zone

**Chapter 13, "Store by Frequency of Use"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: your
easy-reach space is scarce, so the best spot goes to the thing you reach for most. The concept
pairs the hero **Prime Zone** band (the small green easy-reach band on a shelf cross-section) with
the **daily / weekly / rare tiering** beneath, so the viewer sees both the scarce band that is
worth protecting and the three tiers that fill it, and sees at once that use, not category, sets
the address.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_13_final.html`. A green band and
green calm-dots mark the prime, easy-reach spots; slate carries the rule, the tiers, and the far
zones; the single small friction-spark is held back and used sparingly, if at all. The prime zone
is small on purpose, and the far zones are cheaper, not bad.

## Layout notes

- The prime band is the loudest element on the page. Draw it as the small, precious green band at
  the front of the shelf, clearly less than half the shelf, so the eye reads scarcity at once: the
  easy reach is limited, and the rule decides who gets in.
- Hold the color discipline: green means the prime, easy-reach band and the daily tier, slate
  carries the rule, the far zones, and the weekly and rare tiers, and honey stays to small accents
  only. The friction-spark is held back here; use it sparingly or not at all, because nothing in
  this image is a snag.
- The three tiers are exactly daily, weekly, and rare, each mapped to prime, near, and a step
  further off. Keep the mapping one to one and plain: daily to prime, weekly to near, rare to far.
- This image places each home by how often a thing is used and names the scarce prime band. Do NOT
  re-assign homes or reopen that decision (that was Chapter 12). Do NOT add a label, an outline, or
  color-coding to any home as a visual cue (Chapter 14). Do NOT map or plan a whole room's homes at
  once (Chapter 15). Do NOT reopen Sort.
- Anti-shame: a thing sitting in the wrong spot is a placement to slide over, not a verdict on the
  person. The far zones are cheaper, not bad, and the rare pan up top is resting, not banished.
  Keep it calm and non-coercive, no guilt cues, no pressure framing, and no force framing anywhere.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the rule set as if
  jotted rather than typeset.
