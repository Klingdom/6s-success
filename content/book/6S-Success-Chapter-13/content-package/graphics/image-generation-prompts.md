# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 13

**"Store by Frequency of Use"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Four · Straighten*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across all
five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single warm
red `#CB4B36` reserved for rare small accents only. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy
depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the settled entryway, and later one kitchen cabinet. Across Chapter
12, Straighten gave every keeper one clear home: the keys, the dog's leash, the reusable shopping
bag, the folding umbrella, and tomorrow's mail. Those homes are done and good. This chapter, the
second of Part Four, does one narrow thing: it places each home by how often your hand goes to it,
so the busiest things get the easiest reach and the rare things step a little further off. Nothing
is re-decided or removed, no home is re-assigned (that was Chapter 12), no label or visual cue is
added to any home (Chapter 14), and no whole room is mapped at once (Chapter 15). Sort stays
closed. Keep the tone calm and kind: a thing sitting in the wrong spot is a placement to slide
over, not a fault in the reader, and the fix is arrangement, not willpower. Keep it calm and
non-coercive, no shame cues, and no force or pressure framing.

Separator discipline: any incidental label uses the middot, for example `CHAPTER 13 · STRAIGHTEN`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms keep their internal hyphen:
once-a-year, eye-to-waist, arm's-reach, easy-reach, Sort-complete, anti-shame.

---

## 1. The same prime reach for everything (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of a settled entryway in soft morning daylight. Four identical hooks
sit in a tidy row on the wall at the same easy arm's height, each holding one keeper: a set of
keys, a coiled dog's leash, a reusable shopping bag, and a folding umbrella. Below the row, a
small tray holds tomorrow's mail. Everything is placed and calm, nothing loose, yet the four very
different things are all given the exact same good spot, as if the keys grabbed a dozen times a
day and the umbrella reached for twice a month needed identical treatment. Warm paper cream and
honey wood tones, ink-brown shadows, a gentle slate blue wall. Editorial cookbook-style
photography, shallow focus along the row of matching hooks. The room looks settled and well kept,
with one quiet, unstated question hanging over the even row.

- Mood: the homes are done and good, but all four hang at the same prime height, and the eye
  begins to wonder which of these has actually earned the best spot.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, any clutter or mess
  drama, any sense of re-deciding or removing what is placed, price tags, hurry or stress, any
  label or color tag on the hooks.

## 2. The prime zone (the hero device)

A warm editorial illustration of a person standing beside a tall kitchen shelf shown in gentle
cross-section, so the shelf's depth is visible. A soft-green band spans the front of the shelf
from about the person's hip height to shoulder height, the genuinely easy reach, glowing calm and
inviting. Above that band the high part of the shelf reads as a stretch, below it the low part
reads as a bend, and the deep part behind the front row reads as dim and hard to reach; all three
far zones are calm and neutral, plainly just cheaper, never alarming. A light arc from the
person's shoulder shows the reach measured from where they actually stand. Warm paper cream
background, honey wood shelf, ink-brown line work, soft-green calm on the prime band, calm slate
tags on the far zones. Shallow focus on the green band at the front.

- Mood: the prime zone is a small, precious band of effortless reach measured from the body, the
  best storage you own and never very much of it, so it has to be spent with care.
- Avoid: making the green band large or filling most of the shelf, harsh warning colors on the far
  zones, any red danger cue, a sterile diagram look, floating typeset text, any sense that the far
  zones are bad rather than simply cheaper.

## 3. Match the reach to the use (the worked pass)

A warm editorial still-life of one kitchen cabinet shown in gentle cross-section, three shelves,
sorted by how often each thing is used. The middle shelf, at eye-to-waist height and pulled to
the front, is the easy-reach prime band and holds the everyday mugs and plates, calm and ready to
the hand, with soft-green calm notes. A lower shelf holds the weekly things, a mixing bowl and
good glasses, one small reach away. The top shelf, up high, holds the once-a-year roasting pan and
a rarely used platter, sitting calmly out of the way where the occasional reach costs almost
nothing. Soft daylight, honey wood and paper tones, ink-brown shadows, soft-green calm notes on
the daily items only. Documentary and unstaged, as if the cabinet had just been sorted by use for
the first time.

- Mood: the same cabinet sorted by use rather than by kind, so the daily mugs are plainly the
  easiest to reach and the rare pan rests up top without fuss, and the sort reads at a glance.
- Avoid: rows of labels or color-coded tags on the items, any whole-room plan, the rare pan drawn
  as banished or in trouble, clutter or urgency, cold clinical light, shame cues, floating typeset
  text.

## 4. Prime space is expensive (the reframe)

A calm, warm photograph built as a quiet real-estate metaphor in two small vignettes side by side.
On the left, the expensive spot: the front of a shelf at hand height, open and bright, the corner
shop on a busy street, holding the one mug reached for every single morning, a soft-green calm
note. On the right, the cheap spot: a high shelf and the deep back of a cabinet, roomy and dim,
the storage unit across town, holding a novelty teapot poured from once, resting calmly out of the
way. Both scenes are ordinary and un-staged, and neither is good or bad on its own; the only point
is that the right tenant sits in each. Warm paper cream background, honey light, ink-brown shadows,
one soft-green calm note on the everyday mug in its good spot. The image reads as two prices for
two kinds of reach, not two kinds of person.

- Mood: the easy-reach space is scarce and valuable like prime real estate, so it should go to the
  thing your hand wants most, while the once-a-year thing can sit in the cheap seats with no loss.
- Avoid: a shaming before-and-after, a chaotic cabinet, guilt cues, harsh contrast, any red danger
  accent on the cheap spot, any framing of the person as wasteful, glossy retouching, floating
  typeset text.

## 5. The needle steps again, the second step of Straighten (the friction meter)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge
dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right.
The dark live needle has stepped one clear notch further toward the calm side than last chapter
and now rests a little past the spot the meter held at the end of Chapter 12. Just behind the live
needle, a faint dashed ghost needle marks that Chapter 12 position, showing the step just taken. A
small slate marker for the Sort-complete milestone sits further back still, ground covered two
chapters ago. A small green marker labels the new live position as the second step of Straighten,
the second step of the second S. A slate crosshair labeled the goal, carrying its purpose flag,
still sits further out on the green calm side, clearly ahead of the live needle, because two more
Straighten chapters and four more disciplines remain. Warm paper cream background, ink line work,
consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest second step in the second S, nothing left the house yet the room grew easier to
  keep because the busiest things are now the easiest to reach, so the needle stepped once more,
  plainly, with plenty of road still ahead.
- Avoid: the live needle held in place with no move, the needle swung all the way to calm, the
  needle reaching or passing the goal crosshair, the dashed ghost drawn solid or bright,
  celebratory confetti, hype, neon color, any sense of Straighten being finished or the journey
  being done. This is only the second step; Chapters 14 and 15 and four more S's still remain.
