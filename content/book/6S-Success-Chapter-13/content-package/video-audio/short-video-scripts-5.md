## SHORT 1: Prime space is expensive
**Topic: the reframe, easy reach is scarce and valuable. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Some of your storage is worth a fortune, and you are spending it on a waffle iron.

**Script:**
Not all storage is equal. A small band of it is genuinely pleasant to use, right in front of you at hand height. A great deal of it is a nuisance, up high or at the back. The pleasant part is small, and that changes everything, because the best storage in your home is scarce, and like anything scarce, it is valuable. Think of it as real estate. The front of the shelf at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street. The high shelf is the storage unit across town. So here is the principle. Prime space is expensive. Spend it on what you use every day, and send the once-a-year things a step further off. Read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness.

- 12s: "The best storage in your home is scarce."
- 26s: "Prime space is expensive."
- 36s: "Spend it on what you use every day."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A shelf where the front-and-center spot glows like a corner shop; the high shelf and deep back fade like a storage unit across town. Warm daylight.

## SHORT 2: The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most
**Topic: the hero rule. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** When two things want the same good spot, one question settles it.

**Script:**
There is a small band of space in front of you that is genuinely effortless to use. Roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front. Call it the prime zone. It is the most valuable storage you own, and there is never very much of it. So what earns a place in that scarce band? Not size, not category, not how nice a thing looks. Only one thing. The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most. That is the entire rule. When two items want the same good spot, you do not weigh their size or their tidiness. You ask which one your hand goes to more often, and that one wins.

- 12s: "The most valuable storage you own."
- 24s: "The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most."
- 34s: "The one your hand goes to more often wins."

## SHORT 3: Three piles
**Topic: the daily / weekly / rare method. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You do not need a system. You need three loose piles.

**Script:**
Placing things by frequency is a rough sort, and rough is fine. Three loose piles. The first is the everyday things, the ones your hand goes to most, the coffee, the pans you cook with. These go in the prime zone, front and center. The second is the regular but not constant, reached for across a week, the good scissors, the mixing bowl. These go near, one small reach away. The third is the rare and the seasonal, the roasting pan, the holiday box. These go a step further off, up high or at the back, with no loss at all. It fits in a line. The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be. Daily to prime, weekly near, rare a step further off.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Three piles, that is the whole method."
- 12s: "Daily to the prime zone."
- 24s: "Weekly near, one reach away."
- 32s: "Rare a step further off."

## SHORT 4: Paying premium rent
**Topic: the anti-shame audit reframe. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The waffle iron in your best spot is charging you rent.

**Script:**
In most homes the best spots are quietly occupied by things that never earned them. The waffle iron at the front of the cabinet. The wedding china at eye level. The gadget you opened twice. So do a quick, honest look and ask the plainest question. Look at what is sitting in your best, easiest space right now. If it is something you touch twice a year, it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn. And say that without any blame, because none is owed. Nobody placed the waffle iron there as a character flaw. It landed in the good spot and never moved, the way water sits where the ground is lowest. Finding it there is not a verdict. It is just useful information about which spot is about to come free.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Look at your best, easiest spot right now."
- 12s: "Touch it twice a year?"
- 22s: "It is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn."
- 32s: "No blame. It just landed and never moved."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A waffle iron front and center in a cabinet, an everyday mug straining at the back of a high shelf. Then the two trade places in one calm swap. Warm light, no drama.

## SHORT 5: The needle steps again
**Topic: the worked entryway pass and the second Straighten step. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Nothing left the house, and the room still got easier.

**Script:**
Watch the door rearrange itself. The keys are touched most, so they take THE prime spot, the hook closest to the door at hand height. The leash goes out just as often, so it stays right up front beside them. The bag is a few times a week, so it moves near, a hook to the side. The umbrella works on rainy days and does nothing on dry ones, so it steps off into a corner stand. The mail rides the exit path. Then look at the friction meter. Nothing left the house, yet the needle steps again toward calm, because reaching got cheaper for the things you reach for most. This is the second step of Straighten. The goal is still ahead, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain.
