# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Store by Frequency of Use

**Chapter 13, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** store-by-frequency-of-use
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- The hero device is The Prime Zone: the small band of genuinely easy reach, roughly from your hips to your shoulders, within an arm's length, at the front of whatever holds it, and measured from where you actually use the thing. Show it as a green band; show that it is small, front, and scarce.
- The starting point is the settled entryway from Chapter 12. Every keeper has a home now: four identical hooks in a tidy row hold the keys, the dog's leash, a reusable bag, and a folding umbrella, each at the same easy arm's height, with tomorrow's mail in a small tray below. Nothing is loose. A faint question floats above the row: which of these do you actually reach for most?
- The homes are already assigned. The only action this chapter is placing each home by how often the hand goes to it. Do NOT stage re-assigning a home, re-Sorting, or weighing what stays.
- The reframe is drawn, not just stated: easy-reach space is scarce, valuable real estate. The front-of-shelf spot at hand height is the corner shop on the busy street; the high shelf and deep back are the storage unit across town. Show the contrast.
- The friction meter MOVES this chapter, but nothing leaves the house. The live needle steps past Chapter 12's position and a little further toward the calm side, the second step of Straighten. Chapter 12's position becomes a faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle; the Sort-complete milestone sits further back. The GOAL crosshair stays out on the green calm side, because two more Straighten chapters and four more S's remain.
- Warm daylight, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.
- Keep the whole thing calm and unhurried. No force or violence framing. Nothing is purged or attacked. The tone is anti-shame: wrong placement is a fixable first draft, not a character flaw.

- The point: a rough sort into three loose piles, daily to the prime zone, weekly near, rare a step further off. The more often you use a thing, the closer and easier its home should be.
- Tier-to-spot inserts (readable, using the middot): "daily · the prime zone, front and center", "weekly · near, one small reach", "rare or seasonal · a step further off".

### Shot 6: Paying premium rent (the anti-shame audit)
