# Teleprompter Script: Store by Frequency of Use

**Chapter 13, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** store-by-frequency-of-use
**Length:** about 700 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come stand with me at the door one more time.

Every keeper has a home now.

The keys hang on a hook.

The leash beside them, the bag, the umbrella.

Tomorrow's mail waits in a small tray below.

Nothing is loose.

Every thing has a place.

And every place is treated exactly the same.

Look at what your hand actually does here.

You grab the keys a dozen times a day.

You reach for the umbrella twice a month.

Yet both hang at the same easy height,

as though you needed them the same amount.

You do not.

So a home can be well built

and still be in the wrong place.

We are not reopening anything.

The keys still have their hook.

Today we do one quiet thing.

We match each home

to how often your hand goes to it.

Here is the idea it rests on.

Not all storage is equal.

A small band is genuinely pleasant to use.

Right in front of you, at hand height.

A great deal of it is a nuisance.

Up high, or at the back.

The pleasant part is small.

The best storage in your home is scarce.

And like anything scarce, it is valuable.

Think of it as real estate.

The front of the shelf, at hand height,

is the corner shop on the busy street.

The high shelf is the storage unit across town.

So here is the principle.

Prime space is expensive.

Spend it on what you use every day,

and send the once-a-year things a step further off.

Read it as a budget, not a rule of neatness.

Now let me name the good, easy reach.

There is a small band of space in front of you.

Roughly, from your hips to your shoulders.

Within an arm's length.

At the front of whatever holds it.

Call it the prime zone.

It is the most valuable storage you own,

and there is never very much of it.

It is measured from where you stand.

The prime zone for your coffee

is at the coffee maker.

And it is small on purpose.

If you try to make everything prime, nothing is.

So what earns a place in that scarce band?

Only one thing.

The best spot goes to the thing you reach for most.

That is the entire rule.

You do not weigh size or tidiness.

You ask which one your hand goes to more often.

Placing things is a rough sort.

Three loose piles.

The everyday things go in the prime zone.

Front and center.

The weekly things go near.

One small reach away.

The rare and the seasonal go a step further off.

Up high, down low, at the back.

The more often you use a thing,

the closer and easier its home should be.

Now audit your best spots.

In most homes the prime zone is taken

by things that never earned it.

The waffle iron at the front.

The wedding china at eye level.

So ask the plainest question.

Look at what is sitting

in your best, easiest space right now.

If it is something you touch twice a year,

it is paying premium rent on a spot it did not earn.

And say that without any blame.

Nobody placed the waffle iron there as a flaw.

It landed in the good spot and never moved,

the way water sits where the ground is lowest.

Back to the door.

The keys are touched most, so they take the prime spot.

The hook closest to the door, at hand height.

The leash stays right up front beside them.

The bag moves near, a hook to the side.

The umbrella steps off into a corner stand.

The mail rides the exit path.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Because it moves once more.

And again, nothing left the house.

The room holds what it held this morning.

All that changed is the homes slid

to match how often each thing is used.

The keys now sit where your hand already goes.

So that toll drops to nearly nothing.

Twelve times over.

This is the second step of Straighten.

But be honest about what the meter still shows.

Chapter 12's position is now the faint dashed ghost.

The goal is still ahead, on the green calm side.

And it is still a fair way off.

Two more Straighten chapters remain.

And four more S's follow.

So here is your job.

Open one drawer you use every day.

Find the easy spot holding a thing you never use.

Trade it with a daily thing that is awkward to reach.

One swap, and the space is faster.

A room arranged by frequency of use

pays you back on a schedule.

The full chapter is free in the online book.

Go make one swap.
