## The guest-bedroom Shine layer (nine rules from Ch 31 PLUS)
- **Air it, do not chase spills**: nobody sleeps here, so the enemy is settled dust and stale, shut-away air; air the room, the linen, and the empty drawers as much as you wipe.
- **Draw the line**: the room fills up because nothing objects when you set a box down; the guest drawers and the guest rod are never available, and the room's second job gets a taped footprint or moves out.
- **Clean for the first evening, not the photo**: test what a guest depends on in the dark, the lamp lights and the two cables actually charge.
- **The unfamiliar room in the dark**: keep the bed-to-door path clear, tie up looped cords, strap the dresser, take your own medication out.

## Five zones (in order of work; start with the closet, where the overflow lives)
1. **Guest Bed and Linens** - strip, vacuum, and turn the mattress; hot-wash the set on the bed and air the spare; a bed a visitor can get into tonight.
2. **Guest Nightstand** - the one square foot a guest reaches for in the dark; lamp, two tested cables, tissues, coaster, wifi card.
3. **Guest Dresser** - air the empty drawers as much as wipe them; the two top drawers belong to the guest and are never available; strap it.
4. **Guest Closet** - where the overflow actually lives; clean the rod, get into the still corners; tape the line where the guest section ends.
5. **Guest Welcome and Work Surface** - clean it so it works on the first evening; test the desk lamp; name the room's second job and give it a footprint.

## FROZEN STRINGS (byte-verbatim in manuscript AND final.html)

### Five zones
The guest bedroom is five small zones, not one big job: the guest bed and its linens, the guest nightstand, the guest dresser, the guest closet, and the guest welcome and work surface. Do them one at a time and the room that sleeps nobody most of the year is ready for someone by evening, a zone at a time.

### The rule (clean for freshness and the first evening; draw the line)
The guest bedroom is the one room in the house with no daily user, which is exactly why it fills up and why it goes stale, so here you clean for freshness and for a guest's first evening rather than for spills. Nobody disturbs this room, so dust settles in bands and the linen and the drawers take on the shut-away smell of the cupboard they sat in, and you air the room as much as you wipe it. Nothing here objects when you set a box down, so you draw a line between what belongs to a visitor and what the household has stashed here. And you clean it so it works on the first evening, testing the things a guest reaches for in the dark rather than making it look good in a photo.

### The definition (defbox)
A finished guest bedroom is one a visitor can walk into and use tonight without you touching anything first: a bed made up on a clean protector with a second set aired and ready on the shelf, a nightstand with a lamp they can find by feel and two cables that actually charge, empty aired drawers and a clear closet rod, a clear desk that works on the first evening, and a clear path from the bed to the door for the walk they take in the dark.

### Air-not-spills reframe
Nobody sleeps here most nights, so the enemy in a guest room is not spills, it is settled dust and stale, shut-away air. Dust builds in a grey band along a headboard nobody sits up against, and linen folded since spring carries the smell of the cupboard it sat in even though it is clean. So you air the room as much as you clean it: you strip and hot-wash the set on the bed, you shake and air the spare set before it goes back, you leave the empty drawers pulled open with the window cracked while you work, and you make the bed up the week the guest arrives, not the month before.

### Draw-the-line reframe
A guest room fills up because nothing in it ever objects when you set a box down, so it quietly absorbs whatever the rest of the house could not decide about, and then you excavate it every time someone visits. So you do not tidy this room, you draw a line through it: the two top drawers and the guest half of the rod belong to the visitor and are never available, not for a week and not for the box you mean to sort. And you name the room's second job out loud, the office or the sewing table or the exercise bike, and give it a footprint marked in tape that clears into one bin in a single trip, or it moves out for good.

### Clean-for-the-first-evening reframe
You clean a guest room so it works on a guest's first evening, not so it looks right in a photograph, and the difference is that you test the things a guest depends on in the dark. You switch the lamp on to prove the bulb lights before they arrive, not after they are hunting for it. You charge a real device from each of the two cables you leave out, because an untested cable is not hospitality, it is a trap with a bow on it. And you put out the tissues, the coaster, and the wifi card so a guest at two in the morning never has to knock on your door.

### Safety reframe (the unfamiliar room in the dark)
The person who sleeps here does not know this room, and they cross it in the dark on the first night to find the bathroom. So a suitcase, a footstool, or an extension lead left in the path between the bed and the door is a fall waiting for someone who cannot picture the floor, and that path stays clear. Tie up any looped blind or curtain cord within reach of the bed, because a visiting family may put a small child in here, strap the dresser a child left alone might climb, and take your own medication out of the nightstand drawer, because it sits at exactly the height a visiting toddler explores first.

### Before-and-after caption
This is the guest room the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the bed made up and ready, the drawers and closet aired and holding only a decision, the desk clear and working, and a clear path a stranger can cross in the dark. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a guest room someone can walk into and use tonight without you lifting a finger is the one that stays ready, and the photo is what it has to match before the next visit.

### One Idea to Keep
A guest room is not ready because it looks tidy. It is ready because you aired the linen and the drawers, drew a line against the overflow the rest of the house tried to store here, and tested the lamp and the cables a guest reaches for in the dark. Do one zone this week, the closet where the overflow actually lives, and give the next visitor a room that welcomes them without a scramble.

## Four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win (win)**: Open the window and air the room while you work. Strip and hot-wash the set on the bed, shake and air the spare set from the shelf, and leave the empty drawers pulled open so the shut-away smell has somewhere to go. A guest notices the air of a room before they notice anything else, and airing it costs you nothing but an open window.
- **6S Tip (tip)**: Clean it for the first evening, not the photo. Before a guest arrives, switch the lamp on to prove it lights, charge a real device from each cable you leave out, and set out the tissues, the coaster, and the wifi card. Test the things a guest reaches for in the dark, because that is where hosting actually succeeds or fails.
- **The Overflow Challenge (family)**: The guest room's two top drawers and the guest half of the closet rod belong to the visitor and are never available, not for a week, not for the box you mean to sort. Name the room's second job out loud, give it a footprint marked in tape, and prove it clears into one bin in a single trip. If it will not, the second job is too big to share this room, and it moves out for good.
- **Common Mistake (mistake)**: Making the bed up a month early so it smells of the cupboard by the time a guest turns the covers back, offering charging cables nobody has ever tested, and treating the room as free storage all year and then excavating it under time pressure the afternoon before someone arrives.

## The calls that matter here
The demoted sheets (if you would not sleep on it yourself for a week, it is cleaning cloth, not guest linen; keep two complete sets); two cables, tested (an untested cable is a trap with a bow on it); the overflow you told yourself was free (the guest drawers cost the whole point of the room); the box you have not opened since the move (name a person and an occasion, or it is a delayed decision); and the room's second job (name it, give it a taped footprint, or it moves out for good).

## Watch for in the guest bedroom (five safety questions)
- **Fall**: a suitcase, footstool, or extension lead in the path from the bed to the door that a guest crosses in the dark; loose bins on the closet floor.
- **Fall, cut, or crush**: an unstrapped dresser a visiting child climbs; heavy boxes stored above the closet rod over the head of anyone reaching in; a hard case balanced on a desk edge; an untested folding luggage rack.
- **Water and electricity**: a water glass beside a lamp base and a charger head on a small nightstand, the cord crossing the top.
- **Poison, choke, or strangle**: a looped blind or curtain cord within reach of the bed; your own medication in the nightstand drawer at a visiting toddler's height; dry-cleaning film or sealed-bedding bags in a low drawer a small guest can open.
- **Burn or fire**: a warm or scorched extension lead or plug behind the desk; a dusty bulb run hot in a shade.
