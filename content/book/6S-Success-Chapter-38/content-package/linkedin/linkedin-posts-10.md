# Chapter 38 LinkedIn Posts: The Guest Bedroom

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The room with no daily user
The guest bedroom is the one room in the house with no daily user, and that single fact explains everything about it. Nothing in here objects when you set a box down, so it quietly fills up, and it goes stale because nobody disturbs it.

"So here you clean for freshness and for a guest's first evening rather than for spills. Nobody disturbs this room, so dust settles in bands and the linen and the drawers take on the shut-away smell of the cupboard they sat in, and you air the room as much as you wipe it."

## 2. Air it, do not chase spills
Nobody sleeps here most nights, so the enemy is not a spill, it is settled dust and stale, shut-away air. Strip and hot-wash the set on the bed, shake and air the spare set before it goes back, and leave the empty drawers pulled open with the window cracked while you work. A guest notices the air of a room before anything else, and airing it costs you nothing but an open window.

## 3. Make the bed up the week they arrive
Linen folded since spring smells of the cupboard it sat in, even though it is clean, and a guest notices the moment they turn the covers back. So do not make the bed up a month early. Wash the set and make it up the week a guest arrives, and open the window while you do it.

## 4. Draw a line against the overflow
"A guest room fills up because nothing in it ever objects when you set a box down." So do not tidy this room, draw a line through it: the two top drawers and the guest half of the rod belong to the visitor and are never available, not for a week and not for the box you mean to sort.

## 5. Name the room's second job
Almost every guest room is also the office, the sewing room, or the place the exercise bike ended up. Pretending otherwise is why hosting takes an afternoon. Name the second job out loud, give it a footprint marked in tape, and prove it clears into one bin in a single trip. If it will not, the second job is too big to share the room, and it moves out for good.

## 6. Clean it for the first evening, not the photo
You clean a guest room so it works on a guest's first evening, not so it looks right in a photograph. Switch the lamp on to prove it lights before they arrive. Charge a real device from each cable you leave out. Set out the tissues, the coaster, and the wifi card. Test the things a guest reaches for in the dark.

## 7. An untested cable is a trap with a bow on it
A guest will find the cable you left, trust it because it is offered, plug in, and wake to a phone that never charged. That is worse than offering nothing. Keep exactly two cables, one of each connector your visitors carry, and prove each one tonight by charging a real device with it.

## 8. The demoted sheets
The linen in the guest room got here because it stopped being good enough for your own bed. The rule that settles it: if you would not sleep on it yourself for a week, it is not guest linen, it is cleaning cloth. Keep two complete sets that pass that test and let the rest go. Two good sets beat six tired ones.

## 9. The path a stranger crosses in the dark
The person who sleeps here does not know this room, and they cross it in the dark on the first night to find the bathroom. A suitcase, a footstool, or an extension lead in the path between the bed and the door is a fall waiting for someone who cannot picture the floor. Keep that path clear.

## 10. A guest room is not ready because it looks tidy
"A guest room is not ready because it looks tidy. It is ready because you aired the linen and the drawers, drew a line against the overflow the rest of the house tried to store here, and tested the lamp and the cables a guest reaches for in the dark."
