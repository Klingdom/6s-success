# Chapter 38 Infographic Spec: The Guest Bedroom Shine Method

## The single strongest image
The guest-room Shine method plus the kit, because the method (air it, draw the line, clean for the first evening, the room in the dark) is the chapter's centerpiece.

## Anchor string (byte-verbatim)
> The guest bedroom is the one room in the house with no daily user, which is exactly why it fills up and why it goes stale, so here you clean for freshness and for a guest's first evening rather than for spills. Nobody disturbs this room, so dust settles in bands and the linen and the drawers take on the shut-away smell of the cupboard they sat in, and you air the room as much as you wipe it. Nothing here objects when you set a box down, so you draw a line between what belongs to a visitor and what the household has stashed here. And you clean it so it works on the first evening, testing the things a guest reaches for in the dark rather than making it look good in a photo.

## Palette
paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green #6E8B5B (Shine). Fraunces headings, Inter labels.

## Layout, portrait (1080 x 1350)
- Title: The Guest Bedroom Shine Method.
- Band 1, "air it, not spills": an open window, the spare set aired, drawers open.
- Band 2, "draw the line": a taped line on the rod, the guest drawers off-limits.
- Band 3, "clean for the first evening": the lamp tested, the two cables tested.
- Band 4, "the room in the dark": a clear floor path, a tied-up cord, an anti-tip strap.
- Footer strip: air it, draw the line, clean for the first evening.

## Layout, landscape (1200 x 675)
Two panels: the method on the left, the kit on the right.

## Rules
Types only, no brands or logos. Never show an untested cable offered as ready, or the guest drawers used as household storage. No em/en/spaced-hyphen dashes in any label.
