# Facebook Longform Post: Fresh, Ready, and Guest-Proof

A story-driven post from Chapter 38 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the guest bedroom.

---

Here is why getting the guest room ready always seems to take a whole afternoon: you are not cleaning it, you are excavating it.

The guest bedroom is the only room in your house with no daily user, and that single fact explains everything. Nothing in here objects when you set a box down, so the room quietly absorbs whatever the rest of the house could not decide about, and it sits there uncomplaining until someone calls to say they are coming to stay.

So this room is not cleaned like the others. You clean it for freshness, for the overflow, and for a guest's first evening.

Freshness first, because nobody disturbs this room, so the enemy is not a spill, it is settled dust and stale, shut-away air. Strip and hot-wash the set on the bed, shake and air the spare set before it goes back, and leave the empty drawers pulled open with the window cracked while you work. And make the bed up the week a guest arrives, not the month before, because linen folded since spring smells of the cupboard even though it is clean.

Then draw a line against the overflow. The two top drawers and the guest half of the rod belong to the visitor and are never available, not for a week and not for the box you mean to sort. And name the room's second job, the office or the sewing table or the exercise bike, and give it a footprint marked in tape, or it moves out for good.

Last, clean it so it works on the first evening. Switch the lamp on to prove it lights. Charge a real device from each cable you leave out, because an untested cable a guest trusts is worse than none. Set out the tissues, the coaster, and the wifi card.

Do one zone this week, the closet where the overflow lives, and give the next visitor a room that welcomes them without a scramble.

Be honest: what is your guest room actually storing right now?
