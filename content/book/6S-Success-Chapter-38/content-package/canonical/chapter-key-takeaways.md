# Chapter 38 Key Takeaways: The Guest Bedroom

- The guest room has no daily user, which is exactly why it fills up and goes stale, so you clean it for freshness and a guest's first evening, not for spills.
- Five zones, one at a time: the guest bed and linens, the guest nightstand, the guest dresser, the guest closet, and the welcome and work surface. Start with the closet, where the overflow actually lives.
- Air it rather than chase spills: strip and hot-wash the set on the bed, shake and air the spare set, and leave the empty drawers pulled open with the window cracked; make the bed up the week a guest arrives, not the month before.
- Draw a line against the overflow: the guest drawers and the guest half of the rod are never available, and the room's second job gets a footprint marked in tape or it moves out for good.
- Clean it for the first evening: switch the lamp on to prove it lights, charge a real device from each of the two cables, and set out the tissues, the coaster, and the wifi card.
- A vacuum with an upholstery and a crevice tool does most of the room's real work, since settled dust is the enemy.
- Safety is tuned to a stranger in the dark: keep the bed-to-door path clear, tie up looped cords, strap the dresser, and take your own medication out of the drawer.
- Close on a before-and-after photo taped inside the closet door, the standard the room has to match before the next visit.
