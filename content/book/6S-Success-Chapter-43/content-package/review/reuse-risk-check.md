# Chapter 43 Reuse Risk Check: The Laundry Room

## Source provenance
Every zone, its purpose, its passes, its hard call, its watch-for items, and its shine detail (surfaces, methods, products used, inspect-as-you-clean) are folded from the Micro Zone Manual's Laundry Room entry, which is the project's own material. The six calls, the trap note about the counter, and the cleaning note about the gasket and drawer are all the Manual's, elevated into procedure. Nothing in this chapter is drawn from an outside author, method, or publication.

## Third-party IP
- No quotations from any external book, article, or speaker.
- No named methodology other than 6S itself and the Toyota lineage the book already establishes.
- No rival step-systems introduced. The chapter uses the book's own nine rules of Shine plus a four-part room layer, and does not invent a competing numbered method.
- No brand names, product names, model names, or retailer names anywhere in the package.
- No logos, no QR codes, and no readable packaging text called for in any image prompt.

## Claims that need to stay defensible
The chapter makes four factual assertions about appliances. Each is stated in general, non-quantified terms, which is deliberate:

1. Lint accumulates in dryer ducts, particularly at bends, and lint plus heat is a fire risk. Stated qualitatively, with no ignition statistics, no incident counts, and no percentages.
2. Flexible foil accordion ducting traps more lint than smooth rigid metal. Stated comparatively, not numerically.
3. Washing machine gaskets, detergent drawers and drums accumulate residue and biofilm. Stated as a maintenance fact, with the remedy being cleaning, not repair.
4. Detergent pods are a poisoning risk to small children. Stated as the reason for a storage rule.

None of these carries a citation in the chapter, and none needs one at the level it is pitched. If the book later adds a references appendix, these are the four lines that would take one. **No safety statistic was invented to strengthen any of them**, which was the main risk in a chapter about fire.

## Liability posture
- The chapter never instructs a reader to dismantle, service, or repair an appliance. It instructs them to clear, look, and flag, which is the same posture Shine takes everywhere in the book.
- It never advises mixing products, and forbids it explicitly twice.
- It does not tell a reader to work behind a machine that is still plugged in; the b-roll notes forbid depicting it.
- The duct advice is to clear and, if it is foil, to replace. Replacement is named as a job, not walked through as a procedure.

## Cross-chapter reuse
- Uses and credits by reference: Chapter 31 (the nine rules of Shine), Chapter 14 (visual controls, the painted outlines and taped square), Chapter 21 (the hazard hunt), Chapter 24 (named responsibility), Chapter 25 (the ideal-state photo). None is re-taught.
- Does not borrow guest-bathroom (Ch 42) or home-office (Ch 44) material.
- The before-and-after signature is the standard Part 9 close, with a variation documented in `chapter_43_signature.md`.

## Verdict
Low risk. Own source material throughout, no external IP, no invented statistics, and no instruction that crosses from cleaning into repair.
