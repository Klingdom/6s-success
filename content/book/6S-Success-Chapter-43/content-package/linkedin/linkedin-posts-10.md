# Chapter 43 LinkedIn Posts: The Laundry Room

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. A machine you live with
The laundry room is the first room in this book that is not somewhere you live. It is a process room, and it is judged by one thing: whether the work gets through it without stopping.

"Laundry arrives dirty, sits wet, and is not finished until it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any one of those points."

## 2. You are not doing more washing
When a household says it is drowning in laundry, it is almost never doing more washing than it used to. It is running the same washing twice. A dry load goes back in for a second tumble because the counter was full, and that second tumble is pure waste. Fix the jam and the volume of laundry does not change, but the work halves.

## 3. Lint is fuel, not fluff
The lint screen you clear is the part of this room you can see, and it is not the part that burns. The fuel that matters is the soft grey packing where the duct bends behind the dryer, in the room that combines the most heat with the most fine dry tinder outside the kitchen. The test is simple: can you name the month you last pulled the machine out and cleared it?

## 4. The dryer is not slow, it is blocked
You have been telling yourself the dryer just runs a bit slower these days, and that towels have always needed two cycles. That is not age. That is a blocked duct, and it is costing you a second cycle every load. And while the machine is out and the duct is off, look at what it is made of. A flexible foil accordion duct traps far more lint than a smooth rigid metal pipe.

## 5. Clean the machine that cleans
A washing machine is not self-cleaning. It hides its dirt in exactly three places: the folds of the rubber gasket, the detergent drawer and the mould-filmed cavity behind it, and the drum itself. Skip those and you are washing every load in the residue of every load before it. That faint machine smell in clean laundry is not the detergent.

## 6. Nothing goes away wet
Every other room gets dirty. This one gets damp, and damp is the failure that spreads. The washer door stands ajar between loads, the gasket folds are dried rather than merely wiped, the rack is folded flat, and the mop hangs to dry. A thing put away damp in this room becomes a musty one, and then it hands that smell to the next clean load.

## 7. The jam is not the folding
If the folding counter is holding yesterday's basket, the problem is not the counter and it is not the folding. Laundry has four stages: dirty, washed, dry, away. Most households own the first three. The last one, carrying the clothes out and putting them in a drawer, has never been given to a person by name. That is the whole jam.

## 8. The delicates bag that never fills
You sort into five streams because a care label once told you to, and one bag has been sitting a third full for three weeks while the thing you wanted washed waits inside it. A stream earns its own bag only if it has run as a full load, by itself, in the last month. A bag that waits three weeks is not sorting. It is clothes you have taken out of circulation.

## 9. The rag pile
Worn towels and holed shirts quietly become a rag supply large enough to clean a small hotel, because turning a worn item into a rag feels like thrift rather than a decision. Nothing is ever discarded, only reclassified. Rags live in one bin and only one bin, and nothing new becomes a rag until something old leaves as waste.

## 10. The standard with a date on it
"Do one zone this week, and make it the machines, because every other zone in this room is waiting on them. Then write this month on a strip of tape and stick it to the side of the dryer."
