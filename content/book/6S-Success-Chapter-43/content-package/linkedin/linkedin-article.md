# The Laundry Room: Lint Is Fuel, Nothing Goes Away Wet, and the Counter Jams

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 43 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

Every room in this book so far has been a place where somebody does something: sleeps, eats, washes, plays. The laundry room is the first one that is not. It is a process room, and that makes it both simpler than the bedrooms and much less forgiving.

> The laundry room is the first room in this book that is not somewhere you live, it is a machine you live with, and it is judged only by whether the work flows through it. Laundry arrives dirty, sits wet, and is not finished until it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any one of those points. So you clean this room for the flow rather than for the look of it. You clear the lint, because lint is fuel and this is the hottest room in the house outside the kitchen. You put nothing away wet, because damp is what turns a working room sour. You clean the machine that does the cleaning, because it hides its own dirt in three places you have never looked. And you keep the folding counter bare, because that is the surface the whole household jams at, and a room that jams sends the dry load back in for a second tumble.

## Six zones, and the order is not a suggestion

> The laundry room is six small zones, not one big job: the washer and dryer, the detergent and treatment zone, the sorting and hamper zone, the folding surface, the hanging and air-dry zone, and the utility and cleaning zone. Do them one at a time, and start at both machines, because every other zone in this room is waiting on those two being ready to take a load.

## Lint is fuel, not fluff

> The lint screen you clear is the part of this room you can see, and it is not the part that burns. The fuel that matters is the soft grey packing that builds where the duct bends behind the dryer, in the one room in your house that combines the most heat with the most fine dry tinder outside the kitchen.

## The machine that cleans needs cleaning

> The most useful thing to understand about a washing machine is that it is not self-cleaning, and neither is a dryer. A machine that looks spotless from the front hides its dirt in exactly three places.

## The jam is a job nobody was given

> If the folding counter is holding yesterday's basket, the problem is not the counter and it is not the folding. Laundry is a flow with four stages, dirty, washed, dry, away, and a flow stops at whichever stage nobody owns.
