# Chapter 43 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Laundry Room

**Subject:** You are not doing more laundry. You are doing the same laundry twice.

Every room in this book so far has been a place where somebody does something. The laundry room is the first one that is not. It is a process room, judged by exactly one thing: whether the work gets through it without stopping.

Laundry arrives dirty, sits wet, and is only finished when it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any of those three points. A washer you have to empty before you can fill is a stall. A dryer that needs two cycles because its duct is packed is a stall. A counter holding yesterday's basket is the biggest stall of all, because it is the one that sends a dry load back in for a second tumble.

This chapter breaks the room into six zones and gives the exact inputs and numbered steps for each. Four rules sit on top of the nine rules of Shine:

- **Lint is fuel, not fluff.** The screen every load, the duct once a year, on a date you can name.
- **Nothing is put away wet.** The washer door ajar, the gasket folds dried, the rack folded flat.
- **Clean the machine that cleans.** The gasket, the drawer, and the drum are the three hiding places.
- **The flow jams at the counter.** And the jam is a job nobody was ever given by name.

Do one zone this week, and make it the machines. Then write this month on a strip of tape and stick it to the side of the dryer.
