# Chapter 43 Summary: The Laundry Room

The thirteenth room playbook of Part Nine, and the first utility room. The laundry room is the first room in the book that is not somewhere you live but a machine you live with, and it is judged by one thing only: whether the work flows through it. Laundry arrives dirty, sits wet, and is not finished until it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any of those points. When a household says it is drowning in laundry, it is almost never doing more washing than it used to. It is running the same washing twice.

The room breaks into six zones, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the washer and dryer, the detergent and treatment zone, the sorting and hamper zone, the folding surface, the hanging and air-dry zone, and the utility and cleaning zone. The order matters more here than in any other room, because five of the six are downstream of the machines, so both machines come first. On top of the nine rules of Shine, this room adds a layer of four: lint is fuel, not fluff (the screen every load, the duct once a year, on a date you can name); nothing is put away wet (the washer door ajar, the gasket dried, the rack folded flat, the mop hung to dry); clean the machine that cleans (the gasket folds, the detergent drawer, and the drum, the three places a washer hides its own dirt); and the flow jams at the counter (a bare counter, and a person named to carry the clean clothes out).

Safety is not a formality here. Outside the kitchen this is the hottest room in the house and the one with the most fine dry tinder in it, which is why one of its standards carries a date. The chapter closes on a before-and-after room signature whose after photo contains one detail that expires: the month written on a strip of tape stuck to the side of the dryer.
