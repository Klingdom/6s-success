# Chapter 43 Key Takeaways: The Laundry Room

- The laundry room is a process room, not a place you live, and it is judged only by whether the work flows through it: dirty, washed, dry, away.
- Six zones, one at a time, and the order is not a suggestion: start at both machines, because five of the six zones are waiting on them.
- Lint is fuel, not fluff. Clear the screen every load and the duct once a year, and if you cannot name the month you last cleared it, that is today's job.
- Replace a flexible foil accordion duct with rigid metal while the machine is already out, because there is rarely a next time.
- Nothing is put away wet. The washer door stands ajar, the gasket folds are dried, the rack is folded flat, and the mop hangs to dry.
- A washing machine is not self-cleaning. It hides its dirt in three places: the gasket folds, the detergent drawer, and the drum, which needs a hot empty cycle with a drum cleaning tablet.
- The room jams at the folding counter, and the jam is not about folding. It is the one stage of the flow nobody was ever named to own: carrying the clean clothes out.
- The counter is either empty or holding exactly one load. There is no third state.
- Safety here is about fire and heavy chemistry: lint and heat, split fill hoses beside an outlet, pods that look like sweets, bleach never beside an ammonia based cleaner, and nothing with a long handle left leaning.
- Close on a before-and-after photo whose after contains one detail that expires: the month written on tape on the side of the dryer.
