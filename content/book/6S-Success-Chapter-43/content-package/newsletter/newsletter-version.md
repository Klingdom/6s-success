# Chapter 43 Newsletter: The Laundry Room

**Subject:** You are not doing more laundry. You are doing it twice.
**Preview:** The first process room in the book, and the one with the most heat and the most tinder.

---

Every room in this book so far has been a place where somebody does something: sleeps, eats, washes, plays. The laundry room is the first one that is not.

It is a process room. Nobody spends an evening in here. It has no atmosphere to get right, and it is judged by exactly one thing, which is whether the work gets through it without stopping.

That makes it simpler than the bedrooms and much less forgiving. Laundry arrives dirty, sits wet, and is not finished until it is back in a drawer, and it can stall at any one of those three points. A washer you have to empty before you can fill is a stall. A dryer that needs two cycles because its duct is packed is a stall. A counter holding yesterday's basket is the biggest stall of all, because it sends a dry load back in for a second tumble.

**Six zones, and the order is not a suggestion.** The washer and dryer, the detergent and treatment zone, the sorting and hamper zone, the folding surface, the hanging and air-dry zone, and the utility and cleaning zone. Five of the six are downstream of the machines, so the machines come first. Before anything else, clear both lint screens and set the washer running on its longest hottest empty cycle with a drum cleaning tablet, so the machine cleans itself while you work.

**Four rules on top of the nine rules of Shine.**

*Lint is fuel, not fluff.* The screen you clear every load is the part you can see, and it is not the part that burns. The fuel that matters is the soft grey packing where the duct bends behind the dryer. The test is whether you can name the month you last cleared it.

*Nothing is put away wet.* Every other room gets dirty; this one gets damp, and damp spreads. Door ajar, gasket folds dried, drawer returned to a dry cavity, rack folded flat, mop hung to dry.

*Clean the machine that cleans.* A washer hides its dirt in three places: the gasket folds, the detergent drawer and its mould-filmed cavity, and the drum, which needs a hot empty cycle to reach.

*The flow jams at the counter.* And the jam is not about folding. It is that carrying the clothes out and putting them away is the one stage nobody was ever given by name.

**The safety pass here is about fire**, and it is the reason the chapter exists. This is the hottest room in the house outside the kitchen and the one with the most fine dry tinder in it.

Do one zone this week, and make it the machines. Then write this month on a strip of tape and stick it to the side of the dryer.
