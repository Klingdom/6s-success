# Chapter 43 YouTube Script: The Laundry Room

*Six to eight minutes.*

---

**[Cold open, on the room]**

If your house feels like it is drowning in laundry, here is the thing worth knowing: you are almost certainly not doing more washing than you used to. You are doing the same washing twice.

**[Title]**

**[Section 1: the process room]**

Every room we have done so far has been a place where somebody does something. This one is not. It is a process room. Nobody spends an evening in here. There is no atmosphere to get right. It is judged by one thing: whether the work gets through it without stopping.

Laundry has four stages. Dirty. Washed. Dry. Away. And it is not finished until that fourth one happens.

**[Section 2: where it stalls]**

A washer you have to empty before you can fill is a stall. A dryer that needs two cycles is a stall. And a counter holding yesterday's basket is the biggest stall of all, because that is the one that sends a dry load back in for a second tumble.

Here is the surprising part. The counter is not the problem.

A flow stops at whichever stage nobody owns. Most households own the first three without discussing them. Somebody loads it. Somebody moves it. Somebody tips it out. Then it sits, because the fourth stage has never been given to a person by name.

Give it a name. Put one basket per bedroom under the counter so folding and delivering are one motion. The counter empties.

**[Section 3: six zones]**

Six zones: the machines, the detergent shelf, the sorting bags, the folding counter, the drying rack, and the utility corner.

And the order is not a suggestion, because five of the six are downstream of the machines. Start there.

Actually, start ninety seconds before there. Clear both lint screens, and set the washer running on its longest hottest empty cycle with a drum cleaning tablet, so it cleans itself while you work.

**[Section 4: the machine is not self-cleaning]**

Which brings us to the second big idea. A washing machine is not self-cleaning, and it hides its dirt in three places.

The gasket. Peel the folds back one at a time. Coins, hairpins, grey slime.

The drawer. It lifts right out and washes in the sink, and behind it is a cavity whose roof is filmed with black mould.

And the drum, where a biofilm builds deeper than any cloth reaches.

That faint smell in clean laundry is not your detergent. It is the machine.

**[Section 5: lint is fuel]**

Now the part that matters most.

Outside the kitchen, this is the hottest room in your house, and the one with the most fine dry tinder in it. Those two things sit about a metre apart, behind the dryer.

The lint screen is the part you can see. It is not the part that burns. The fuel that matters is the soft grey packing where the duct bends behind the machine.

One question: can you name the month you last pulled it out and cleared that duct?

If you cannot, that is today's job. And while it is out and the duct is off, look at what it is made of. Flexible foil accordion traps far more lint than smooth rigid metal, so replace it now rather than next time. There is rarely a next time.

**[Section 6: nothing goes away wet]**

Quick one, but it fixes the smell most people blame on the machine. Every other room gets dirty. This one gets damp. Door ajar between loads. Gasket folds dried, not just wiped. Rack folded flat. Mop hung to dry. Nothing goes away wet.

**[Close]**

Take a before photo. Work the six zones. Take the after, and tape it up as the standard.

But this room's after photo has something no other room's does: a detail that expires. A strip of tape on the side of the dryer with a month written on it. Everything else in that picture you can restore in an evening. That one you have to earn again next year.

Do one zone this week, and make it the machines. Chapter 43. Next: the home office.
