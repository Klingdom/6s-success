# Chapter 3 Signature Plan
## "The Six Steps That Transform Any Space"
### 6S Success: Home Edition

---

## Signature Overview

**Length:** 18 pages (9 spreads)
**Part:** Part 1, Discovering 6S
**Reading time:** 11 to 14 minutes
**Text-to-visual ratio target:** 58% text, 42% visual
**Job of this chapter:** Walk the complete 6S loop once, end to end, on a single real space, so the reader sees how each step hands off to the next before the book slows down to master each step in Parts 2 through 8. Demonstrate the method, do not list it. Pay off Chapter 2's promise of "a quick lap around the track before the real training begins." End with the reader taking their own first small lap, which earns the friction meter its first honest movement.

**Throughline space:** the cabinet under the kitchen sink. Universal, small enough to walk in one chapter, and naturally rich in every step, including a genuine cleaning-as-inspection moment (a slow leak found while wiping) and a genuine Safety moment (chemicals stored low, within a child's reach). Fresh, not the Chapter 2 drawer.

**Design continuity:** Same system as Chapters 1 and 2. Warm paper, Fraunces display, Newsreader body, terracotta and honey accents, slate for data and for Safety. Green calm-dot and red friction-spark vocabulary returns. The friction meter appears at the close.

**New hero motif introduced:** **the 6S Loop**. A six-node cycle (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain) with each step handing the next one something, and Sustain closing back to Sort. Safety rendered in slate to echo Chapter 2's growth diagram. This loop becomes a recurring callback device the Part openers (Parts 3 through 8) can reuse, lighting up one node at a time.

**Friction meter call:** first honest movement, a small step from rest toward calm. The chapter ends with one real small space actually done, so the needle has earned a real, modest move. We stay honest about scale: small space, small move. This is the pivot Chapter 2 promised ("Chapter 3 is where it starts to move").

## Page-by-Page Layout

### PAGE 1 (recto) | Chapter Opener
- **Spread objective:** Open inside a tiny, recognizable disaster so the abstract phrase "the six steps" lands on a space the reader already owns.
- **Layout:** Full-bleed image. Title lower third. Kicker "Part 1 - Discovering 6S."
- **Visual:** [VISUAL: Full-page photo] The cabinet under a kitchen sink, doors open, seen straight on. Half-used spray bottles, a crusted sponge, three brands of trash bags, a forgotten bucket, a faint water stain at the back. Honest, not staged-messy.
- **Caption:** "Six steps. One small cabinet. By the end of this chapter you will have watched the whole method walk through a space exactly like this one."

### PAGE 2-3 (Spread A) | The Cabinet That Went Feral
- **Spread objective:** Drop into the opening scene. Name the feeling. Make the promise: we walk all six steps through this one cabinet before we slow down for any of them.
- **Layout:** Left page narrative. Right page the "before" illustration with red friction-sparks marking each snag.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Opening" plus the top of "One Loop, Walked Once," roughly 480 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Hand-drawn under-sink cabinet, before] The same cabinet as a warm line drawing, red friction-sparks on the snag points (the leak, the chemical bottle down low, the tangle).
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > The order is not bureaucracy. It is closer to physics. You cannot give something a home until you know it is staying, and you cannot see what a space is hiding until it is empty and clean.

### PAGE 4-5 (Spread B) | Six Steps, One Loop
- **Spread objective:** Show the whole shape at once. Introduce the loop, the idea of handoffs, and Safety's place in the middle. This is the map for the whole chapter and a good chunk of the book.
- **Layout:** Short intro text, then the hero loop infographic dominating the spread.
- **Body copy:** "One Loop, Walked Once" continued, roughly 400 words.
- **Visual:** [INFOGRAPHIC] "The 6S Loop." Six nodes in a ring, clockwise: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety (slate), Standardize, Sustain. Short handoff verbs riding the arrows. Sustain loops back to Sort.
- **Boxed definition:**
  > **The loop in one line**
  > Sort makes room. Straighten gives things a home. Shine reveals the truth. Safety protects the people. Standardize makes the good way the easy way. Sustain keeps it alive. Then it goes around again.

### PAGE 6-7 (Spread C) | Sort, then Straighten
- **Spread objective:** Walk the first two steps on the cabinet. Sort empties and decides. Straighten assigns homes by frequency. Show the handoff: Sort makes the room that Straighten needs.
- **Layout:** Text with a small two-panel spot illustration (cabinet emptied onto a towel, then cabinet with a caddy and frequency zones).
- **Body copy:** "Sort: Make Room" and "Straighten: Give It a Home," roughly 520 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Two small panels] Panel 1, everything pulled out onto a towel, sorted into keep, belongs-elsewhere, and gone. Panel 2, the cabinet with a caddy up front and a clear empty zone where the leak can be seen.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Buying bins before you sort. The container aisle is step two's reward, not step one's shortcut. Containers bought too early just give your clutter a nicer place to live.

### PAGE 8-9 (Spread D) | Shine, and the Truth It Tells
- **Spread objective:** The memorable beat. Cleaning is partly inspection. Wiping out the empty cabinet reveals the slow leak that the clutter was hiding. "I never looked at cleaning that way."
- **Layout:** Text left, a close-up spot illustration of the wipe revealing a dark water stain and a swollen patch of board.
- **Body copy:** "Shine: Clean Until It Talks," roughly 420 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Close-up] A hand with a cloth wiping the cabinet floor, a dark ring and a soft warped patch appearing where the cloth has passed. One small red friction-spark on the stain.
- **Callout:**
  > **Common Mistake**
  > Cleaning fast to get it over with. Clean slowly enough to notice. The leak, the crumb trail, the loose hinge, the rust ring: Shine is the moment a space finally tells you the truth.

### PAGE 10-11 (Spread E) | Safety: Now Look Low
- **Spread objective:** Safety as the fourth S, riding on the back of the first three. The empty, clean cabinet makes the hazard obvious: the drain cleaner sits at a toddler's eye level. You only saw it because Sort emptied it and Shine cleaned it.
- **Layout:** Text with a small before/after Safety spot (chemical low with a spark, then chemical moved up high with a calm-dot and a simple latch on the door).
- **Body copy:** "Safety: Now Look Low," roughly 420 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Safety swap] Left, a drain-cleaner bottle low with a red spark and a small reaching hand. Right, the bottle moved to a high shelf with a green calm-dot, and a child latch on the cabinet door.
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Open one low cabinet and crouch to a child's height. Anything down here that could burn, poison, or cut belongs up high. Moving one bottle is a real Safety step, done in under a minute.

### PAGE 12-13 (Spread F) | Standardize, then Sustain
- **Spread objective:** Lock the gain and keep it. Standardize makes the good arrangement the obvious default (a photo taped inside the door, the caddy that answers "where does this go"). Sustain is the tiny recurring habit (the ten-second reset, the monthly glance for the leak).
- **Layout:** Text with a spot illustration of the inside-the-door photo label and a small "reset" loop.
- **Body copy:** "Standardize: Make the Good Way the Easy Way" and "Sustain: Keep It Alive," roughly 500 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Standard and reset] The open cabinet door with a small printed photo and label taped inside, and a tiny circular "10-second reset" arrow over the caddy.
- **Callout:**
  > **Family Challenge**
  > Everyone picks one small space and walks it through all six steps this week. At dinner, each person reports the single thing Shine revealed: the hidden leak, the expired jar, the long-lost remote. Best discovery wins.

### PAGE 14-15 (Spread G) | How the Steps Hand Off
- **Spread objective:** Consolidate the chapter's core idea. The power is in the handoffs. Each step hands the next one something it could not have done alone. Then show the before and after of the cabinet so the abstract loop lands on the concrete space.
- **Layout:** A tight synthesis section, then a before/after illustration of the cabinet across the spread.
- **Body copy:** "How Each Step Hands Off the Next," roughly 360 words.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Before / After split] Left, the original cabinet covered in red friction-sparks. Right, the same cabinet, sorted, with a caddy, the leak fixed, the chemical up high, the door label, and green calm-dots throughout.
- **Callout:**
  > **6S Tip**
  > If a space keeps sliding back, look for the step you skipped. A drawer that will not stay neat was usually never truly sorted. A standard that nobody follows usually skipped the part where you made the right way the easy way.

### PAGE 16-17 (Spread H) | Your First Lap
- **Spread objective:** Turn understanding into a single, real, small action. Invite the reader to pick one small space and walk it through the loop now, at compressed scale. This is the lap around the track that earns the meter its first move.
- **Layout:** A short, encouraging section, a compact "one small space, six steps" recap, and a checklist.
- **Body copy:** "Your First Lap," roughly 320 words.
- **Checklist:**
  > **One small space, six steps**
  > - Empty it. Keep, relocate, or remove each item. (Sort)
  > - Put back only what stays, easy things in front. (Straighten)
  > - Wipe it out, and notice what the mess was hiding. (Shine)
  > - Crouch low. Move anything dangerous up and away. (Safety)
  > - Make the good arrangement obvious for next time. (Standardize)
  > - Pick one tiny habit that protects it. (Sustain)
- **Callout:**
  > **Quick Win**
  > Set a fifteen-minute timer and point it at one drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink. Do not aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap. The first lap is the one that teaches your hands the whole method.

### PAGE 18 (verso) | Chapter Close and What's Next
- **Spread objective:** Land warmly, mark the pivot from understanding to doing, tee up Chapter 4.
- **Layout:** Short close, "One Idea to Keep" box, friction-meter spot (first small move), "Next" strip.
- **Body copy:** "Chapter Close," roughly 240 words.
- **Boxed recap:**
  > **One Idea to Keep**
  > Each step makes the next one possible. That is why 6S is an order, not a menu. You do not pick your favorite three. You walk the loop, and the loop does the work.
- **Visual:** [ILLUSTRATION: Friction-meter spot] The meter taking its first small step from rest toward calm, with a note that this was a small space, so it is a small move, and the bigger moves start when the reader chooses a real target in Chapter 4.
- **Next teaser:** "Next: Chapter 4 - How to Choose Your First Target Area. The loop works on anything, which is exactly why the first question is where to point it. We will pick a target you can win."

## Production Notes / Review Flags
- Demonstrate, do not list. The whole chapter rides one cabinet so the loop is shown working, not described. Keep the cabinet consistent across every figure (same leak, same caddy, same bottle moved up).
- Friction meter takes its first small honest move here. Confirm we are comfortable with that read of Chapter 2's promise. If review prefers the needle to keep holding until Part 2, the close text and meter art swap easily.
- Two steps share several spreads to keep pace (Sort plus Straighten, Standardize plus Sustain). Parts 3 through 8 are where each step gets its own deep treatment, so the brief two-up pairing here is intentional, not a shortcut.
- Reuse Chapters 1 and 2's exact type, palette, and callout styling. The loop motif should be drawn so a single node can be highlighted later at each Part opener.
