# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 3 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 3 adds a specific job: it builds momentum. It turns six understood words into a method the reader can feel in their own hands, and it rewards starting over perfection. The voice must stay encouraging and concrete.

## Voice fingerprint (from the final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," uses a space the reader already owns (the cabinet under the sink).
- **Encouraging, not demanding.** "Do not aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap."
- **Plain over technical.** Explains the loop and the handoffs without jargon.
- **Concrete and specific.** A sponge that has given up on life, a bucket with a crack, a dollar latch, a photo taped inside the door.
- **Gently witty.** "A rag bag that has tripled in size through some process nobody witnessed."
- **Honest about scope.** Small space, small move. The friction meter earns only a modest first move.

## Specific voice wins
- The handoff idea is preserved everywhere, which matters because it is the chapter's real subject, not the six words. "Each step hands the next one something it could not have done alone" carries into LinkedIn, X, the carousel, and the discussion questions.
- "A plumber would have charged you to discover this. Your sponge found it for free." survives intact as the chapter's most memorable line, across video, social, and a quote card.
- "6S is a loop, not a menu" is used as a clean, repeatable refrain.

## Voice risks to watch
- **Contractions on X.** The book uses few; X uses several. Deliberate platform choice. Revise only if brand guidelines forbid contractions everywhere.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the sign-offs so they do not feel templated.
- **The word "transform."** It is on the avoid-list, but it is also in the literal chapter title. Every asset keeps it confined to the title, never as filler. Confirmed clean.
- **Emoji on Facebook.** Used sparingly. Confirm it matches your brand's emoji policy.

## Banned-language audit
- No em dashes anywhere (verified after fixing the newsletter subject-lines file, zero hits).
- No en dashes used as separators.
- No prohibited hype words as filler. "Transform" only in the title.
- No "in today's fast-paced world" or similar.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all twelve packages, and the momentum-building job of Chapter 3 is honored throughout. Approve, with the CTA-rotation and contractions notes above.
