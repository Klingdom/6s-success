# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 3 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it.

## High-frequency elements (used in many assets)

| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The feral under-sink cabinet | LinkedIn post 1, X thread 1, FB longform, YouTube open, podcast open, landing intro, before/after graphic, image prompt | A follower on several platforms sees the same opening repeatedly | Calendar staggers these across Days 1 to 10. Each asset retells it slightly differently. |
| "Your sponge found it for free" (the leak) | LinkedIn post, FB post, short video, quote card, podcast | Medium | The most shareable line in the chapter. Spread it out; do not stack it twice in one week on one channel. |
| The 6S Loop graphic and "loop, not a menu" | Hero infographic, carousel, deck, LinkedIn post, X post | Medium | The loop is the chapter's hero motif and a recurring book device, so repetition reinforces. Vary the caption. |
| The fifteen-minute-lap CTA | Most assets (it is the hero action) | Low to Medium | This is the chapter's main conversion event, so frequent use is intended. Vary the wording. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings from the CTA bank. Most likely thing to feel templated. |
| "Crouch to a toddler's height" (Safety) | LinkedIn post, FB post, short video, quote card, discussion question | Low | Strong, specific beat. Used across formats but not stacked on the same day. |

## Same-day collision check
The calendar was built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. Confirmed clear at the planned spacing. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push FB post 1 (Day 4) to Day 5 so the feral-cabinet hook does not land twice in one day.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/`) cover the same six-steps content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version and the email newsletter version share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, etc.) to point back with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 3)
- This chapter and Chapter 2 both name and discuss the six S's. When both are live, keep their social angles distinct. Chapter 2's angle is "what 6S is and where it came from." Chapter 3's angle is "watch the loop work on one cabinet, and the handoffs are the real lesson." Do not run both chapters' "meet the six steps" content in the same week.
- The cabinet under the sink (Chapter 3) and the bottle-opener drawer (Chapter 2) are distinct hero spaces by design. Keep them distinct in graphics so the two chapters do not blur.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- Low. Nothing is borrowed from outside the book. All quotes are the author's own words.
- One graphics item (the skipped-step diagnostic diagram) extends the chapter's idea rather than reproducing a drawn figure. It is labeled. Keep that label if you publish it as "based on" the chapter.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 social angles distinct when both are live.
5. Lead the graphics rollout with the 6S Loop, since it recurs at every later Part opener in the book.
