# Your Sponge Can Find a Leak a Plumber Would Charge You For

Hi, and welcome back.

I want to tell you about a cabinet. The one under your kitchen sink, where the dish soap lives next to a dead flashlight, a bottle of something blue from a previous decade, and a sponge that has clearly given up on life.

That cabinet did not become a disaster on purpose. It happened one item at a time, each one perfectly reasonable, none of them ever leaving. And here is what most people miss about it. It is not even that full. The problem is not the amount of stuff. The problem is that nothing in there has a job, a home, or a reason. It stopped being storage and became a place where things land.

Not filthy. Not overflowing. Just feral, quietly running itself badly while you live your life. Most trouble spots in a home are exactly like this.

## The part people get wrong

When folks meet 6S, they treat the six steps like a menu. They like the sound of Sort, they enjoy a good Shine, and they quietly skip the ones that sound like homework.

It never works, and the reason is simple. The steps are not six separate chores that happen to start with the same letter. They are a chain. Each one sets up the next. You cannot give something a home until you know it is staying. You cannot see what a space is hiding until it is empty and clean.

The order is not bureaucracy. It is closer to physics.

So the real subject is not the six words. You already know those. The real subject is the handoffs between them, the moment one step passes the next something it could not have done alone.

## One slow lap around the cabinet

Sort empties the cabinet onto a towel. Keep, relocate, or release. You are not organizing yet, you are deciding, and Sort just made every other step smaller. It hands Straighten an empty space.

Straighten puts back only the keepers, daily things at the front where your hand lands without looking. Tidy means lined up. Easy means you reach what you need first. It hands Shine a clear view.

Now Shine, which is the step that surprised me most. Shine is not really about clean. It is about inspection. As the cloth reaches the back corner, the wood is darker, a little soft, and a faint ring gives when you press it. That is a slow leak the clutter had hidden for months. You did not find it by looking for leaks. You found it because you were cleaning slowly, with your hands already there. A plumber would have charged you to discover this. Your sponge found it for free.

Then Safety. Stay crouched, because now you are at a toddler's height, and from down there the drain cleaner is at grabbing level behind a door that does not lock. Move it up. A latch costs about a dollar. You only saw the danger because the first three steps cleared the way to it.

Standardize is a thirty-second photo taped inside the door, so the right arrangement stops living in your busy head and becomes a picture anyone can match.

And Sustain is tiny. Ten seconds when you close the door, a monthly glance at the leak. Then it loops back to Sort, because a home is alive and its contents keep changing.

## The one thing to carry out

If a space keeps sliding back into chaos no matter how many times you redo it, you did not fail at willpower. You skipped a step. A drawer that will not stay neat was usually never truly sorted. Find the skipped step, and the space stops fighting you.

So here is my ask. Pick one small space today. One drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink. Set a fifteen-minute timer and walk all six steps once. Do not aim for perfect. Aim for one full lap. The goal is just to feel all six handoffs once, in your own hands.
