# The Cabinet Under Your Sink Went Feral, and It Can Teach You the Whole Method

The cabinet under your kitchen sink did not become a disaster on purpose. Nobody decided one morning to store the dish soap next to a dead flashlight, a bottle of something blue from a previous decade, and a sponge that has clearly given up on life.

It happened the way these things always happen. One item at a time, each one perfectly reasonable, none of them ever leaving. And somewhere under all of it, a slow drip has been quietly warping the wood for months.

Here is what I want you to notice. That cabinet is not even that full. The problem is not the amount of stuff. The problem is that nothing in there has a job, a home, or a reason. It stopped being storage and became a place where things land. Most trouble spots in a home are like this. Not filthy. Not overflowing. Just feral.

So let me walk you through the fix. All six steps, in order, on this one small cabinet.

## 6S is a loop, not a menu

People new to 6S treat the steps like a menu. They like the sound of Sort, they enjoy a good Shine, and they quietly skip the ones that sound like homework. This never works. The steps are not six separate chores that happen to start with the same letter. They are a chain. Each one sets up the next.

The order is not bureaucracy. It is closer to physics. You cannot give something a home until you know it is staying, and you cannot see what a space is hiding until it is empty and clean. Do the steps in order and each one makes the next one easier. The work compounds. By the end, the space almost finishes itself.

The real secret is not the six words. It is the handoffs between them. The moment Sort passes Straighten an empty space. The moment Shine hands Safety a clear view of the danger.

## One slow lap

**Sort: make room.** Take everything out onto a towel on the floor. Out on the towel, alone, each thing has to answer one honest question: do I actually use this? Most of it cannot pass. Keep, relocate, or release. You are not organizing yet. You are deciding. Sort makes every other step smaller, and it just handed you an empty cabinet.

**Straighten: give it a home.** Put back only the keepers, and let frequency of use decide where things go. Daily things at the front, where your hand lands without looking. Monthly things toward the back. Tidy means everything looks lined up. Easy means the thing you need most is the thing you reach first. Leave the back corner clear. That matters more than you think.

**Shine: clean until it talks.** Wipe it out. But Shine is not really about clean. Shine is about inspection. Watch what happens as the cloth reaches that clear back corner. The wood is darker. A little soft. A faint ring stains the board, and when you press it, it gives. That is the slow drip the clutter was hiding for months. You did not find it by looking. You found it because you were cleaning slowly, with your hands already there. A plumber would have charged you to discover this. Your sponge found it for free.

**Safety: now look low.** Stay crouched, because you are at the height of a two-year-old. From down here, that bottle of drain cleaner is not in storage. It is on display, at grabbing height, behind a door that does not lock. You could not see this clearly until now. So you fix it. The poison goes up high. A latch costs about a dollar and clicks shut in a minute. At work, somebody is paid to spot these hazards. At home, the inspector is you.

**Standardize: make the good way the easy way.** The cabinet will not stay nice on its own, and willpower is not the answer. Take a quick photo of the finished cabinet, print it small, and tape it inside the door. Now the standard is not a memory. It is a picture anyone can match, including future you at the end of a long day.

**Sustain: keep it alive.** A standard that needs a weekly overhaul was a bad standard. Every time you pull out the spray bottle, take ten seconds to put back anything that wandered. Once a month, glance at the corner where the leak was. And here is the part that makes 6S a loop. Sustain does not lead to a tidy ending. It leads back to Sort, because a home is alive and its contents keep changing.

## The skipped step

The magic was never in any single step. It was in the chain. Skip Sort and you straighten junk. Skip Shine and you miss the leak and the hazard both. Skip Standardize and the space drifts back the first busy week.

This is also how you fix a space that will not stay fixed. When a drawer keeps sliding back into chaos no matter how many times you redo it, you have not failed at willpower. You skipped a step. A drawer that will not stay neat was usually never truly sorted. Find the skipped step, and the space stops fighting you.

## Your first lap

The fastest way to make this yours is to stop reading and take one lap today, on a space small enough that you cannot lose. Not the garage. Not the closet that haunts you. One drawer, one shelf, or the cabinet under your own sink.

Set a fifteen-minute timer. The goal is not a finished, spotless home. The goal is to feel all six handoffs once, in your own hands. It will not be perfect, and it does not need to be. Perfect Sorting and nothing else is just a very tidy pile of unfinished work.
