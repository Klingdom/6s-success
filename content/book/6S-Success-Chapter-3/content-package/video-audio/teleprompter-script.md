# Teleprompter Script: The Six Steps That Transform Any Space

**Chapter 3, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Roughly 650 words. About 4 to 5 minutes spoken. Narration only.**
**Read it warm and unhurried. Short lines, one breath each.**

---

The cabinet under your kitchen sink

did not become a disaster on purpose.

Nobody decided to store the dish soap

next to a dead flashlight

and a bottle of something blue

from a previous decade.

It happened one item at a time.

Each one perfectly reasonable.

None of them ever leaving.

---

And here is the strange part.

It is not even dirty.

It is not even that full.

The problem is that nothing in there

has a job, a home, or a reason.

It stopped being storage

and became a place where things land.

Most trouble spots in a home are like that.

Not filthy. Not overflowing.

Just feral.

---

There is a way to fix it.

Six steps.

Sort, Straighten, Shine,

Safety, Standardize, Sustain.

But most people treat them like a menu.

They do the ones they like

and skip the ones that sound like homework.

That never works.

Because these six steps are not separate chores.

They are a chain.

Each one sets up the next.

---

You cannot give something a home

until you know it is staying.

You cannot clean around a pile.

And you will miss the hazards

hiding behind the clutter.

So picture the steps as a loop.

Walk it once, in order,

and each step makes the next one easier.

The work compounds.

By the end, the space almost finishes itself.

---

Let me show you.

Sort.

Take everything out onto a towel,

where you can see it in daylight.

Alone, each bottle has to answer one question.

Do I actually use this?

Keep, relocate, or release.

You are not organizing yet.

You are deciding.

---

Straighten.

Give each survivor a home,

by how often you use it.

Daily things go to the front,

where your hand lands without looking.

Tidy means everything looks lined up.

Easy means the thing you need most

is the thing you reach first.

---

Shine.

Wipe it out, slowly.

Because Shine is not really about clean.

Shine is about inspection.

And as the cloth reaches the back corner,

the wood is darker.

A little soft.

Press it, and it gives.

That is a slow leak,

hidden by the clutter for months.

A plumber would have charged you

to discover this.

Your sponge found it for free.

---

Safety.

Now stay crouched.

Because down here,

you are at the height of a two-year-old.

And that drain cleaner,

sitting on the cabinet floor,

is not in storage.

It is on display,

at grabbing height.

So move it up.

Anything that can burn, poison, or cut

goes high, behind a latch.

A dollar. A minute.

At home, the inspector is you.

---

Standardize.

The cabinet will not stay nice

on willpower alone.

So take a photo of the finished cabinet

and tape it inside the door.

Now the right way is not a memory.

It is a picture anyone can match.

---

Sustain.

Keep it alive with something tiny.

A ten-second reset before you close the door.

A monthly glance at the corner

where the leak was.

And here is what makes it a loop.

Sustain does not end the work.

It hands you back to Sort.

Because a home is alive,

and its contents keep changing.

---

So that is the whole method.

Each step makes the next one possible.

That is why 6S is an order,

not a menu.

You do not pick your favorite three.

You walk the loop,

and the loop does the work.

---

Now it is your turn.

Pick one small space.

A drawer. A shelf.

Or the cabinet under your own sink.

Set a fifteen-minute timer.

Walk all six steps once.

Do not aim for perfect.

Aim for one full lap.

Because a perfect Sort and nothing else

is just a very tidy pile

of unfinished work.

One full, imperfect lap

teaches your hands the whole method.

---

It is all in Chapter 3

of 6S Success, Home Edition.

Read it free in the online book.

Then go set your timer.

Your first lap is waiting.
