# Teaching Slide Deck Outline

**Chapter 1 - Why Some Homes Feel Effortless**
*6S Success: Home Edition - Part One, Discovering 6S*

A 13-slide teaching deck for a workshop, webinar, or read-along session. Each slide lists a title, a few short bullets, and a visual note tied to the book's design system. Tone stays warm and low-pressure, the same as the chapter. The goal is relief, not pressure.

## Slide 2 - Two Tuesdays, Same Morning

- 7:42 on a Tuesday. Two families try to get out the door.
- Home one has a rhythm: bags on hooks, shoes paired, keys in their three-year dish. Door closes at 7:55.
- Home two is a search party: no dish for keys, one shoe missing, the permission slip lost in the paper drifting across the chairs. They leave at 8:09, frayed.

## Slide 3 - The Part That Matters

- The second family is not lazier, messier, or less loving.
- Most mornings they are doing more, not less, on a problem the first family quietly solved.
- The first family did not get lucky. They built a system.

[VISUAL] Hold on the right (frantic) panel, then dim it and let the left (calm) panel brighten. A single honey underline under the word "system." 6S Tip strip along the bottom in slate: "The calm home is not cleaner this morning. It is better designed."

## Slide 4 - Effortless Is a System, Not a Personality

- We explain other homes with personality: "she is just naturally organized."
- We explain our own less kindly: "I am just a messy person."
- This is the most common belief about home order, and it is almost entirely wrong. Organization is the visible result of a system running quietly in the background.

[VISUAL] The two-theories diagram. Left: Willpower, a faded loop arrow circling back to a red tangle of mess. Right: Systems, a steady slate line with a row of green calm-dots and check marks. Caption beneath: willpower runs out, a system holds.

## Slide 5 - The Right Action Becomes the Easy Action

- The key dish is effortless not because of superior character. The dish is right there.
- It costs no thought and half a second.
- The other family is asked to invent a brand new plan for the keys every single day, while tired. Of course it breaks down.

[VISUAL] Close-up of the slate key dish on the entry table with a small green calm-dot above it. Beside it, a faint red spark hovering over an empty counter where no dish exists. Common Mistake strip in friction-spark color: "Believing you need more discipline. Usually you need fewer decisions."

## Slide 6 - Why Homes Quietly Get Harder

- A home is not a finished thing. Objects flow in every week: mail, groceries, school papers, the small plastic mystery.
- Stuff flows in fast, but systems update slowly. New things arrive daily. New homes for them almost never do.
- Every object without a home asks a question. A home full of unanswered questions is not dirty, it is loud, and that hum is tiring.

[VISUAL] The junk-drawer stages band: four drawers left to right, EMPTY, USEFUL, "JUST FOR NOW," and ???. Honey arrows between them. The last drawer tangles into chaos with a small red spark in the corner. Caption: no single step is a failure, it accumulates.

## Slide 7 - It Is Not Mess. It Is Friction.

- Set down the word "messy" and the guilt that rides along with it.
- What you are seeing is a system that has not kept up with your life.
- That is a fixable thing. In fact, fixing it is the rest of the book.

[VISUAL] A drawer photo with the word "MESSY" stamped across it, then crossed out in slate and replaced by "FRICTION" in terracotta. Quick Win strip in green: "Name one surface that collects 'I'll deal with it later' items. You just found your first project."

## Slide 8 - Cleaning vs Organizing vs 6S

- Cleaning removes dirt. Lasts hours. A clean mess is still a mess.
- Organizing arranges your stuff. Lasts weeks, then quietly drifts back, because it arranges without building the system that keeps it arranged.
- 6S is the missing part: a system you build into a room so it stays sorted, clean, and safe, and gets a little better over time, with less effort.

[VISUAL] The shelf-life bars. Three stacked bars on the same track: Cleaning, a short honey bar (hours). Organizing, a medium terracotta bar (weeks). 6S, a full green bar on a slate card with an arrow pushing further right (lasts and improves). Caption: same effort, very different shelf life.

## Slide 10 - Meet the Six S's

- Six steps, each a single word starting with S. No mastery required yet. Just shake hands.
- Sort: keep what belongs, remove what does not. Straighten: a home for everything, by how often you use it. Shine: clean in a way that reveals problems early.
- Safety: protect the people who use the space first. Standardize: lock in the best way you know today. Sustain: keep the gains, keep improving, gently.
- The order is not decoration. The sequence is the method.

[VISUAL] The six-S loop. Six tiles in two rows of three, numbered 1 to 6. Terracotta number badges on Sort, Straighten, Shine; slate on Safety, Standardize; green on Sustain. A dashed honey loop arrow runs from Sustain back to Sort. Footer: "And then you go around again, a little better each time."

## Slide 11 - Spot the Friction

- Friction is the small daily resistance a space adds to your life.
- The half-second of hunting. The extra trip across the room. The "where does this go" pause. It hides because it is never dramatic.
- Once you can see friction, you cannot unsee it, and that turns out to be a wonderful problem to have.

[VISUAL] The find-the-friction scene: an ordinary kitchen, entry, and living room with eight red numbered friction-spark markers (keys on the counter, cable nest, mail stack, shoes in the walkway, remote on the floor, coat over the chair, drop-zone table, the wandering scissors). Invite the room to find them before the key appears.

## Slide 12 - Your First Move

- Do not reorganize your house this weekend. Please do not. Big bursts end in a bigger mess and a sore back.
- Choose one small area. One drawer, the table by the door, a single counter. Take an honest before photo.
- Do not tidy first. The mess is the point. An honest before is what makes the after mean something.
- Save it in a folder named "6S Before," with the date. Under five minutes, no cleaning required.

[VISUAL] Three "BEFORE" photo frames (a cluttered drawer, an entry table, a bathroom counter), each with a small slate BEFORE tab in the corner. A short checklist card beside them in the dashed-border checklist style from the book.
