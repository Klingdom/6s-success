# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 1 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it.

## High-frequency elements (used in many assets)

| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The two Tuesdays (7:55 vs 8:09) | LinkedIn post 1, X thread 1, FB longform, YouTube open, podcast open, landing intro, two-Tuesdays infographic, image prompt | A follower on several platforms sees the same opening repeatedly | Calendar staggers these across Days 1 to 8. Each asset retells it slightly differently. |
| "System, not a personality" | LinkedIn post, X post, newsletter hook, short video, quote card, deck title | Medium | Spread across the two weeks. It is the core claim, so some repetition reinforces. |
| "Fewer decisions, not more discipline" | LinkedIn post, FB post, X post, quote card, comment prompt | Medium | Use as a recurring refrain, not twice in one week on the same channel. |
| The before-photo CTA | Most assets (it is the hero action) | Low to Medium | This is the chapter's main conversion event, so frequent use is intended. Vary the wording. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate phrasings from the CTA bank. Most likely thing to feel templated. |
| "Rooms are not messy people" | LinkedIn post, FB group, X post, quote card, discussion question | Low | Strong line, used across formats but not stacked on the same day. |

## Same-day collision check
The calendar was built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. Confirmed clear at the planned spacing. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push FB post 1 (Day 4) to Day 5 so the two-Tuesdays story does not land twice in one day.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/`) cover the same cleaning-vs-organizing-vs-6S content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version and the email newsletter version share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, etc.) to point back with a canonical link or a "first published at" note.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 1)
- This chapter introduces the six S's, which Chapter 2 also covers. When both chapters are live, make sure their social assets do not run the same "meet the six words" angle in the same week. Chapter 1's angle is "systems vs personality." Chapter 2's is "where 6S came from." Keep those distinct so the two chapters do not compete.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- Low. Nothing is borrowed from outside the book. All quotes are the author's own words from the final HTML.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 social angles distinct when both are live.
5. If LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap, add a day of spacing between the two two-Tuesdays posts.
