# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions

*Chapter 1: Why Some Homes Feel Effortless*
*6S Success: Home Edition, Part One, Discovering 6S*

These questions are open-ended. There are no right answers. They work for a book club, a family conversation, or a quiet think on your own.

1. The chapter opens with two families on the same Tuesday morning. Which home felt more like yours, and what was the first detail that made you recognize it?

2. The book says the calm family did not get lucky, they built a system, and that effortless is a system, not a personality. Where in your own life have you explained someone's tidy home with their personality? Where have you explained your own home that way, only less kindly?

3. The author argues you usually do not need more discipline, you need fewer decisions. Does that ring true for you, or does it let you off the hook too easily? Talk about a moment when willpower ran out at the same time it always does.

4. Walk through your own home for a minute. Where is the friction, the small daily resistance, the half-second of hunting or the extra trip across the room? Name one or two specific spots out loud.

5. The chapter separates cleaning, organizing, and 6S. Before reading, did you treat cleaning and organizing as the same thing? Where do you see the difference in your own house?

6. Think about a time you organized a space beautifully and watched it drift back within a few weeks. The book says that was not because you are bad at this. What do you now think was actually missing?

7. The junk drawer forms one reasonable "for now" at a time. Pick a drawer, a surface, or a chair in your home and trace its history. What were the small, sensible decisions that built the pile?

8. The author writes that a home full of unanswered questions is not dirty, it is loud, and that hum is tiring in a way that is hard to name. Have you felt that hum? What does it feel like for you?

9. The first move is one honest before photo, with no tidying first, because a staged before is a lie you tell only to yourself. How do you feel about photographing a small messy area exactly as it is? What makes that easy or hard?

10. The chapter ends by saying none of the friction in your house is evidence of a flaw in you, it is only a system waiting to be built. How does it change things to look at your home that way instead of as a personal failing?
