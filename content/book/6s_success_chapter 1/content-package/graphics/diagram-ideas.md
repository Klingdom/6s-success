# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 1

*6S Success: Home Edition, "Why Some Homes Feel Effortless"*

Five diagram concepts, each remixing or extending a figure that already lives in the chapter. All use the book palette (paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, green `#6E8B5B`, spark `#CB4B36`) and the flat hand-drawn line style of the SVG figures.

---

## 1. The Shelf-Life Bars (cleaning vs organizing vs 6S)

**Purpose:** Show the single most useful idea in the chapter, that cleaning, organizing, and 6S are three different things with very different staying power.

**What it shows:** Three stacked "how long it lasts" bars on one shared scale, so the contrast is immediate.
- Cleaning, honey `#DDA63A` fill, bar reaches about one-eighth across. Label: "Hours. Then you do it again."
- Organizing, terracotta `#BC4B2A` fill, bar reaches about four-tenths across. Label: "Weeks. Then it quietly drifts back."
- 6S, on a slate `#3C5A6B` card, green `#6E8B5B` fill running the full width with a small arrowhead pushing past the end. Label: "It lasts, and it keeps getting better."

**Rough layout:** Three horizontal rows stacked top to bottom. Left column holds the name in Fraunces and a one-line definition in Newsreader (Removes dirt / Arranges your stuff / Builds a system). Right four-fifths holds the bar on a muted `#EDE3CE` track. The 6S row inverts to a dark slate card so it visually wins. Footer line in Inter: "Same effort, very different shelf life."

---

## 2. Every Object Asks a Question

**Purpose:** Make the "loud home" idea visible, that clutter is not dirt, it is a pile of unanswered decisions.

**What it shows:** A countertop line drawing with three or four homeless objects, each with a small speech bubble holding its real question, pulled from the chapter.
- A charger: "Where do I go?"
- A stack of mail: "Are any of us important?"
- A lone AA battery: "Am I full or empty?"
- A spare key: "Just for now?"
Each bubble is outlined in spark red `#CB4B36`. A small caption ties it together: "Each question is tiny. They do not leave. They pile up."

**Rough layout:** Horizontal counter line across the lower third in wood `#E7C58B`. Objects sit on it in simple ink line art, evenly spaced. Question bubbles float above each, staggered in height so they do not collide. Title top-left in Fraunces: "A home can get loud." This extends the chapter's friction theme into a new, quotable image.

---

## 3. The Six S Loop, simplified to a ring

**Purpose:** A cleaner, more shareable version of the chapter's six-S loop figure, sized for social rather than a full book spread.

**What it shows:** The six steps arranged as a circle with a honey `#DDA63A` dashed arrow flowing clockwise and looping back to the start, making the "go around again" point obvious.
- Six nodes around the ring, each a numbered dot and a single word: 1 Sort, 2 Straighten, 3 Shine (terracotta dots), 4 Safety, 5 Standardize (slate dots), 6 Sustain (green dot). The color shift from terracotta to slate to green signals the journey from clearing out to holding steady.
- Center label in Fraunces: "6S" with a small line beneath, Newsreader: "a loop, a little better each time."

**Rough layout:** Single centered ring on panel ground. Each node has its word on the outside of the ring and a four-word gloss under it (Sort: "keep what belongs"). The dashed honey arrow runs just inside the ring. Works square at 1080 x 1080. Keep the one-line definitions short so the ring stays legible at thumbnail size.

---

## 4. Friction Audit Checklist (find-the-friction, made usable)

**Purpose:** Turn the chapter's "find the friction" scene into a thing the reader can actually run in their own home.

**What it shows:** The eight named friction points from the chapter as a tickable checklist, each with its red spark marker and a tiny line-art icon.
1. Keys nowhere near the door
2. Charging cables with no home
3. A stack of mail hiding the one that mattered
4. Shoes in the walkway
5. The remote that lives nowhere
6. A coat over the chair back
7. A drop-zone table
8. Scissors in three rooms and none when needed
Header: "How many can you find in your home right now?" Footer, in green: "Found one? You just found your first project."

**Rough layout:** Two columns of four, dashed-border checklist card in the book's checklist style (`#FBF7EF` ground, `#D9CDB8` dashed border). Each row: an empty slate checkbox, a small spark marker, the item in Newsreader, a faint icon at the right. Portrait 1080 x 1350 suits the eight rows. This bridges the find-the-friction figure and the chapter's first-move assignment.

---

## 5. Before, the First Move (one honest photo)

**Purpose:** Visualize the chapter's only assignment so it feels small and doable, not like a weekend project.

**What it shows:** A single phone frame with a "BEFORE" tag in the corner, pointed at one small, honestly cluttered area (a drawer or the entry table), with three tiny rules of a good before photo arranged around it.
- "Stand in one fixed spot."
- "Frame the whole area."
- "Do not tidy first. The mess is the point."
A small green-dot stamp at the foot: "Under five minutes. No cleaning required."

**Rough layout:** Center the phone frame (ink outline, slate `#3C5A6B` BEFORE tag, matching the book's before-photo figure). The three rules sit as short captioned arrows pointing at the frame from the margins. Title top in Fraunces: "Your first move." Keep it warm and low-pressure. The whole design should say easy, not homework. Pairs naturally with the checklist in idea 4 and the "Small is what lasts" line.
