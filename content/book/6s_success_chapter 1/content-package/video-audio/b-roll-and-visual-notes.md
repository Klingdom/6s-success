# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Why Some Homes Feel Effortless

**Source:** 6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 1
**Slug:** why-some-homes-feel-effortless
**Use with:** the YouTube script, the short-form scripts, and the teleprompter narration.

## Visual identity (carry across every clip)
Warm, lived-in, real. Natural light, a touch of dawn warmth. Not a showroom, not a staged catalog. The chapter's whole job is to remove guilt, so nothing should feel judgmental or "look how perfect this is."

**Motif colors (from the book):**
- Green calm-dots: things that have a home and are working. Color reference: a soft sage green (#6E8B5B).
- Red friction-sparks: points where life snags. Color reference: a warm red (#CB4B36).
- Honey accent for the loop arrow and labels (#DDA63A). Slate blue for structure (#3C5A6B). Paper/cream background (#F7F2E9).

**Recurring graphic devices:**
- A small green dot that gently pulses on over an object that has a home.
- A small red four-point spark that pops over a friction point.
- The Six S loop (six tiles plus a curved arrow back to the start).
- The friction meter (a dial from CALM on the left to FRICTION on the right).

## SHOT LIST

### 1. The calm entryway (hero / opener / closer)
- **Wide:** a real entryway in warm morning light. Hooks with a packed bag, a low basket of paired shoes, a small key dish on a console table, a wall calendar everyone can see.
- **Insert:** a hand drops keys into the dish without looking. Half a second, effortless.
- **Insert:** a kid lifts a packed backpack off a hook and walks out of frame.
- **Insert:** a permission slip clipped to the fridge.
- **Wide:** the door closes, the room settles into quiet.
- **Motif:** green calm-dots pulse on over the dish, the hooks, the basket.
- **Use:** opens the YouTube video and Short 1, and returns at the close.

### 2. The frantic morning search (contrast to #1)
- **Insert:** hands opening and shutting the same three drawers, faster each time.
- **Insert:** one shoe held up, the other nowhere.
- **Insert:** a hand patting down yesterday's coat pocket.
- **Insert:** papers fanned across the dining chairs, getting shuffled.
- **Wide:** the family heading out a beat late, slightly frayed.
- **Motif:** red friction-sparks pop over the counter, the chairs, the empty doorway.
- **Note:** keep it human and a little funny, never shaming. Same family could be either house.

### 3. The junk drawer (how disorder accumulates)
- **Build sequence:** start on an empty, tidy drawer. Drop in one object per beat: a spare key, a takeout menu, batteries, rubber bands, a warranty card. Speed up.
- **Reveal:** a real, full junk drawer slides open.
- **Slow pull-back:** no judgment, just "ah, there it is."
- **Optional graphic:** four-stage band labeled EMPTY, USEFUL, "JUST FOR NOW", ??? to match the book illustration.

### 4. Find-the-friction scene (the eight points)
Build a single room (kitchen, entry, and living area) and reveal a red spark over each point in order. Each can be its own insert for short-form.
1. **Keys on the counter,** nowhere near the door.
2. **A nest of charging cables** with no home.
3. **A stack of mail** hiding the one envelope that mattered.
4. **Shoes in the walkway,** turning the hall into an obstacle course.
5. **The remote on the floor,** because it lives nowhere.
6. **A coat over the chair back,** because there is no hook.
7. **A drop-zone table,** a home for everything and nothing.
8. **Scissors,** appearing in three rooms and none when needed (show two or three identical scissors in different rooms).
- **Motif:** each spark numbered 1 to 8. End wide with all eight glowing.

### 5. Cleaning vs Organizing vs 6S (graphics-led)
- Three cards animate in. Each gets a "how long it lasts" progress bar:
  - CLEANING, removes dirt, bar fills a little, "Hours."
  - ORGANIZING, arranges your stuff, bar fills partway, "Weeks, then it drifts back."
  - 6S, builds a system, bar fills all the way with an arrow continuing past the end, "Lasts, and keeps getting better." Use the slate-blue card for 6S to set it apart.
- **B-roll bed:** washing dishes (cleaning), tidying a cupboard (organizing), a kitchen where things flow back to their spots on their own (6S, implied).

### 6. The Six S loop (graphics-led)
- Six tiles animate in: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain, each with its one-line meaning. A dashed honey arrow curves from Sustain back to Sort.
- Caption line: "And then you go around again, a little better each time."

### 7. The before-photo moment (the first move)
- **Insert:** a phone held up, framing a cluttered drawer. Shutter. Stamp "BEFORE" in the corner.
- **Insert:** a hand reaching to straighten one item, then pulling back. "Do not tidy first."
- **Insert:** the photo saving into a folder titled "6S Before" with today's date.
- **Tone:** honest, real, a little playful about the urge to stage it.

### 8. The friction meter (close)
- A dial graphic, CALM (green) on the left, FRICTION (red) on the right, needle just past the middle.
- Animate the needle ticking one notch toward calm.
- Caption: "One notch calmer already. You have not moved a single object yet."

## EDIT / TONE NOTES
- Cut to the rhythm of the narration, not faster. This is a calm topic; let shots breathe.
- Pair every red spark with the matching narration beat so viewers learn the word "friction" by association.
- Reuse the calm entryway at the open and the close to bookend the piece. Same room, same light.
- Avoid "perfect home" imagery that could make viewers feel worse. Real, a little lived-in, well lit.
