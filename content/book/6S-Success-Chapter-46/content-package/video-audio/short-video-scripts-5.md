# Chapter 46 Short Video Scripts: The Workshop

Five scripts, 30 to 60 seconds each.

## 1. The room it came from
**Hook:** This is the room 6S came from.
**Body:** The outlines, the labels, the ideal-state photo: none of it was invented for pantries. It was invented on shop floors, where a missing tool was a stopped line and a sticky guard was a lost hand.
**Close:** Every other room fails into inconvenience. This one fails into injury.

## 2. Start at the smallest zone
**Hook:** In a workshop, you do the smallest zone first.
**Body:** The PPE station is a board of hooks by the door and it takes under an hour. Everything else in there involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust, so you want your glasses findable before you handle any of it.
**Close:** It is not the reward at the end. It is the permission to begin.

## 3. Never sweep a workshop
**Hook:** Stop sweeping your shop.
**Body:** A broom does not remove the finest sawdust, it launches it. Back into the air, where it settles onto wet finish and into your lungs. And the fines are the fraction that actually burns.
**Close:** Vacuum instead. Empty the extractor at the end of the session, not the start of the next one.

## 4. Three verdicts
**Hook:** Your workshop's mess is not about storage.
**Body:** It is three verdicts you keep postponing: offcuts, half-finished projects, and paint. And you know which you avoid, because you will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once.
**Close:** The zones you keep skipping are the zones holding the room back.

## 5. The guard that degrades silently
**Hook:** Here is the most dangerous thing in a home workshop.
**Body:** A blade guard is a spring-loaded part living in sawdust, and sawdust makes springs sticky. It degrades slowly, silently, and the first time you notice is the time it does not come back.
**Close:** Thumb-test it every clean. If it hesitates, the tool is out of service.
