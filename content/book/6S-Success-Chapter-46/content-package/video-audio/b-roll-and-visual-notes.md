# Chapter 46 B-Roll and Visual Notes: The Workshop

## Must-get shots
- Safety glasses being lifted off a labelled hook by the door, first thing, before anything else in the film.
- Lenses being washed in soapy water and dried on a clean microfiber cloth, deliberately not a shop rag.
- An extinguisher gauge wiped clean, with the needle clearly in the green.
- A thumb working a retracting blade guard open and letting it snap back. Shoot this twice, tight, with the tool unplugged.
- The bench cleared to bare wood, ideally as a time-lapse.
- A vacuum crevice nozzle going into a bench dog hole, and a plastic scraper taking a dried glue drip off flat.
- A drop of oil laid along a vise screw.
- A blade being wiped with resin remover, with the pitch visibly coming off.
- Screws being tipped into a compartment beside a sample screw glued to the drawer face.
- The offcut bin tipped out, one short piece leaving, the fill line visible.
- An oil finish rag going into a self-closing metal can and the lid falling shut on its own weight.

## Do not shoot
- Any tool running. This is a cleaning and inspection film, not a woodworking one.
- Any guard held, taped, or wedged open beyond the moment of the thumb test on an unplugged tool.
- Any readable brand name, logo, or label text. Types only.
- Sweeping. It contradicts the chapter's single strongest instruction.
- Any chemical being decanted, mixed, poured out, or burned. Coated and treated offcuts never go on a fire.
- Anyone in the shop without eye protection, including in the background.

## Tone
Workmanlike and warm. This is a homecoming, so the register can be slightly more affectionate than the other room films, right up until the safety material, which is delivered plainly and once.
