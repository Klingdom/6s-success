# Chapter 46 Key Takeaways: The Workshop

- This is the room 6S came from, and the only one where a bad standard can cost you a finger rather than an afternoon.
- A traced outline is not decoration. It is how you know a chisel is loose somewhere in the room.
- The smallest zone leads. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean, it is the permission to begin.
- Vacuum, never sweep. A broom launches the finest sawdust into the air, where it settles onto wet finish and into your lungs, and the fines are the part that actually burns.
- Empty the dust extractor at the end of a session rather than the start of the next one.
- The workshop's mess is never really about storage. It is three verdicts you keep postponing: offcuts, half-finished projects, and paint.
- You will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once. The zones you keep skipping are the zones holding the room back.
- A rag soaked in oil finish generates its own heat as it cures and can ignite with nothing touching it, so it goes in a lidded metal can.
- A blade guard degrades slowly and silently, because it is a spring-loaded part living in sawdust. Work it with your thumb every time you clean the zone.
- Settle the offcut pile with two limits rather than judgment: a size floor, and one bin with no overflow.
- Decide the visitor rule once and hang it on the wall, because saying it in advance is far easier than saying it while holding a running grinder.
