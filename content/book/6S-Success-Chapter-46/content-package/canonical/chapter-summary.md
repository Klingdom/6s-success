# Chapter 46 Summary: The Workshop

The sixteenth room playbook of Part Nine, and a homecoming: this is the room 6S came from, and the only one in the house where a bad standard can cost you a finger rather than an afternoon. Everywhere else in this book a slipped standard costs time, or a smell, or a morning putting things right. Here it costs a hand, which is why a traced outline is not decoration and a guard that springs back is not tidiness.

The room breaks into six zones, each with its exact inputs and numbered steps: the safety and PPE station, the main workbench, the power tool storage, the fastener and hardware zone, the material rack, and the finishing and chemical zone. The smallest zone leads, which happens nowhere else in the book. Everything in this room involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust, so the glasses need to be findable and the extinguisher gauge green before a Saturday is spent handling any of it. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean; it is the permission to begin.

On top of the nine rules of Shine the room adds four: the stakes here are physical rather than inconvenient; the smallest zone leads; you vacuum rather than sweep, because a broom launches the finest sawdust into the air where it settles onto wet finish and into lungs, and the fines are the fraction that burns; and the mess is never really about storage but about three verdicts the reader keeps postponing, on offcuts, on half-finished projects, and on paint.

That last is the whole diagnosis. Most people will sort the fasteners twice before touching the offcut pile once, because tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress and putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste. The zones you keep skipping are the zones holding the room back. Six hard calls follow, including the visitor rule, the only call in the book about a person rather than an object.
