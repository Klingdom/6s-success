# Chapter 46 Facebook Longform Post: The Workshop

---

This is the room 6S came from.

Everything in this book, the traced outlines and the labels and the photograph of the ideal state, was invented somewhere like this. On a shop floor, by people for whom a missing tool was a stopped line and a sticky guard was a lost hand.

Which makes this chapter a homecoming, and also the one chapter where the stakes are genuinely different.

Every other room in this book fails into inconvenience. A drifted pantry costs you a duplicate jar. A drifted laundry room costs you a second tumble. This room fails into injury.

So the order of work here is unlike anywhere else. You start at the smallest zone in the room, the PPE station, because everything else in a workshop involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust, and you want your glasses findable before you spend a Saturday handling any of it. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean. It is the permission to begin.

Then one instruction that overrides habit: vacuum, never sweep. A broom does not remove the finest sawdust, it launches it, back into the air where it hangs and then settles onto wet finish and into your lungs. And the fines are the fraction that actually burns.

But here is the part that decides whether a workshop actually changes.

The mess in there is not about storage. It is three verdicts you have been postponing: on offcuts, on half-finished projects, and on paint.

And you already know which ones you avoid, because you will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once. Tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress. Putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste. So the drawers get neat while the pile actually holding the room back stays exactly where it was.

The zones you keep skipping are the zones holding the room back. That is the whole diagnosis.

One safety note worth carrying away even if you do nothing else. A blade guard is a spring-loaded part that lives in sawdust, and sawdust is exactly what makes a spring sticky. It degrades slowly, it degrades silently, and the first time you notice is the time it does not come back. Work it with your thumb every time you clean that zone.

Do one zone this week, and make it the PPE station.
