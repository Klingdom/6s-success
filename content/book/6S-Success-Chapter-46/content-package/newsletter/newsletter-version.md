# Chapter 46 Newsletter: The Workshop

**Subject:** The room 6S came from
**Preview:** A homecoming, and the one chapter where the stakes are different.

---

We have come a long way around to arrive here, and it is worth saying plainly: this is the room 6S came from.

Everything you have applied to a pantry and a nursery and a laundry room started on a shop floor, where a tool out of place was not untidy but expensive, and where an outline traced on a board was not a decoration but a way of knowing that a chisel was loose somewhere in the room.

So this chapter is a homecoming, and it is also the one chapter where the stakes are genuinely different. Every other room in this book fails into inconvenience. This one fails into injury.

**The smallest zone leads.** You start at the PPE station, because everything else in here involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust, and you want your glasses findable before you spend a Saturday handling any of it. Do it the other way round and you spend the whole day improvising: working without muffs because they are on the far wall, cutting with hazed glasses pushed up on your forehead. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean. It is the permission to begin.

**Vacuum, never sweep.** A broom does not remove the finest sawdust, it launches it, back into the air where it hangs and then settles onto wet finish and into your lungs. And the fines are the fraction that actually burns. So the vacuum comes out instead of the broom, and the extractor gets emptied at the end of a session rather than the start of the next one.

**And the diagnosis.** The workshop's mess is never really about storage. It is three verdicts you keep postponing: on offcuts, on half-finished projects, and on paint. You already know which ones you avoid, because you will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once. Tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress and putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste, so the drawers get neat while the pile actually holding the room back stays exactly where it was.

Six zones, six hard calls, including the visitor rule, which is the only call in the book about a person rather than an object.

Do one zone this week, and make it the PPE station.
