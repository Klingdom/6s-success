# Chapter 46 Brand Voice Check: The Workshop

Warm, practical, expert, and serious about injury without dramatising it. Second person. This chapter is allowed slightly more affection than the other room chapters, because it is a homecoming, and the register shifts down to plain and level for the safety material.

## Tone risks specific to this chapter, and how they were handled

**Reverence.** The homecoming framing risks tipping into nostalgia about craftsmanship, which would be both sentimental and slightly exclusionary to readers who do not have a workshop. It is controlled by keeping the claim factual (this is where the outlines and labels were invented) and by cashing it out immediately into a practical consequence (so the stakes here are injury, not inconvenience). The reflection is placed once, at the end, and not repeated in any social asset except the podcast close.

**Condescension about the avoided zones.** "You will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once" is an accusation. It works because the very next sentences explain why anyone would do that, and treat the reason as reasonable: tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress, and putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste. The reader is described, not scolded.

**Fear.** The chapter contains a self-heating fire mechanism, a silently degrading guard, and several ways to lose a hand. Each is stated once, at normal volume, with the mechanism attached. The blade guard gets one extra paragraph because it is the one hazard that gives no warning, and that paragraph ends in an action rather than a consequence.

**Gatekeeping in the visitor rule.** The call tells the reader to refuse a family member access to their own shop. It avoids sounding officious by naming the alternative honestly: you have been handling it case by case, which means you have been handling it badly, and saying it in advance is easier than saying it while holding a running grinder. The rule protects the visitor, not the tools, and the text keeps that clear.

On voice. A homecoming that stays useful rather than becoming a tribute, and safety material delivered at working volume.
