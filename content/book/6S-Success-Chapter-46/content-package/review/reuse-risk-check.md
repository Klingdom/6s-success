# Chapter 46 Reuse Risk Check: The Workshop

## Source provenance
Every zone, its purpose, its passes, its hard call, its watch-for items, and its shine detail are folded from the Micro Zone Manual's Workshop entry, which is the project's own material. The six calls are the Manual's `the_call` entries. The room's intro and its three tips supplied the layer: the stakes being physical, the PPE-first order, vacuum never sweep, and the three postponed verdicts. Nothing here is drawn from an outside author, method, or publication.

**One deliberate departure, documented.** The Manual lists the Main Workbench as zone one and the Safety and PPE Station as zone six, but its own "Where to start" tip instructs the reader to take the PPE station first. The chapter follows the tip rather than the listing, and says so in the text. This is the first structural disagreement between a chapter and the Manual in Part Nine, and it is flagged in the editorial review as needing a decision.

## Third-party IP
- No quotations from any external book, article, or speaker.
- No named methodology other than 6S itself and the Toyota lineage the book already establishes. The claim that 6S originated in workshop and factory settings is general industrial history, stated without attributing it to a person or firm.
- No brand names, product names, model numbers, or retailer names anywhere in the package.
- No logos, no QR codes, no readable label text called for in any image prompt.

## Claims that need to stay defensible
1. Fine sawdust becomes airborne when swept, settles on wet finish, and is respirable. Stated qualitatively, as the reason for vacuuming.
2. Fine wood dust is the fraction that presents a combustion risk. Stated qualitatively, with no concentration figures, no ignition data, and no dust-explosion detail.
3. A rag soaked in oil finish self-heats as it cures and can ignite unaided. Stated as the reason for a lidded metal can.
4. A blade guard's return spring degrades in a dusty environment. Stated as the reason for a thumb test.
5. Lithium packs should not be left charging unattended on a combustible surface. Stated as a habit.
6. A loaded small-parts cabinet is heavier than it looks and must be fixed into studs. Stated as the reason for a fixing rule.
7. Sheet goods leaning at a shallow angle can slide flat without warning. Stated as a stacking rule.

None carries a citation at the level it is pitched, and **no safety statistic was invented**. Claim 2 is the one to watch if a references appendix is ever added, because dust combustion is a technical subject and the chapter deliberately stays at the level of "the fines are the part that burns" without going near quantities or mechanisms.

## Liability posture, and the hard limits
- **No machine setup, tuning, alignment, blade-changing, or repair instruction anywhere.** Tools are cleaned, inspected, and taken out of service. The chapter never tells a reader to fix one.
- **No guard is ever modified, taped, wedged, or bypassed**, and the only guard interaction described is a thumb test on an unplugged tool with the battery out.
- No tool is depicted running in any b-roll note; the film is cleaning and inspection only.
- Disposal is household hazardous waste or metal recycling. Coated, treated, and pressure-treated offcuts are explicitly excluded from burning, which is the one place a reader could genuinely poison themselves acting on a casual instruction.
- No electrical work. Cords are flexed, inspected, and flagged, never opened or repaired, and taped cords are named as not being tools.
- No respirator fit or selection advice beyond keeping masks boxed and dated.

## Cross-chapter reuse
- Uses and credits by reference: Chapters 14, 21, 22, 24, 25, and 31. None re-taught.
- Shares its chemistry and weight rules with Chapter 45 without duplicating them; the self-closing oily rag can added for this chapter was also mapped back to the Garage, where Chapter 45 had named it as an ordinary noun.
- Does not borrow garage (Ch 45) or mudroom (Ch 47) material.
- The before-and-after signature is the standard Part 9 close with no variation, documented in `chapter_46_signature.md`.

## Verdict
Low risk. Own source material throughout, no external IP, no invented statistics, and the two genuine traps in a workshop chapter, telling someone how to fix a tool and telling someone to burn treated timber, are both closed explicitly.
