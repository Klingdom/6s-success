2/
The traced outlines, the labels, the photograph of the ideal state: none of it was invented for pantries. It came off shop floors, where a missing tool was a stopped line and a sticky guard was a lost hand.

(209 chars)

3/
Which makes this the one chapter where the stakes are genuinely different. Every other room in this book fails into inconvenience. This one fails into injury.

(161 chars)

4/
So the order of work changes. In a workshop you start at the SMALLEST zone, which happens nowhere else in the book.

(118 chars)

5/
The PPE station. A board of hooks by the door, under an hour of work. It goes first because everything else in there involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust.

(173 chars)

---

6/
Do it last and you know what happens: working without muffs because they are on the far wall, cutting with hazed glasses pushed up on your forehead. The PPE station is not the reward at the end. It is the permission to begin.

(228 chars)

---

7/
Then one instruction that overrides habit: vacuum, never sweep.

(66 chars)

---

8/
A broom does not remove the finest sawdust. It launches it, back into the air, where it settles onto wet finish and into your lungs. And the fines are the fraction that actually burns.

(187 chars)

---

9/
Now the part that decides whether a shop changes. The mess in a workshop is never really about storage. It is three verdicts you keep postponing: on offcuts, on half-finished projects, and on paint.

(201 chars)

---

10/
And you know which you avoid, because here is the pattern in every shop: you will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once.

(149 chars)

---

11/
Tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress. Putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste. So the drawers get neat and the pile holding the room back does not move.

(181 chars)

---

12/
The offcut fix works by removing your judgment. Two limits: a size floor, and one bin with no overflow. When it is full, the shortest piece leaves. You only judge whether a piece beats the worst one already in there.

(220 chars)

---

13/
One safety point worth the whole thread. A blade guard is a spring-loaded part living in sawdust, and sawdust makes springs sticky. It degrades slowly, silently, and the first time you notice is the time it does not come back.

(230 chars)
