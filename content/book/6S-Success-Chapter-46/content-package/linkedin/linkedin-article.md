# The Workshop: The Room 6S Came From

*A LinkedIn article from Chapter 46 of 6S Success: Home Edition.*

We have come a long way around to arrive here, and it is worth saying plainly: this is the room 6S came from. Everything you have applied to a pantry and a nursery and a laundry room started on a shop floor, where a tool out of place was not untidy but expensive.

> This is the room 6S came from, and the only one in the house where a bad standard can cost you a finger rather than an afternoon. Everywhere else in this book, a standard that slips costs you time, or a smell, or a morning putting things right. Here it costs you a hand.

## The smallest zone leads

> The smallest zone in this room goes first, and it is the only room in the book where that is true. Everything else in here involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust, so you want your glasses findable and your extinguisher gauge green before you spend a Saturday handling any of it. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean. It is the permission to begin.

## One instruction that overrides habit

> There is one cleaning instruction in this room that overrides habit, and it is that you vacuum rather than sweep. A broom does not remove the finest sawdust, it launches it: back into the air, where it hangs, then settles onto wet finish and into your lungs.

## And the actual diagnosis

> The workshop's mess is never really about storage. It is three verdicts you keep postponing: on offcuts, on half-finished projects, and on paint. And you already know which ones you avoid, because you will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once.
