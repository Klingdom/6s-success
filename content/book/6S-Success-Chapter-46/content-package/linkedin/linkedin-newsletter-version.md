# Chapter 46 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Workshop

**Subject:** The room 6S came from

Everything in this book started here. The traced outlines, the labels, the photograph of the ideal state: all of it was invented on a shop floor, by people for whom a missing tool was a stopped line and a sticky guard was a lost hand.

So this chapter is a homecoming, and it is also the one chapter where the stakes are genuinely different. Every other room in this book fails into inconvenience. This one fails into injury.

Which is why the order of work is unlike anywhere else. You start at the smallest zone in the room, the PPE station, because everything else involves a blade, a solvent, or a cloud of fine dust. The PPE station is not the reward at the end of the clean. It is the permission to begin.

Four rules on top of the nine rules of Shine:

- **Here the stakes are physical.** A standard is not a preference in this room, it is a guard.
- **The smallest zone leads.**
- **Vacuum, never sweep.** A broom launches the finest sawdust into the air, where it settles onto wet finish and into your lungs, and the fines are the part that burns.
- **Three postponed verdicts.** The mess is never really about storage. It is offcuts, half-finished projects, and paint.

That last one is the whole diagnosis. You will sort the fasteners twice before you touch the offcut pile once, because tipping out a jar of screws feels like progress and putting good wood in the fire bucket feels like waste. The zones you keep skipping are the zones holding the room back.

Do one zone this week, and make it the PPE station.
