# Chapter 46 Signature: The Workshop

The Part 9 recurring close. The friction meter is retired (it reached GOAL at Chapter 30); each room playbook ends instead on a matched before-and-after room signature: the before kept per Chapter 6, the after held as the ideal-state standard per Chapter 25.

## The before-and-after room signature
Stand at the door you actually come in through. Get the bench, the tool wall, and the material rack into one frame. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and leave the half-finished project exactly where it is, because it is the truest thing in the picture. Then work the six zones, starting with the smallest, and take the after from the same spot.

## No variation, but a different measure
This chapter uses the plain Part 9 close. What changes is not the mechanic but the criterion: in this room the standard is not about appearance, it is about readiness. The question the after photo answers is whether you could walk in and start cutting inside a minute.

Compare with the variations used so far: Ch 39 the child takes the after photo; Ch 40 the after is the nightly safe state; Ch 43 the after contains one detail that expires; Ch 44 the after is taped inside a drawer and matched nightly; Ch 45 the after keeps working as a diagnostic floor. Here the after is hung where you stand, and it is judged on time-to-first-cut.

## The before-and-after caption (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> This is the workshop the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, the glasses and muffs on labelled hooks at the door, the extinguisher gauge in the green with clear floor in front of it, a bare bench holding the vise and the lamp, every outline on the tool wall filled, the offcut bin at its written size floor, and every finish sealed in its labelled container behind a metal door. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard. Then hang it where you stand, because in this room the standard is not about how it looks. It is about whether you can start work in a minute and still have ten fingers at the end of it.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> A workshop is not tidy because you sorted the screws. It is safe because you took the three verdicts you had been avoiding, on the offcuts, the half-finished projects, and the paint, and because the gear you need to begin is hanging where you walk in. Do one zone this week, and make it the PPE station, because it is the smallest zone in the room and it is the one that gives you permission to do the other five.

## A note on the closing passage
This chapter carries a short reflection after the frozen caption that no other room chapter has: an explicit acknowledgement that the whole method returns here to its source, having been applied to bedrooms and pantries for forty-five chapters. It is deliberate and it is placed once. If Part 9 ever gets a closing chapter of its own, that passage is the seed of it.
