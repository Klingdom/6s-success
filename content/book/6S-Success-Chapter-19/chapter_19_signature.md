- **The drift reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A home does not get messy in one big event. It drifts, a little at a time, so the thing that keeps it is not a big clean now and then but a small reset, often. Fight a slow daily drift with a rare heroic clean and you will always be behind; meet it with a short daily reset and you are never more than fifteen minutes from baseline.
- **The time-box payoff (frozen, pull-quote):** The timer is the whole device, because a hard stop is what makes people willing to start. An open-ended clean is the dreaded afternoon all over again; a clean that is definitely over in fifteen minutes is one anyone will begin, and a clean you will begin is the only kind that keeps a home.
- **The shared-reset payoff (frozen, pull-quote):** Fifteen minutes is nothing on your own and a great deal shared out. Four people resetting together for a quarter of an hour is a full hour of cleaning that costs each of them only fifteen minutes, and it lands all at once, so the whole home returns to baseline in the time it takes one person to clean a single room.
- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** the 15-minute reset / a little, often, together / the timer, not a standard / back to baseline. The reframe, list, and methods exist (Ch 16 to 18); Chapter 19 makes keeping-it-clean a short shared habit.

## Hero device: The 15-minute reset
A short, time-boxed pass through the home, done together on a regular rhythm, that returns every zone to its clean and read baseline, without a project. Three levers: SHORT (a hard 15-minute stop, set by a timer), SHARED (the whole household at once, using Ch 17's assignments), REGULAR (anchored to a daily cue so it is a habit, not a decision). Source: Seiso's initial cleaning and inspection made repeatable, home-scaled. Referenced lightly, no overclaiming.

- **The timer is the device.** A hard stop is what makes people willing to start.

## Emotional purpose
Earned accomplishment plus practical relief, without hype. This closes the third S, so a quiet pride is right, and the deeper relief is a home that keeps itself on fifteen minutes a day. No shame: homes drift because drift is natural and the job as pictured is too big, not because anyone is lazy. The payoff is getting your weekends back: little-and-often dissolves the need for the dreaded deep clean.

Teach the reader to run a short, time-boxed, shared reset on a daily rhythm: set a timer and stop when it rings, send everyone to their own zones at once, anchor it to a daily cue, and keep it a light reset (not a deep clean). Do NOT re-teach the reframe (Ch 16), the list (Ch 17), or the methods (Ch 18); the reset uses all three. Do NOT teach safety (Ch 20). Do NOT build documented standards or whole-method schedules (Standardize, Ch 23 to 25). Do NOT solve long-term adherence, backsliding, audits, or family problem-solving (Sustain, Ch 26 to 30).
