# Chapter 19 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: The 15-Minute Reset

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, anti-shame voice, relief and permission not lecture: a home drifts because drift is natural and "clean the house" is too big to start, not because anyone is lazy, and the fix is a smaller unit of cleaning on a rhythm, not more willpower.

---

1. Be honest with me here. A few days after you clean, does your home quietly drift back? Not because anyone stopped caring, but because the mug lands on the counter, the coat misses the hook, and a week of tiny nothings undoes an afternoon of work. What is the first thing that always creeps back in your home?

2. A home is not a thing you clean; it is a thing you keep clean, and those are completely different problems. Which one has been eating your weekends: the rare heroic clean that knocks the mess down and lets it climb again, or a short daily reset that keeps you always near baseline? Which would you rather live in?

3. Here is the whole idea in one line: a little, often, together. If your household spent fifteen minutes tonight, everyone to a different zone at once, and stopped when a timer rang even if it was not perfect, how much of the house do you think would come back to baseline? Try it and tell me what you found.

4. The trick is not discipline; it is attachment. Anchor the fifteen minutes to something you already do without thinking, after dinner, or the moment the kids' show ends, and it borrows that thing's reliability. What daily cue in your home would you hang a reset on, so it stops being a decision and becomes a habit?

5. Reset, do not deep-clean. When a short reset runs every day, the drift never accumulates and the dreaded whole-Saturday deep clean simply stops being necessary. Has a small daily habit ever quietly dissolved a big dreaded job for you? What was it, and did you notice your weekends coming back?
