# Infographic Spec: The 15-Minute Reset

**Chapter 19, "The 15-Minute Reset"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter. It carries the whole idea in one glance: a home
cleaned once drifts back, not because anyone stopped caring but because "clean the house" is too big to
ever start, so this chapter shrinks the whole job to a short, time-boxed, shared, recurring pass, the
15-minute reset, that returns every zone to its clean and read baseline. The concept pairs the hero
device, **the 15-minute reset**, with its frozen rule, **A little, often, together.**, and shows the
picture at its heart: a small daily drift met with a small daily reset holds a home near baseline, where
a rare heroic clean only swings between clutter and exhausting catch-up. So the viewer sees the whole
move at once, the shape of the problem, the shape of the fix, and the rule that ties them.

It stays consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_19_final.html`. Slate carries the rule,
the definition, the axes, the headings, and the tags; wood fill warms the small object glyphs; soft
green marks the baseline, the daily-reset line, and the calm-dots; a single small warm red is held back
for the saw-tooth of the rare big clean on the without-a-reset side and is used nowhere else on this
calm image. "Spotless" does not appear; the target is a home that is clean and read, kept at baseline by
a short shared rhythm, not a spotless one. Anti-shame throughout: a home drifts because drift is natural
and the job as pictured is too big to start, not because anyone is lazy, and the fix is a smaller unit
of cleaning on a rhythm, not more willpower.

## Layout notes

- The rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole of the chapter compressed to three
  load-bearing words: a little, so the job is small enough to start; often, so the drift never gets
  ahead; together, so it is fast and fair. The eye should read the rule, then see the drift picture prove
  it, the daily-reset line holding near baseline while the rare big clean only swings.
- The drift picture is the hero, so make the contrast unmistakable: two lines and no more, the saw-tooth
  exhausting and never settling, the daily-reset line calm and steady near the dashed baseline. Keep the
  two honest; the big clean is not a villain and the daily reset is not a triumph, and little-and-often
  simply wins because it is the one that holds.
- Hold the color discipline: the single small warm red is reserved for the saw-tooth of the rare big
  clean and is used nowhere else; slate carries the rule, the definition, the axes, and the headings;
  soft green marks the baseline, the daily-reset line, and the calm-dots. This image is calm, so no
  fill-line red anywhere except that one saw-tooth.
- This image makes the device, the 15-minute reset and its rule. Do NOT re-teach the reframe (Chapter
  16, done), the list or its names (Chapter 17, done and closed), or the methods (Chapter 18, done); the
  reset simply runs them, briefly, at once. Do NOT teach safety (Chapter 20, the next S; handoff only).
  Do NOT build documented standard-work, written checklists-as-standards, or formal schedules across the
  six S's (Standardize, a later S), and do NOT solve long-term adherence, backsliding, or audits
  (Sustain, the last S); the reset is a light Shine habit, not the documented Standardize system. And
  name no brand and draw no specific product; the glyphs are plain and generic, teaching the habit, not a
  shopping list.
- Anti-shame: a home drifts because drift is natural and the job as pictured is too big to start, not
  because anyone is lazy, and the fix is a smaller unit of cleaning on a rhythm, not more willpower.
  Shrink the job small enough and the not-starting simply stops. Keep the register relief and earned
  accomplishment, no guilt cues, no pressure framing, no force framing anywhere. "Spotless" does not
  appear; the target is a home kept at baseline by a short shared rhythm.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the axes and lines drawn
  by hand rather than as a clinical chart, the glyphs sketched rather than typeset, the whole image the
  calm, warm register of a good idea rather than an instruction poster.
