# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 19

**"The 15-Minute Reset"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Five · Shine*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two chapter
fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are working specs, not
finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in `chapter_19_final.html`: the
freshly cleaned home from the last chapter caught in the first hours of drifting back, a dozen small
signs creeping in; the whole household mid-reset, a large timer reading 15:00 counting down, each
person in a different zone at once with green calm-dots blooming and a faint greyed inset of a lone
solo clean; a two-week line chart of two homes, a violent saw-tooth without a reset against a nearly
flat line with a short daily reset; and the friction meter, which reaches a marked SHINE COMPLETE
milestone this chapter, the third tick on the dial after Sort complete and Straighten complete, with
the goal still ahead.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`, ink `#2B2622`,
soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`, calm green `#6E8B5B`,
friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`, wood fill `#E7C58B`, cream `#FCF8F0`.

Scope fence: this chapter is the FOURTH and FINAL chapter of Part Five, and it COMPLETES Shine, the
third S. Chapter 16 reframed cleaning as inspection and made one worked first pass. Chapter 17 turned
that single pass into a plan for the whole home, grouped by four kinds of target with a name against
every line, and HELD the needle with a dashed forward projection. Chapter 18 gave each target a working
method and, doing them, the targets actually got cleaned across the home, so the needle STEPPED. Chapter
19 does one narrow thing: it turns "keep it clean and read" into a short, time-boxed, shared, recurring
pass, the 15-minute reset, so the home stays at baseline without a project. It USES the reframe (Chapter
16), the list and names (Chapter 17), and the methods (Chapter 18), and it does NOT re-teach any of them
and opens no new Shine tool beyond the reset. It does NOT teach hazards, protection, or safety (Chapter
20 and Part Six, the fourth S; teaser and handoff only). CRITICAL fence: the reset is a light Shine
habit, NOT the Standardize documented standard-work, written checklists-as-standards, or formal
schedules across all six S's (Standardize, a later S), and NOT the long-term adherence, backsliding,
family problem-solving, audits, or "your next 6S event" of Sustain (the last S); note lightly that
Sustain returns to habits later, do not build it. It does NOT re-open Sort or Straighten. Because the
reset turns the one-time clean into a self-running baseline, the friction needle reaches a marked SHINE
COMPLETE milestone, the third on the dial, honoring the book's rule that the meter moves only when the
room does; three of six S's are done and the goal is still ahead. Separator discipline: labels use the
middot, for example `CHAPTER 19 · SHINE`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words.
Compound terms keep their internal hyphen: fifteen-minute, time-boxed, self-keeping, self-running,
whole-Saturday, deep-clean, saw-tooth, quarter-hour, after-dinner, low-handle, launch-pad,
line-and-flat-color, Shine-complete, Straighten-complete, Sort-complete, calm-dot, friction-spark,
little-and-often. "Spotless" does not appear here; it is a negated non-goal at most, never the target.
Anti-shame throughout: a home drifts because drift is natural and "clean the house" is too big to start,
not because anyone is lazy; the fix is a smaller unit of cleaning on a rhythm, not more discipline. No
force or violence framing anywhere; no attacking grime, no war on dirt, no blitz, no blast, no purge;
the whole move is a short shared pass on a rhythm. No hype words. Avoid brand names and specific product
endorsements; teach the habit, not a shopping list.

## 1. The Clean Home Begins to Drift (the opener)

**Purpose:** Show the whole chapter's problem in one image. A home cleaned properly is a photograph,
and what you live in is the film, and the film drifts, a little at a time, while nobody does anything
wrong. The freshly cleaned home from the last chapter is caught in the first hours of the slide, still
basically good, with a dozen tiny signs that a clean home does not stay clean by itself. Mirrors Figure
1 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The cleared, arranged, freshly cleaned home from Chapter 18, drawn as one warm wide
scene, caught a day after a good clean. Nothing is a mess yet; the slide has simply started, and you
read it in the small signs: a single mug left on the wiped kitchen counter, the faint return of crumbs
by the toaster, a coat slipping off its hook toward the floor, a couple of shoes nudged out of the
launch pad, a small stack of mail beginning to pile in the tray. On the wall, a clock reading just
after dinner. Small terracotta drift-marks sit over each creeping sign, quiet, not alarming.

**Rough layout:**
- A warm dawn wash (a soft honey-to-cream vertical gradient `#FBE9C8` to `#FBF7EF`). A faint Newsreader
  italic line across the top, muted: `a day after a good clean · the drift has already begun`.
- A single ink floor line across the base, faint `#E2D8C4` side walls, the whole home read left to
  right as one continuous space, warm and lived-in rather than staged.
- Centered high on the wall, a small clock in cream `#FCF8F0` with an ink outline, its hands reading
  just after dinner, a muted Newsreader italic tag beneath, `just after dinner`.
- Left, the kitchen counter in `#EFE7D6` with an ink edge: a small toaster, a light scatter of crumbs
  returning beside it in terracotta at reduced opacity, and one mug left on the wiped counter, with a
  small terracotta drift-mark above and a muted italic line beneath, `one mug, a few crumbs`.
- Middle-left, a row of coat hooks: one coat neatly on the middle hook in honey, and one coat in
  terracotta slipping off the left hook toward the floor, a small drift-mark above it, a muted italic
  line beneath, `a coat off its hook`.
- Middle-right, the launch-pad mat drawn as a dashed slate rectangle: two pairs of shoes in place and
  one shoe in honey nudged out of the mat, a drift-mark above, a muted italic line beneath, `shoes
  nudged out`.
- Right, a small mail tray on a shelf with a short stack of letters beginning to pile, slightly askew,
  a drift-mark above, a muted italic line beneath, `the mail begins to pile`.
- A small sans caps line low across the scene, terracotta: `CLEAN ONCE IS NOT CLEAN KEPT`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "A day after a good clean, the drift has already begun: one mug, one coat,
  a few crumbs. Nothing here is a mess. It is the quiet, daily slide that turns a clean home back into a
  cluttered one, and the only thing that stops it is a rhythm."

**Note for the illustrator:** the whole point is that nothing here is a mess, so keep every sign small
and quiet; this is the first hours of a slide, not a neglected home, and it must read as still basically
good. The home is the same home from Chapter 18, cleared and arranged and freshly cleaned, so draw it
warm and cared for, with only the dozen tiny drifts to say a clean home does not hold by itself. Hold
the terracotta discipline: the small drift-marks and the faint crumbs are the only warm-red on the
image, used to mark the quiet slide, never to dramatize dirt. Do not draw anyone at fault and do not
draw a disaster; the drift happened while nobody did anything wrong, and that is the entire idea. Warm
line-and-flat-color style, the register of a gentle observation, not a warning.

## 2. The Reset in Action (the hero, everyone together)

**Purpose:** Show the together lever literally, and its arithmetic. Fifteen minutes is nothing on your
own and a great deal shared out: many hands, for one short bounded burst, return the whole home to
baseline at once, where one person alone would face a long slog. This is the chapter's hero image, the
device in motion. Mirrors Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A cutaway of the whole home mid-reset, energetic and calm at once. A large timer at
the top reads 15:00, mid countdown, a small honey knob at its crown and a green arc just begun. Inside a
shared house frame divided into four zones, a different member of the household is doing a quick light
pass at the same moment: a child at the launch pad returning shoes and wiping a low handle; an adult at
the kitchen counter clearing and wiping; a teenager carrying two mugs to the dishwasher; someone
straightening the living room. Small green calm-dots bloom in each finished spot. Off to one side, a
faint greyed-out inset shows a single lone person facing a long solo clean, the thing the shared reset
replaces.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `EVERYONE,
  AT THE SAME MOMENT, FOR THE SAME FIFTEEN MINUTES`.
- The big timer centered near the top: a cream `#FCF8F0` face with an ink ring, `15:00` set in Fraunces
  ink, a small green sans line beneath, `COUNTING DOWN`, a short green arc along the top of the ring to
  read as time just begun, and a small honey knob at the crown.
- A rounded shared house frame in quiet fill `#F3EEE2` with a `#E2D8C4` outline, split by a faint slate
  cross into four zones. Each zone a small figure with a colored head and a quick-pass gesture, one
  clear task apiece, plus a small green calm-dot in the spot just finished. Top-left, `LAUNCH PAD`: a
  child (honey head) returning a shoe to a dashed launch-pad mat and wiping a low handle, a green
  calm-dot beside the finished handle. Top-right, `KITCHEN COUNTER`: an adult (terracotta head) wiping
  the counter, two short green wipe lines, a green calm-dot at the clean end. Bottom-left, `TO THE
  DISHWASHER`: a teenager (slate head) carrying two mugs toward an open dishwasher, a green calm-dot
  behind them. Bottom-right, `LIVING ROOM`: someone (honey head) straightening a sofa and cushion, a
  small coffee table cleared, a green calm-dot at the tidy spot.
- A Newsreader italic line beneath the frame, soft gray: `four zones, four people, one quarter of an
  hour · the whole home back to baseline at once`.
- To one side, a faint greyed inset in `#F0ECE2` with a dashed `#C9BEA8` border, headed `THE SOLO SLOG`
  in muted gray sans caps: a single grey figure facing a long clean, a long clock beside them, a muted
  Newsreader italic line, `one person, a long afternoon`. Hold the whole inset at low opacity so it
  reads as the thing the shared reset replaces, not a competing scene.
- Caption (from the chapter): "Everyone, at the same moment, for the same fifteen minutes. Four people
  resetting together is an hour of cleaning that costs each of them a quarter of one, and it lands all
  at once, so the whole home comes back to baseline while a lone cleaner would still be on the first
  room."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the hero and it should feel brief, communal, and light, almost a
game with the youngest, never a chore montage. Everyone is doing a quick light pass at the same moment,
so keep the four figures in motion but relaxed, each to a single clear task, each in their own zone, so
the eye reads at once that nobody waits and nobody carries the home alone. The timer is load-bearing:
draw it large and legible at 15:00, mid countdown, because the hard stop is what makes people willing to
start. The green calm-dots are the only bright accent, one per finished spot; use no spark red anywhere.
Keep the solo-slog inset faint and grey and small, a quiet contrast, not a scolding; it is simply the
long lonely version the shared reset replaces. Do not draw a deep clean; nobody here is scrubbing an
oven, everyone is just undoing today's drift with methods they already know. Warm line-and-flat-color
style, the register of a household in easy motion.

## 3. A Little, Often (the drift line, no rare-and-heroic swing)

**Purpose:** Show why little-and-often beats rare-and-heroic. The same or less total effort, spread
daily, holds a home near baseline; saved up for a rare big clean, it swings between clutter and
exhausting catch-up and never settles. A problem that arrives a little at a time has to be met a little
at a time. Mirrors Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A simple line chart of clutter and grime over two weeks, showing two homes. The first
line, WITHOUT a reset, saws violently: it climbs as drift accumulates, drops sharply on the rare day of
a big clean, then climbs again, never settling. The second line, WITH a short daily reset, stays low and
nearly flat, a small daily dip keeping it always near the clean baseline. A dashed baseline runs across
near the bottom. Small markers show the daily resets on the flat line and the rare exhausting big cleans
on the saw-tooth line.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel background `#FBF7EF`. Top, a small sans caps label, letter-spaced, soft gray: `TWO HOMES,
  TWO WEEKS`.
- Two slate axes `#3C5A6B` with a light frame. Vertical axis labeled in slate sans, rotated, `clutter
  and grime`; horizontal axis labeled `two weeks, day by day`.
- A dashed green baseline `#6E8B5B` running across near the bottom, tagged `baseline` in green sans at
  the right end, marking the clean-and-read level the home was left at.
- WITHOUT a reset: a violent friction-spark `#CB4B36` saw-tooth. Drift climbs steeply, a sharp vertical
  drop marks each rare big clean, then it climbs again, three times across the fortnight, never settling
  near baseline. Small spark dots at the top of each drop, two of them tagged in Newsreader italic `big
  clean`, and a slate-red sans caps label to the side, `without a reset`.
- WITH a short daily reset: a nearly flat green `#6E8B5B` line hugging just above the baseline, a small
  daily dip each day, nine small green reset dots along it. A green sans caps label, `with a short daily
  reset`, and a green Newsreader italic note, `a small daily dip keeps it near baseline`.
- Caption (from the chapter): "Two homes, two weeks. The saw-tooth is the rare big clean: drift builds,
  a heroic afternoon knocks it down, and it builds again. The flat line is the daily reset: a little
  each day keeps the home always near baseline, for less total effort and no dreaded afternoons."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is a picture of a principle, not a dataset, so keep it warm and
legible at a glance, two lines and no clutter of gridlines. The saw-tooth must read as exhausting and
never settling, its peaks high and its drops sharp, so the eye feels the swing between clutter and
catch-up; the flat green line must read as calm and steady, always near the dashed baseline, its daily
dips small and regular. The contrast is the whole argument: match the rhythm of the fix to the rhythm of
the mess and the mess never builds. Keep the friction-spark red only on the saw-tooth line and its big-
clean markers; the green line and the baseline carry the calm. Do not label the big clean as a villain
or the daily reset as a triumph; both are honest, and little-and-often simply wins because it is the one
that holds. Calm slate axes, warm accents, the register of a quiet proof.

## 4. Shine, Complete (the Part Five retrospective, no HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the chapter's closing beat as a picture: with the reset in place, the whole third S
stands finished, and Part Five is done. Sort emptied the home of what did not belong; Straighten gave
what stayed a place; and Shine made the whole of it clean, read, and self-keeping. This diagram has no
direct HTML twin; it renders the "Shine, Complete" retrospective section as a three-panel progression,
carrying a frozen close line beneath it, and it hands off to Safety without teaching it. It is the
earned-accomplishment heart of the chapter close.

**What it shows:** Three calm facing panels of the same home, read as the three finished S's, left to
right. Panel one, SORT: the home emptied of what did not belong, drawn as clear space and a single kept
set of things, a small slate `SORT COMPLETE` tab. Panel two, STRAIGHTEN: what stayed given a place, the
launch pad settled by the door, a place for each thing, a small slate `STRAIGHTEN COMPLETE` tab. Panel
three, SHINE: the whole of it clean, read, and self-keeping, a small timer and a green calm-dot marking
the reset that keeps it, a small slate `SHINE COMPLETE` tab. To the right, a quiet, dimmer fourth panel
outline, unfinished and unlit, tagged `SAFETY · NEXT`, the fourth S the book turns to, with two fainter
panel outlines behind it for the two S's after that. Three panels lit and finished; three ahead, quiet.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `THE THIRD S, STANDING FINISHED`, with a Fraunces ink line beneath
  carrying the retrospective's spine: "Sort emptied the home of what did not belong; Straighten gave
  what stayed a place; and Shine made the whole of it clean, read, and self-keeping."
- Three warm cream `#FCF8F0` panels with ink outlines, left to right, each headed with a small slate
  tab. Panel `SORT COMPLETE`: the home with clear surfaces and only what belongs, a small green
  calm-dot, a muted Newsreader italic note, `emptied of what did not belong`. Panel `STRAIGHTEN
  COMPLETE`: the settled launch pad by the door and a place for each thing, a small green calm-dot, a

  small timer glyph and a green calm-dot marking the reset that keeps it, a muted italic note, `clean,

- To the right, a dimmer honey-outlined panel, unlit, tagged `SAFETY · NEXT` in muted honey sans caps,

- Beneath the panels, a slate bordered strip carrying the frozen One Idea to Keep: "A little, often,
  together. Shrink cleaning to a short shared reset on a regular rhythm, stop when the timer rings, and

- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "Three of the six S's are done. The home is clean and kept. Next, we
  make it safe."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is earned accomplishment without hype, so keep it warm and quietly
sure, never triumphant; three of six is a real milestone and it is only halfway, and both are true at
once. Draw the three finished panels lit and settled and equal, one weight of heading across all three,
so the third S reads as the sum of Sort, Straighten, and Shine standing together, not as Shine alone.
Keep the Safety panel and the two behind it dim, unlit, and clearly ahead; you may name Safety as next,
but teach nothing of it, no hazards, no protection, only the quiet fact that the book turns from the
state of the room to the people in it. Hold the color discipline: green calm-dots mark the finished
work, slate carries the tabs and the strip, and no spark red appears on this calm close. The register is
the practical relief of a home that keeps itself and the quiet satisfaction of a part completed, a
weight set down, not a victory lap.
