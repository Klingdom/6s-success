## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
You have the reframe, the list, and the methods, and a home cleaned once still drifts back, not because anyone stopped caring but because "clean the house" is too big to ever start. A one-time clean, however good, is a photograph; what you live in is the film, and the film drifts, a little at a time, until an afternoon of work is quietly undone. The fix has to match the shape of the problem: a small, constant drift met with a small, constant response. Chapter 19 is that response, the 15-minute reset: a short, time-boxed pass through the home, done together on a regular rhythm, that returns every zone to its clean and read baseline, so the whole house stays cared for without anyone ever setting aside an afternoon. Its rule is a little, often, together, and its three levers are short (a hard 15-minute stop, so it starts), shared (the whole household at once, so it is fast and fair), and regular (anchored to a daily cue, so it is a habit and not a decision). The timer is the whole device, because a hard stop is what makes people willing to begin; set the timer, and stop when it rings, even if it is not perfect. Fifteen minutes is nothing on your own and a great deal shared out, so four people resetting together return the whole home to baseline in the time it takes one person to clean a single room. Each reset simply exercises what the earlier chapters built: the assigned targets from Chapter 17, their working methods from Chapter 18, read as you go from Chapter 16, all at once and briefly. Then it works one reset in action, timer on, everyone to a zone, quick light passes, timer rings, stop, back to baseline. Because the reset removes the last of Shine's friction, so the home no longer needs a fresh weekly act of will to stay clean, the friction meter reaches a marked SHINE COMPLETE milestone, the third on the dial after Sort complete and Straighten complete, notably calmer than Chapter 18, with Chapter 18's position now the dashed ghost just behind. The goal is still ahead, because three of six S's are done and Safety, Standardize, and Sustain remain. Hands off to Chapter 20 · Safety: Protect People First, which opens Part Six and turns from the state of the room to the people in it.
