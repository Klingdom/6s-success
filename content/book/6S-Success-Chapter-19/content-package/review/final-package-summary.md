- **The rule (frozen):** A little, often, together.
- **The support line (frozen):** Set the timer, and stop when it rings.
- **The definition (frozen):** The 15-minute reset is a short, time-boxed pass through the home, done together on a regular rhythm, that returns every zone to its clean and read baseline, so the whole house stays cared for without anyone ever having to set aside an afternoon for it.
- **The drift reframe (frozen):** A home does not get messy in one big event. It drifts, a little at a time, so the thing that keeps it is not a big clean now and then but a small reset, often. Fight a slow daily drift with a rare heroic clean and you will always be behind; meet it with a short daily reset and you are never more than fifteen minutes from baseline.
- **The time-box payoff (frozen):** The timer is the whole device, because a hard stop is what makes people willing to start. An open-ended clean is the dreaded afternoon all over again; a clean that is definitely over in fifteen minutes is one anyone will begin, and a clean you will begin is the only kind that keeps a home.
- **The shared-reset payoff (frozen):** Fifteen minutes is nothing on your own and a great deal shared out. Four people resetting together for a quarter of an hour is a full hour of cleaning that costs each of them only fifteen minutes, and it lands all at once, so the whole home returns to baseline in the time it takes one person to clean a single room.
- **One Idea to Keep (frozen):** A little, often, together. Shrink cleaning to a short shared reset on a regular rhythm, stop when the timer rings, and the home stays clean and read without a single lost afternoon. That steady, self-running baseline is what completes Shine.
