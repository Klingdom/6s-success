# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (a home cleaned once drifts back, and the fix is a smaller unit of cleaning on a rhythm, not more discipline) then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad. No em dashes.

---

Okay, one small idea that finally made keeping my house clean feel possible, and then I want to hear how you all stay on top of yours.

For years my pattern was the same. I would clean the whole house properly on a weekend, feel great about it, and within a few days watch it quietly slide back. A mug on the counter. A coat off the hook. The mail starting its little pile. Nothing dramatic. Just the slow return of the mess I thought I had beaten, and me wondering why cleaning never seems to stick.

For a long time I thought that was a me problem, that I just was not disciplined enough. Then I read one line that put it somewhere else entirely.

"A home does not get messy in one big event. It drifts, a little at a time, so the thing that keeps it is not a big clean now and then but a small reset, often."

That was the whole thing right there. I had been meeting a slow daily drift with a rare heroic clean, so I was always behind. The book's answer is almost embarrassingly simple: instead of the occasional enormous clean, everyone in the house does fifteen minutes together, at the same time, on a regular rhythm, and stops when the timer rings. A little, often, together. Four people for a quarter of an hour is a full hour of cleaning, and it lands all at once.

We started doing ours after dinner, and honestly it has changed the feel of the whole week.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

How do you keep your home from drifting back after a good clean? Does anyone here run some kind of daily reset or tidy with the whole family, and if so, when do you fit it in? And for anyone still stuck in the weekend deep-clean cycle, what is the job that always tips you back into the big Saturday slog?

I will go first in the comments. 😄

(If anyone wants it written out properly, this is from Chapter 19 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, just the idea that finally got me off the weekend deep-clean treadmill.)
