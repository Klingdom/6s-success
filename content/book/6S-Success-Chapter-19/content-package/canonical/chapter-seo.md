## Search intent
Keeping a home clean without a project every time. Readers arriving here already know why they clean (Chapter 16), what to clean and who cleans it (Chapter 17), and how to clean each target quickly and well (Chapter 18), but their home still drifts back a few days after a good clean and they cannot see why. They want a way to hold the clean without setting aside an afternoon or summoning fresh willpower every week. The chapter answers with one hero device and one rule: the 15-minute reset, a short, time-boxed, shared, recurring pass that returns every zone to its clean and read baseline; and a little, often, together. It teaches the reset's three levers (short, a timer with a hard stop; shared, the whole household at once, using Chapter 17's assignments; regular, anchored to a daily cue so it becomes a habit), runs a reset in action, and closes Part Five. Because the reset removes the last of Shine's friction, the friction meter reaches a marked Shine-complete milestone.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Frames the problem as drift, small and daily, rather than a failure of effort, so keeping a home clean becomes a matter of matching the rhythm of the fix to the rhythm of the mess, instead of the usual willpower-and-schedule advice
- Names the second, kinder reason homes drift, that "clean the house" is too big and vague to ever start, and fixes it by shrinking the job rather than demanding more discipline
- Makes the timer the device, not a detail: a hard stop is what makes people willing to start, so the page explains why good enough beats perfect and why stopping mid-task is the mechanism
- Shows the arithmetic of the shared reset, that four people for a quarter of an hour is a full hour of cleaning landing all at once, rather than treating cleaning as a solo chore
- Anchors the reset to a daily cue so it becomes a habit not a decision, and shows how little-and-often dissolves the need for the dreaded deep clean
- Keeps scope tight: this completes Shine with a light rhythm; it does not re-teach the reframe, the list, or the methods (Chapters 16 to 18), does not teach safety (Chapter 20), and is not the documented Standardize system or the long-term Sustain work
- Reaches the Shine-complete milestone honestly, three of six S's done and the goal still ahead, rather than claiming the home is finished
