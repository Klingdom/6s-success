Okay, the one kitchen trick I wish someone had shown me years ago, and then I want to hear yours.

For the longest time I cleaned my kitchen by scrubbing harder. The hob, the grates, the splash wall behind the cooker, all of it fought back, and I put it down to elbow grease I did not have. It was never about the elbow.

Here is what I had never been taught. The thing beating you in a kitchen is grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

"Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around."

So now, before I touch anything, I lift the burner grates and caps and the hood filter into hot degreasing water and just leave them. While they soften on their own, I degrease the splash wall and the hood from the top down. By the time I come back, the grates scrub in a fraction of the time. A few things I now do every single time:

- Soak first, so time does the scrubbing instead of my arm.
- Top to bottom, so the grease I loosen falls onto what I have not cleaned yet.
- Give the degreaser its dwell time, spray it and walk away and let the label do the work.
- Lift the appliances rather than wipe around them, because the crumbs live under the toaster's feet.
- Pull the cloth into the fridge gasket fold and clear the dishwasher filter, the two things everyone misses.

And it matters most right here, because the kitchen is the busiest, dirtiest, highest-payback room in the house. It is worked hardest and gets dirtiest, and a real clean pays you back the fastest.

The other thing that made it feel doable was refusing to see it as one big job.

"The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

If you did just one kitchen zone this weekend, to the method, which would you point at first, the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the cabinets, the drawers, or the fridge? And what is the one spot in yours that never actually gets cleaned, the gasket, the dishwasher filter, the top of the cabinets, under the toaster?

I will go first in the comments. 😄
