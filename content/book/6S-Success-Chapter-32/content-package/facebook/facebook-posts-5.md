## Post 1 · The kitchen is beaten by grease, and grease hides upward

Most of us clean a kitchen by scrubbing harder, which is exactly why it fights back. The thing beating you is grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

"Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around."

Soak first so time does the scrubbing. Work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet. And the second your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around, switch to the degreaser. That is it, and it turns the hardest room in the house into a manageable one. ## Post 2 · The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job

The reason the whole kitchen feels like too much is that you are looking at it as one job. It is not.

"You do not clean "the kitchen"; you clean the cooking zone, which is done when the hood, the grates, the splash wall and the hob are degreased and the crock is wiped."

So here is the map for the whole room:

"The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time."

Pick one. The cooking zone is the dirtiest corner and the biggest single win, so it is a good place to start. Read the free chapter for the exact steps.

## Post 3 · The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is time, not a scrubbing arm

The cheapest upgrade to a kitchen clean is not a stronger product or a harder scrub. It is letting time do the work for you.

"The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would."

That is the whole trick. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything, degrease the splash wall and hood from the top down while they soften, then come back to grates that wipe clean. Spray, walk away, let the label do the work. More in Chapter 32, The Kitchen, of 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online.

## Post 4 · A wipe around the clutter is not a clean

Here is the one that quietly makes a kitchen tidy but not hygienic. The mess you never see is the mess that stays.

"The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic."

That last line is the one that changes how people clean. Lift the toaster instead of wiping around it, empty the shelf instead of wiping its front edge, and pull the cloth right into the gasket fold. ## Post 5 · The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from

Here is the zone people rinse and call clean, and it is the one that matters most.

"The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most."

So clean the sponge or replace it, and clear the dishwasher filter, because those two are the things you never think to clean and exactly the things that matter. An empty, dry, genuinely clean sink is the point everything else resets from.
