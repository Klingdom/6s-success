For years I cleaned my kitchen by scrubbing harder, and it never got me anywhere. The hob, the grates, the wall behind the cooker, all of it fought back. I blamed my arms. It was never my arms.

Here is what I had never been taught. The thing beating you in a kitchen is grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

"Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around."

That matters most in here, because the kitchen is the busiest, dirtiest, highest-payback room in the house. It is worked hardest and gets dirtiest, so this is the room where a real method pays off fastest.

The second thing that made it doable was refusing to treat it as one job.

"The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time."

So one Saturday I did just the cooking zone. Before I touched anything, I lifted the burner grates and the hood filter into hot degreasing water and left them. While they softened on their own, I degreased the splash wall and the hood from the top down and let the degreaser sit its dwell time. Then I came back and scrubbed the loosened grates in a fraction of the time. The dirtiest corner of the kitchen, done.

The last piece surprised me. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean.

"The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic."

Lift, soak, and give it time. That is the whole trick.
