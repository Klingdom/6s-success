## The Shine method: the nine rules, plus the grease layer

Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean, and it is the same method you learned at the door. It carries whole into the kitchen.

> You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix. Every one of those still holds. The kitchen simply adds the hardest opponent in the house, grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

That last rule is the oldest idea in the book. Cleaning is how you inspect. When your hand passes over a frayed cord or a rocking grate, your eye catches it, you flag it, and you keep cleaning. **Shine finds it; the safety pass fixes it.**

The kitchen adds one principle on top of the nine, and it governs everything below.

> Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around.

So the kitchen keeps the nine rules and adds four moves.

> The kitchen keeps the nine rules from the door and adds four moves: soak first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, and switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky. The one input that changes everything here is the food-area degreaser.

Two of those four are worth pausing on, because they do most of the work. The first is soaking, which is the single biggest time-saver in the room.

> The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would.

The second is lifting rather than wiping around the clutter.

> The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic.

## A worked zone: the cooking zone, start to finish

To make it real, here is one zone done the whole way, the cooking zone, the space either side of the burners and the hood above. Grease is the whole job here, and it hides upward, so you soak first and work from the top down.

**Gather these inputs into your caddy first, so you never walk off mid-clean:**

- Food-area degreaser
- Neutral pH multi-surface cleaner
- A colour-coded microfiber cloth set (a surface cloth, a dry cloth)
- A small detail brush set
- Hot water and dish soap, for soaking, in the sink or a basin
- Your portable caddy

**Then work the steps in this order:**

1. Soak the soakable parts first. Lift out the hood's metal grease filter and the burner grates and caps, and set them to soak in hot water with degreasing dish soap. Leave them while you do everything else; the soak does what scrubbing cannot.
2. Degrease the hood, top down. Spray the underside and face of the hood with degreaser, give it the label's dwell time, wipe it down with a cloth, and finish with a clean damp pass.
3. Degrease the splash wall, top to bottom and back to front. Spray it, let it sit, then wipe from the top down so the loosened grease runs toward the hob and not onto clean wall. Give any baked-hard spatter a second pass.
4. Clean the hob and burner wells. With the grates off, degrease the exposed hob and work the burner wells where boil-overs bake into a ring. Pull the knobs off if they lift, wash them, and run a detail brush around the knob stems where sticky grime collars them.
5. Scrub and dry the soaked grates. Come back to the grates and caps, scrub the softened baked rings with a detail brush, rinse them, and dry them fully before they go back so they do not rust onto the hob. Rinse and dry the hood filter and refit it.
6. Do the oven door front and the panel. Wipe the oven door front and its glass window, then a clean damp buff so no film clouds the glass you check a roast through. Clean the control panel and dials with a barely damp cloth, not a wet one, so nothing runs in behind the knobs.
7. Wipe the crock, the hook, and the spice fronts. Empty the utensil crock, wash and dry it, and wipe each tool before it goes back. Wipe the spice jar fronts and shoulders, since airborne grease settles into a tacky coat on everything standing near the burners.
8. Finish with the landing space. Degrease, wipe, and dry the stretch of counter you set hot pans down on, so the one surface a hot pan lands on is never slick.

And while your hands are there, your eyes inspect: a hood filter so grease-soaked it stays soft and drips, a fan behind it that sounds strained; a burner that lights slow or burns yellow and sooty, or a grate that rocks on its seat; a chip or spidered line in the hob surface, found once the baked ring is cleared; a frayed or heat-stiffened cord on any appliance on the burner side. Flag each one and keep cleaning. That is the dirtiest corner of the kitchen, genuinely clean. The other six zones run the same way, each with its own inputs and steps.

The sink runs by the same logic, and it earns a word of its own, because it is where the whole room resets from.

> The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most.

**To try this week:** Pick one zone, the cooking zone or the sink, and clean it start to finish, to the method. For the cooking zone, do the soak-first trick: before anything else, lift the burner grates and caps and the hood filter into hot degreasing water and leave them, then degrease the splash wall and the hood from the top down while they soften on their own. Come back, scrub the loosened grates in a fraction of the time, dry them fully, and the dirtiest corner of the kitchen is done. Then, if you want the whole room, do a zone a session across a week, and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time.

> A kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear. > **6S Tip:** Give the degreaser its dwell time instead of scrubbing: spray it, leave it the time on the label, and it lifts the grease for you. Keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink, because together they make a gas that will drive you out of the room.
