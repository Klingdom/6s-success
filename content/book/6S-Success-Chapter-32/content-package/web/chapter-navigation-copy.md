## You are here blurb
You are at Chapter 32, the second chapter of Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks, and the second of twenty room playbooks in the applied half of the book. Behind you is Chapter 31, the entryway, which proved the format on the smallest room; here the same method meets the busiest one. If the entryway was the room to start with, the kitchen is the room that matters most, the busiest space in the house, the dirtiest by a distance, because heat bakes grease onto surfaces, food leaves residue in every corner, and water and steam get everywhere, and the one a proper 6S pays back the fastest. That is why this chapter, more than any other, is about the clean, and why its centerpiece is the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine each zone with the precise inputs. A playbook does not re-teach you Sort or Shine, and it does not stop at telling you what to do: for every small zone it gives you the exact inputs to gather and the exact, ordered, step-by-step way to clean and shine it, done the way a professional would rather than the way most of us wipe at a surface until it looks a bit better. The reason a whole kitchen feels like too much to face is that you are looking at it as one big job, and it is not: it is seven small zones wearing a single name, and the trick to all of Part 9 is to stop seeing the room and start seeing the zones. The hero move, frozen: the kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job, the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer, and you do them one at a time. It moves briskly through the quick passes and then slows right down for Shine, recapping the universal nine-rule Shine method and adding the kitchen's one big new element, grease, which does not behave like ordinary dirt: grease is the whole job and grease hides upward, so soak the soakable parts first (the single biggest time-saver in the room), work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, lift the appliance rather than wipe around it, and reach for the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky, holding food-safety cloth discipline throughout. Then, as the heart of the chapter, Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It gives every zone the same three things, the exact inputs to gather into the caddy, the numbered steps in the order you actually do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. It lands the definition, frozen: a finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut, a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it. It names the room's hard calls, runs the kitchen's real hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the Part 9 signature, the before-and-after pair of the working heart, the before kept as private proof and the after kept as the standard the kitchen has to match. The rule to carry: a kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear, it is clean because you lifted the appliances, soaked the grease, pulled the cloth into the gasket, and cleared the filter, cleaning to a method zone by zone and inspecting as you went, so point the loop at one zone this weekend, the sink or the cooking zone, and cook in a room that is finally clean underneath. You have done the two hardest-working rooms in the house, the door and the kitchen, and Part Nine hands off to Chapter 33, The Pantry, worked the same way.
