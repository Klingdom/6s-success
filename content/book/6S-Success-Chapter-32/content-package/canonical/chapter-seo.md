## Search intent
Getting one real room, the kitchen, genuinely, degreased, and inspectably clean, after the reader has learned the whole 6S method and wants to apply it to the busiest, dirtiest, highest-payback room in the house. This chapter is the second of Part Nine, the applied half of the book, and its centerpiece is the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine each of the seven kitchen zones, with the exact inputs to gather. Readers arriving here are facing a kitchen that heat, grease, food, and water make a mess of several times a day, and they do not just want to tidy it, they want to clean it to a professional standard and know precisely how, degreasing and all. The chapter answers with one freeing idea and one method plus one new element. The idea: you do not clean the kitchen, you clean one zone of it, then the next, and the kitchen is only seven small zones (the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer). The method: the universal nine-rule Shine method carried from Chapter 31 (work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix). The new element: grease, which hides upward, so soak the soakable parts first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky, and hold food-safety cloth discipline. Then, zone by zone, it gives the exact INPUTS (cleaners including the food-area degreaser, colour-coded cloths with a dedicated fridge/raw-meat colour, detail brushes, a vacuum with attachments) and the NUMBERED steps in the true order of work, plus an inspect-and-flag list. It names the room's hard calls (the loved-but-unused appliance, the honest spice rack, whether the sink is allowed to be a staging area, the guilt-leftover container), runs the kitchen's hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after kept as the standard. The friction meter is retired, replaced by each room's own transformation.
