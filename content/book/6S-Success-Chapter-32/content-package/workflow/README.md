## What the chapter says (in one paragraph)
If the entryway was the room to start with, the kitchen is the room that matters most: the busiest space in the house, the one the whole family works in every day, and, minute for minute, the one where a proper 6S pays back fastest and hardest. It is also, by a distance, the dirtiest, because everything that makes a kitchen a kitchen also makes a mess: heat bakes grease onto surfaces, food leaves residue in every corner, water and steam get everywhere. That is why this chapter, more than any other, is about the clean. You already own the whole six-S method and the loop that renews it, and you carry the nine rules of Shine straight from the door, so a playbook does not re-teach them; it takes the kitchen, breaks it into the seven small zones it is really made of, and, for every zone, gives the exact inputs to gather and the exact, ordered, step-by-step way to clean and shine it to a professional standard, degreasing and all. The freeing idea carries over: a room is only ever a handful of small zones wearing a single name, so you do not 6S a whole kitchen, you 6S one of its seven zones, then the next, the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer, and if you want the biggest win for the least effort you start with the cooking zone or the sink, because those two carry the most grease and the most grime. The quick passes get a brisk treatment (Sort each zone first because a buried surface cannot be cleaned; Straighten survivors into a home chosen by use; Safety, Standardize, and Sustain, each short, after the shine), and then the chapter slows right down for Shine, keeps the nine rules from the door, and adds the kitchen layer: grease is the whole job and grease hides upward, so set the soakable parts (the hood filter, the burner grates and caps) to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, give every degreaser its dwell time because the most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is time and not a scrubbing arm, switch from the everyday cleaner to the degreaser the moment a surface feels tacky rather than dirty, lift the appliance and empty the shelf rather than wipe around them, and hold food-safety cloth discipline. Then, as the heart of the chapter, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It" gives every one of the seven zones the same three things: the exact INPUTS to gather into the caddy, the NUMBERED steps in the true order of work (soak first where a zone needs it, then clear, dust or degrease high and dry, glass, surfaces, details, floor and toe-kick last), and an inspect-and-flag list, because Shine finds the hazard and the safety pass fixes it. It lands the definition of a finished kitchen, one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut; it names the room's four genuinely hard calls, the appliance you love but rarely use (count real uses; under a couple a week comes off the counter, not switched on in a year leaves), the spice rack you are not honest about (smell each jar over the bin; keep spices for food you cooked in the last year), whether the sink is allowed to be a staging area (a deadline, not a ban), and the guilt-leftover container you have moved to the front three times (name the meal, day, and eater, or bin it today and cook a little less); it runs the room's real hazards through the five safety questions and acts on everything the clean flagged; and it closes with the Part 9 signature that replaces the friction meter, the before-and-after pair of the working heart and the two photos to take, the before you keep and the after you keep as the standard the kitchen has to match. Then a light hand-off to the pantry. It does NOT re-teach the six S's or the nine Shine rules; it points the loop the reader already owns at the hardest-working room and shows them precisely how to clean it, grease and all.

## Frozen strings (copy verbatim; do not paraphrase)
- The seven zones (frozen, structural): **The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time.**
- The grease rule (frozen, kitchen Shine principle): **Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around.**
- The definition box (frozen): **A finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut: a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it.**
- The lift-do-not-wipe-around reframe (frozen, pull-quote): **The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic.**
- The soak-and-dwell reframe (frozen, pull-quote): **The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would.**
- The reset-point reframe (frozen, pull-quote): **The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most.**
- The nine Shine rules recap (frozen, carried from Ch 31): **You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix.**
- The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure): **This is the kitchen the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, cleared, degreased, and genuinely clean, from the hood down to the toe-kick. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a kitchen this clean is one you will actually want to cook in, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.**
- One Idea to Keep (frozen close): **A kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear. It is clean because you lifted the appliances, soaked the grease, pulled the cloth into the gasket, and cleared the filter, cleaning to a method zone by zone and inspecting as you went. Do one zone this weekend, the sink or the cooking zone, and cook in a room that is finally clean underneath.**
- Callout (Quick Win): **Do the cooking zone's soak-first trick. Before anything else, lift the burner grates and caps and the hood filter into hot degreasing water and leave them. While they soften on their own, degrease the splash wall and the hood from the top down. Come back, scrub the loosened grates in a fraction of the time, dry them fully, and the dirtiest corner of the kitchen is done.**
- Callout (Family Challenge): **Give each person a zone to own on reset day, and put the fridge eat-first bin right where the door opens onto it, so the answer to what is for dinner comes from the bin rather than the cupboard, and far less food is thrown away.**
- Callout (6S Tip): **Give the degreaser its dwell time instead of scrubbing: spray it, leave it the time on the label, and it lifts the grease for you. Keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink, because together they make a gas that will drive you out of the room.**
- Callout (Common Mistake): **Wiping around the appliances and the clutter instead of lifting and emptying, so the crumbs under the toaster, the dust felt on the cabinet tops, the oil film on the shelf floor, and the mold in the fridge gasket all stay. A kitchen cleaned only where you can see is tidy, not hygienic. Lift, empty, and pull the cloth into the fold.**
