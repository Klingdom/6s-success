# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 32

**"The Kitchen"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE SECOND PLAYBOOK*

Six prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across all
six: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the warm
accent, the eyebrows, the "before, kept" note), honey `#DDA63A` (the single accent, the family note), slate blue
`#3C5A6B` (data, labels, the plan, the "glass" cloth), soft green `#6E8B5B` (the SHINE STEP accent, the method
arrows, the numbered nodes, the "after" standard), a deeper label green (a darker shade of the Shine green) for
small labels on the Shine figures, a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only and, in this
room, for the one true hazard mark, the never-mix-bleach warning, used barely. Natural soft daylight, shallow but
not showy depth, real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is one real kitchen, the busiest and dirtiest room in the house and the one a proper
6S pays back fastest, because everything that makes a kitchen a kitchen also makes a mess: heat bakes grease onto
surfaces, food leaves residue in every corner, water and steam get everywhere. This is the SECOND of twenty room
chapters, following the entryway of Chapter 31 and handing off to the pantry of Chapter 33: the six-S method is
finished, and the nine-rule Shine method was taught at the door in Chapter 31, so it is recapped here, not
re-taught. The spine of this chapter is a single addition to the door's method: the kitchen adds the hardest
opponent in the house, grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt, so the kitchen keeps the nine rules
and adds four moves, soak first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, and switch to the degreaser
the moment a surface is tacky. It delivers the frame (the kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep
counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility
drawers, and the fridge and freezer); the heart (the kitchen Shine method: grease hides upward, soak the soakable
parts first, give the degreaser its dwell time, work top to bottom, lift do not wipe around, hold food-safety
cloth discipline); the seven zone-by-zone procedures with their exact inputs and ordered steps; the hard calls
(the appliance you love but rarely use, the honest spice rack, the staging sink, the guilt-leftover container);
and the Part 9 close (a matched before-and-after pair of the kitchen's working heart). The friction meter, which
reached its goal at Chapter 30, is RETIRED for Part 9 and replaced by the before-and-after room signature: do not
draw a gauge. Keep the tone warm, practical, and expert, sleeves-rolled-up instruction rather than a lecture,
capability and a little pride, no shame about the before. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated
house style: no corporate or boardroom look, no hype (no unlock, elevate, supercharge, optimize), no scolding, no
photoreal polish, and no brand names or real logos anywhere, every cleaner, cloth, brush, tool, and appliance is
a plain generic TYPE, never a recognisable product.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in the
scene, a hand-lettered card, a hand-drawn label on a plan or a bottle, a mark on a cloth, or a note under a
photo, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered label: use the middot,
for example `CHAPTER 32 · THE KITCHEN`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound terms
keep their internal hyphen: soak-first, top-to-bottom, lift-not-wipe-around, degrease-the-tacky, back-to-front,
clean-to-dirty, mist-the-cloth, two-cloth, colour-coded, food-area, food-safety, raw-meat, eat-first,
before-and-after, matched-pair, zone-by-zone, six-S, step-by-step, toe-kick, dwell-time.

## 4. The kitchen Shine method (the grease method as a visual)

## 5. How to degrease the cooking zone (the step ribbon)

A warm editorial illustration of a hand-made step ribbon titled how to degrease the cooking zone, on a soft cream
panel, in soft daylight, drawn in the soft green Shine accent, showing the order of work for the dirtiest corner
of the kitchen as a gentle left-to-right ribbon of small step cards, each with a tiny hand-drawn type icon and a
hand-numbered green node. First, soak the grates and filter, the burner grates and caps and the hood's metal
grease filter lifted into a basin of hot degreasing water and left, drawn largest and greenest with a small clock
because setting the soak first is the whole trick. Second, hood, top down, the underside and face of the hood
sprayed with degreaser, given its dwell time, wiped down and finished with a clean damp pass. Third, splash wall,
sprayed and left, then wiped from the top down so the loosened grease runs toward the hob and not onto clean
wall. Fourth, scrub the softened grates, a hand coming back to the soaked grates and scrubbing the softened baked
rings with a detail brush in a fraction of the time, then rinsing. Fifth, dry and refit, the grates and caps
dried fully before they go back so they do not rust onto the hob, and the hood filter rinsed, dried, and refitted.
A faint hand-drawn dashed loop runs from the first step to the fourth to show the soak working on its own in the
background while the hood and wall are degreased. Across the foot, a single hand-lettered line reads soak first,
hood and wall top down, then scrub the softened grates dry. Warm paper cream background, ink-brown line work, soft
green for the step nodes and the enlarged soak step, a deeper label green for labels, no warm-red. A picture of
one plain idea: set the soak going first and it softens the grease for you while you degrease from the top down.

- Mood: do the cooking zone's soak-first trick, before anything else lift the grates and the hood filter into hot
  degreasing water and leave them, degrease the hood and wall from the top down while they soak, then scrub the
  loosened grates in a fraction of the time; the dirtiest corner of the kitchen, done; the register is calm,

- Avoid: a cluttered checklist, gridlines, neon color, floating typeset text, a corporate look, brand names on
  the degreaser or brush, a friction gauge; keep it a calm five-step ribbon, the soak step largest and greenest
  with a clock, a faint dashed loop from the soak to the scrub so the soak plainly works in the background, the
