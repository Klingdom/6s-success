## Seven zones, not one room

The kitchen looks like the most daunting room in the house to deep clean, but only if you face it as one job. Faced as zones, it is a series of finishable pieces.

> The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time.

Each zone has a clear edge and a clear finish. You do not clean the kitchen; you clean the cooking zone, which is done when the hood, the grates, the splash wall and the hob are degreased and the crock is wiped. Start with the cooking zone or the sink, because those two carry the most grease and grime and give the biggest win.

## How to Shine: the method, plus the grease layer

You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix. Every one still holds. The kitchen simply adds the hardest opponent in the house, grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

> The kitchen keeps the nine rules from the door and adds four moves: soak first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, and switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky. The one input that changes everything here is the food-area degreaser.

Two of those moves carry most of the win. The first is the grease rule itself:

> Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around.

The second is soaking, which is really about time, not effort:

> The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would.

And the move that separates tidy from hygienic:

> The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic.

## Zone by zone: exactly how to clean it

Each zone gets the same three things: the inputs to gather into your caddy, the numbered steps in the order you actually do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there. Where a zone soaks, that step comes first, so the soak works while you do the rest.

**Zone 2, the cooking zone.** Inputs: food-area degreaser, multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded cloth set, a small detail brush set, hot water and dish soap for soaking, the caddy. Steps: soak the soakable parts first (hood filter, burner grates and caps); degrease the hood top down; degrease the splash wall; clean the hob and burner wells; scrub and dry the soaked grates; do the oven door front and panel; wipe the crock, hook, and spice fronts; then finish with the landing space. Grease is the whole job here, and it hides upward.

**Zone 3, the sink and dishwashing zone.** Inputs: multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded cloth set, a small detail brush set, the caddy. Steps: clean the tap, base, and aerator; clean the basin, drain, and overflow; wipe the dispensers and the rim; deal with the sponge, brush, and rack; clean the dishwasher gasket and filter; then clear under the sink.

> The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most.

The other five zones, the prep counter, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil drawers, and the fridge and freezer, run the same way: gather the inputs, soak first where it applies, work top to bottom and back to front, degrease the tacky, clean cloth then dry cloth, and inspect every surface your hand passes over. And at the fridge, the parts that keep food safe, the gasket, the vents, and the coils, are exactly the ones a quick wipe always misses.

> **6S Tip.** Give the degreaser its dwell time instead of scrubbing: spray it, leave it the time on the label, and it lifts the grease for you. Keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink, because together they make a gas that will drive you out of the room.

## What a finished kitchen looks like

Cleaning to a method makes the room shine, but the target is worth naming, because it is what you are cleaning toward.

> A finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut: a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it.

Shine finds the hazards along the way; the safety pass fixes them. When you find the grease-soaked hood filter, the rocking grate, the slow leak under the sink, or the slack fridge gasket, you note it or flag it and keep cleaning.
