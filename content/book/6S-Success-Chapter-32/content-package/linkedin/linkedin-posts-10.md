## 2. Keep the nine rules, add four kitchen moves

You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door. Every one still holds. The kitchen simply adds the hardest opponent in the house, grease.

"The kitchen keeps the nine rules from the door and adds four moves: soak first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, and switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky. The one input that changes everything here is the food-area degreaser."

Grease does not behave like ordinary dirt. An everyday cleaner just smears a tacky film around it. The degreaser lifts it. Match the input to the job, and half the scrubbing disappears.

## 3. Seven small zones, not one big job

The kitchen looks like the most daunting room in the house to deep clean, but only if you face it as one job. Faced as zones, it is a series of finishable pieces.

"The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time."

You do not clean the kitchen. You clean the cooking zone, then the sink, then the next. Each has a clear edge and a clear finish.

## 4. Grease is the whole job, and it hides upward

If you learn one thing about cleaning a kitchen, learn where the grease goes and how it behaves.

"Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around."

Grease drifts up onto the hood, the cabinet undersides, and the wall above the hob. So you clean from the top down, on purpose, and let gravity carry the loosened grease onto surfaces you have not reached yet.

## 5. The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is time

The cooking zone is the dirtiest corner of the kitchen, and the trick to it is to start it before you touch it.

"The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would."

So: lift the grates, caps, and hood filter into hot degreasing water first. While they soften, degrease the hood and splash wall top down. Come back and it wipes off in a fraction of the time.

## 6. The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from

Here is the zone with the biggest gap between what you clean and what actually matters.

"The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most."

Two culprits nobody checks: the sponge, the dirtiest object in the kitchen, and the dishwasher filter, which quietly puts the grit back on your clean glasses. Deal with both, and the room resets from a sink that is genuinely clean.

## 7. The fridge gasket folds are holding mold

The parts of a fridge that keep food safe are exactly the ones a quick wipe always misses.

For Zone 7, gather a multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded cloth set with a dedicated colour for the fridge interior and raw-meat shelf, a compact vacuum with the brush attachment, and warm water and dish soap for the crisper drawers. The steps that matter most:

- Top of the unit first, then the exterior doors and handle.
- Interior shelves and walls, top to bottom.
- Pull and wash the crisper drawers.
- Do the door bins, seals, and gasket, pulling the cloth into every fold.
- Clear the vents, then vacuum the condenser coils.

## 8. Clean to inspect, and flag, do not fix

The last of the nine rules pays for the whole clean twice over, and it works the same in the kitchen as at the door.

Your hand passes over every surface, so your eye should too. While you clean you inspect: the grease-soaked hood filter, the rocking grate, the slow leak under the sink, the slack fridge gasket. You do not stop to fix it. You note it, or flag it, and keep cleaning.

Shine finds hazards, the safety pass fixes them.

The clean leaves the zone genuinely clean and hands the safety pass a ready-made list. You find the hidden hazard exactly because your hand was already there.

## 9. Never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner

Two cloth-and-cleaner habits keep a kitchen clean without making it unsafe.

"Give the degreaser its dwell time instead of scrubbing: spray it, leave it the time on the label, and it lifts the grease for you. Keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink, because together they make a gas that will drive you out of the room."

One colour for the raw-meat shelf and fridge interior means you never carry that onto a food surface. And the bleach rule is not caution, it is chemistry: the two together release a gas. Keep them apart.
