## Stop seeing the room. Start seeing the zones.

The kitchen looks like the most daunting room in the house to deep clean, and if you face it as one job it is. Faced as its zones, it is a series of finishable pieces you can do across a few sessions or knock out the worst two on a Saturday morning. As at the door, the trick is to stop seeing the room and see the zones.

> You do not clean "the kitchen"; you clean the cooking zone, which is done when the hood, the grates, the splash wall and the hob are degreased and the crock is wiped.

> The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time.

You can run them in any order, but the biggest payback for the least effort is the cooking zone or the sink, because those two carry the most grease and the most grime. Within each one the loop runs in the same fixed sequence, and the deep, detailed part of it, the part this chapter is really about, is Shine. Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain get a brisk pass, because you already own them. Then the chapter slows right down.

## Shine is where a room stops being tidy and becomes clean.

The kitchen is where that difference is starkest. You already know the method, so the chapter recaps it rather than re-teaching it, and then adds the one thing that makes a kitchen different.

> You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix. Every one of those still holds. The kitchen simply adds the hardest opponent in the house, grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

That one addition drives the whole clean.

> Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around.

Three things follow. The first is soaking, the single biggest time-saver in the room.

> The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would.

The second is matching the input to the job: a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner for everyday food residue, and the food-area degreaser the moment a surface feels tacky rather than dirty, followed by a clean damp pass so no residue is left where food will touch. The third is lifting, not wiping around.

> The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic.

## Zone by zone: exactly how to clean it.

Here is the core of the chapter. Each of the seven zones gets the same three things: the exact inputs to gather into your caddy before you start, the numbered steps in the order you actually do them, and what to inspect and flag while your hands are already there.

For the **prep counter**, you clear it completely, do the backsplash first top to bottom, clean the knife block, lift the toaster and kettle to clean under them, wipe the open run and degrease the tacky, and finish with the board and edges, because the crumbs live under the appliance feet. The **cooking zone** soaks the hood filter and burner grates first, then degreases the hood top down, the splash wall, and the hob, scrubs the softened grates, and does the oven door, because grease is the whole job here and it hides upward. The **sink and dishwashing zone** cleans the tap and aerator, the basin, drain and overflow, deals with the sponge and rack, and clears the dishwasher gasket and filter, because the two things you never think to clean are the ones that matter most. The **upper cabinets** start with the greasy tops and take each shelf completely empty. The **lower cabinets and cookware** go after the shelf floor, where oil creeping down from pan bases holds dust into a tacky film. The **utensil and utility drawers** come out insert and all, because the crumbs that jam a drawer are underneath the divider and down in the runner track. And the **fridge and freezer** clean the parts that keep food safe, the gasket, the vents, and the condenser coils, exactly the ones a quick wipe always misses.

> A finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut: a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it.

## The calls you have been avoiding.

Cleaning to a method will make the room shine, but a handful of genuinely hard decisions decide whether it stays clear enough to keep clean, and a playbook earns its keep by naming them. The appliance you love but rarely use gets counted by real uses over the past month. The spice rack gets smelled jar by jar over the bin. The sink is not allowed to be a permanent staging area. And the container of good leftovers you have moved to the front three times gets a named meal, a day, and an eater, or it goes in the bin today. The sink one is the reframe worth taping to the cupboard.

> The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most.

## Do not wipe around the clutter. Lift, empty, and clean.

One caution, so the kitchen ends up genuinely hygienic and not just tidied.

> Wiping around the appliances and the clutter instead of lifting and emptying, so the crumbs under the toaster, the dust felt on the cabinet tops, the oil film on the shelf floor, and the mold in the fridge gasket all stay. A kitchen cleaned only where you can see is tidy, not hygienic. Lift, empty, and pull the cloth into the fold.

Gather the inputs first, set the soak going, work the order top to bottom, mist the cloth and not the surface, switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky, and keep a dedicated cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf. The method is not extra effort; it is less, because it works the first time.
