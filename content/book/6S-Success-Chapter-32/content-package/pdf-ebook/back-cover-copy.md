# Back Cover Copy: The Kitchen

## Back-cover text

If the entryway was the room to start with, the kitchen is the room that matters most. You have all six S's now and the loop that keeps them turning, and you have the nine-rule Shine method the last chapter taught you at the door. What you need here is not another lesson but somewhere to point it, and a set of instructions precise enough to follow with your sleeves rolled up. So this chapter, more than any other, is about the clean. It does not stop at telling you what to do. For every small zone of the kitchen it gives you the exact inputs to gather into your caddy and the exact, numbered, step-by-step way to clean and shine it, done the way a professional would do it, degreasing and soaking and all, rather than the way most of us wipe at a surface until it looks a bit better. The kitchen is the busiest room in the house and, by a distance, the dirtiest, because everything that makes a kitchen a kitchen also makes a mess: heat bakes grease onto surfaces, food leaves residue in every corner, and water and steam get everywhere, several times a day. That is exactly why the clean-and-shine step matters most here, and why this chapter spends most of its length on precisely how to do it. By the end you will be able to take the room apart zone by zone and put it back genuinely, inspectably clean, from the hood down to the toe-kick, and know what you are holding in your hand at every step.

Chapter 32 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the second chapter of Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks, and the second of twenty room playbooks that point the finished loop at one real room at a time. Its freeing idea carries from the door: "You do not clean "the kitchen"; you clean the cooking zone, which is done when the hood, the grates, the splash wall and the hob are degreased and the crock is wiped." The kitchen is seven of those zones: "The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time." Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain get a brisk pass; then the chapter slows right down for Shine, recaps the nine rules from the door, and adds the one thing that changes everything here: "Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around." Then, as the heart of the chapter, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It" gives each of the seven zones the same three things: the exact inputs to gather into the caddy, the numbered steps in the true order of work, soak first, degrease top down, lift do not wipe around, and floor last, and an inspect-and-flag list, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. It lands the definition box, "A finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut: a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it." It names the room's genuinely hard calls, the appliance you love but rarely use, the spice rack you are not honest about, and the leftovers you have moved to the front three times, runs the kitchen's real hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the Part 9 signature that replaces the friction meter, a before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after taped up as the standard. Book the room with **The Kitchen Reset card**, the seven zones and the soak-first order to run them, and gather what it needs with **The Kitchen Kit list**, the product types this room actually uses, grouped by job. The kitchen is done, and genuinely clean underneath. One Idea to Keep: "A kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear. It is clean because you lifted the appliances, soaked the grease, pulled the cloth into the gasket, and cleared the filter, cleaning to a method zone by zone and inspecting as you went. Do one zone this weekend, the sink or the cooking zone, and cook in a room that is finally clean underneath." Previous: Chapter 31, The Entryway. Next: Chapter 33, The Pantry.
