## SECTION 2: THE KITCHEN SHINE METHOD

[HOST]
You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door, and every one still holds. The kitchen simply adds the hardest opponent in the house, grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt.

[VISUAL: A kitchen elevation with the order of work as a numbered path: soak the grates and hood filter in a basin, then an arrow down from the hood and cabinet tops to the counter to the toe-kick, a toaster shown lifted with crumbs under it, and a cloth switching from a general spray bottle to a degreaser bottle. Small icons for dwell time, the two-cloth technique, and a flagged fridge cloth colour. A foot line reads "soak first, top to bottom, lift do not wipe around, degrease the tacky".]

[HOST, voice over]
Here is the principle, frozen. Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around. So the kitchen keeps the nine rules from the door and adds four moves: soak first, work top to bottom, lift rather than wipe around, and switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky. The one input that changes everything here is the food-area degreaser.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Soak first. Top to bottom. Lift, do not wipe around. Degrease the tacky.]

[HOST, on camera]
And the single biggest time-saver in the room is soaking, because the most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and the chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere. One thing particular to this room, food safety: keep a dedicated cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, treat the sponge as the dirtiest object in the room, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the basin. Shine finds the hazards; the safety pass fixes them.

## SECTION 3: ZONE BY ZONE, EXACTLY HOW TO CLEAN IT

[HOST]
So let me show you the method cleaning the showcase zone, the cooking zone, and then take you fast through the rest.

[VISUAL: The cooking zone cleaned in numbered sequence, 1 to 8. A caddy set down first with its inputs as labelled icons: food-area degreaser, multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a detail brush set, hot soapy water in a basin. Then the filter, grates and caps dropped in to soak first, the hood sprayed and wiped top down, the splash wall wiped downward, the hob and wells worked, the soaked grates scrubbed and dried, the oven glass buffed, the spice fronts wiped, the landing space degreased last.]

[HOST, voice over]
Gather the inputs into a caddy first: a food-area degreaser, a multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a detail brush set, and hot soapy water for soaking. Then, in order. One, soak first: lift out the hood filter and the burner grates and caps and set them to soak, and leave them while you do everything else, because the soak does what scrubbing cannot. Two, degrease the hood top down, give it its dwell time, then a clean damp pass. Three, degrease the splash wall from the top down, so the grease runs toward the hob, not onto clean wall. Four, clean the hob and the burner wells. Five, come back to the soaked grates, scrub the softened rings, and dry them fully before they go back. Six, do the oven door glass and the panel. Seven, wipe the crock and the spice fronts, where airborne grease settles. Eight, the landing space last, so the surface a hot pan lands on is never slick.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Soak first. Hood. Splash wall. Hob. Scrub the grates. Landing space last.]

[HOST, on camera]
And the other six run the same way. The prep counter, where you lift the toaster and kettle and clean under them, then degrease the tacky run. The sink, where you twist the dishwasher filter out and rinse it, because that filter is why glasses come out gritty. The upper cabinets, where you vacuum the greasy dust off the tops first, then take each shelf empty. The lower cabinets, where you degrease the oily shelf floor and give it dwell time, because a dry cloth just slides over it. The drawers, where you lift the insert right out and tip the grit. And the fridge, where you pull the crisper drawers and wash them in the sink, pull the cloth into the gasket folds, and vacuum the condenser coils behind, because that dust mat makes the motor run hot.

## SECTION 4: THE CALLS, AND THE HAZARDS YOU FLAGGED

[HOST]
Cleaning to a method makes the kitchen shine, but a few hard calls decide whether it stays clean, and while you cleaned, you were flagging hazards to fix now.

[VISUAL: Three quick beats, the counter appliance counted by real uses this month, the spice jar smelled over the bin, the sink held to genuinely clean not just rinsed. Then the safety pass acting on what the clean flagged: the loose knife sheathed, the pan handles turned in over the hob, the tall unit anchored.]

[HOST, voice over]
The appliance you love but rarely use is counted by real uses over the past month, not the ones you intended; anything you reach for less than a couple of times a week comes off the counter. The spice you are not honest about gets smelled, one jar at a time over the bin; if it smells of dust rather than of itself, it is no longer seasoning anything. And the sink is not allowed to be a staging area, because the sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: Count the appliance by real uses. Hold the sink to genuinely clean.]

[HOST, on camera]
And the kitchen holds more real hazards than any other room, so run the five safety questions once directly. Burn or fire: never leave oil heating unattended, and keep tea towels away from a live burner. Fall, cut, or crush: never leave a knife blade-up in the rack, turn pan handles in over the hob, store blades sheathed. Water and electricity: keep the kettle and a charging phone out of splash distance of the basin. Poison, choke, or strangle: never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner, and seal raw meat on the lowest shelf. And the plain fall: reach the top shelf from a stable step stool, never a chair. Shine found them; the safety pass fixes them.
