## SHORT 1: Grease hides upward, so soak first
**Topic: the kitchen Shine method, the grease layer on top of the nine rules. Runtime ~60 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The reason the kitchen fights you is grease, and grease does not behave like ordinary dirt. It hides upward, and it does not wipe off. It smears.

**Script:**
You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, and clean to inspect every surface your hand passes over. The kitchen keeps all of that and adds one big new opponent, grease, so it adds four moves. Here they are. Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around. So, in order: soak first, because the most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Work top to bottom, because grease hides upward, on the hood and the cabinet tops. Lift the appliance, do not wipe around it. And the moment a surface feels tacky rather than dirty, switch from your multi-surface cleaner to the degreaser and give it its dwell time. One more thing, particular to this room: keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink.

## SHORT 2: Seven zones, not one room
**Topic: the hero device, the kitchen as micro-zones. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The kitchen looks like the most daunting room in the house to deep clean, and if you face it as one job, it is. Faced as its zones, it is a series of finishable pieces.

**Script:**
Here is the trick that makes the busiest room in the house doable. You do not clean the kitchen. You clean one zone of it, then the next. The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time. Each one has a clear edge and a clear finish. You do not clean the kitchen, you clean the cooking zone, which is done when the hood, the grates, the splash wall and the hob are degreased and the crock is wiped. And if you want the highest payback for the least effort, start with the cooking zone or the sink, because those two carry the most grease and the most grime and give the biggest visible and hygienic win. You never have to face the kitchen. You face one zone, with a clear finish, and then you are done for the day.

## SHORT 3: How to deep-clean the cooking zone, step by step
**Topic: the showcase zone, Zone 2 inputs and numbered steps. Runtime ~75 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The dirtiest corner of your kitchen is the cooking zone, the hood, the grates, the splash wall. Here is exactly how a professional degreases it, in order, and the first step does most of the work for you.

**Script:**
First, gather the inputs into a caddy: a food-area degreaser, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a small detail brush set, and hot water with dish soap in the sink or a basin. Now the steps, in the order you actually do them. One, soak first: lift out the hood's metal grease filter and the burner grates and caps, and set them to soak in the hot degreasing water. Leave them while you do everything else, because the soak does what scrubbing cannot. Two, degrease the hood top down: spray it, give it its dwell time, wipe, then a clean damp pass. Three, degrease the splash wall from the top down, so the loosened grease runs toward the hob and not onto clean wall. Four, clean the hob and the burner wells where boil-overs bake into a ring, and run a detail brush around the knob stems. Five, come back to the soaked grates: scrub the softened rings, rinse, and dry them fully before they go back so they do not rust onto the hob. Six, do the oven door glass and the control panel, with a barely damp cloth so nothing runs behind the knobs. Seven, wipe the crock, the pot-holder hook, and the spice fronts, because airborne grease settles on everything near the burners. Eight, finish with the landing space, the one surface a hot pan lands on, so it is never slick. That is the dirtiest corner of the kitchen, cleaned to a professional standard.

## SHORT 4: The two things you never think to clean
**Topic: Zone 3, the sink, the sponge and the dishwasher filter. Runtime ~55 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, so it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. And the two things you never think to clean are the two that matter most.

**Script:**
The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most. Gather your inputs: a multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a detail brush set, and your caddy. Then, in order. Clean the tap, base, and aerator, pushing a detail brush around the base collar where limescale rings it. Clean the basin, and scrub the drain flange and run the brush into the overflow slot, which grows its own slime unseen. Wipe under the soap dispensers where a tacky ring of dried soap hides. Wring the sponge out hard and stand it on edge to drain, and retire it the day it smells while dry. Then the two that matter most: wipe the dishwasher gasket all the way around, pulling gently into its folds where black specks hide, and twist the filter out of the floor of the machine and rinse it under the tap, because a filter that has never come out is why things come out gritty. Finish under the sink, wiping the cabinet floor into the back corners.

## SHORT 5: Take the before before you touch a thing
**Topic: the before-and-after pair, the Part 9 signature. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The most valuable photo of your kitchen is the one you take before you soak a single grate, because within a month you will not believe the room was ever the other one.

**Script:**
Here is the ritual that closes every room from here on, and it is what makes the clean stick. Before you touch a thing, stand at the doorway and photograph the kitchen exactly as it is, framing the run of counter, the hob and hood, and the sink together, because that is the working heart and where the most change shows. Then do the work, seven zones cleaned to the method, one at a time, the grease soaked off and the appliances lifted. And when it is done, stand in exactly the same spot and take the second photo. The two do different jobs. The before is the valuable one, the proof of how far the room came. The after is the standard, the picture the kitchen has to match from now on. Here is the frozen line for it. This is the kitchen the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, cleared, degreased, and genuinely clean, from the hood down to the toe-kick. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a kitchen this clean is one you will actually want to cook in, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.
