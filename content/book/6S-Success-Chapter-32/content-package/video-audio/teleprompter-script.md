# Teleprompter Script: The Kitchen

**Chapter 32, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** the-kitchen
**Length:** about 950 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading. This is the SECOND room of Part Nine, and its heart is HOW TO DEEP-CLEAN each kitchen zone, degreasing and all, so read it warm, capable, and a little proud; you already own the method, now clean the room that matters most to a professional standard. No hype, no shame about the before.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

You have done the door. Now the room that matters most.

The kitchen is the busiest space in the house.

The one the whole family works in every day.

And, minute for minute, the one 6S pays back the fastest.

It is also, by a distance, the dirtiest.

Because everything that makes a kitchen a kitchen also makes a mess.

Heat bakes grease onto surfaces. Food leaves residue in every corner.

Water and steam get everywhere, several times a day.

So this chapter, more than any other, is about the clean.

A word before we start.

Anyone can clear a counter.

A kitchen that is genuinely clean is a different thing.

The crumbs under the toaster are gone.

The grease is off the top of the cabinets.

The filter in the dishwasher has been rinsed.

The mold is out of the fridge gasket.

That is the difference between tidy and hygienic.

Now, a whole kitchen feels like too much.

Because you are looking at it as one big job.

It is not.

The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job.

The prep counter. The cooking zone. The sink.

The upper cabinets. The lower cabinets and cookware.

The utensil drawers. And the fridge and freezer.

Do them one at a time, and the busiest room comes back to you.

Before the deep clean, four passes are quick.

Sort each zone first, because a buried surface cannot be cleaned.

Straighten what survives into a home chosen by use.

Safety, Standardize, and Sustain come after the shine.

Now we slow right down, for Shine.

You already know the nine rules from the door.

Top to bottom. Dry or clear before wet. Back to front, clean to dirty.

Mist the cloth, not the surface. Two cloths, one to clean, one to dry.

Colour-code your cloths. Match the cleaner to the surface.

Give it dwell time. And clean to inspect and flag, do not fix.

The kitchen adds the hardest opponent in the house: grease.

And grease hides upward.

So the kitchen adds four moves.

Soak first. Work top to bottom. Lift, do not wipe around.

And switch to the degreaser the moment a surface is tacky.

The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm.

It is time.

Set the grates and the hood filter to soak before you touch anything else.

The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere.

One more thing, particular to the kitchen: food safety.

Keep a dedicated cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf.

Treat the sponge as the dirtiest object in the room.

And never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the basin.

Now watch that method clean the showcase zone, the cooking zone.

Gather the inputs into your caddy first.

Degreaser, multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded cloth set.

A detail brush set, and a basin of hot soapy water.

Step one. Soak first.

Lift out the hood filter, the burner grates and caps.

Set them to soak in hot degreasing water, and leave them.

Step two. Degrease the hood, top down.

Spray, give it its dwell time, wipe, then a clean damp pass.

Step three. Degrease the splash wall, top to bottom.

Wipe downward, so the grease runs toward the hob, not onto clean wall.

Step four. Clean the hob and the burner wells.

Work the wells where boil-overs bake into a ring.

Step five. Come back to the soaked grates.

Scrub the softened rings, rinse, and dry them fully before they go back.

Step six. Do the oven door glass, then the panel.

Step seven. Wipe the crock, the hook, and the spice fronts.

Step eight. Finish with the landing space, the surface hot pans land on.

That is the dirtiest corner of the kitchen, cleaned to a method.

The other six zones run the very same way.

The prep counter, where you lift the toaster and clean under it.

The sink, where you pull the dishwasher filter and rinse it clear.

The upper cabinets, tops vacuumed, each shelf taken empty.

The lower cabinets, where you degrease the oily shelf floor with dwell time.

The drawers, where you lift the insert right out and tip the grit.

And the fridge, where you pull the crisper drawers and wash them in the sink.

Pull the cloth into the gasket fold, and vacuum the coils behind.

Then a few hard calls hold the room.

The appliance you love but rarely use, counted by real uses this month.

The spice you are not honest about, smelled one jar at a time over the bin.

And whether the sink is allowed to be a staging area.

When the seven zones are done, take the second photograph.

Stand at the doorway, framing the counter, the hob and hood, and the sink.

The before is the valuable one, the proof.

The after is the standard, the picture the kitchen has to match.

A kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear.

It is clean because you lifted the appliances and soaked the grease.

Because you pulled the cloth into the gasket and cleared the filter.

It is the biggest single win in the house, and now it is done.
