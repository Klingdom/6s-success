## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The seven zones (frozen, structural list):** The kitchen is seven small zones, not one big job: the prep counter, the cooking zone, the sink, the upper cabinets, the lower cabinets and cookware, the utensil and utility drawers, and the fridge and freezer. Do them one at a time and the busiest room in the house comes back to you a zone at a time.

- **The grease rule (frozen, kitchen Shine principle):** Grease is the whole job in a kitchen, and grease hides upward. Set the soakable parts to soak before you do anything else, work top to bottom so what you loosen falls onto what you have not cleaned yet, and reach for the degreaser the moment your everyday cleaner only smears a tacky film around.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished kitchen is one you could start cooking in with your eyes shut: a clear run of prep counter, oil and your turner within one reach of a clean hob, an empty dry sink the room resets from, daily dishes between shoulder and eye height, pans that come out in one motion, drawers that close on one push, and a fridge where the eat-first bin sits right where the door opens onto it.

- **The lift-do-not-wipe-around reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The crumbs and grease you never see are the ones living under the toaster's feet, on top of the cabinets, and in the fold of the fridge gasket. A wipe around the clutter is not a clean; lifting the appliance, emptying the shelf, and pulling the cloth into the fold is. In a kitchen, that difference is the difference between a room that looks tidy and a room that is actually hygienic.

- **The soak-and-dwell reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The most powerful tool in a kitchen clean is not a scrubbing arm, it is time. Set the burner grates and the hood filter to soak in hot degreasing water before you touch anything else, and let every degreaser sit for its dwell time. The chemistry lifts baked grease while you work elsewhere, so you scrub a fraction of what you otherwise would.

- **The reset-point reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The sink is the point the whole kitchen resets from, which means it has to be genuinely clean, not just rinsed. The sponge is the dirtiest object in the room and the dishwasher filter is why your glasses come out gritty, so in this one zone the things you never think to clean are exactly the things that matter most.

- **The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure):** This is the kitchen the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, cleared, degreased, and genuinely clean, from the hood down to the toe-kick. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a kitchen this clean is one you will actually want to cook in, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A kitchen is not clean because the counters are clear. It is clean because you lifted the appliances, soaked the grease, pulled the cloth into the gasket, and cleared the filter, cleaning to a method zone by zone and inspecting as you went. Do one zone this weekend, the sink or the cooking zone, and cook in a room that is finally clean underneath.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / soak first, then scrub / lift and empty, do not wipe around. The nine-rule Shine method from Chapter 31 still governs; the kitchen adds grease, soaking, dwell time, and food safety.

## The four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win:** Do the cooking zone's soak-first trick. Before anything else, lift the burner grates and caps and the hood filter into hot degreasing water and leave them. While they soften on their own, degrease the splash wall and the hood from the top down. Come back, scrub the loosened grates in a fraction of the time, dry them fully, and the dirtiest corner of the kitchen is done.
- **Family Challenge:** Give each person a zone to own on reset day, and put the fridge eat-first bin right where the door opens onto it, so the answer to what is for dinner comes from the bin rather than the cupboard, and far less food is thrown away.
- **6S Tip:** Give the degreaser its dwell time instead of scrubbing: spray it, leave it the time on the label, and it lifts the grease for you. Keep a separate cloth colour for the fridge interior and the raw-meat shelf, and never mix bleach with an acidic or ammonia cleaner in the sink, because together they make a gas that will drive you out of the room.
- **Common Mistake:** Wiping around the appliances and the clutter instead of lifting and emptying, so the crumbs under the toaster, the dust felt on the cabinet tops, the oil film on the shelf floor, and the mold in the fridge gasket all stay. A kitchen cleaned only where you can see is tidy, not hygienic. Lift, empty, and pull the cloth into the fold.

## The room: source facts (from the Micro Zone Manual, Kitchen, 7 zones)
Carry specific nouns; never a generic sentence that fits any room. Each zone's full Inputs + numbered Steps + inspect-and-flag are authored in the manuscript from the Manual's shine_detail (surfaces, methods, products_used, inspect_as_you_clean).

1. **Primary prep counter.** Job: where food is cut, mixed, plated. Done: one clear board-wide run holding only board, knife block/strip, and salt; kettle and toaster back against the wall, cords coiled; no mail, no fruit bowl, no phone. Shine: lift the appliances (crumbs live under their feet); backsplash and the counter-to-backsplash seam; degrease greasy splatter; front lip and underside overhang. Call: the stand mixer question (count real uses; under a couple a week comes off the counter; not switched on in a year leaves). Hazards: knife blade-up in the rack or under suds (fall/cut/crush); toaster under a cabinet or against paper towel (burn/fire).
2. **Cooking zone.** Job: the space either side of the burners. Done: oil/salt/pepper within one reach; a crock of no more than six used-this-month tools; pot holders on a hook; weekly spices at the front; a clear hot-pan landing space. Shine (grease is the whole job): soak the hood filter and burner grates/caps first; degrease hood underside and splash wall top-down; hob, burner wells, knobs; oven door front and glass; utensil crock and spice-jar fronts (tacky grease coat); the landing space. Call: the spice rack you are not honest about (smell each jar over the bin; keep spices for food cooked in the last year). Hazards: oil unattended, tea towel near a live burner (burn/fire); pan handle turned out over the front edge (fall/cut/crush).
3. **Sink and dishwashing zone.** Job: the washing point the whole kitchen resets from. Done: empty sink, dry basin, wiped drain flange; one brush and one sponge standing to drain; drying rack empty or only-wet; under-sink one tray with soap, tablets, one all-purpose, pipes visible. Shine: tap/base/aerator (limescale); basin, drain flange, overflow; soap dispenser rings; the sponge (dirtiest object) and brush stood to drain; the dishwasher door gasket and the FILTER (why glasses come out gritty); under-sink cabinet floor and pipes. Call: whether soaking is allowed (a deadline not a ban: only until the current cycle ends or you next leave the room). Hazards: appliance/socket/phone within splash of the basin (water/electricity); bleach mixed with acid or ammonia (poison/choke/strangle).
4. **Upper cabinet zone.** Job: eye-level storage for daily dishes. Done: daily between shoulder and eye height, one category per stack, no plate stack over six, every mug liftable without moving another, top shelf only for less-than-monthly. Shine: cabinet TOPS (dust-and-grease felt, vacuum then wipe); interior shelves and the dust band at the back (empty each shelf); the greasy underside nearest the hob (degrease); glass fronts (glass cleaner on the cloth); doors, handles, hinges, shelf-pin holes (detail brush). Call: the second set of dishes (keep one everyday set sized to the household plus two; the good/old set goes if not used in two years). Hazards: glassware stacked at head height (fall/cut/crush); reaching a top shelf from a chair or counter (fall).
5. **Lower cabinet and cookware zone.** Job: deep low storage for pots, pans, lids, bakeware. Done: every pan out in one motion, nested no more than three or on edge in a divider, lids racked, sheets on end, the two nightly pans at the front. Shine (everything comes out): the shelf floor and its tacky oil film (degrease, dwell, wipe; a dry cloth just skates over it); interior walls and back; door interior and lid rail; wipe each pan and twist the handle; pull-out runners and the toe-kick vent (vacuum). Call: the third frying pan (cap at your busiest-week count plus one; the expensive unloved one goes). Hazards: a tall pan nest or cast iron above waist height (fall/cut/crush); loose blades in a bowl in a low cabinet (fall/cut/crush).
6. **Utensil and utility drawers.** Job: shallow drawers of hand tools, flatware, wraps. Done: one job per drawer named inside the front edge, flatware in a divided insert, foil/film/paper on end, each drawer closes flush on one push. Shine: lift the divided insert right out and tip the grit (the crumbs that jam a drawer live under the divider and in the runner track); the drawer base (degrease if near the hob); both runner tracks and rollers (detail brush); the inner front edge (keep the name readable) and handle; the foil/wrap boxes. Call: the drawer everyone calls the junk drawer (one named drawer with a list and a divider, not an amnesty). Hazards: an unsheathed mandoline or paring knife loose among the spatulas (fall/cut/crush); loose button batteries at child height (poison/choke/strangle).
7. **Refrigerator and freezer.** Job: cold storage that keeps food safe and shows what you own before you shop. Done: raw meat sealed on the lowest shelf, dated clear leftover containers at eye level, one eat-first bin where the door opens onto it, rear vents clear, door for condiments only, nothing unlabeled in the freezer. Shine: the top of the unit (greasy dust felt, vacuum then wipe); exterior doors and handle (follow the grain, no abrasive); interior shelves and walls (top to bottom); pull the crisper drawers and WASH in the sink, clear the sticky ring beneath; door bins, seals, and the GASKET FOLDS (pull the cloth in, mold gathers there); rear vents and freezer interior; the CONDENSER COILS (vacuum, the dust mat makes the motor run hot). Call: the container you keep moving to the front (name the meal, day, and eater or bin it today; fix the cause of cooking too much). Hazards: raw-meat juices dripping onto ready-to-eat food (poison/choke/strangle); heavy glass jars on the top shelf or door (fall/cut/crush).

## The kit for the kitchen (product TYPES; no brand names)
- **Clean and shine (the inputs that do the work here):** a food-area degreaser (the kitchen's key input, for the hood, grates, splash wall, and the oil film on shelf floors); a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner; a streak-free glass and mirror cleaner (glass cabinet fronts, the oven door window); a colour-coded microfiber cloth set (with a dedicated colour for the fridge interior and raw-meat shelf); a small detail brush set (burner wells, knob stems, drain flange, hinges, runner tracks); a compact vacuum with attachments (cabinet tops, condenser coils, toe-kick); a portable caddy. Plus dish soap and hot water for soaking grates and washing crisper drawers.
- **Sort and routing:** a Keep, Relocate, Donate, Recycle, Trash container set.
- **Homes (Storage & Organization):** airtight food containers; a drawer divider set; a lid organizer; a pan and bakeware divider; single and two-tier slide-out organizers; a spice organizer; refrigerator and produce-keeper bins; small and medium clear lidded bins; a leak tray (under-sink).
- **Standardize (Visual Controls):** a label maker; removable write-on labels; shelf location labels; a food date-label kit and a FIFO date marker (leftovers and the freezer); a moisture or humidity monitor (enclosed storage).
- **Safety:** anti-tip anchors and hardware (a tall unit or a loaded rack); a stable step stool (the top shelf, never a chair); a surge protector with overload protection; impact-rated safety glasses if a deep clean calls for it.
