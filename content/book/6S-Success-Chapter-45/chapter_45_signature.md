# Chapter 45 Signature: The Garage

The Part 9 recurring close. The friction meter is retired (it reached GOAL at Chapter 30); each room playbook ends instead on a matched before-and-after room signature: the before kept per Chapter 6, the after held as the ideal-state standard per Chapter 25.

## The before-and-after room signature
Stand in the doorway with the whole depth of the garage in frame: bench, walls, and slab. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and do not move the pile inside the door first, because that pile is the most honest thing in the picture. Then work the seven zones, finish on the floor, and take the after from the same spot.

## No variation, but one thing worth naming
This chapter uses the plain Part 9 close. It needed no device, because the room supplies its own: the floor is the part of the picture that keeps working after the photo is taken.

Every other room in the book stays finished by being maintained. This one starts working for you. A pale slab is a diagnostic surface, and the frozen caption carries that idea rather than a separate signature mechanic. Compare with the variations used so far: Ch 39 the child takes the after photo; Ch 40 the after is the nightly safe state; Ch 43 the after contains one detail that expires; Ch 44 the after is taped inside a drawer and matched nightly. Here, the after simply keeps reporting.

## The before-and-after caption (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> This is the garage the whole loop was for: the same room you started with, a bare bench holding the vise and the task light, tools hanging on their drawn outlines, fluids upright and dated, the long handles head-up on their rail, the bins overhead labelled to be read from the slab, and concrete you can see the colour of. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard. Then keep the floor pale, because from now on the slab is the instrument that tells you what your car and your house are doing before either of them tells you themselves.

## One Idea to Keep (frozen, byte-verbatim)
> A garage is not clear because you stacked it neatly. It is clear because you stopped letting the rest of the house postpone its decisions in here: everything that came in got a verdict the same day, everything that stayed got a rail, a hook, an outline, or a dated bin, and the floor got clean enough to read. Do one zone this week, and make it the workbench, because until one solid surface is clear, everything you pick up just goes back down somewhere else.
