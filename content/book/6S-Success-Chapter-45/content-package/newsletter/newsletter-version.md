# Chapter 45 Newsletter: The Garage

**Subject:** Your garage does not fail from use. It fails from arrival.
**Preview:** The room that takes what every other room evicts, and never forces a verdict.

---

Every other room in this book fails from use. The kitchen fails from cooking, the laundry room from washing, the bathroom from water.

The garage does not fail from use at all. It fails from arrival.

Things come in from every other room in the house, they are set down, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever comes along to force a verdict on any of them. That is how your best chisel and a box you have not opened since you moved in end up on the same shelf, treated exactly alike.

So the sorting question here is unusual, and it is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or was it only postponed". The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack that has stood by the side door since spring. None of those is in storage. Each is a decision somebody put down and walked away from.

**Seven zones, and the order matters at both ends.** The primary workbench, the hand tool wall, the power tool and battery zone, the automotive care zone, the sports and recreation zone, the lawn and garden tool zone, and the bulk and overhead storage. The bench comes first because it is the only surface anything can be laid out on. The slab comes last, because until the walls and racks have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile to work around.

**Four rules on top of the nine rules of Shine.**

*Nothing here is being stored, it is being postponed.* Anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day.

*Get it all off the floor, then read the floor.* Degrease the slab properly once, and a sweep holds it. What you buy is a pale floor, and a pale floor turns every new drip into information.

*Weight and height are the hazard.* Heavy bins low, light bulky things high, rack bolts into joists and never plasterboard alone, and nothing overhanging the bay where a person opens a car door.

*This room holds the real chemistry.* Petrol, antifreeze, weed killer, oxidising feed, oily rags, lithium packs. None of it is dangerous by itself. All of it is dangerous by proximity.

Two facts worth carrying away even if you do nothing else: oily rags can heat on their own with no flame anywhere near them, and antifreeze tastes sweet enough that a dog will drink a puddle of it.

Do one zone this week, and make it the bench.
