# X Thread: The Garage

Chapter 45 of *6S Success: Home Edition*, the fifteenth room playbook of Part Nine and the longest room in the book. One thread, 14 posts. Each at or under 280 characters, counting its number line. Plain "1/" numbering on its own line.

1/
Part Nine reaches the biggest, heaviest room in the house, and the one nobody knows how to start: the garage.

(112 chars)

---

2/
Every other room in this book fails from use. The kitchen from cooking, the bathroom from water. The garage does not fail from use at all. It fails from arrival.

(164 chars)

---

3/
Things come in from every other room, get set down, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever forces a verdict on any of them.

(149 chars)

---

4/
That is how your best chisel and a box you have not opened since you moved in end up on the same shelf, treated exactly alike.

(129 chars)

---

5/
So the sorting question here is not the usual one. It is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or was it only postponed".

(152 chars)

---

6/
The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack by the side door since spring. The printer you replaced. None of that is storage. It is a decision somebody put down and walked away from.

(189 chars)

---

7/
The rule that keeps it clear afterwards is a one-day rule: anything carried in with no rail, shelf, hook, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day.

(169 chars)

---

8/
Seven zones, and the order matters at both ends. Start at the bench, because until one solid surface is clear, everything you pick up just goes back down somewhere else.

(172 chars)

---

9/
And finish on the floor. It sounds backwards. Everything in a garage wants to end up on the slab, so until the walls and racks have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile.

(202 chars)

---

10/
Then degrease it properly, once, and a sweep holds it after that. Here is what you actually bought with that afternoon.

(123 chars)

---

11/
A pale floor turns every new drip into information. You see the gearbox weeping or the water heater starting to go the week it starts, not the month it fails.

(162 chars)

---

12/
Two dangers people underrate. Oily rags balled in an open bin can heat on their own as the oil cures. No spark, no flame nearby. They belong in a metal can with a lid.

(171 chars)

---

13/
And antifreeze tastes sweet. Not a metaphor: a dog will drink a puddle of it. Nothing in this room is ever poured into a container that does not carry its own label.
