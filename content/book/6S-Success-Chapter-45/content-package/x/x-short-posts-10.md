# X Short Posts: The Garage

Chapter 45 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts, each at or under 280 characters counting its number line. Plain numbering.

---

1/
Your garage does not fail from use. It fails from arrival. It takes what every other room evicts, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever forces a verdict on any of it.

(193 chars)

---

2/
The sorting question in a garage is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or only postponed".

(124 chars)

---

3/
The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack by the side door. The printer you replaced. None of that is storage. Each one is a decision somebody put down and walked away from.

(182 chars)

---

4/
The rule that keeps a garage clear: anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day. Otherwise it quietly becomes part of the floor.

(188 chars)

---

5/
Clean the garage floor last, not first. Everything in the room wants to end up on the slab, so until the walls have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile to work around.

(201 chars)

---

6/
Degrease the slab properly once and a sweep holds it. What you bought is a pale floor, and a pale floor turns every new drip into information.

(145 chars)

---

7/
You will see the gearbox weeping or the water heater starting to go the week it starts, not the month it fails. On a dark stained floor all of that is invisible until it is expensive.

(186 chars)

---

8/
Oily rags do not need a flame. Rags soaked with oil, stain, or solvent, balled in an open bin, can heat on their own as the oil cures. Metal can, lid on.

(156 chars)

---

9/
Antifreeze tastes sweet. That is not a metaphor, and it is why nothing in a garage is ever poured into a container that does not carry its own label.

(152 chars)

10/
