# Chapter 45 Facebook Longform Post: The Garage

---

Here is the thing about garages that took me a while to see.

Every other room in your house fails from use. The kitchen gets dirty because you cook. The bathroom gets dirty because of water. The laundry room fails because you do laundry.

The garage does not fail from use. It fails from arrival.

Things come in from every other room in the house, they get set down, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever comes along to force a verdict on any of them. That is how your best chisel and a box you have not opened since you moved in end up sitting on the same shelf, treated exactly alike.

So the sorting question here is not the usual one. It is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or was it only postponed".

The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack by the side door. The printer you replaced. None of that is storage. Each one is a decision somebody put down and walked away from.

The rule that keeps it clear afterwards: anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day. Otherwise it becomes part of the floor.

Two practical things.

The first sounds backwards: clean the floor last. Everything in a garage wants to end up on the slab, so until the walls and racks have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile to work around.

Then degrease it properly, once, and a sweep holds it. What you bought is a pale floor, and that is not cosmetic. It is an instrument: you will see the gearbox weeping or the water heater going the week it starts, not the month it fails. On a dark stained floor, all of that is invisible until it is expensive.

The second is a safety fact most people do not know. Rags soaked with oil, stain, or solvent, balled up in an open bin, can heat on their own as the oil cures. No spark, no flame anywhere near them. They belong in a metal can with a lid.

And one more, because it matters if you have a dog or small children: antifreeze tastes sweet. That is not a metaphor. Nothing in a garage should ever be poured into a container that does not carry its own label.

Do one zone this week, and make it the bench.
