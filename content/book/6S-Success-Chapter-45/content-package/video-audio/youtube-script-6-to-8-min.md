# Chapter 45 YouTube Script: The Garage

*Six to eight minutes.*

---

**[Cold open, on the room]**

Every other room in this book fails from use. Your kitchen gets dirty because you cook. The garage does not fail from use at all. It fails from arrival.

**[Title]**

**[Section 1: the diagnosis]**

Things come in from every other room in the house. Somebody sets them down. And because the door stays shut most of the week, nothing ever comes along to force a verdict on any of them.

That is how your best chisel and a box you have not opened since you moved in end up on the same shelf, treated exactly alike.

So the sorting question here is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or was it only postponed".

The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack by the side door. The printer you replaced. None of those is in storage.

**[Section 2: the one-day rule]**

And here is what keeps it clear afterwards. Anything carried in with no rail, shelf, hook, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day. Not next weekend, because next weekend is exactly how it got here.

**[Section 3: the order of work]**

Seven zones, and the order matters at both ends.

Start at the workbench. Not the pile inside the door, which is what everybody does. The bench, because until one solid surface is clear you have nowhere to lay anything out while you judge it.

And finish on the floor. That sounds backwards. It is the most useful bit of sequencing in the chapter. Everything in a garage wants to end up on the slab, so until the walls and racks have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile to work around.

**[Section 4: the pale floor]**

Then degrease it properly. Once. Degreaser, a stiff brush, absorbent left on the old stains overnight. After that, a sweep holds it.

And what you actually bought is this: a pale floor turns every new drip into information.

On a dark stained slab, a fresh oil spot is invisible. On a pale one it is obvious the day it appears. You see the gearbox weeping, or the water heater starting to go, the week it starts rather than the month it fails.

The floor becomes an instrument. It is the only room in this book that ends up working for you.

**[Section 5: weight and chemistry]**

Two dangers, because this is the heaviest room in the house.

Weight and height. Heavy bins on the lowest rack. Light bulky things highest. Rack bolts into joists, never plasterboard. Nothing overhead you cannot bring down with both hands on a stable ladder. Nothing overhanging the bay where a car door opens.

And the chemistry, where the key idea is that almost none of it is dangerous by itself. It is dangerous by proximity. Petrol away from the water heater's pilot, because vapour pools low. Fertiliser is an oxidiser and never shares a shelf with fuel. Lithium packs never sleep on a charger.

Two facts most people do not know. Oily rags can heat on their own as the oil cures, with no flame anywhere near them, which is why they go in a metal can with a lid. And antifreeze tastes sweet, which is why nothing here is ever decanted into an unlabelled bottle.

**[Close]**

Seven hard calls, one per zone, all versions of the same question: stored, or postponed?

Take the before photo, work the seven zones, finish on the floor, and take the after.

Do one zone this week, and make it the bench. Chapter 45. Next: the workshop.
