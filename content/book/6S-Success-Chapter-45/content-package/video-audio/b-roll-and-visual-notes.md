# Chapter 45 B-Roll and Visual Notes: The Garage

## Must-get shots
- The workbench cleared to bare wood, ideally as a time-lapse. This is the image of the chapter.
- The vacuum crevice nozzle going into a bench dog hole, tight and slow.
- A drop of oil laid along a vise screw, then the vise wound open and closed.
- Hand tools coming off a pegboard, wiped with an oiled rag, and going back onto painted outlines. Hold on one empty outline.
- A soft brush or air clearing packed dust out of a drill's motor vents.
- A battery pack lifted off a charger and set on a shelf, deliberately, at the end of a job.
- A bottle shoulder wiped and a month written on it in marker.
- A drip tray slid out with one fresh ring visible on it.
- Absorbent granules worked into an old stain with a stiff brush, left, then swept off with a push broom.
- The finished slab: a slow pan across pale, swept concrete with nothing standing on it.
- Oily rags going into a metal can and the lid going on.

## Do not shoot
- Any readable brand name, logo, or label text. Types only.
- Anyone under a raised vehicle, or a car on a jack at all. The jack appears in the hazard list, never on camera as a procedure.
- Any chemical being decanted, mixed, or poured out onto ground, into a gutter, or down a drain.
- Anyone working from a ladder on a sloped surface, or carrying a bin down a ladder with no free hand.
- Fuel containers near any heat source, even staged as a "wrong way" shot.

## Tone
Workmanlike and unhurried. The safety material is delivered as maintenance, not alarm, and the two surprising facts (oily rags, antifreeze) are stated plainly and once.
