# Chapter 45 Reuse Risk Check: The Garage

## Source provenance
Every zone, its purpose, its passes, its hard call, its watch-for items, and its shine detail are folded from the Micro Zone Manual's Garage entry, which is the project's own material. The seven calls are the Manual's `the_call` entries. The room's intro and its three tips supplied the layer: postponed rather than stored, the bench-first and slab-last order, the degrease-once instruction, and the pale-floor-as-information idea. Nothing here is drawn from an outside author, method, or publication.

## Claims that need to stay defensible
This chapter makes more physical-safety assertions than any other in the book, so each is listed with how it is pitched:

1. Rags soaked with oil, stain, or solvent can self-heat as the oil cures. Stated qualitatively, as the reason for a closed metal can. No temperature, time, or incident data.
2. Antifreeze is sweet-tasting and toxic to pets and children. Stated as the reason for the no-decanting rule. No toxicology, no dose, no treatment advice.
3. Petrol vapour pools low. Stated as the reason for keeping fuel away from a pilot flame.
4. Fertiliser is an oxidiser and should not be stored with fuel. Stated as a separation rule.
5. Lithium packs should not be left charging unattended on a combustible surface. Stated as a habit, with no chemistry.
6. A loaded tall unit tips forward when its top is loaded and pulled. Stated as the reason for anchoring and loading low.
7. Overhead rack fixings must land in structural members rather than plasterboard alone. Stated as a rule, not an installation guide.

None carries a citation at the level it is pitched, and **no safety statistic was invented**. If a references appendix is ever added, these seven are the lines that would take one.

## Liability posture, and the two hard limits
- **Nobody is ever under a raised vehicle.** The trolley jack and axle stands appear once, in the hazard list, as something to flag. No lifting procedure is described anywhere, and the b-roll notes forbid filming a car on a jack at all.
- **No disposal route other than hazardous waste.** Fluids, chemicals, and unlabelled containers all route to a household hazardous waste collection, and the chapter says three separate times that nothing goes down a drain, into a gutter, or onto the ground.
- No electrical work: the bench outlet is inspected and flagged, never opened.
- No blade-grinding procedure. Running a flat file along a spade edge is garden-tool maintenance, and it is the only sharpening step in the chapter.
- No advice on handling, opening, or identifying an unlabelled chemical. The rule is explicitly that you cannot safely use what you cannot name, so it goes to hazardous waste unopened.

## Verdict
Low risk, with the caveat that this is the chapter whose safety claims would matter most if the book were ever challenged. All seven are pitched qualitatively, none is quantified, and the two genuine liability traps, working under a vehicle and improper chemical disposal, are closed explicitly rather than left to inference.
