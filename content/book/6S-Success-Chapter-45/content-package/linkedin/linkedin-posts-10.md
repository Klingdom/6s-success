# Chapter 45 LinkedIn Posts: The Garage

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. It fails from arrival, not use
Every other room fails from use. The kitchen from cooking, the bathroom from water. The garage fails from arrival.

Things come in from every other room, get set down, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever forces a verdict on any of them. That is how a good chisel and a box nobody has opened since the move end up on the same shelf.

## 2. Stored, or postponed?
The sorting question in a garage is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or only postponed". The chair with the wobbly leg. The charity sack by the side door. The printer you replaced. None of those is in storage. Each is a decision somebody put down and walked away from.

## 3. The one-day rule
Here is the rule that keeps a garage clear once you have cleared it: anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day. Not next weekend. The same day. Otherwise it quietly becomes part of the floor.

## 4. Clean the floor last
Counterintuitive, but it is the right order. Everything in a garage wants to end up on the slab, so until the walls and racks have taken what belongs on them there is no floor to clean, only a pile to work around. Bench first. Floor last.

## 5. What a pale floor buys you
Degrease the slab properly once, then a sweep holds it. And here is what you actually bought: a pale floor turns every new drip into information. You see the gearbox weeping or the water heater starting to go the week it starts, not the month it fails. On a dark stained floor, all of that is invisible until it is expensive.

## 6. Oily rags do not need a flame
This one genuinely surprises people. Rags soaked with oil, stain, or solvent, balled up in an open bin, can heat on their own as the oil cures. No spark, no flame, nothing nearby. That is why they belong in a metal can with a lid rather than a plastic bucket under the bench.

## 7. Antifreeze tastes sweet
That is not a metaphor. A dog will drink a puddle of it, and a child will drink from a decanted bottle. It is the single strongest reason nothing in a garage is ever poured into a container that does not carry its own label, and why a spill on the slab gets cleaned rather than left to dry.

## 8. Weight goes low, bolts go into joists
The garage is the only room where the ordinary hazards are heavy and stored above somebody's head. Heavy bins on the lowest rack. Light bulky things highest. Rack bolts into joists, never plasterboard alone. And nothing overhanging the bay where a person opens a car door.

## 9. The third hammer
You own three claw hammers and four adjustable wrenches, and you have never thinned them because each one still works. Working is not the test. The test is which one your hand actually goes to. A hammer you keep and never choose is taking the hook that would have made the hammer you love findable in one glance.

## 10. Everything that stays gets a home
"A garage is not clear because you stacked it neatly. It is clear because you stopped letting the rest of the house postpone its decisions in here."
