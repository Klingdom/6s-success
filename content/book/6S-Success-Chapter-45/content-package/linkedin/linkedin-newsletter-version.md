# Chapter 45 LinkedIn Newsletter: The Garage

**Subject:** Your garage does not fail from use. It fails from arrival.

Every other room in this book fails from use. The kitchen from cooking, the laundry room from washing, the bathroom from water. The garage does not. Things come in from every other room in the house, they are set down, and because the door stays shut most of the week nothing ever forces a verdict on any of them.

That is how a good chisel and a box nobody has opened since the move end up on the same shelf, treated exactly alike.

So the sorting question here is unusual. It is not "do I still want this". It is "was this ever actually stored, or was it only postponed". And the rule that keeps a garage clear afterwards is a one-day rule: anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day.

Seven zones, and four rules on top of the nine rules of Shine:

- **Nothing here is being stored, it is being postponed.**
- **Get it all off the floor, then read the floor.** The slab is cleaned last and degreased properly once, because a pale floor turns every new drip into information.
- **Weight and height are the hazard.** Heavy low, bolts into joists, nothing overhanging where a car door opens.
- **This room holds the real chemistry.** None of it is dangerous by itself. All of it is dangerous by proximity.

Do one zone this week, and make it the bench, because until one solid surface is clear, everything you pick up just goes back down somewhere else.
