# Chapter 45 Key Takeaways: The Garage

- The garage does not fail from use, it fails from arrival. It takes what every other room evicts, and a shut door means nothing ever forces a verdict.
- The sorting question here is not do I still want this. It is was this ever stored, or only postponed.
- Anything carried in with no rail, shelf, or bin already waiting for it gets its verdict the same day, or it becomes part of the floor.
- Seven zones, and the order matters at both ends: the bench first because it is the only surface you can lay anything out on, the slab last.
- Degrease the slab properly once, then a sweep holds it. What you buy is a pale floor, and a pale floor turns every new drip into information.
- You will see the gearbox weeping or the water heater going the week it starts, not the month it fails.
- Weight and height are this room's hazard: heavy bins low, light bulky things high, rack bolts into joists and never plasterboard alone.
- Nothing goes overhead you cannot bring down with both hands on a stable ladder, and nothing overhangs the bay where a car door opens.
- The real chemistry is dangerous by proximity: petrol away from the pilot, fertiliser never beside fuel, oily rags in a closed metal can, lithium packs never asleep on a charger.
- Oily rags can heat on their own with no flame anywhere near them, and antifreeze tastes sweet enough that a dog will drink a puddle of it.
- Nothing in this room is ever decanted into an unlabelled bottle.
