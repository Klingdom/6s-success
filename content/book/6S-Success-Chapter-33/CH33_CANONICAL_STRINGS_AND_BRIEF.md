## The frozen lines (copy verbatim in manuscript and HTML)

- **The five zones (frozen, structural list):** The pantry is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Do them one at a time and the storeroom that feeds the kitchen comes back to you a zone at a time.

- **The dust rule (frozen, pantry Shine principle):** In a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job, and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So the rule is dry before wet, without exception: vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take the board bone-dry before anything goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling.

- **Definition (frozen, defbox):** A finished pantry is one you can shop from at a glance: every opened bag sealed in a dated container you can read the level of from the doorway, cans in rows with the label out and the oldest date at the front, one lift-out baking bin, a child-height snack bin holding only pre-approved snacks, and a backstock shelf where every pack carries its arrival month and nothing is stacked in front of anything else.

- **The dates reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** A pantry does not go off all at once, it goes off one forgotten item at a time, at the back, in the dark. The fix is not more willpower, it is a rule the shelf keeps for you: every container dated the day you open it, every row loaded from behind so the oldest stands at the front, and nothing bought until you can see that you are out.

- **The inspection reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The pantry is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety, because the things you find as you wipe, a chewed corner, fine webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, a soft dark patch of damp cardboard, are early warnings you cannot afford to miss. You are cleaning the shelf, yes, but really you are checking that nothing has got in and nothing is quietly turning.

- **The sticky-ring reframe (frozen, pull-quote):** The sticky ring under a honey jar is not just untidy, it is an invitation. A tacky edge left on a shelf is exactly how ants find the row, so in the pantry you clean until the shelf squeaks, because clean here is the difference between a store cupboard and a feeding station.

- **The before-and-after caption (frozen, governs the signature figure):** This is the pantry the whole loop was for: the same shelves you started with, cleared, dusted dry, and sorted so you can shop them at a glance. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a pantry you can read from the doorway is one you stop wasting food from, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

- **One Idea to Keep (frozen, close box):** A pantry is not clean because the shelves are wiped. It is clean because you took every board dry-first, dated every open bag, faced the oldest to the front, and flagged the chewed corner and the bulging lid you found on the way. Do one zone this weekend, the dry goods or the cans, and shop from a store cupboard you can actually see.

- **Outcome vocabulary (frozen):** one zone at a time / dry before wet, always / date it, oldest to the front. The nine-rule Shine method from Chapter 31 still governs; the pantry adds dust, dates, pests, and damp.

## The four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win:** Do the dry goods shelf dry-first. Clear one board completely, vacuum the flour and grain dust off it while it is dry, then, and only then, wipe it and take it bone-dry. Reseal or bin every open bag as it comes off, dating the keepers. One board, and the shelf stops smelling and starts reading at a glance.
- **Family Challenge:** Set a maximum for each line on the shelf edge and always pull from the front, and let a child own facing up the can rows on recycling night, labels out and the oldest date to the front. The shelf then tells everyone what is running low without anyone having to count.
- **6S Tip:** Dry before wet, every time, in this room: flour and grain dust turn to a grey paste the instant a wet cloth touches them, so vacuum or brush first, wipe second, dry last. And wash the scoop that lives in the flour rather than burying it back damp, because a damp scoop seeds a rock-hard clump.
- **Common Mistake:** Wiping a floury shelf with a wet cloth, which smears the dust into a grey skin you then have to scrape, and wiping around the containers instead of lifting them off, which leaves the dust band and the sticky ring exactly where the pests are looking. Dry-first, lift everything off, and clean until the shelf squeaks.

## The room: source facts (from the Micro Zone Manual, Pantry, 5 zones)
Carry specific nouns. Each zone's full Inputs + numbered Steps + inspect-and-flag are authored in the manuscript from the Manual's shine_detail.

1. **Dry goods shelves.** Job: grains, pasta, cereals you cook from weekly. Done: every open bag sealed in a dated container, staples in blocks, levels readable from the doorway. Shine (grain dust is the whole story, DRY FIRST): top shelf + back wall first (vacuum brush, then damp wipe, dry); each board fully cleared (vacuum, wipe, dry bone-dry); the front lip (dust drifts forward); containers, lids, scoops (wash scoop, dry fully); back corners and the board-to-upright seam (dry brush/crevice first); the floor under the bottom shelf last. Call: the bag opened for one recipe (open + no named meal in 14 days leaves). Hazards: reaching the top shelf from a chair (fall); heavy glass grain jars above eye level with wet hands (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: chewed corners/droppings, webbing/moths/clumping, a lid that no longer seals, a bowing board, damp/musty back wall or rust on a wire shelf.
2. **Canned and jarred goods.** Job: the cans and jars that make dinner from an empty fridge. Done: cans on risers, labels out, one category per row, oldest date at the front, no dented seams or domed lids. Shine (hunt the sticky ring): boards and riser tiers (vacuum, wipe, dry); the sticky rings under honey/syrup/sauce (warm damp until it squeaks, or ants follow); jar threads and lids (dry detail brush then damp); can tops and rims (greasy dust film); the front edge and the back board; the floor under the bottom row. Call: the best-before you keep arguing with (eat within 7 days or it goes; best-before is quality not safety). Hazards: a bulging/leaking/hissing can (poison/choke/strangle, bag it, no cooking makes it safe); a dense row of cans on a high shelf (fall/cut/crush). Inspect: domed/weeping/hissing can, rust ring under a can, a fresh sticky drip, an ant trail, a bowing board or a label peeling with damp behind.
3. **Baking zone.** Job: start a bake without a shop trip. Done: flour/sugar/brown sugar in sealed one-handed containers, leaveners dated in one box, a lift-out bin of cutters/cases/tips, allergen flours sealed with their own scoop. Shine (flour to paste, DRY FIRST always): the board under the flour/sugar (brush or vacuum dry, then barely-damp wipe); the containers (the one-handed lid where flour stops it clicking); the scoop (wash, dry, do not bury it damp); the lift-out bin (tip out, vacuum corners, dry); the allergen containers at the end with their OWN cloth and separate scoop (cross-contact); the floor (flour is slick underfoot with a hot tray). Call: the kit for the baker you meant to become (keep tools for what you baked twice). Hazards: nut-flour cross-contact + small decorating pieces (poison/choke/strangle); spilled flour/sugar on a hard floor is slick (fall). Inspect: weevils/beetles/webbing in flour, hard clumping (damp in a failed seal), a lid that no longer clicks, a hairline crack or rust bloom on a cutter/tip, an allergen container sharing a scoop.
4. **Snack and lunch zone.** Job: what goes in a lunchbox and what a child may take without asking. Done: one child-height bin of pre-approved snacks refilled to a marked line, a higher adult shelf, every reusable container with its own lid, containers matched to lunches packed. Shine (mess hides in the bin corners and an un-emptied lunch bag): the higher shelf first (top down); the low bin emptied and wiped into the bottom corners; reusable containers matched to lids (wash leakers); drink bottles and spouts (bottle brush down inside, work the spout/straw/threads where a sour film hides, dry upside down); lunch bags turned inside out, shaken, liners washed and dried open; the bin exterior and shelf edge (sugary film draws ants). Call: whether the answer is yours or the bin's (decide snacks once, in daylight; put exactly those in the bin). Hazards: hard sweets/whole nuts/small cheeses in a bin a two-year-old can reach (poison/choke/strangle); a child climbing the shelving (fall). Inspect: a forgotten sandwich/fruit going off, mould in a bottle or fruit-pouch seam, a split/warped lid, the unit rocking or its anchor loosening, a sticky drip a scout ant found.
5. **Backstock and bulk.** Job: the reserve that feeds the working shelves, nothing else. Done: sacks/cases/packs each written with the month they arrived, all at or below waist height, a max per line on the shelf edge, nothing stacked in front. Shine (a floor-and-underside zone): the shelf edges with the max-count labels (keep the numbers readable, dry); the cases and sacks (dust off the tops, arrival month facing out); the underside of the bottom case (lift and feel for damp, where a concrete or outside wall sweats); the floor (sweep/vacuum back-to-door); the back wall and doorway floor (nothing at shin height in the dark). Call: the warehouse pack that was not a saving (max = the largest you have ever finished before it turned). Hazards: cases above chest height, a heavy sack lifted with a twist (fall/cut/crush); a floor-stacked case at a dim doorway (fall trip). Inspect: soft/dark cardboard at a case bottom (moisture warning before the food spoils), a wall stain/mould/condensation, droppings or a chewed sack low down, a bulging/split/leaking sack, a case crushed under the weight above or rust up a metal leg near the damp floor.

## The kit for the pantry (product TYPES; no brand names)
- **Clean and shine (the inputs that do the work here):** a compact vacuum with brush AND crevice attachments (the pantry's key input, because dust is the enemy); a dry hand brush and dustpan (for flour, dry-first); a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner; a colour-coded microfiber cloth set (a dedicated cloth for the allergen shelf); a small detail brush (jar threads, corners, the board-to-upright seam); mild dish soap and warm water (scoops, containers, bottles); a bottle and spout brush (the snack-zone drink bottles); a broom and dustpan (the backstock floor). No degreaser is needed in a pantry.
- **Sort and routing:** a Keep, Relocate, Donate, Recycle, Trash container set.
- **Homes (Storage & Organization):** an airtight food container set (the pantry's key storage input: seal and date); can risers (so every label reads at a glance); clear latching and open-front bins; a spice organizer; one lift-out baking bin.
- **Standardize (Visual Controls):** a food date-label kit and a FIFO date marker (date on opening, oldest to the front); min-max inventory labels (the max per line written on the shelf edge); water-resistant permanent labels, a label maker, and removable write-on labels; shelf location labels; a marker that lives on the backstock shelf.
- **Health and safety:** a moisture absorber or humidity monitor (damp is the pantry's quiet enemy); a stable step stool (the top shelf, never a chair or a shelf edge); impact-rated safety glasses if a deep clean overhead calls for it.
