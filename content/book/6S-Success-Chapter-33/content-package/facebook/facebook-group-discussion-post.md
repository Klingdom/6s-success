Okay, the one pantry trick I wish someone had shown me years ago, and then I want to hear yours.

For the longest time I cleaned my pantry by grabbing a damp cloth and wiping the shelves, and every few months I threw out food I never knew I had back there. I put it down to being disorganised. It was never about being disorganised.

Here is what I had never been taught. In a pantry the thing beating you is not grease, it is dust and damp, and there is one rule that overrides everything.

"In a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job, and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So the rule is dry before wet, without exception: vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take the board bone-dry before anything goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling."

So now, before any water touches a shelf, I vacuum or brush the flour and grain dust off it while it is bone-dry, then wipe, then dry. A few things I now do every single time:

- Dry before wet, always, so the flour never smears into a grey paste I have to scrape.
- Lift everything off the board rather than wipe around it, because the dust band and the sticky ring are exactly where the pests look.
- Hunt the sticky ring under the honey and jam, and clean until the shelf squeaks.
- Date every open bag the day I open it, and load every row from behind so the oldest stands at the front.
- Read the clean as an inspection, for a chewed corner, fine webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, a soft dark patch of damp cardboard.

And it matters here more than most rooms, because the pantry is where the food waits, and it quietly decides how much of your grocery money you throw in the bin. A pantry you cannot see grows a layer of forgotten, out-of-date food at the back, in the dark.

The other thing that made it feel doable was refusing to see it as one big job.

"The pantry is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Do them one at a time and the storeroom that feeds the kitchen comes back to you a zone at a time."

So here is what I am genuinely curious about.

If you did just one pantry zone this weekend, to the method, which would you point at first, the dry goods shelves, the cans, the baking zone, the snacks, or the backstock? And what is the one thing you always seem to find two of when you finally clear the shelf, the pasta, the tinned tomatoes, the baking powder that expired years ago?
