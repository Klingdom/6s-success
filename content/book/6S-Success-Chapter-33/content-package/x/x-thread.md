Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. Chapter 32 did the kitchen; Part Nine now steps into its storeroom, the pantry. Like the two before it, this chapter does not just say what to do, it gives the exact inputs and steps to clean each of the pantry's five zones.

2/
"The pantry is where the food waits, and it is the room that quietly decides how much of your grocery money you throw in the bin." A pantry you cannot see grows a layer of forgotten, out-of-date food at the back, in the dark. Clean it and you stop wasting food.

3/
"The pantry is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk." Run them one at a time, not as a lost weekend.

(223 chars)

---

4/
"You know the nine rules of Shine from the door, and they all hold here. The pantry adds one overriding principle and one shift in what you are really doing." The principle: dry before wet. The shift: the clean is an inspection.

(231 chars)

---

5/
The principle the whole room turns on. In a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job, and "flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it." So dry before wet: vacuum or brush the board dry, wipe afterward, and take it bone-dry before anything goes back.

(264 chars)

---

6/
The shift in what you are doing. "The pantry is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety." A chewed corner, webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, damp cardboard are early warnings you find because your hands are on every surface.

(260 chars)

---

7/
"The sticky ring under a honey jar is not just untidy, it is an invitation. A tacky edge left on a shelf is exactly how ants find the row, so in the pantry you clean until the shelf squeaks, because clean here is the difference between a store cupboard and a feeding station."

(279 chars)

8/
Zone 1, the dry goods shelves, worked. Start at the top shelf and the back wall, and go dry-first: vacuum the flour and grain dust off each board while it is dry, then wipe, then take it bone-dry. Reseal or bin every open bag as it comes off, dating the keepers.

(265 chars)

---

9/
Zone 3, the baking zone, is dry-first without exception. Brush the board dry before anything wet. "Wash the scoop that lives in the flour rather than burying it back damp, because a damp scoop seeds a rock-hard clump." Give the allergen shelf its own cloth and scoop.

(270 chars)

---

10/
"A pantry does not go off all at once, it goes off one forgotten item at a time, at the back, in the dark." The fix is not willpower, it is a rule the shelf keeps for you: date every container the day you open it, and load from behind so the oldest stands at the front.

(273 chars)

---

11/
Zone 2, the canned and jarred goods. Clear the boards in rows and vacuum, then hunt the sticky ring: work the tacky edge under the honey and syrup jars until the shelf squeaks. Wipe the cans and set them back label-out, with the oldest date at the front.

(258 chars)

---

12/
Zone 4, the snack and lunch zone. The mess hides in the bottom corners of the bin and in a lunch bag nobody emptied. Empty and wipe the low snack bin, bottle-brush the drink spouts and dry them upside down, and turn the lunch bags inside out to shake the crumbs.

(266 chars)

---

13/
Zone 5, the backstock and bulk, is a floor-and-underside zone, and the cardboard is your damp warning system. Wipe the cases with the arrival month facing out, then lift the bottom case and check its underside for the soft dark cardboard that warns of damp.

(261 chars)

---

14/
"A pantry is not clean because the shelves are wiped." It is clean because you took every board dry-first, dated every open bag, and faced the oldest to the front. Do one zone this weekend, the dry goods or the cans.
