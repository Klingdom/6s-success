# Back Cover Copy: The Pantry

## Back-cover text

You have done the kitchen; now step into its storeroom. The pantry is where the food waits, and it is the room that quietly decides how much of your grocery money you throw in the bin, because a pantry you cannot see is a pantry that grows a layer of forgotten, out-of-date, half-used food at the back, in the dark. You have all six S's now and the loop that keeps them turning, and you have the nine-rule Shine method the door taught you and the kitchen let you use. What you need here is not another lesson but somewhere to point it, and a set of instructions precise enough to follow with your sleeves rolled up. So this chapter, like the two before it, is mostly about the clean. It does not stop at telling you what to do. For every small zone of the pantry it gives you the exact inputs to gather into your caddy and the exact, numbered, step-by-step way to clean and shine it, dry-first, the way a professional would, rather than the way most of us wipe at a shelf until it looks a bit better. And there is one rule here that overrides almost everything else, so we say it up front: in a pantry you clean dry before wet, always. Vacuum or brush first, wipe second, dry last, because flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. By the end you will be able to take the room apart zone by zone and put it back genuinely, inspectably clean, dated, and readable at a glance, and, because your hands are on every surface, you will read the clean as an inspection: the chewed corner, the fine webbing, the bulging can lid, the soft dark patch of damp cardboard, all caught early.

Chapter 33 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the third chapter of Part Nine, Room-by-Room Playbooks, and the third of twenty room playbooks that point the finished loop at one real room at a time. Its freeing idea carries from the door: "You do not clean "the pantry"; you clean the dry goods shelf, which is done when every board is dust-free and bone-dry and every open bag is sealed and dated or gone." The pantry is five of those zones: "The pantry is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Do them one at a time and the storeroom that feeds the kitchen comes back to you a zone at a time." Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain get a brisk pass; then the chapter slows right down for Shine, recaps the nine rules from the door, and adds the one thing that changes everything here: "In a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job, and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So the rule is dry before wet, without exception: vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take the board bone-dry before anything goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling." Then, as the heart of the chapter, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It" gives each of the five zones the same three things: the exact inputs to gather into the caddy, the numbered steps in the true order of work, dry first, vacuum or brush, wipe second, dry last, hunt the sticky ring, and floor last, and an inspect-and-flag list, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. The clean is genuinely an inspection here: "The pantry is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety, because the things you find as you wipe, a chewed corner, fine webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, a soft dark patch of damp cardboard, are early warnings you cannot afford to miss. You are cleaning the shelf, yes, but really you are checking that nothing has got in and nothing is quietly turning." It lands the definition box, "A finished pantry is one you can shop from at a glance: every opened bag sealed in a dated container you can read the level of from the doorway, cans in rows with the label out and the oldest date at the front, one lift-out baking bin, a child-height snack bin holding only pre-approved snacks, and a backstock shelf where every pack carries its arrival month and nothing is stacked in front of anything else." It names the room's genuinely hard calls, mostly about food you feel guilty binning, the bag you opened for one recipe, the best-before you keep arguing with, the baking kit for the baker you never became, and the warehouse pack that was not a saving, runs the pantry's real hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the Part 9 signature that replaces the friction meter, a before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after taped up as the standard. Book the room with **The Pantry Reset card**, the five zones and the dry-first order to run them, and gather what it needs with **The Pantry Kit list**, the product types this room actually uses, grouped by job. The pantry is done, dated, and legible, and it is the room that quietly hands back a chunk of your grocery budget. One Idea to Keep: "A pantry is not clean because the shelves are wiped. It is clean because you took every board dry-first, dated every open bag, faced the oldest to the front, and flagged the chewed corner and the bulging lid you found on the way. Do one zone this weekend, the dry goods or the cans, and shop from a store cupboard you can actually see." Previous: Chapter 32, The Kitchen. Next: Chapter 34, The Dining Room.
