## Search intent
Getting one real room, the pantry, genuinely clean, dated, and readable at a glance, after the reader has learned the whole 6S method and wants to apply it to the kitchen's storeroom. This chapter is the third of Part Nine, the applied half of the book, and its centerpiece is the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine each of the five pantry zones dry-first, with the exact inputs to gather. Readers arriving here are facing a cupboard that grows a layer of forgotten, out-of-date, half-used food at the back, in the dark, and they do not just want to tidy it, they want to clean it properly and know precisely how, dry-first so flour never turns to paste. The chapter answers with one freeing idea and one method plus one overriding rule. The idea: you do not clean the pantry, you clean one zone of it, then the next, and the pantry is only five small zones (the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk). The method: the universal nine-rule Shine method carried from Chapter 31 (work top to bottom, dry or clear before wet, back to front and clean to dirty, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, colour-code your cloths, match the cleaner to the surface, give it dwell time, and clean to inspect and flag rather than fix). The overriding rule: dust and damp are the whole job and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it, so dry before wet always, vacuum or brush first, wipe second, dry last, hunt the sticky ring, and treat the clean as a genuine inspection for pests and damp. Then, zone by zone, it gives the exact INPUTS (a vacuum with brush and crevice attachments above all, a dry hand brush, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, colour-coded cloths with a dedicated allergen-shelf colour, a detail brush, a bottle brush, a broom, plus airtight dated containers, can risers, and date labels) and the NUMBERED steps in the true order of work, plus an inspect-and-flag list. It names the room's food-guilt calls (the bag opened for one recipe, the best-before you keep arguing with, the baking kit for the baker you meant to become, the warehouse pack that was not a saving), runs the pantry's hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the before-and-after pair, the before kept and the after kept as the standard. The friction meter is retired, replaced by each room's own transformation.
