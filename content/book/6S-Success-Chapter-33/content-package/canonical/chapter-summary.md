## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 33 is the third room playbook of Part Nine, following the instruction-centric template proven at the entryway in Chapter 31 and the kitchen in Chapter 32. The pantry is the kitchen's storeroom, the room that quietly decides how much of your grocery money you throw in the bin, and it is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety: not for grease, but for dust, dates, damp, and pests. That is why this chapter, like the two before it, is mostly about the clean, and why its centerpiece is the exact, step-by-step way to clean and shine each zone dry-first, with the precise inputs.

The freeing idea carries from the door: the pantry is five small zones, not one big job, the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Sort, Straighten, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain get a brisk pass; then the chapter slows down for Shine. It carries the nine rules and adds the pantry layer: dust and damp are the whole job, so dry before wet without exception, vacuum or brush first, wipe second, dry last, hunt the sticky ring, and treat the clean as an inspection for pests and damp. The centerpiece, "Zone by Zone: Exactly How to Clean It," gives each of the five zones the same three parts: the exact inputs to gather into the caddy, the numbered steps in the true order of work, and an inspect-and-flag list, because Shine finds the hazards and the safety pass fixes them. It lands the definition box, names the room's food-guilt calls, runs the hazards through the five safety questions, and closes with the before-and-after pair before a light hand-off to Chapter 34, the dining room.
