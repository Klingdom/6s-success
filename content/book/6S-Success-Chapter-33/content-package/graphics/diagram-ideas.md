## 5. How to Clean a Dry-Goods Shelf Dry-First (the step ribbon, no direct HTML twin)

**Purpose:** Render the exact order of work for the pantry's first and highest-leverage zone, the dry goods shelf,
so the reader sees the dry-first sequence as one calm left-to-right ribbon and understands that the dust comes
off dry, before a wet cloth ever touches the board. This diagram has no direct HTML figure twin; it makes the
Quick Win concrete, the dry-first trick that stops the shelf smelling and starts it reading at a glance.

**What it shows:** A gentle left-to-right ribbon of the moves the dry goods shelf runs in order, each a small step
card with a tiny type icon and a green-numbered node. `CLEAR THE BOARD`, take everything off one board completely,
so nothing is wiped around, reselling or binning every open bag as it comes off and dating the keepers. `VACUUM
THE DUST DRY`, run the vacuum brush and the dry hand brush over the bare board, the front lip where dust drifts
forward, and the back corners and the board-to-upright seam, all while it is dry, drawn largest and greenest with
the note that flour plus a wet cloth makes paste. `WIPE`, only now go over the dusted board with a barely damp
cloth, second not first. `TAKE IT BONE-DRY`, finish with a dry cloth so the board is bone-dry before anything
goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling. `RESEAL, DATE, RELOAD`, put
back only sealed dated containers, load the row from behind so the oldest stands at the front, and wash and dry
the scoop rather than burying it back damp. A faint note under the ribbon reminds that the dust comes off dry,
always, before the wet cloth.

**Rough layout:**
- A calm panel `#FBF7EF`. Top, sans caps letter-spaced soft: `HOW TO CLEAN A DRY-GOODS SHELF DRY-FIRST`, a
  Fraunces line beneath in the label green: "clear the board, vacuum the dust dry, wipe, take it bone-dry".
- Five step cards left to right, each a green-numbered node with a Fraunces label and a Newsreader italic gloss:
  - `1 · clear the board` · everything off, reseal or bin every open bag, date the keepers
  - `2 · vacuum the dust dry` · brush and crevice, front lip, back corners, the seam, all dry
  - `3 · wipe` · a barely damp cloth, second not first
  - `4 · take it bone-dry` · a dry cloth, before anything goes back
  - `5 · reseal, date, reload` · sealed dated containers, oldest to the front, the scoop washed and dry
- The vacuum step (`2`) drawn a notch larger and greener with a small dust-brush icon, because taking the dust off
  dry is the whole trick; a faint barred `never wet first` mark sits under step 2 to hold the dry-first rule.
- A foot line in Fraunces slate: `clear it, vacuum it dry, wipe second, dry last, then reseal and date`.
- Caption (from the chapter Quick Win): "Do the dry goods shelf dry-first. Clear one board completely, vacuum the
  flour and grain dust off it while it is dry, then, and only then, wipe it and take it bone-dry. Reseal or bin
  every open bag as it comes off, dating the keepers. One board, and the shelf stops smelling and starts reading
  at a glance."

**Note for the illustrator:** this is the dry-first order distilled, so keep it a calm ribbon, five moves left to
right, plainly one repeatable sequence rather than a checklist. Make the vacuum step largest and greenest with a
dry-brush icon because taking the dust off dry, before any wet cloth, is what keeps the flour from turning to
paste, and draw the wipe and dry steps clearly after it, damp second and bone-dry last. Draw the clear step with
open bags coming off, some resealed into dated containers and some binned, so the reader sees you never wipe
around a container. On the reload step, show the row loaded from behind with the oldest date to the front and the
scoop washed and drying, not buried back damp. Label the vacuum, the cloths, the containers, and the labels by
type only, no brand. Hold the color discipline: the green Shine accent for the step nodes and the enlarged vacuum
step, the deeper label green for labels, spark-red does not appear here. Warm line-and-flat-color style, friendly
and capable, the register of a professional's order of work that gets the pantry's highest-leverage shelf clean,
dry, dated, and legible without ever smearing the dust into paste.

light, and do not tidy the "before", because the before is the valuable one, the proof of how far the cupboard
came. Make the after clearly the standard, the ideal-state photo from Standardize, kept as the picture the pantry
has to match. Draw the secondary pairs (the can rows, the baking shelf) only if a small inset is wanted; the
head-on shelves frame is the primary. Do NOT draw the friction meter; this pair replaces it for all of Part 9.
Hold the color discipline: slate for the "before" tag, the "before, kept" note in terracotta, green for the
"after" tag and the standard note, spark-red does not appear. Calm, warm, illustrated, not photoreal, the
register of a quiet, real transformation the reader is about to photograph for themselves.
