# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 33

**"The Pantry"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE THIRD PLAYBOOK*

Six prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel, in reading order. Palette to hold across all
six: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`, terracotta `#BC4B2A` (the warm
accent, the eyebrows, the "before, kept" note), honey `#DDA63A` (the single accent, the family note), slate blue
`#3C5A6B` (data, labels, the plan), soft green `#6E8B5B` (the SHINE STEP accent, the method arrows, the numbered
nodes, the "after" standard), a deeper label green (a darker shade of the Shine green) for small labels on the
Shine figures, a single warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for the smallest marks only and, in this room, for the one
true hazard mark, the bulging-can warning, used barely. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth, real
textures over polish.

The named space throughout is one real pantry, the kitchen's storeroom and the room that quietly decides how much
of your grocery money you throw in the bin, because a pantry you cannot see grows a layer of forgotten,
out-of-date, half-used food at the back, in the dark. This is the THIRD of twenty room chapters, following the
entryway of Chapter 31 and the kitchen of Chapter 32 and handing off to the dining room of Chapter 34: the six-S
method is finished, and the nine-rule Shine method was taught at the door in Chapter 31, so it is recapped here,
not re-taught. The spine of this chapter is a single overriding addition to the door's method: the pantry's
enemies are not grease but dust, dates, damp, and the things that get in, and flour turns to paste the instant a
wet cloth touches it, so the pantry keeps the nine rules and adds one overriding principle, dry before wet,
always, and one shift, the clean is genuinely an inspection for pests and damp. It delivers the frame (the pantry
is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the
snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk); the heart (the pantry Shine method: dry before wet always,
vacuum or brush the dust off dry, then wipe, then take it bone-dry, hunt the sticky ring, and clean to inspect
for pests and damp); the five zone-by-zone procedures with their exact inputs and ordered steps; the hard calls
(the bag opened for one recipe, the best-before you keep arguing with, the baking kit for the baker you meant to
become, the warehouse pack that was not a saving); and the Part 9 close (a matched before-and-after pair of the
pantry shelves from the doorway). The friction meter, which reached its goal at Chapter 30, is RETIRED for Part 9
and replaced by the before-and-after room signature: do not draw a gauge. Keep the tone warm, practical, and
expert, sleeves-rolled-up instruction rather than a lecture, capability and a little pride, no shame about the
before. Crucially, describe only the calm, warm illustrated house style: no corporate or boardroom look, no hype
(no unlock, elevate, supercharge, optimize), no scolding, no photoreal polish, and no brand names or real logos
anywhere, every cleaner, cloth, brush, tool, container, and label is a plain generic TYPE, never a recognisable
product.

No typeset text is baked into these images. Any label, name, or word mentioned below is a physical object in the
scene, a hand-lettered card, a hand-drawn label on a plan, a container, or a bottle, a mark on a cloth, or a note
under a photo, not a typeset overlay. Separator discipline for any such incidental hand-lettered label: use the
middot, for example `CHAPTER 33 · THE PANTRY`, never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words. Compound
terms keep their internal hyphen: dry-first, dry-before-wet, top-to-bottom, back-to-front, clean-to-dirty,
mist-the-cloth, two-cloth, colour-coded, sticky-ring, dust-band, bone-dry, one-handed, child-height, lift-out,
waist-height, arrival-month, best-before, oldest-to-front, before-and-after, matched-pair, zone-by-zone, six-S,
step-by-step, dwell-time, food-safety, cross-contact, max-per-line, shelf-edge.
