# Infographic Spec: The Pantry Shine Method

**Chapter 33, "The Pantry"**
**6S Success: Home Edition · Part Nine · Room-by-Room Playbooks · THE THIRD PLAYBOOK**

This is the single strongest image in the chapter: the PANTRY SHINE METHOD, the dust layer and the dry-first rule
that make a pantry clean different from a kitchen clean, built on the one overriding move that you clean dry
before wet, always, and the exact inputs. It carries the chapter's spine in one glance: you know the nine rules
of Shine from the door, and they all hold here, and the pantry adds one overriding principle and one shift in
what you are really doing. The concept pairs the hero dust rule, **In a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job,
and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So the rule is dry before wet, without exception:
vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take the board bone-dry before
anything goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling.**, with the picture at
its heart: the dust taken off dry first (the vacuum brush and the dry hand brush lifting grain dust off a dry
board), the order of work drawn top to bottom (top shelf and back wall, then the working shelves, then the
floor), the wipe coming second and the dry cloth last, the sticky ring hunted until the shelf squeaks, and the
pantry kit, the exact inputs, laid out by type. So the viewer sees the whole move at once: this is how you clean
a pantry to a professional standard, and every zone procedure in the chapter is just this method applied to a
particular surface.

It stays consistent with the four inline figures in `chapter_33_final.html`, and in particular with Figure 3, the
two-panel `THE PANTRY SHINE METHOD AND THE KIT`, drawn in the accent greens of the Shine step. The Shine step
owns the green accent: the method arrow, the numbered nodes, and the dry-first move are green; slate carries the
labels; terracotta carries the warm accents. The single small warm red is held back entirely except for the one
true hazard mark in this room, the bulging can lid on the inspection note. This is not a lecture and not a
scolding: it is a professional's method laid out plainly so anyone can follow it with their sleeves rolled up.
Types, never brands: every cleaner, cloth, brush, tool, container, and label is a plain generic type, never a
brand name or a real logo, and there is no degreaser here, because a pantry has no grease. The register is warm,
practical, and expert, capability and a little pride, no shame about the before, the confidence of a method that
is faster because it actually works. Clean to a method, not until it looks better.

## Layout notes

- The dust rule is the loudest words on the page. Draw it as the whole pantry Shine pass compressed: dust and damp
  are the whole job, flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it, so you clean dry before wet, always,
  vacuum or brush the board dry, wipe only afterward, and take it bone-dry before anything goes back. The eye
  should read the dust rule, then see the order of work (dry first, then top to bottom, top shelf and back wall
  down to the floor, then wipe, then dry) and the four moves make it concrete.
- The order of work and the four moves are the hero, so make the dry-first move and the top-to-bottom direction
  unmistakable: the vacuum brush and the dry hand brush lifting grain dust off a dry board at the head, drawn
  largest and greenest with the barred `flour + water = paste` warning, then a single green arrow running down a
  pantry elevation from top-shelf-and-back-wall to the floor. Keep the wipe-second and dry-last order plainly
  after the dry lift, a barely damp cloth second, a dry cloth taking the board bone-dry last, never wet first.
  Set the dust rule byte-for-byte as it reads in the canonical quotes; it is the frozen string.
- The kit sits in support, not competing with the method: the exact inputs laid out by TYPE, a compact vacuum
  with brush and crevice attachments (the key input, because dust is the enemy, not a bottle of anything), a dry
  hand brush and dustpan, a broom, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, mild dish soap, a detail brush, a bottle
  brush, a colour-coded microfiber set with one colour dedicated to the allergen shelf, airtight dated
  containers, can risers, and date labels. Label every one by type, never by brand, draw no logo and no
  recognisable packaging, and draw no degreaser, because a pantry has no grease.
- Hold the color discipline: the green Shine accent carries the method arrow, the numbered nodes, and the
  dry-first move; slate carries the labels; terracotta carries the warm accents; the deeper label green carries
  the small labels; the single small warm red is held back entirely except for the one bulging-can hazard mark on
  the inspection note, because this is a calm method card and the pantry has exactly one chemistry-and-spoilage
  hazard worth a red mark.
- This image makes the concept, the thing that makes a pantry clean different from a kitchen clean: the enemy is
  dust and damp, not grease, and dust comes off dry, before any wet cloth, or the flour turns to paste, so you
  clean dry before wet, always. It also carries the one shift: the clean is genuinely an inspection for pests and
  damp, so the chewed corner, the webbing in the flour, the bulging can lid, and the damp cardboard are found on
  the way. Do NOT re-teach the six S's or fully re-teach the nine-rule Shine method (recap only, it was taught at
  the door in Chapter 31), and do NOT teach the dining room of Chapter 34, which is named only as where the reader
  points the loop next. Draw no friction meter, retired for Part 9. And name no brand and draw no specific
  product; the vacuum, brushes, cloths, cleaner, containers, and labels are plain and generic, teaching the
  method, not a shopping list.
- Register: warm, practical, and expert, sleeves-rolled-up instruction rather than a lecture; the method exists
  because it is faster and it actually works. No force or discipline-as-punishment language, no scolding, no
  hype, no corporate or boardroom look, no smugness, and no shame about the before. Give the dry-first move its
  due, because in a pantry the dry lift is the whole clean: take the dust off dry with a vacuum brush and a hand
  brush before any wet cloth, and the flour never turns to paste and the board goes back bone-dry.
- Hand-made feel: a faint paper grain on the background, slightly soft corners, the order of work and the four
  moves drawn by hand as a calm method card rather than a clinical diagram, the green arrow a hand-drawn stroke
  over a pantry elevation, the vacuum brush and the dry board plain and friendly, the whole image the warm,
  capable register of a professional method taught plainly: not a tidy cupboard, a genuinely clean one, dry,
  dated, and readable at a glance.
