# Chapter 33 LinkedIn Comment Prompts: The Pantry

Five prompts to start genuine discussion. Drop one in the comments of a post or article to invite replies. Warm, practical, expert voice, the friendly pro who shows you how. Foreground the how-to method without lecturing. No corporate framing, no hype, no force or shame language, no brand names, product types only.

---

1. Be honest about how you clean a pantry shelf: do you reach for a wet cloth first, or do you vacuum the dust off dry before anything wet touches the board? The claim in this chapter is that dry before wet is the one rule that overrides everything else here, because flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it, and a wet wipe on a floury shelf just smears it into a grey skin you then have to scrape. Had anyone ever actually told you to vacuum a shelf before you wiped it, or is that new?

2. Here is the money line from the chapter: a pantry does not go off all at once, it goes off one forgotten item at a time, at the back, in the dark. The fix it offers is not more willpower, it is a rule the shelf keeps for you: date every container the day you open it, load every row from behind so the oldest stands at the front, and buy nothing until you can see you are out. Be honest, how much do you reckon you bin each month from the back of the cupboard that you simply forgot was there?

3. The reframe I keep coming back to is that the pantry is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety. The things you find as you wipe, a chewed corner, fine webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, a soft dark patch of damp cardboard, are early warnings you cannot afford to miss. When did you last actually look at the underside of the case on the bottom shelf, or behind the flour, rather than just wiping the fronts you can see?

4. A small one that changes the whole shelf: the sticky ring under a honey jar is not just untidy, it is an invitation, because a tacky edge is exactly how ants find the row. The chapter says to lift every jar off and clean until the shelf squeaks, rather than wiping around them. Be honest, do you lift the jars off to clean under them, or wipe around the bottles and leave the ring exactly where the pests are looking?

5. The playbook works the pantry as five small zones, not one big job, and says to start with the dry goods or the cans because those two hold the most that can spoil and the most that hides. Most of us look at "sort out the pantry" as a dreaded lost afternoon and shut the door on it. Does having the exact inputs to gather into a caddy and the numbered steps for a single zone, done dry-first, change whether you would actually start this weekend? Which zone would you clean first?
