# Chapter 33 LinkedIn Carousel Outline: The Pantry

Twelve slides. Slide 1 hooks, last slide carries the soft CTA. Each slide has a headline and one or two lines of body. Warm, practical, expert voice, the friendly pro who shows you how; foreground the how-to value, the pantry Shine method, the dry-before-wet rule, the per-zone tips, the money-saving angle, and clean to inspect while the safety pass fixes; no hype, no brand names, product types only.

---

## Slide 1: Hook

**Headline:** A pantry is not clean because the shelves are wiped. Tidy and legible are not the same thing.

**Body:** The kitchen is done. Now its storeroom. Swipe for exactly how to clean and shine the pantry to a professional standard, dry-first so flour never turns to paste, one small zone at a time.

---

## Slide 2: The room that decides your grocery bill

**Headline:** The pantry is where the food waits, and it quietly decides how much of your grocery money you throw in the bin.

**Body:** A pantry you cannot see grows a layer of forgotten, out-of-date, half-used food at the back, in the dark. That is why this chapter, like the two before it, is about the clean.

---

## Slide 3: One rule overrides everything

**Headline:** In a pantry you clean dry before wet, always.

**Body:** Flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take it bone-dry before anything goes back.

---

## Slide 4: Five small zones, not one big job

**Headline:** The pantry is five small zones, not one big job.

**Body:** The dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Do them one at a time and the storeroom comes back to you a zone at a time.

---

## Slide 5: Keep the nine rules, add two moves

**Headline:** You know the nine rules of Shine from the door, and they all hold here.

**Body:** The pantry adds one overriding principle, dry before wet, and one shift in what you are really doing: the clean is genuinely an inspection for pests and damp.

---

## Slide 6: The clean is really an inspection

**Headline:** The pantry is the one room where cleaning is genuinely an inspection for safety.

**Body:** A chewed corner, fine webbing in the flour, a bulging can lid, a soft dark patch of damp cardboard: early warnings you cannot afford to miss. You are checking that nothing got in and nothing is quietly turning.

---

## Slide 7: Hunt the sticky ring

**Headline:** The sticky ring under a honey jar is not just untidy, it is an invitation.

**Body:** A tacky edge is exactly how ants find the row, so in the pantry you clean until the shelf squeaks. Clean here is the difference between a store cupboard and a feeding station.

---

## Slide 8: The dry goods shelf, step by step

**Headline:** Do the dry goods shelf dry-first.

**Body:** Clear one board completely, vacuum the flour and grain dust off it while it is dry, then, and only then, wipe it and take it bone-dry. Reseal or bin every open bag as it comes off, dating the keepers.

---

## Slide 9: One move per zone

**Headline:** Each zone has a single move that carries it.

**Body:** Cans: work the sticky ring labels-out, oldest to the front. Baking: wash the scoop, do not bury it damp. Lunch: a bottle and spout brush for the drink spouts. Backstock: lift the bottom case and check under it for damp.

---

## Slide 10: Date it, oldest to the front

**Headline:** A pantry does not go off all at once; it goes off one forgotten item at a time.

**Body:** The fix is not more willpower, it is a rule the shelf keeps for you: date every container the day you open it, load every row from behind so the oldest stands at the front, and buy nothing until you can see you are out.

---

## Slide 11: Clean to inspect, the safety pass fixes

**Headline:** Your hand passes over every board, so your eye should too.

**Body:** The chewed corner, the fine webbing, the bulging lid, the damp cardboard: note it and keep cleaning. Shine finds hazards, the safety pass fixes them.
