## SHORT 1: Dust and damp are the whole job, so dry before wet
**Topic: the pantry Shine method, the dry-first layer on top of the nine rules. Runtime ~60 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The reason your pantry shelf smears instead of cleaning is one thing: you reached for a wet cloth. Flour turns to paste the instant water touches it.

**Script:**
You already know the nine rules of Shine from the door: work top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth not the surface, two cloths one to clean and one to dry, and clean to inspect every surface your hand passes over. The pantry keeps all of that and adds one rule that overrides everything else here. As the book puts it, in a pantry, dust and damp are the whole job, and flour turns to paste the instant a wet cloth touches it. So the rule is dry before wet, without exception: vacuum or brush every board clean while it is dry, wipe only afterward, and take the board bone-dry before anything goes back, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling. So, in order, on every board: vacuum or brush first, wipe second, dry last. The key tool is not a bottle of anything, it is a vacuum with a brush attachment, because the pantry's enemy is dust, not grease. And one more thing particular to this room: hunt the sticky ring, because a tacky edge under a honey jar is exactly how ants find the row.

- 12s: "Dust and damp are the whole job. Dry before wet."
- 30s: "Vacuum or brush first. Wipe second. Dry last."
- 50s: "Hunt the sticky ring. A tacky edge is how ants find the row."

## SHORT 2: Five zones, not one cupboard
**Topic: the hero device, the pantry as micro-zones. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A pantry looks like one jumbled job, and faced that way it is the sort of thing you close the door on. Faced as its zones, it is five short, finishable pieces.

**Script:**
Here is the trick that makes the cupboard you keep avoiding doable. You do not clean the pantry. You clean one zone of it, then the next. The pantry is five small zones, not one big job: the dry goods shelves, the canned and jarred goods, the baking zone, the snack and lunch zone, and the backstock and bulk. Do them one at a time and the storeroom that feeds the kitchen comes back to you a zone at a time. Each one has a clear edge and a clear finish. You do not clean the pantry, you clean the dry goods shelf, which is done when every board is dust-free and bone-dry and every open bag is sealed and dated or gone. And if you want the highest payback for the least effort, start with the dry goods or the cans, because those two hold the most that can spoil and the most that hides. You never have to face the pantry. You face one zone, with a clear finish, and then you are done for the day.

- 12s: "It is five small zones, not one big job."
- 28s: "Each zone has a clear edge and a clear finish."

## SHORT 3: How to clean the dry goods shelves, step by step
**Topic: the showcase zone, Zone 1 inputs and numbered steps. Runtime ~75 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The shelf that pays back the most in your pantry is the dry goods shelf. Here is exactly how a professional cleans it, in order, and every step is dry before wet.

**Script:**
First, gather the inputs into a caddy: a compact vacuum with the brush and crevice attachments, a neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set, a small detail brush, and mild dish soap with warm water for the scoops. Now the steps, in the order you actually do them. One, start at the top shelf and the wall behind it, and vacuum first, so nothing you knock loose lands on shelves you have already done, then wipe and take it dry before you move down. Two, clear and do each board in turn, take everything off rather than wiping around it, vacuum the loose flour and grain dust first, then wipe with a well-wrung cloth, then change to a dry cloth and take the board bone-dry, because a damp board plus grain dust is what starts a pantry smelling. Three, do the front lip and shelf edge where flour dust drifts forward and hands brush. Four, wipe the containers, lids, and scoops, and wash any scoop that lives in the grain in warm soapy water and dry it fully. Five, clear the back corners and the seam with the crevice tool or a dry detail brush, dry brush first always, because a wet cloth just turns flour to paste in the corner. Six, do the floor last, vacuum under the lowest board, then wipe and take it dry. That is the shelf that pays back the most, cleaned dry-first to a professional standard.

- 0s: "How to clean the dry goods shelves, in order."
- 12s: "Step one: top shelf and back wall. Vacuum first."
- 35s: "Each board: vacuum, wipe, then a dry cloth bone-dry."
- 60s: "Wash the scoop. Dry-brush the corners. Floor last."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The dry goods shelf cleaned in sequence, numbered 1 to 6: the caddy set down first, the top shelf and back wall vacuumed, each board cleared and the flour vacuumed off before any cloth touches it, the board taken bone-dry with a second dry cloth, the scoop washed and dried, the back corners dry-brushed, the floor last. ## SHORT 4: Brush the board dry first, because flour is paste
**Topic: Zone 3, the baking zone, the dry-first rule and the allergen discipline. Runtime ~55 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The fastest way to make a baking shelf worse is to wipe it with a wet cloth. Flour turns to paste the instant water touches it, so this whole zone is dry-first, without exception.

**Script:**
The baking zone is everything you need to start a bake without a shop trip first, and flour is the whole problem. Gather your inputs: a compact vacuum with the brush attachment, a dry hand brush and dustpan, a multi-surface cleaner, a colour-coded microfiber set with a dedicated cloth for the allergen shelf, and mild dish soap with warm water. Then, in order. One, brush or vacuum the loose flour off the board completely while it is dry, then and only then wipe with a barely damp cloth, because wetting flour first just smears it into a grey skin you have to scrape. Two, wipe the flour and sugar containers, paying attention to the one-handed lid where a dusting of flour stops it clicking shut. Three, wash the scoop in warm soapy water and dry it fully, because a damp scoop seeds a rock-hard clump. Four, tip the lift-out bin of cutters and cases out, vacuum its corners, and wipe it dry. And five, the one people skip, wipe the sealed allergen containers, the nut flours and sesame, with their own dedicated cloth, and wash their scoops separately, so no trace travels down the shelf. Then sweep any spilled flour off the floor, because it is genuinely slick underfoot.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Never wet a floury board first. Flour is paste."
- 14s: "Brush or vacuum the board dry. Then, only then, wipe."
- 32s: "Wash the scoop. A damp scoop seeds a rock-hard clump."
- 46s: "Allergen containers: own cloth, own scoop, washed separately."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The baking zone worked in order: a hand brush and dustpan taking the loose flour off the board while it is dry, a wet cloth shown smearing flour into a grey skin as the thing NOT to do, then the containers and the one-handed lid wiped, the scoop washed and dried, the lift-out bin tipped and vacuumed, and the allergen container wiped with its own flagged-colour cloth and its scoop washed separately. Warm paper, slate labels, green Shine accents, the allergen cloth in its own flagged colour.

## SHORT 5: Take the before before you touch a thing
**Topic: the before-and-after pair, the Part 9 signature. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The most valuable photo of your pantry is the one you take before you clear a single shelf, because within a month you will not believe the cupboard was ever the other one.

**Script:**
Here is the ritual that closes every room from here on, and it is what makes the clean stick. Before you touch a thing, stand in the doorway and photograph the pantry exactly as it is, framing the open shelves head-on, because a pantry's whole point is that you can read it in a glance and the before-and-after difference is exactly that readability. Then do the work, five zones cleaned dry-first, one at a time, every board dusted dry and every open bag dated. And when it is done, stand in exactly the same spot and take the second photo. The two do different jobs. The before is the valuable one, the proof of how far the cupboard came. The after is the standard, the picture the shelves have to match from now on. Here is the frozen line for it. This is the pantry the whole loop was for: the same shelves you started with, cleared, dusted dry, and sorted so you can shop them at a glance. Take the before photo before you touch a thing, and take the after as the standard, because a pantry you can read from the doorway is one you stop wasting food from, and the photo is what it has to match from now on.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Take the before before you touch a thing."
- 12s: "Five zones, cleaned dry-first."
- 28s: "Same spot. Same angle. Take the after."
- 40s: "The after is the standard the pantry has to match."

**[VISUAL] direction:** The matched before-and-after pair shot from the doorway, framing the open shelves head-on. On the left, "before": open bags slumped and shedding dust, cans facing every way with hidden dates, a floury baking shelf. On the right, "after": the same shelves in matching airtight dated containers whose levels read from the doorway, cans in labelled rows on risers with the oldest date at the front, a dust-free baking shelf with one lift-out bin. A small camera icon and two notes, "the before, kept" and "the after, kept as the standard". Warm, illustrated, honouring the before, never mocking it.
