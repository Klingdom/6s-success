## The room in one line
The nursery is the one room where safety outranks everything, so every standard is built for an exhausted adult working one-handed at 2 a.m.: a bare crib, everything inside one arm's sweep, and the surfaces skin and a mouth touch cleaned after every use with fragrance-free, infant-safe cleaners.

## The nursery Shine layer (nine rules from Ch 31 PLUS)
- **Safety leads every zone**: this is the one room where safety is not the last pass, it runs through all of them, because the person it protects can reach nothing and ask for nothing.
- **The bare crib, a number you never negotiate down**: one fitted sheet, nothing else over the rail, ever.
- **One arm's sweep, never turn your back**: everything a change or a feed needs is touchable without moving your feet or lifting your hand off the baby.
- **Fragrance-free, wipe-after-every-use, nothing put away wet**: clean the surfaces skin and a mouth touch after every single use; a damp valve in a closed bin is where mould starts.

## Six zones (start with the crib, the quickest and the one where getting it wrong matters most)
1. **Crib and Sleep Zone** - the one surface a baby is left alone on; strip it bare, clean the mouthed rail and every slat, check the bolts, one fitted sheet back and nothing more.
2. **Changing Station** - a pad you work at one-handed; supplies inside one arm's sweep, wipe the pad after every change, pull the drawer and pail.
3. **Baby Clothing Zone** - one size per drawer, labelled large; empty each drawer, check the anti-tip strap, keep two garments per size, move outgrown out fast.
4. **Feeding Station** - a clean assembled bottle in your hands at 2 a.m.; nothing goes away wet, descale the kettle or sterilizer, clean under the drying rack.
5. **Diaper and Care Backstock** - the reserve, one size ahead at most; wipe the shelf bare, read every date, creams and medicines in a lockable box, nothing heavy above the crib.
6. **Books and Quiet Play Zone** - what a baby mouths and reaches; wipe the board books with plain water, wash the soft toys, fix the ledge to the wall.

## FROZEN STRINGS (byte-verbatim in manuscript AND final.html)

### Six zones
The nursery is six small zones, not one big job: the crib and sleep zone, the changing station, the baby clothing zone, the feeding station, the diaper and care backstock, and the books and quiet play zone. Do them one at a time, and start with the crib, because it is the quickest zone in the room and the one where getting it wrong matters most.

### The rule (safety leads; built for 2 a.m. one-handed)
The nursery is the one room in the house where safety outranks everything else, and where the person using it is usually exhausted and working one-handed at two in the morning. So every standard here is built for that person and that hour rather than for a tidy shelf: the crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else, ever; everything a change or a feed needs sits inside one arm's sweep so you never turn your back on the baby; and the surfaces a mouth and bare skin touch get cleaned after every single use, with fragrance-free, infant-safe cleaners and nothing put away wet. In this room you do not round a safety number up, and you do not leave the baby to fetch a thing you forgot to keep in reach.

### The definition (defbox)
A finished nursery is one an exhausted adult can run one-handed in the dark and trust completely: a bare crib with one fitted sheet pulled tight and nothing over the rail, a changing pad with the diapers, wipes, and cream all inside one arm's sweep, one bin of clean assembled bottles ready to grab, drawers holding only the size the baby fits this month, creams and medicines in a lockable box up high, cords looped short and clipped back from the crib, and every tall thing strapped to the wall.

### Bare-crib reframe (a number you cannot negotiate down)
The crib is the one surface in the house where your baby is left alone, so it holds a fitted sheet pulled tight to the corners and nothing else, ever. No bumper, no pillow, no loose blanket, no plush toy, and nothing draped over the top rail, because loose bedding is a suffocation risk to an infant who cannot roll off it. This is the one standard in the whole book you never round up: not for a cold night, not for a nap, and not for the beautiful quilt somebody made, which goes on the wall above the crib and never inside the rails.

### One-arm's-sweep reframe (never turn your back)
Every task in this room is done one-handed, with your other hand on the baby, so nothing a task needs may sit outside your arm's sweep. The diapers, the wipes, and the cream all have to be touchable from the changing pad without moving your feet or lifting your hand, because the moment you turn to reach a shelf is the moment a baby rolls off the pad. The same rule builds the feeding station: a clean assembled bottle and a set of pump parts land in your hands at two in the morning without a single step or decision, because at that hour you have neither to spare.

### Wipe-after-every-use reframe (the surfaces skin and a mouth touch, and nothing goes away wet)
The surfaces that matter most in a nursery are the ones a mouth and bare skin touch, and they are cleaned after every single use, not saved for the weekly reset. You wipe the changing pad after every change, because it is the surface bare skin touches most in the house; you wash the bottle parts, sterilize them, and stand every one separately to air dry, because a damp valve in a closed bin is where mould starts; and you clean the mouthed crib rail with plain water or a baby-safe wipe rather than a scented cleaner. Fragrance-free and infant-safe throughout, and nothing in this room goes away wet.

### Safety reframe (the whole room, elevated)
Safety is not a pass at the end in this room, it runs through every zone, because the person it protects can reach nothing and ask for nothing. Cords on the monitor, the blind, and the feeding appliances are looped short and clipped an arm's length back from the crib, so nothing can be grabbed or pull boiling water down. Nothing heavy is ever stored on a shelf above or beside the crib. The mattress drops to its lowest setting the day the baby can pull to stand, the chest and the ledge a baby will haul themselves up on are strapped to the wall, and every cream, gel, and infant medicine lives in a lockable box up high, out of a standing child's reach.

### One Idea to Keep
A nursery is not safe because it looks calm and clean. It is safe because the crib holds one sheet and nothing else, because every change and every feed sits inside one arm's sweep, and because the cords are short, the medicines are locked up high, and nothing heavy sits above the crib. Do one zone tonight, the crib, and give the person who runs this room at two in the morning a room they can trust without looking.

## Four callouts (frozen)
- **Quick Win (win)**: Empty the crib before you touch anything else in the room. It is one armful of work, and the standard is a number you cannot negotiate down: one fitted sheet pulled tight to the corners, and nothing else over the rail. Strip it, hot-wash the sheet, wipe the mouthed rail with plain water, and put back one sheet and nothing more.
- **6S Tip (tip)**: Keep everything a task needs inside one arm's sweep, and clean the surfaces skin and a mouth touch after every single use. Wipe the changing pad after every change, stand every washed bottle part separately to air dry, and use fragrance-free, infant-safe cleaners only. Nothing in this room goes away wet.
- **The Bare-Crib Challenge (family)**: The crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else, ever, and this is the one standard you never round up. Say it once, plainly, to anyone who gives you crib bedding, in the same breath as showing them the wall above the crib where the beautiful quilt is hung instead. A bare crib is not cold or unloving; it is the safest sleep a baby can have.
- **Common Mistake (mistake)**: Buying ahead, a bigger diaper size, next winter's clothes, a second bottle brand in case the first fails, so the room fills with supplies bought for a baby who never arrived in that shape; and softening the bare-crib rule for one cold night, which is the night it matters most.

## The calls that matter here
The quilt your mother made (sentimental bedding on the wall or in the keepsake box, never inside the rails; say it once, plainly); the second station downstairs (run it as a full station or not at all, or replace it with one caddy you carry); the outfit she never wore (keep two garments per size, chosen by what the baby actually lived in); the bottle she rejected (three honest feeds across a week, then the whole system leaves); the good deal in the wrong size (one size ahead, two packs at most, whatever the price); the books you bought for yourself (the low ledge holds only board books, the paper-paged ones read from your lap in your room).

## Watch for in the nursery (five safety questions, and here safety leads)
- **Poison, choke, or strangle**: loose bedding, bumpers, and plush in the crib; a blind or monitor cord reachable through the slats; diaper creams, teething gels, and infant medicines within a standing child's reach; loose wipe-refill and pail-liner plastic film; button batteries behind an unscrewed toy flap; loose stitched eyes and long ribbon tags.
- **Fall**: a baby rolling off the changing pad while you turn to reach a shelf; a crib mattress left high after the baby can pull to stand.
- **Fall, cut, or crush**: an unanchored chest or an unfixed low ledge a baby pulls up on and brings down; heavy cartons on a shelf above or beside the crib.
- **Burn or fire**: a kettle, warmer, or sterilizer cord left long enough to be grabbed and pull boiling water down.
- **Water and electricity**: a water jug or a wet drying rack beside the sterilizer socket and the monitor plug.

## Kit (product TYPES only)
Key inputs, all fragrance-free and infant-safe: a colour-coded microfiber cloth set, a fragrance-free neutral pH multi-surface cleaner, plain warm water or a baby-safe wipe for the mouthed rails and board books, a compact vacuum with attachments, a food-area degreaser for the feeding station, a descaler or citric acid for the kettle or sterilizer, a bottle brush and baby-safe dish soap, and a washing machine on a hot wash. Storage and safety: a lockable medicine box, a diaper pail with liner, a bottle drying rack, front-facing book bins and toy bins with picture labels, a moisture absorber or humidity monitor, large marker size labels, and an anti-tip anchor and hardware for the chest and the ledge. No scented or harsh chemistry anywhere in this room.
