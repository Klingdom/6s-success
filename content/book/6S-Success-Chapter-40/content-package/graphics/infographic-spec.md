# Chapter 40 Infographic Spec: The Nursery Shine Method

## The single strongest image
The nursery Shine method plus the kit, because the method (safety leads, the bare crib, one arm's sweep, clean after every use) is the chapter's centerpiece.

## Anchor string (byte-verbatim)
> The nursery is the one room in the house where safety outranks everything else, and where the person using it is usually exhausted and working one-handed at two in the morning. So every standard here is built for that person and that hour rather than for a tidy shelf: the crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else, ever; everything a change or a feed needs sits inside one arm's sweep so you never turn your back on the baby; and the surfaces a mouth and bare skin touch get cleaned after every single use, with fragrance-free, infant-safe cleaners and nothing put away wet. In this room you do not round a safety number up, and you do not leave the baby to fetch a thing you forgot to keep in reach.

## Palette
paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green #6E8B5B (Shine). Fraunces headings, Inter labels.

## Layout, portrait (1080 x 1350)
- Title: The Nursery Shine Method.
- Band 1, "safety leads": a shield over the whole room.
- Band 2, "the bare crib": one fitted sheet, nothing else.
- Band 3, "one arm's sweep": an arc of reach around the changing pad.
- Band 4, "after every use": the pad wiped, a bottle part drying, nothing wet.
- Footer strip: safety leads, crib bare, arm's sweep, nothing wet.

## Layout, landscape (1200 x 675)
Two panels: the method on the left, the kit on the right.

## Rules
Types only, no brands or logos. Never show anything in the crib but a fitted sheet, a scented cleaner on a mouthed surface, or weight above the crib. No em/en/spaced-hyphen dashes in any label.
