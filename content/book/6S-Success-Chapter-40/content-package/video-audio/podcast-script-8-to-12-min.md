# Chapter 40 Podcast Script (8 to 12 min): The Nursery

Single host, warm but sober.

## Cold open
There is one room in the house where cleaning and safety are the same job, because the person it protects can reach nothing and the person running it is exhausted at two in the morning. Today we do the nursery, and we hold every safety number exactly.

## Part 1: The idea
Welcome back to the room-by-room playbooks. We finish the sleeping rooms with the nursery, and it is unlike every room before it, because here safety does not come last, it leads every zone. Every standard is built for a depleted adult working one-handed in the dark. Three rules run through it: the crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else ever, everything a change or a feed needs sits inside one arm's sweep, and the surfaces a mouth and bare skin touch are cleaned after every single use.

## Part 2: The six zones
The room is six zones: the crib and sleep zone, the changing station, the baby clothing zone, the feeding station, the diaper and care backstock, and the books and quiet play zone. Start with the crib, because it is one armful of work and the standard is a number you cannot argue with.

## Part 3: The bare crib
The crib is the one surface your baby is left alone on, so it holds a fitted sheet pulled tight to the corners and nothing else. No bumper, no pillow, no loose blanket, no plush, and nothing over the rail, because loose bedding is a suffocation risk to an infant who cannot roll off it. This is the one standard in the whole book you never round up, not for a cold night and not for a gift.

## Part 4: One arm's sweep
Every task here is done with one hand on the baby, so nothing a change needs may sit outside your arm's sweep. The diapers, the wipes, and the cream are all touchable from the pad without moving your feet, because the moment you turn to reach a shelf is the moment a baby rolls off it. The feeding station is built the same way: a clean assembled bottle in your hands at two in the morning without a single decision.

## Part 5: After every use, nothing wet
The surfaces a mouth and bare skin touch are cleaned after every single use. You wipe the changing pad after every change. You wash the bottle parts and stand each one separately to air dry, because a damp valve in a closed bin is where mould starts. Fragrance-free cleaners only, plain water on the mouthed rail, and nothing put away wet.

## Part 6: Safety and the calls
Safety runs through the whole room: cords short and clipped back, nothing heavy above the crib, the mattress dropped the day the baby can pull to stand, furniture strapped, medicines locked up high. And the calls are all loving gifts or good deals meeting a safety number that does not bend: the quilt someone made, the half-station downstairs, the unworn outfit, the rejected bottle, the bulk buy in the wrong size.

## Close
A nursery is not safe because it looks calm and clean. It is safe because the crib holds one sheet, every change and feed sits inside one arm's sweep, the cords are short, and the medicines are locked up high. Do one zone tonight, the crib. Next time: the primary bathroom.
