# Chapter 40 LinkedIn Posts: The Nursery

Ten standalone posts, each under 150 words. Product types only, no brands.

## 1. The one room where safety leads
The nursery is the one room in the house where safety outranks everything else, because the person it protects can reach nothing, hold nothing, and ask for nothing.

"So every standard here is built for that person and that hour rather than for a tidy shelf: the crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else, ever; everything a change or a feed needs sits inside one arm's sweep so you never turn your back on the baby."

## 2. The bare crib
"The crib is the one surface in the house where your baby is left alone, so it holds a fitted sheet pulled tight to the corners and nothing else, ever. No bumper, no pillow, no loose blanket, no plush toy, and nothing draped over the top rail." This is the one standard in the whole book you never round up: not for a cold night, not for a nap, and not for the beautiful quilt somebody made.

## 3. One arm's sweep
Every task in a nursery is done one-handed, with your other hand on the baby. So nothing a change needs may sit outside your arm's sweep. The diapers, the wipes, and the cream all have to be touchable from the pad without moving your feet, because the moment you turn to reach a shelf is the moment a baby rolls off it. Build the room for that, not for a photo.

## 4. Nothing goes away wet
The surfaces that matter most in a nursery are the ones a mouth and bare skin touch, so they are cleaned after every single use, not saved for the weekly reset. Wipe the changing pad after every change. Wash the bottle parts, sterilize them, and stand every one separately to air dry, because a damp valve in a closed bin is where mould starts. Fragrance-free throughout, and nothing put away wet.

## 5. Everything for the 2 a.m. version of you
The person running the nursery is exhausted and working one-handed at two in the morning, and every standard is built for that person, not the calm daytime one. A clean assembled bottle lands in your hands without a single decision. The wipe is where your hand already is. The room is designed for the worst moment, so it holds through it.

## 6. Cords short, weight low, medicines locked
Safety runs through every zone here, not just a final check. Cords on the monitor, the blind, and the feeding appliances are looped short and clipped an arm's length back from the crib. Nothing heavy sits on a shelf above the crib. And every cream, gel, and infant medicine lives in a lockable box up high, out of a standing child's reach.

## 7. Drop the mattress the day they pull to stand
A crib mattress left at the high setting after your baby can pull to stand puts the rail below their chest, and the fall is over the side onto a hard floor. The day they can haul themselves upright is the day the mattress drops to its lowest setting. It is a two-minute job and it closes the most predictable fall in the room.

## 8. Do not buy ahead
The nursery quietly fills with supplies bought for a baby who never arrived in that shape: the bigger diaper size, next winter's clothes, a second bottle brand in case the first fails. Babies skip sizes and reject teats. Buy no more than one size ahead and no more than two packs of it, whatever the price, and move outgrown stock out fast.

## 9. Say it once, plainly
Someone who loves you will give you bedding for the crib, and it cannot go in there. The rule that keeps the peace and the baby safe: sentimental bedding hangs on the wall above the crib or folds into the keepsake box, never inside the rails, and you say so once, plainly, in the same breath as showing them where you hung it.

## 10. A room you can trust without looking
"A nursery is not safe because it looks calm and clean. It is safe because the crib holds one sheet and nothing else, because every change and every feed sits inside one arm's sweep, and because the cords are short, the medicines are locked up high, and nothing heavy sits above the crib."
