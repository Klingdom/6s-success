# Facebook Longform Post: Safe Sleep, One Arm's Sweep, After Every Use

A calm, safety-first post from Chapter 40 of 6S Success: Home Edition, the nursery.

---

The nursery is the one room in the house where cleaning is not really about cleaning. It is about safety, because the person it protects can reach nothing, hold nothing, and ask for nothing, and the person running it is usually exhausted and working one-handed at two in the morning.

So every standard in this room is built for that person and that hour. Three rules run through all of it.

The crib holds one fitted sheet and nothing else, ever. No bumper, no pillow, no loose blanket, no plush toy, and nothing draped over the rail, because loose bedding is a suffocation risk to an infant who cannot roll off it. This is the one standard you never round up, not for a cold night and not for the beautiful quilt someone made, which hangs on the wall above the crib instead.

Everything a change or a feed needs sits inside one arm's sweep. The diapers, the wipes, and the cream are all touchable from the changing pad without moving your feet, because the moment you turn to reach a shelf is the moment a baby rolls off the pad. The feeding station works the same way: a clean assembled bottle lands in your hands at two in the morning without a single step or decision.

And the surfaces a mouth and bare skin touch are cleaned after every single use, not saved for the weekly reset. You wipe the changing pad after every change. You wash the bottle parts and stand each one separately to air dry, because a damp valve in a closed bin is where mould starts. Fragrance-free throughout, and nothing goes away wet.

Around all of that, safety leads: cords looped short and clipped back, nothing heavy above the crib, the mattress dropped the day the baby can pull to stand, and every medicine in a lockable box up high.

Do one zone tonight, the crib, and give the person who runs this room in the dark a room they can trust without looking.

Is your crib truly bare?
