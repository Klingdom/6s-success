## Hero device (frozen): The Use Test
Among the things that belong here, run one honest question on each:
> **When did you last use it?**
- Used it recently, or you would replace it if it vanished → **necessary.** Keep it.
- Only plausibly useful, kept just in case, not actually used → **unnecessary.** It goes in the out box.
The reframe that powers it (frozen): **Useful is not the same as used.**
The follow-up for genuine hard cases (frozen): **If it vanished today, would you buy it again?**

## The two patterns the Use Test catches (frozen framing)
1. **The just-in-case trap:** a plausibly useful thing kept for a someday that rarely comes, quietly crowding out the things you actually use.
2. **Duplicates and surplus:** you need one umbrella, not three; a bag or two, not a wad of them. Necessary includes a sensible quantity; the rest is surplus.

## Outcome vocabulary (frozen — use these exact words)
- Keepers: **necessary** (you use it / would replace it).
- Releases: **unnecessary** (only kept just in case), which go in **the out box**.
- Do NOT decide the out box's fate here (donate / sell / store / recycle / throw is Chapter 11). The out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination.
- Do NOT call this "does it belong here" (that was Chapter 8's question).

Everything in that basket *belonged* at an entryway. Most of it was still unnecessary, because you do not use it. ## One Idea to Keep (frozen)
> The hardest clutter to see is the useful kind you never actually use. Keep what earns its place by getting used, and let go of what you were only keeping just in case.

## Definition box (frozen)
> Necessary means you actually use it, or would replace it if it vanished. Unnecessary means it is only plausibly useful, kept just in case, while the things you truly use get crowded out. Sort by use: keep the first, release the second.

## Anti-shame reframe (frozen, set as a pull-quote)
> Letting go of what you do not use is not wasteful. The money was spent when you bought it, and keeping it unused does not earn that back. It only charges you rent in space and attention.

## Callouts (frozen wording)
- **Quick Win:** Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case. Ask when you last used it. If you cannot remember, put it in an out box today. One honest release is a full necessary pass.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone finds one duplicate, something you own several of and need one of, and keeps their favorite. The rest go in the out box. No voting on anyone else's duplicates.
- **6S Tip:** Sort by use, not by potential. Almost anything might be useful someday. The question that matters is when you last actually used it.
- **Common Mistake:** Keeping the worse duplicate just in case, and keeping the good one too. If two things do one job, one of them is unnecessary, usually the one you never reach for.

## Voice and anti-shame guardrails
- Warm, plain, concrete, quietly confident, gently witty, non-prescriptive.
- Unnecessary does not mean worthless, and letting go is not confessing a mistake. Frozen line above carries this.
- NO force / violence metaphors ("purge," "attack the pile," "war on clutter," "pull the trigger"). NO hype words (unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, seamless, game-changing, "almost magical", "fast-paced", etc.).
- Lexical discipline: the topic forces "use / used / useful" and "need / necessary"; vary the surrounding language and cap other payoff words (watch "just in case," "lighter," "surplus", "crowd") at ~3 to 4 uses each. Vary sentence cadence; do not reuse a loaded praise-word on both sides of a contrast.
