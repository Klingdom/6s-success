# 6S Success: Home Edition (Sales Page Copy)

## The surface is clear. The basket beside it is not.

You did the hard part. You cleared the surface, carried off what did not belong, and it stayed clear. Then you looked down at the basket on the floor, the one that has been catching the overflow for years, and it is still full. Three umbrellas. A wad of tote bags. A spare key to a car you sold. And here is the strange part: every single thing in there belongs at an entryway. None of it is in the wrong place. You still hardly touch most of it.

That is the gap this chapter closes, and almost everyone misses it, because the things hiding in that gap all have a good reason to stay.

## Useful is not the same as used

Chapter 9 of *6S Success: Home Edition*, "Necessary vs. Unnecessary," is the second Sort chapter. The first pass sorted by belonging, where a thing lives. This one sorts by need, whether you actually use it. And it turns on one quiet line worth taping to the fridge: useful is not the same as used.

Almost everything you own is useful in some imaginable circumstance. That is exactly the trap. When the test for keeping a thing is whether it could ever come in handy, the test keeps everything, and "a test that passes everything is not a test at all." So the chapter hands you a narrower, honest one instead: when did you last use it? If a fast, specific memory comes back, the thing is necessary and it stays. If you have to invent a scenario rather than recall a time, it is unnecessary, and it goes in the out box.

## What you get in this chapter

- The Use Test, one honest question ("When did you last use it?") that sorts what your first pass left behind
- The buy-it-again tiebreaker for the genuinely hard ones, which quietly strips away the sunk cost
- How to handle duplicates and surplus: keep your favorite, retire the understudy, and stop storing the worse copy of a thing you own a better version of
- A worked pass through the entryway basket, object by object, that you can copy onto your own space
- The anti-shame reframe that undoes the guilt that keeps unused things for a decade: letting go of what you do not use is not wasting

You will not shop for anything. You will not throw a single thing while you decide, because the out box is only a set-aside spot; where those things go from there is a later chapter. You separate useful from used, keep what you actually reach for, and set the rest aside, all of it subtraction, none of it spending. The friction meter takes its second, smaller step, an honest one, because Sort is not finished yet.

Read the chapter, pick up one thing you keep just in case, and ask when you last used it. One honest release is a full pass.
