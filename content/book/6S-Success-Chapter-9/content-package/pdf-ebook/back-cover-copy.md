# Back Cover Copy: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

## Back-cover text

You cleared the surface, and it stayed clear. Then you looked at the basket beside it, still full after years, and noticed something odd: everything in it belongs at an entryway, and you still hardly touch most of it. Umbrellas, tote bags, a spare key, a dead cable. All of it useful. None of it used.

Chapter 9 of *6S Success: Home Edition* is the second Sort chapter, and it closes that gap. Chapter 8 sorted by belonging, where a thing lives. This chapter sorts by need, whether you actually use it. The line that carries it: useful is not the same as used. The tool is one honest question you ask of everything that stayed: when did you last use it? A fast, specific memory means it is necessary and it stays. A shrug means it is unnecessary, and it goes in the out box, a set-aside spot, not a destination. For the hard ones, a tiebreaker that strips away the sunk cost: if it vanished today, would you buy it again?

And it undoes the guilt that keeps unused things for a decade. Letting go of what you do not use is not wasting. The money was spent when you bought it; keeping it unused only charges you rent in space and attention. "There is nothing to be ashamed of in owning a thing you no longer need."

Ask one question. Keep what you use. Set the just-in-case surplus aside, without spending a cent.
