# Necessary vs. Unnecessary

**Part Three, Sort**
Chapter 9 of *6S Success: Home Edition*

Subtitle · Everything left belongs here. That is not the same as needing it. Keep what you actually use, and let the just-in-case pile go.

---

## Chapter descriptor

The second Sort question. Among the things that all belong in a space, run the Use Test on each one, when did you last use it, keep what you actually reach for, and set the just-in-case surplus aside in an out box.

---

## How to read this chapter

This is the second chapter of Part Three, Sort, and it picks up exactly where the first pass left off. By now you have cleared a space of what does not belong. This chapter asks the harder, narrower question about what stayed: not where a thing lives, but whether you need to keep it at all.

Read it through once for the idea, then open the basket, drawer, or shelf you cleared and run a real pass. Pick up each thing that stayed and ask the Use Test: when did you last use it? A fast, specific memory means it is necessary and it stays. A shrug means it is unnecessary, and it goes in the out box, a set-aside spot where the releases wait together while you finish. For a genuinely hard one, ask whether you would buy it again today. When you find several of a thing you need one or two of, keep your favorite and let the rest go. The honest maybes you truly cannot call get set apart on their own; do not force them.

Nothing gets bought and nothing gets thrown while you decide. This chapter keeps what you use and sets aside what you do not. It does not yet decide where the out box goes, which is a later chapter, and it does not yet handle the things you honestly cannot decide, which is the next one.

If you only do one thing: pick up one item you keep just in case, ask when you last used it, and if you cannot remember, put it in an out box today. One honest release is a full pass.
