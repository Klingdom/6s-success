# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 9

**"Necessary vs. Unnecessary"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are
working specs, not finished art. They stay consistent with the four inline SVG figures in
`chapter_09_final.html`: the tipped basket opener, the Use Test branch, the duplicates
sweep, and the friction meter moved a second, smaller time.

Palette tokens (from the chapter HTML :root): paper `#F7F2E9`, panel `#FBF7EF`,
ink `#2B2622`, soft `#6A625A`, terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate `#3C5A6B`,
calm green `#6E8B5B`, friction spark `#CB4B36`, rule `#E2D8C4` / `#D9CDB8`.

Scope fence: this chapter sorts by "do you actually use it," not by "does it belong in this
space" (that was Chapter 8). It does not build the red-tag or holding-area system for the
genuinely undecided items (Chapter 10), it does not decide where the out box goes next,
donate, sell, store, recycle, or throw away (Chapter 11), and it does not store the keepers
by frequency of use (Chapter 13). "The out box" is always a set-aside spot, never a
destination. Separator discipline: labels use the middot, for example `CHAPTER 9 · SORT`,
never a floating hyphen or a floating dot between words.

## 1. The Use Test (the hero tool)

**Purpose:** Show the whole device in one glance: one gate, two answers, plus a quiet side
gate for the hard cases. This is the diagram the chapter is built around, and it mirrors
Figure 2 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** Each thing that stayed after Chapter 8 meets one narrower question, and
the answer routes it. A fast, specific memory keeps it. A shrug sends it to the out box.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE USE TEST`.
- Left: a single small object glyph (an umbrella) labeled beneath in sans, soft gray:
  "one thing that stayed". A slate flow arrow carries it right into the gate.
- Center: a slate-bordered rounded gate card. Fraunces in ink: "When did you last use it?"
  Helper line beneath in sans, soft gray: "recently · or cannot say".
- Up-right branch, green: a green-bordered zone tab `USED IT RECENTLY` with a green calm-dot,
  Fraunces "necessary", and a Newsreader italic line "keep it".
- Down-right branch, slate: a slate-bordered zone tab `CANNOT REMEMBER` with Fraunces
  "unnecessary" and a Newsreader italic line "the out box".
- A honey dashed side gate dropping from the question card down to a small honey-bordered
  card, `FOR THE HARD CASES`, Newsreader italic: "If it vanished today, would you buy it
  again?"
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "One question about the past, not the maybe."

## 2. Useful Is Not Used (the reframe)

**Purpose:** Make the core insight visible as a picture, not just a line. Almost anything is
useful; only some things are used, and the gap between the two is where the clutter hides.

**What it shows:** Two facing cards weigh a claim against a record, with the honest test line
sitting between them.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `USEFUL IS NOT USED`.
- Left card, honey header bar `USEFUL`: a small thought-cloud glyph over an object, sans
  helper "a claim about the future", Newsreader italic beneath: "this could do something for
  me, someday". A soft note: "passes everything".
- Right card, green header bar `USED`: the same object with a small calendar or last-used tag,
  sans helper "a fact about the past", Newsreader italic beneath: "this actually did something
  for me, recently". A green calm-dot marks it.
- Between the two, a slate rule and the Fraunces line, ink: "Useful is not the same as used."
- Bottom, Newsreader italic, slate: "The hardest clutter to notice is not the junk. It is the
  useful surplus you never actually use." (This paraphrases the figure's idea; use the
  verbatim line from the quote file where a quote is wanted.)

## 3. Duplicates and Surplus (the sweep)

**Purpose:** Show the second pattern the Use Test catches: you need one, not five. Mirrors
Figure 3 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** A row of five identical tote bags. One is kept, the other four are swept
out to the out box on a single slate arrow.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `DUPLICATES AND SURPLUS`.
- Far left: one tote drawn in the green keep-zone tint, a green calm-dot above it, Fraunces
  beneath "keep your favorite", Newsreader italic "the one you carry".
- Center: four identical totes in a muted bone tint, clearly copies of the first.
- A slate sweep arrow curving from the four copies over to a right-side out box drawn as a
  simple slate carton, labeled `THE OUT BOX`.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "If two things do one job, one of them is surplus. You
  need one, not five."
- Note for the illustrator: keep the four copies plainly identical to the keeper, so the point
  is quantity, not quality. Necessary includes a sensible amount; the copies past that are the
  surplus.

## 4. The Out Box, and the Honest Maybe (the destination, fenced)

**Purpose:** Say plainly what the out box is and is not, and give the one or two truly
undecidable items a spot of their own without stalling the pass. This diagram carries the
chapter's two forward fences.

**What it shows:** Three lanes leaving the pass: clear keepers, clear releases, and a small
honest maybe set aside on its own.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: sans caps label, soft gray, `WHERE THINGS GO TODAY`.
- Left, a green `KEEP` tab with a calm-dot: Newsreader italic "the things you use", staying
  in place.
- Center, a slate `THE OUT BOX` carton: Newsreader italic "the things you do not use, waiting
  together". A soft-gray fence note beneath, in sans: "a set-aside spot, not the donation
  pile, not the trash. Where it goes from here is a later chapter." No bin, no giveaway pile,
  no truck in frame.
- Right, a small honey-outlined `AN HONEST MAYBE` card set slightly apart: Newsreader italic
  "one or two you truly cannot call". A one-line honey teaser beneath, sans: "these get a
  system of their own next chapter." Do not draw that system here.
- Footer, Newsreader italic, slate: "Today you decide. Later you route."

## 5. The Friction Meter, Second Step (the payoff)

**Purpose:** Close the chapter with the honest landmark. The needle moved for the first time
in Chapter 8; here it steps again, further toward calm, but by a visibly smaller amount,
because this pass cleared a smaller surplus. Mirrors Figure 4 in the HTML.

**What it shows:** The recurring half-circle gauge with two needle marks: a faint dashed ghost
where the needle sat at the end of Chapter 8, and the live needle a little further toward calm,
the short gap between them being the whole point.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored calm green (left) through honey (middle) to friction spark
  (right), on a pale `#EDE3CE` track.
- A dashed, muted ghost needle at Chapter 8's resting position in the honey middle, tagged in
  small sans, muted gray: "Ch. 8 was here".
- The live needle in ink, stepped a little further toward the green calm side, tagged `NOW`.
  The step from ghost to live is short, and clearly shorter than the first move was.
- A slate `GOAL` crosshair fully on the green calm side, carrying its `PURPOSE` flag, still
  clearly ahead of the live needle.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "A second step, shorter than the first."

**Honesty note for the illustrator:** the live needle must sit only a little past the Chapter 8
ghost, a smaller move than the first, still inside the honey middle, and still short of the
goal crosshair. Do not swing it to calm, do not hold it exactly where Chapter 8 left it, and
do not let it reach the goal. Sort is not finished (Chapters 10 and 11 remain), and Straighten,
Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain are all still ahead.
