# Image Generation Prompts: Chapter 9

**"Necessary vs. Unnecessary"**
*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Three · Sort*

Five prompts written for the book's warm, editorial, hand-made feel. Palette to hold across
all five: warm paper `#F7F2E9`, panel cream `#FBF7EF`, deep ink brown `#2B2622`,
terracotta `#BC4B2A`, honey `#DDA63A`, slate blue `#3C5A6B`, soft green `#6E8B5B`, a single
warm red `#CB4B36` reserved for tension. Natural soft daylight, shallow but not showy depth,
real textures over polish.

The named space throughout is the entryway drop zone, the console by the front door, and the
basket on the floor beside it that has caught the overflow for years. Chapter 8 already
cleared the console surface of what did not belong. This chapter opens the still-full basket
and asks the harder question: not where a thing lives, but whether you actually use it.
Nothing is bought, nothing is cleaned, nothing is routed anywhere yet; unused things simply
move to a set-aside out box. Keep the tone calm and kind: letting go of the unused is not a
verdict on the object, not a confession of a mistake, and never a purge.

---

## 1. The basket, tipped open (the opener)

A warm, low-angle photograph of a wicker entryway basket tipped open on a wood floor in early
morning light, its contents fanned out across the boards. The spread is honest and specific:
three nearly identical umbrellas, a thick wad of folded reusable tote bags, a fan of old paper
takeout menus, a tangled dead charging cable, and a lone spare key on a faded fob. Above and
behind, on the console, only five working things sit in calm order. One umbrella and one bag
in the spread catch a small touch of soft green, the things that get used; the rest sit plain
and a little dusty, kept just in case. Warm paper cream and honey wood tones, ink-brown
shadows, a slate blue wall behind. Editorial cookbook-style photography, shallow focus on the
fanned contents, the cleared console soft behind. The surface is clear; the basket that fed it
is not.

- Mood: a quiet second look, the harder question after the easy win.
- Avoid: glossy real-estate staging, cold blue tints, floating text overlays, a garbage bag or
  donation box in frame, any sense of throwing away, dramatic mess for shock value.

## 2. The one you actually grab (the necessary keeper)

A close still-life photograph of a single well-used umbrella resting in a hand or leaning ready
by the door, the one an actual person reaches for when the sky looks wrong. Faint honest wear
on the handle, the fabric a little relaxed from real use. Warm side light, honey light on the
wood, paper cream background, ink-brown shadows, shallow focus on the handle. A single small
soft-green note in frame, a calm-dot feel, marks it as a keeper. The image should read as
recognition, your hand already knows this is the one, not as a proud display.

- Mood: easy certainty, the fast yes of a necessary thing.
- Avoid: a brand-new catalog umbrella, price tags, a matched set on display, harsh flash,
  showroom gloss, any sense of shopping.

## 3. The wad of tote bags (duplicates and surplus)

A warm editorial still-life of a compressed brick of folded reusable tote bags spilling from a
kitchen drawer, far more than any household could carry, with two or three set slightly apart
as the ones actually used. The point is quantity: many near-identical copies, one or two that
earn their place. Soft daylight, honey and paper tones, ink-brown shadows, shallow depth on the
few set aside. Documentary and unstaged. One good bag is necessary; the fortieth is only
surplus, not because it is a bad bag but because the job is already done by the first.

- Mood: gentle recognition of the sensible surplus, no scolding.
- Avoid: a trash bag as the framing, a face judging, urgency, clutter drama, cold clinical
  light, any bin or giveaway pile in frame.

## 4. The honest maybe (one thing held, unhurried)

A close, calm photograph of a hand holding a single ordinary object up to the light, weighing
it, the small everyday moment of asking when you last used it. The object is neutral and
familiar, a small kitchen gadget or a scarf, nothing precious or comic. Warm paper cream
background, honey light on the fingers, ink-brown shadows, a single warm-red note only if a
touch of tension is wanted. Editorial, tactile, shallow focus shared between the hand and the
object. The image should read as an honest question asked gently, not as guilt or regret.

- Mood: quiet, fair, unhurried appraisal of one's own use, not the object's worth.
- Avoid: a frowning face, shame cues, a bin or donation box as the answer, harsh contrast,
  glossy retouching, floating typeset text.

## 5. The needle steps again (the friction meter, second step)

A hand-made editorial illustration of the book's recurring friction meter, a half-circle gauge
dialing from calm green on the left, through honey in the middle, to friction red on the right.
The dark live needle has stepped a little further toward calm than last chapter, a visibly
smaller move than the first one. Just behind it, a faint dashed ghost needle marks where
Chapter 8 left it, tagged in small quiet lettering "Ch. 8 was here", so the short second step
is unmistakable. A slate crosshair labeled the goal sits fully on the green calm side, carrying
its purpose flag, still clearly ahead of where the needle now rests. Warm paper cream
background, ink line work, consistent stroke weight, friendly but not childish.

- Mood: an honest, earned second step, real but smaller, and not finished.
- Avoid: the needle swung all the way to calm, the needle held exactly where Chapter 8 left it,
  a step as large as or larger than the first, celebratory confetti or hype, neon color, any
  completion or victory feel. Sort is not done, and five S's still remain.
