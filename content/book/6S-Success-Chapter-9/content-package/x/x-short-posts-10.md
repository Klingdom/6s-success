# X Short Posts: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

Chapter 9 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. Ten standalone posts. Each is at or under 280 characters and makes sense on its own. Soft CTA on a few, not all.

---

1/
Chapter 8 cleared your surface by asking what belongs here. Chapter 9 asks the harder question: of the things that belong, which do you actually use? Everything left after a Sort belongs. That is not the same as needing it. Chapter 9 of 6S Success: Home Edition, free online.

(approx. 270 chars)

---

2/
Almost everything you own is useful, which is exactly the problem. A test that keeps anything that could ever be useful keeps everything. So the chapter turns on one line: "Useful is not the same as used." One is a story about an object; the other a record of what it did.

(approx. 268 chars)

---

3/
The fix is one honest question, asked of one object at a time: "When did you last use it?" A fast, specific memory means necessary, and it stays. A shrug means unnecessary, and it goes in the out box, a set-aside spot you deal with later.

(approx. 233 chars)

---

4/
Necessary does not mean you would die without it; it means you use it, or would replace it if it vanished. Unnecessary does not mean worthless; it means kept just in case while the things you truly use get crowded out. Sort by use, not by potential.

(approx. 245 chars)

---

5/
Stuck on a hard one? One tiebreaker cuts the fog: "If it vanished today, would you buy it again?" It strips the sunk cost. An easy yes, keep it. A slow no, a wince, a well, not really, and you already have your answer.

(approx. 214 chars)

---

6/
You do not need three umbrellas. You need the one you grab when the sky looks wrong. "If two things do one job, one of them is surplus, and it is almost always the one you never reach for." Keep the one your hand goes to; let the understudy retire.

(approx. 243 chars)

---

7/
The basket by the door: every umbrella, bag, menu, and cable in it belonged at an entryway. Most was still unnecessary, because you never used it. Belonging got you a clear surface. Use gets you a basket you can see the bottom of.

(approx. 226 chars)

---

8/
Where a good pass most often dies: cost. You pick up something unused, and a voice reminds you what it cost. But the money was spent the day you bought it. "The waste, if there is any, already happened. Letting go is not the waste. It is the end of paying twice."

(approx. 258 chars)

---

9/
Letting go is not a confession either. Deciding a thing is unnecessary is not deciding you were foolish to buy it. The reason it made sense simply passed. "There is nothing to be ashamed of in owning a thing you no longer need."

(approx. 224 chars)

---

10/
The friction meter steps again, toward calm, because objects left again. A shorter step than last chapter's, honestly so: the first pass cleared the big surplus, this one the smaller surplus of what you do not use. Still the first S, still short of the goal. Read free online.

(approx. 276 chars)
