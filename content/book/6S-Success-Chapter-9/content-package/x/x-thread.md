# X Thread: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

Last chapter you cleared the surface, and it stayed clear. But there is still a basket by the console, full of things that all belong at an entryway and never get touched. Chapter 9 of 6S Success: Home Edition asks the harder question. Sleeves rolled up.

2/
Chapter 8 sorted by belonging: does this live here? Chapter 9 sorts by use. "Last chapter asked where a thing lives. This chapter asks whether you need to keep it at all." Everything left belongs here. That is not the same as needing it.

(approx. 232 chars)

---

3/
Here is the trap, gentle and expensive. Almost everything you own is useful, so "useful" keeps everything. "When the test for keeping a thing is whether it could ever be useful, the test passes everything, and a test that passes everything is not a test at all."

(approx. 259 chars)

---

4/
So the whole chapter turns on one line, worth putting on the fridge: "Useful is not the same as used." Useful is a claim about the future. Used is a fact about the past. Homes fill up not with useless things but with useful ones you never use.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

5/
Name the pattern so you can see it. "The just-in-case thing is a plausibly useful object kept for a someday that rarely comes, quietly taking the place of the things you use now." It is not stupid to own one. It is only costly to own a basketful.

(approx. 242 chars)

---

6/
The fix is one honest question, asked of one object at a time: "When did you last use it?" A fast, specific memory means it is necessary, and it stays. A shrug, a scenario you have to invent, means it is unnecessary, and it goes in the out box.

(approx. 240 chars)

---

7/
Two words, plainly. Necessary does not mean you would die without it; it means you use it, or would replace it if it vanished. Unnecessary does not mean worthless; it means kept just in case while the things you truly use get crowded out.

(approx. 233 chars)

---

8/
"Necessary and unnecessary are not judgments of the object's quality. They are readings of your own behavior, which is the only honest evidence either way." Trust the fast answer. Your hands know the truth before your pride does.

(approx. 225 chars)

---

9/
For the genuinely hard cases, one tiebreaker cuts the fog: "If it vanished today, would you buy it again?" It strips the sunk cost. An easy yes, keep it. A slow no, a wince, a well, not really, and you already have your answer.

(approx. 224 chars)

---

10/
Second pattern the test catches: duplicates. You do not need three umbrellas. You need the one you grab when the sky looks wrong. Necessary quietly includes a sensible quantity. Keep the one your hand goes to; let the understudy retire.

(approx. 232 chars)

---

11/
"If two things do one job, one of them is surplus, and it is almost always the one you never reach for." Keep the umbrella that opens without a fight, the good scissors over the dull ones. You are picking the copy that works, not living without.

(approx. 242 chars)

12/
Run it on the basket. Keep: one umbrella, a bag or two, the keys, the leash, each with a fast memory of its last use. Out box: the two extra umbrellas, the wad of spare bags, a spare key to a sold car, old menus, a dead cable for a gone phone.

(approx. 238 chars)

13/
Every one of those belonged at an entryway. Most was still unnecessary, because you do not use it. What is the out box? A set-aside spot where releases wait while you finish the pass. Not donation, not trash. Today you decide. Later you route.

(approx. 238 chars)

---

14/
A few things you honestly cannot call. Do not force them. One or two undecidable things are the normal residue of a careful pass, not a hole in your resolve. The ones you honestly cannot call get a system of their own next chapter.

(approx. 227 chars)

---

15/
Here is where a good pass most often dies, and it has nothing to do with the objects. You pick up something you do not use, and a small voice reminds you what it cost. Cost enters, and letting go starts to feel like admitting you wasted money.

(approx. 239 chars)

---

16/
Look straight at the arithmetic, kinder than it feels. "Letting go of what you do not use is not wasteful. The money was spent when you bought it, and keeping it unused does not earn that back. It only charges you rent in space and attention."

(approx. 238 chars)

---

17/
"Holding an unused object to honor the money you spent is like leaving a light on in an empty room to get your money's worth from the bulb." The waste already happened. Letting go is not the waste. It is the end of paying twice.

(approx. 224 chars)

---

18/
And it is not a confession. Deciding a thing is unnecessary is not deciding you were foolish to buy it. The reason it made sense simply passed. "There is nothing to be ashamed of in owning a thing you no longer need."

(approx. 212 chars)

19/
The friction meter steps again, toward calm, because objects left again. But it is a shorter step than last chapter's, and it should be: the first pass cleared the big surplus; this one clears the smaller surplus of what you do not use. Still short of the goal.
