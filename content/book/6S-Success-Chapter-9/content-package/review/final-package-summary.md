## Key extracted assets
- **Title:** Necessary vs. Unnecessary
- **Slug:** necessary-vs-unnecessary
- **Hero device:** The Use Test, "When did you last use it?" A fast, specific memory means the thing is necessary and stays; a shrug means it is unnecessary and goes in the out box. The reframe that carries it: "Useful is not the same as used." For hard cases, the tiebreaker "If it vanished today, would you buy it again?" (lightly attributed as "not mine" in the chapter).
- **Two patterns:** the just-in-case trap (plausibly useful things kept for a someday) and duplicates / surplus ("One good umbrella is necessary.").
- **Worked example:** a pass through the basket beside the entryway console. Kept (necessary): one umbrella, a bag or two, the keys, the leash. To the out box (unnecessary): the two extra umbrellas, the wad of surplus tote bags, a spare key to a car "you sold two summers ago," old takeout menus, a dead cable.
- **Anti-shame sunk-cost line (verbatim):** "Letting go of what you do not use is not wasteful. The money was spent when you bought it, and keeping it unused does not earn that back. It only charges you rent in space and attention."
- **Hero CTA:** run the Use Test, even a one-object pass. Pick up one just-in-case thing, ask when you last used it, and set it in the out box if you cannot remember.
- **One Idea to Keep (verbatim):** "The hardest clutter to see is the useful kind you never actually use. Keep what earns its place by getting used, and let go of what you were only keeping just in case."
