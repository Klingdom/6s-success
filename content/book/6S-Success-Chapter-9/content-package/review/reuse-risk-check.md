# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 9 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty-plus assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it. Every element named below was grep-verified in the manuscript, and every asset placement was read in the file named, before being listed.

## Same-day collision check
The calendar is built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, do not let the "letting go is not wasting" sunk-cost reframe and the "would you buy it again?" tiebreaker both land the same day, since they carry the same anti-guilt beat.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/linkedin-carousel-outline.md`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) cover the same Use Test content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version (`linkedin/linkedin-newsletter-version.md`) and the email newsletter version (`newsletter/newsletter-version.md`) share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, and the like) to point back with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Part 3, Sort, Chapters 8 to 11)
- **Keep Chapter 9 "sort by need" distinct from Chapter 8 "sort by belonging."** The manuscript itself draws the line, verbatim: "Last chapter asked where a thing lives. This chapter asks whether you need to keep it at all." and "You sorted by belonging, and belonging held." Do not let Chapter 9 assets drift back into the belonging question, and do not let Chapter 8 assets creep forward into the own-it-or-not question. When both chapters' assets are live in the same weeks, keep the two questions clearly separated so neither payoff is muddied.
- **Hold the seam to Chapter 10.** Chapter 10 is named in the handoff, verbatim: "Next: Chapter 10 · Red Tags, Holding Areas, and Sorter's Remorse." That chapter handles the genuine maybes and the second thoughts, so keep Chapter 9's out box as a set-aside spot only ("Today you decide. Later you route.") and do not let a Chapter 9 post build the holding-area system early.
- **Hold the seam to the disposal chapter.** Where the out box goes (donate, sell, store, recycle, discard) is deferred in the manuscript to "a whole chapter of its own a little further on." Keep that as a teaser in the social assets; do not let a Chapter 9 post promise or instruct disposal.
- **The friction meter is a moving asset.** From Chapter 8 on it keeps stepping toward calm. Chapter 9's step is deliberately shorter than Chapter 8's, and the manuscript says why ("A shorter step for a smaller clearing is not the meter losing steam. It is the meter being accurate."). Make sure no asset shows the Chapter 9 needle further along than the manuscript does, and preserve the "Ch. 8 was here" ghost so the second, smaller step stays visible and honest.
- **The drop-zone example keeps running.** The console and its basket are the same entryway carried since Part 2. When Part 2 and Part 3 assets are live together, vary which step each post leads with so the same console does not feel overused.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter itself is a draft authored in the book voice, not supplied source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft. If the author revises the chapter, refresh the assets so they do not drift from the published version.
- Nothing is borrowed from outside the book without saying so. The one external idea, the "would you buy it again?" tiebreaker, is lightly attributed in the chapter itself ("not mine; versions of it have kept honest people honest for years", verified), so no asset should present it as the book's invention.
- The ten quote cards are verbatim from the manuscript (each string grep-verified; the file marks Cards 1 through 10 as **verbatim** and labels any trimmed alternate as **lightly trimmed**). That labeling is accurate.

## Recommendations
1. Rotate the six CTA phrasings across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep Chapter 9 "sort by need" clearly separate from Chapter 8 "sort by belonging," and hold the Chapter 10 and disposal-chapter teasers as teasers.
5. Preserve the "second step, shorter than the first, and that is accuracy" friction-meter framing and the "Ch. 8 was here" ghost in every asset that shows the gauge.
6. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
