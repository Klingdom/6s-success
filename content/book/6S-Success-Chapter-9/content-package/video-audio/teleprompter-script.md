# Teleprompter Script: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

**Chapter 9, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** necessary-vs-unnecessary
**Length:** about 660 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

Come back to the drop zone one more time.

The console you cleared last chapter is still clear.

The things you carried off did not creep back overnight.

You did not just tidy them. You sent them home.

It holds only its five working things now.

A keys tray, a leash, an umbrella, a bag, and tomorrow's mail.

You sorted by belonging. And belonging held.

But look down.

There is a basket on the floor beside the console.

And we both know what is in it.

Three umbrellas, only one of which you ever open.

A thick wad of folded tote bags.

A spare key for a car you sold two summers ago.

A fan of old takeout menus.

A dead charging cable for a phone you replaced ages ago.

Here is the strange part.

Every one of those things belongs at an entryway.

And you have not touched most of them in a year.

So this is a different question from last time.

Last chapter asked where a thing lives.

This chapter asks whether you need to keep it at all.

Belonging is not the problem anymore.

Use is.

Here is the trap.

Almost everything you own is useful.

The spare key could open a car.

The extra umbrella could keep a guest dry.

Point at anything, and you can make a case for someday.

That is exactly the problem.

When the test is whether a thing could ever be useful,

the test passes everything.

And a test that passes everything is not a test at all.

So the whole chapter turns on one short line.

Useful is not the same as used.

Useful is a claim about the future.

This could do something for me, someday.

Used is a fact about the past.

This actually did something for me, recently.

A home does not fill up with useless things.

It fills up with useful things you never use.

So here is the tool.

One honest question, asked of one object at a time.

When did you last use it?

If a real memory comes fast, it is necessary. It stays.

If you have to invent a scenario, it is unnecessary.

And it goes in the out box.

Necessary does not mean you would die without it.

It means you use it, or you would replace it if it vanished.

Unnecessary does not mean worthless.

It means it is only plausibly useful, kept just in case.

These are readings of your own behavior.

Not judgments of the object.

Most things answer instantly.

Trust the fast answer.

Your hands know the truth before your pride does.

For the hard ones, there is a tiebreaker.

If it vanished today, would you buy it again?

That strips away the sunk cost.

An easy yes, keep it.

A slow no, or a wince, is your answer.

Now, duplicates.

You do not need three umbrellas.

You need the one you actually grab.

If two things do one job, one of them is surplus.

Almost always the one you never reach for.

Keep the one your hand goes to.

Let the understudy retire.

Now, the part that stalls most people.

You pick up something you do not use.

And a small voice reminds you what it cost.

So letting it go feels like admitting you wasted money.

And the hand stops.

So look straight at the arithmetic.

The money is already gone.

It went the day you bought it.

Keeping the thing will not return a cent.

It only adds a second cost.

The room it takes. The attention it draws.

The waste, if any, already happened.

Letting go is not the waste.

It is the end of paying twice.

And it is not a confession either.

The reason you bought it passed. That is all.

Now go look at the friction meter.

Last chapter, it moved for the first time.

Today, objects left again.

So the needle steps again, toward calm.

But the step is smaller than the last one.

And it should be.

The first pass cleared the big backlog.

This pass cleared a smaller pile.

A shorter step for a smaller clearing is honest, not weak.

And the goal is still ahead.

Because Sort itself is not even finished.

Still inside the first S. Still short of the goal.

So here is your job.

Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case.

Ask when you last used it.

If you cannot remember, put it in an out box today.

One honest release is a full pass.

Keep what earns its place by getting used.

And let go of what you were only keeping just in case.

Go run one pass.
