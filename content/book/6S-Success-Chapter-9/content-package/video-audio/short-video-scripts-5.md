# Short-Form Scripts: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

**Chapter 9, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** necessary-vs-unnecessary
**Five scripts for Reels, Shorts, and TikTok, each 30 to 90 seconds.**

---

## SHORT 1: One question keeps what you use
**Topic: the hero device, the Use Test. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Everything in this basket belongs here. Most of it still has to go.

**Script:**
Everything in this basket belongs at a front door. Umbrellas, tote bags, a spare key, some old menus. And most of it still has to go. Here is why. Almost everything you own is useful in some imaginable circumstance, so "could this be useful" keeps everything, and a test that keeps everything is not a test. So ask a narrower question. When did you last use it? If a real memory comes fast, this week, last month, the last time it rained, it is necessary, and it stays. If you have to invent a scenario instead of recalling one, it is unnecessary, and it goes in an out box. Useful is not the same as used.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "It all belongs here. Most of it still goes."
- 12s: "'Could this be useful?' keeps everything"
- 24s: "When did you last use it?"
- 34s: "Useful is not the same as used."

**[VISUAL] direction:** Open on a full entryway basket tipped out: three umbrellas, a wad of bags, old menus, a dead cable, a spare key. On the question, one umbrella and one bag get a green dot; the rest get a small honey or slate mark and slide toward an out box.

## SHORT 2: Useful is not the same as used
**Topic: the core reframe. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** The hardest clutter to see is not the junk.

**Script:**
The hardest clutter to see is not the junk. Junk is easy, you spot it and release it. The hard stuff is the useful things you never actually use, because every one of them has a good reason to stay. So here is the line that sorts them. Useful is not the same as used. Useful is a claim about the future: this could do something for me someday. Used is a fact about the past: this actually did something for me, recently, in the real world. One is a story you tell about an object. The other is a record of what it has done. Keep the record. Let the story go.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "The hardest clutter isn't the junk"
- 10s: "It's the useful stuff you never use"
- 20s: "Useful = a claim about the future"
- 28s: "Used = a fact about the past"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Split screen. Left, labeled "useful," a thought bubble of someday scenarios. Right, labeled "used," a hand actually reaching for the thing. Warm light, calm and unhurried.

## SHORT 3: The one-object pass
**Topic: the Quick Win, lowest-stakes start. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You can run a real pass in the next sixty seconds.

**Script:**
You can run a real pass in the next sixty seconds. You do not need a free weekend or a single bin. Here is the whole move. Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case. Ask one question. When did you last use it? If you cannot remember, put it in an out box today. That is it. And the out box is not the trash and not the donation pile, it is just a set-aside spot where the thing waits while you finish. You decide today. You route it later. One honest release is a full pass.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A real pass in 60 seconds"
- 10s: "Pick up ONE just-in-case thing"
- 20s: "When did you last use it?"
- 30s: "One honest release is a full pass"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Real-time, single take feel. Person opens a drawer, lifts one clearly unused item, pauses on the question, sets it into a plain box by the door. Simple and calm.

## SHORT 4: Do not keep the worse duplicate
**Topic: the Common Mistake, duplicates and surplus. Runtime ~38 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You are keeping the bad one in case the good one breaks. Stop.

**Script:**
You are keeping the worse one in case the good one breaks. The dull scissors, the umbrella that sticks, the second peeler. And you are keeping the good one too, so now you own two things to do one job, and the bad one sits in the drawer for a decade guarding against a failure that costs almost nothing to fix. Here is the rule. If two things do one job, one of them is surplus, and it is almost always the one you never reach for. So keep the one your hand goes to. Let the understudy retire. Necessary quietly includes a sensible quantity: one good umbrella, not three; a handful of bags, not forty.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Keeping the bad one 'just in case'?"
- 12s: "Now you own two things for one job"
- 24s: "If two do one job, one is surplus"
- 32s: "Keep the one your hand goes to"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A drawer with two pairs of scissors, one good, one dull. The good pair gets a green dot. The dull pair is lifted out to an out box. Then a row of five identical tote bags collapses to one kept, four arrowed away.

## SHORT 5: Letting go is not wasting
**Topic: the anti-shame, sunk-cost reframe. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** You are keeping it because it cost money. That is costing you more.

**Script:**
You are keeping it because it cost money. And that is exactly why the pass stalls. Your hand starts toward the out box, a small voice says "but it was not free," and the thing goes back to keep costing you quietly for another year. So look straight at the arithmetic. The money is already gone. It went the day you bought it, and storing the thing unopened will not return a cent. Keeping it only adds a second cost on top: the room it takes and the attention it draws. Holding an unused thing to honor the money you spent is like leaving a light on in an empty room to get your money's worth from the bulb. The waste, if any, already happened. Letting go is the end of paying twice.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Keeping it because it cost money?"
- 12s: "The money is already gone"
- 24s: "Keeping it adds a second cost"
- 34s: "Letting go ends paying twice"

**[VISUAL] direction:** A hand hovers over an unused item, hesitates on the guilt tug, then steadies and sets it calmly in the out box. Optional insert: a lamp glowing in an empty room. Warm, unhurried.
