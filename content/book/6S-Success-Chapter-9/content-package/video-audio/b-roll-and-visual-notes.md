# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

**Chapter 9, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** necessary-vs-unnecessary
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- Green calm-dots glow on objects that get used (they are necessary and stay).
- A small honey or slate mark sits on objects kept just in case (they are unnecessary and go to the out box).
- The Use Test is the recurring device: one object passes a gate that reads "when did you last use it?", branching up to necessary (green, keep) and down to unnecessary (slate, the out box), with a honey side gate for the hard cases.
- The out box is a set-aside spot, a plain box or bag or corner. It is never a trash can, a donation bin, or a sale table. Nothing is disposed of on camera this chapter.
- The friction meter MOVES again this chapter, a second time, but by a visibly smaller step than last chapter. Chapter 8's needle position becomes the faint dashed ghost; the live needle sits a little further toward calm; the GOAL crosshair stays ahead on the green side.
- Warm morning light, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.
- Nothing is bought and nothing is cleaned on camera. The only thing that happens is that some objects move to an out box.

## CORE SHOT LIST

### Shot 1: The cleared console, then the full basket beside it (hero shot)
- The entryway console cleared last chapter, still clear, holding only a keys tray, a leash, an umbrella, a bag, and tomorrow's mail.
- The camera tips down to the floor beside it, where a full basket has been catching years of overflow: three umbrellas, a wad of folded tote bags, old takeout menus, a tangled dead cable, a lone spare key on a faded fob.
- Critical detail: one umbrella and one bag wear a green calm-dot; most of the rest wear a small honey or slate mark. It all belongs at an entryway; almost none of it gets used.
- Warm morning light. Slow, deliberate camera move from clear surface to full basket.
- Coverage: one wide of console and basket together, one macro on the tipped basket mouth, one push-in on the green-dotted keeper among the marked surplus.
- **On-screen text moment:** "The surface is clear. The basket that fed it is not."
- **Lower third:** Chapter 9 · Necessary vs. Unnecessary

### Shot 2: The Use Test gate (hero device)
- One object that stayed enters a gate. On the gate: the question. It branches up into a green zone, "used it recently, necessary, keep it," and down into a slate zone, "cannot remember, unnecessary, the out box." A honey dashed side gate reads, "for the hard cases."
- This is the operational heart of the chapter: one question about the past, two outcomes, plus a quiet tiebreaker for the few that will not answer.
- **On-screen text moment:** "The Use Test: when did you last use it?"
- **Lower third:** The Use Test: when did you last use it?

### Shot 3: Necessary versus unnecessary, reading behavior not quality
- Close on a hand holding the good umbrella, then opening it in one clean motion: a fast, specific memory of use. Green dot. It stays.
- Cut to the same hand holding an extra umbrella that will not open easily and clearly has not been touched in a year. No memory comes. It goes toward the out box.
- The point: same kind of object, opposite verdict, decided entirely by whether you use it.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Necessary means you use it." then "Unnecessary means you only kept it just in case."
- **Lower third:** A reading of your behavior, not the object

### Shot 4: The pass through the basket (worked example)
- A compact two-column layout of the basket's contents, or a real tabletop split into two groups by hand.
- Left, "necessary, keep" in green, each with a calm-dot: one umbrella (the one you grab), one or two reusable bags, the keys, the leash.
- Right, "unnecessary, out box" in slate, each with an out-arrow: two extra umbrellas, the wad of surplus tote bags, a spare key to a car you sold, a fan of old takeout menus, a dead charging cable.
- Footer caption available: "It all belonged here. Most of it still goes."
- **On-screen text moment:** "Necessary: keep" (green) / "Unnecessary: out box" (slate)
- **Lower third:** A pass through the basket

### Shot 5: Duplicates and surplus
- A row of five identical tote bags. The first gets a green dot and stays. The other four are arrowed, calmly, to the out box.
- Optional insert: two pairs of scissors, one good with a green dot, one dull lifted out; the "understudy" retiring.
- Sell the rule: if two things do one job, one of them is surplus, usually the one you never reach for.
- **On-screen text moment:** "You need one, not five." then "Keep your favorite. Retire the rest."
- **Lower third:** Duplicates and surplus: keep your favorite

### Shot 6: The out box is a set-aside spot
- A hand sets a few unnecessary items into a single plain box in a corner. Then a quick beat that makes the fence clear: the box is not carried to a trash can, not to a car for donation. It just sits and waits.
- Shows the discipline: decide today, route later. Where the box goes from here is a later chapter.
- **On-screen text moment:** "The out box is a set-aside spot." then "Decide today. Route later."
- **Lower third:** The out box: a set-aside spot, not a destination

### Shot 7: Letting go is not wasting (the anti-shame beat)
- A hand lifts an unused item toward the out box, pauses on the guilt tug as if remembering what it cost, then steadies and sets it down calmly.
- Optional insert: a lamp glowing in an empty room, for "leaving a light on to get your money's worth from the bulb."
- The tension is the sunk-cost pull and its release. The money is already gone; keeping the thing only charges rent in space and attention.
- **On-screen text moment:** "The money was already spent." then "Letting go is the end of paying twice."
- **Lower third:** Letting go is not wasting

### Shot 8: The maybes wait in their own spot
- A hand sets one genuinely undecidable object apart, on its own, separate from the clear releases, then keeps moving to the next easy call.
- Shows the discipline: do not force a maybe, park it, finish the many easy yeses and noes. It gets a system of its own next chapter.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Cannot call it? Set it apart." then "An honest maybe is normal, not a failure."
- **Lower third:** Park the maybe · finish the easy calls

### Shot 9: The friction meter moves again, a smaller step (close)
- The recurring friction meter. The live needle steps a little further toward calm than last chapter, a visibly shorter move than the first.
- Chapter 8's needle position is now a faint dashed ghost just behind the live needle, so the second, smaller move is unmistakable.
- The GOAL crosshair still sits ahead on the green calm side, carrying its purpose tag, clearly still ahead of where the needle now rests.
- Emphasize honesty: a smaller clearing earns a smaller step. This is not the meter losing steam; it is the meter being accurate.
- **On-screen text moment:** "A second step, shorter than the first." then "Still within the first S, still short of the goal."
- **Lower third:** The needle moves again · a smaller, honest step

## EDITING NOTES

- This is the second action chapter, and a quieter win than the first. The first pass made the change you could see across the room; this one makes the change you feel when you open the basket and it does not sigh back at you. Let the cut breathe.
- Keep the tone calm and non-prescriptive. The whole move is subtraction, done without spending a cent and without deciding anyone was foolish.
- Do show objects moving to the out box this chapter. But never show anything thrown in the trash, donated, or sold. Where the out box goes from here is a later chapter; this cut only sets things aside.
- Never show a maybe being forced into a yes or no. Show it parked on its own, calmly, as normal residue of a careful pass.
- Avoid any force or violence framing in visuals: no dramatic sweeping-off of surfaces, no aggressive dumping, no "purge" montage. Objects are lifted and carried, calmly, to a set-aside spot.
- The friction-meter move in Shot 9 must read as a smaller step than Chapter 8's. If it looks as big or bigger, it is wrong. Keep Chapter 8's ghost needle visible so the shorter travel is obvious. The GOAL crosshair stays ahead.
- Color and warmth should match Chapters 1 through 8: warm paper tones, terracotta and honey accents, slate for any data overlays and for the out box, green for the calm-dots on things that get used.
