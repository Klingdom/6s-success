# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (useful is not the same as used), then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad.

---

Okay, the decluttering reframe that finally got me past the stuff I "might need someday," and then I want to hear what is hiding in your basket.

For years I thought the mess by my door was a tidying problem. Turns out the surface was fine. The problem was the basket underneath it, full of things that all belonged there and never got used. Three umbrellas, a wad of tote bags, a dead cable, a key to a car I sold two summers ago. Every single one of them belongs near a front door. I just never touch them.

That is the trap. Almost everything you own is useful in some imaginable situation, so "could I use this?" keeps everything. The question that actually sorts is narrower: when did you last use it? Fast, specific memory means it stays. A shrug means it was only useful in theory.

The line that made it click for me: useful is not the same as used. Useful is a story you tell about an object. Used is a record of what it has actually done.

And the part that took the guilt out. Letting go of what you do not use is not wasting. The money was spent when you bought it. Keeping it unused does not earn that back, it just charges you rent in space and attention. Once I stopped treating it as admitting I wasted money, I actually finished for once.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about:

What is in your version of the basket? The stuff that absolutely belongs where it is, but that you honestly cannot remember the last time you used? And when you ask "when did I last use it," what stays and what quietly goes in the out box?

I will go first in the comments with my three umbrellas. 😄
