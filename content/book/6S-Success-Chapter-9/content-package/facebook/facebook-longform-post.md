# Facebook Longform Post: The Useful Stuff You Never Actually Use

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 9 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA to run a one-object Use Test. No em dashes.

---

I cleared the surface by my front door months ago, and here is the surprising part: it stayed clear. But there was still a basket on the floor catching the overflow, and if I am honest, I knew exactly what was in it.

Three umbrellas, one of which I ever open. A thick wad of folded tote bags. A spare key to a car I sold two summers ago. A fan of takeout menus from before my phone knew every restaurant in town. A tangled, dead charging cable for a phone I replaced ages ago.

Here is what stopped me. Every one of those things belongs by a front door. Umbrellas, bags, keys, cables. Put any of them to a stranger and they would nod, yes, that lives near the entryway. So they all passed the "does it belong here" test. And I had not touched most of them in a year.

That is the harder question. Not where a thing lives, but whether you actually use it. Because almost everything you own is useful in some imaginable situation, and that is exactly the trap. When the test is whether a thing could ever be useful, the test passes everything.

So try a sharper one, asked object by object:

When did you last use it?

If the answer comes fast, this week, last month, the last time it rained, it stays. If you have to invent a scenario rather than remember a real one, it was only useful in theory, and it goes in a set-aside box for now.

The line that makes this stick: useful is not the same as used. Useful is a story you tell about an object. Used is a record of what it has done.

And letting go is not wasting. The money was spent the day you bought it. Keeping the thing unused does not earn a cent of that back. It only charges you rent in space and attention.

So here is your low-pressure homework for the comments, if you want it. Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case and ask when you last used it. If you cannot remember, set it aside today. One honest release is a full pass.
