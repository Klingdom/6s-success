# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## Necessary vs. Unnecessary

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 9. On-slide copy, ready to set. 12 slides. This is the copy-ready, post-ready version, consistent with linkedin-carousel-outline.md.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, soft #6A625A, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**The clutter you cannot see is the useful kind.**

You cleared the surface. The basket beside it is still full of things that all belong here and never get used.

Swipe for the one question that finds them.

[VISUAL] The entryway basket tipped open beside the now-clear console. A couple of items wear a green calm-dot, the rest wear a small honey or red mark. Warm paper, slate chapter label.

---

### Slide 2

**Everything left belongs here.**

That is not the same as needing it.

The last chapter asked where a thing lives. This one asks whether you use it at all.

[VISUAL] The clear console above holding its five working things, the still-full basket below. A soft slate line between them: "belonging held. use is the next question."

---

### Slide 3

**A test that passes everything is not a test.**

Almost anything is useful in some imaginable circumstance. So "could this ever be useful" keeps everything.

You need a narrower question.

[VISUAL] A basket where a few crisp items sit at the front and a soft crowd of greyed "just in case" items fills the space behind. Slate label: "THE SENSIBLE SURPLUS."

---

### Slide 4

**Useful is not the same as used.**

Useful is a claim about the future. Used is a fact about the past.

One is a story you tell about an object. The other is a record of what it has done.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. Two small tags: "future claim" and "past fact."

---

### Slide 5

**One question tests what stayed.**

When did you last use it?

A fast, specific memory means necessary, and it stays. A shrug means unnecessary, and it goes in the out box.

[VISUAL] The Use Test gate. An object flows into "When did you last use it?" and splits: up into a green "necessary · keep it" zone, down into a slate "unnecessary · the out box" zone.

---

### Slide 6

**Necessary and unnecessary describe you, not the object.**

Necessary means you use it, or would replace it if it vanished.

Unnecessary means it is only kept just in case. Neither judges the object's quality.

[VISUAL] Definition card on panel with a slate border. The two words stacked, a green calm-dot beside "necessary," a slate marker beside "unnecessary."

---

### Slide 7

**Stuck on a hard one?**

If it vanished today, would you buy it again?

It strips away the sunk cost. An easy yes keeps it. A slow no is your answer.

[VISUAL] A small honey side gate branching off the main flow with a dashed line, reading "If it vanished today, would you buy it again?" Label: "FOR THE HARD CASES."

---

### Slide 8

**You do not need three umbrellas.**

You need the one you actually grab. Necessary quietly includes a sensible quantity.

Keep your favorite and let the rest go.

[VISUAL] A row of five identical tote bags. The first kept with a green calm-dot, labeled "keep your favorite." The other four arrowed to a slate out box.

---

### Slide 9

**The spare that guards nothing.**

If two things do one job, one of them is unnecessary, usually the one you never reach for.

Keep the one your hand goes to. Let the understudy retire.

[VISUAL] Two pairs of scissors: the good one with a green calm-dot, the worse one arrowed to the slate out box. Caption: "one job, one keeper."

---

### Slide 10

**Decide today, route later.**

The out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination. Where things go from there is a later chapter.

Today you decide. Later you route.

[VISUAL] A plain slate out box holding the released items, calm and set aside. A small teaser: "where it goes: a later chapter." No trash, no donation, no sale.

---

### Slide 11

**The money was spent the day you bought it.**

Keeping the thing unused does not earn it back.

The waste, if any, already happened. Letting go is the end of paying twice.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The anti-shame reframe set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below. No sparks or dots; slate does the work.

---
