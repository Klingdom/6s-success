# Visual Storyboard

## Necessary vs. Unnecessary

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 9. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

---

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_09_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper #F7F2E9: slide and card backgrounds
- Panel #FBF7EF: card fill, definition and idea boxes
- Ink #2B2622: primary text, object outlines
- Soft #6A625A: captions, secondary axis labels
- Terracotta #BC4B2A: primary accent, chapter kicker
- Honey #DDA63A: secondary accent, the middle of the meter, the just-in-case marks, the honest maybe, the hard-case side gate
- Slate #3C5A6B: chapter labels, the gate border, the GOAL flag, the unnecessary side, the out box
- Green calm-dot #6E8B5B: necessary, the things you actually use
- Friction spark #CB4B36: the friction side of the meter, and the never-used marks in the opener
- Ghost needle #B9AC96: Chapter 8's old needle position, dashed
- Rules and hairlines #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8: dividers, dashed lines
- Display font Fraunces: titles, pull quotes, the gate question, the two column headers
- Body font Newsreader: worked pass, captions, running text
- Label font Inter: uppercase, wide letter-spacing, for kickers, tags, axis labels

**The recurring motif.** A green calm-dot #6E8B5B marks what is necessary, the things you actually use and keep. Slate #3C5A6B marks what is unnecessary and goes to the out box. This green-and-slate pairing carries the chapter's argument visually; keep it consistent on every beat. Honey #DDA63A stays warm and neutral, never a verdict: it carries the middle of the meter, the just-in-case marks in the opener, the hard-case side gate, and the one honest maybe. The red spark #CB4B36 belongs to the friction side of the meter and to the never-used marks in the opener only.

**One fence to hold visually.** The out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination. Never draw a trash can, a donation truck, or a sale table on it. Where things go from there is Chapter 11.

## Beat 1 · The basket, tipped open

The chapter and both decks open the same way. The entryway basket beside the now-clear console, tipped open with its contents fanned out.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm morning light. Above, the console holds only its five working things (keys tray, leash, umbrella, bag, mail), each with a small green calm-dot. Below on the floor, the basket is tipped open, its contents spread: three umbrellas, a wad of tote bags, a fan of old takeout menus, a tangled dead cable, a lone spare key.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, ink #2B2622 outlines. Green #6E8B5B calm-dots on the one umbrella and one bag that get used. Honey #DDA63A marks on the spare key and the surplus bags; red spark #CB4B36 marks on the extra umbrellas, the menus, and the dead cable.
- Motif: everything here belongs at an entryway. The dots and marks say which ones actually get used. Keep the tagging clear: a couple of green, the rest honey or spark.
- Caption line, from the book: "The surface is clear. The basket that kept feeding it is not. Everything in it belongs at an entryway. Almost none of it gets used."

## Beat 2 · Useful is not used

The core insight, given the book's pull-quote treatment before the tool arrives.

- Composition: the pull-quote card. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "Useful is not the same as used."
- Beneath it, two small Inter tags side by side: "USEFUL: a claim about the future" and "USED: a fact about the past."
- Palette: no dots, no sparks. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the sentence you write on the fridge.
- Point: usefulness passes everything, so it is not a test. Used is the honest, narrower reading.

## Beat 3 · The clutter you cannot see

Name the just-in-case pattern before running the tool on it.

- Composition: the basket seen from above. A few crisp, ink-outlined items sit at the front, the things you reach for. Behind them, a soft crowd of faintly greyed items fills the space, each defensible, each unused.
- Palette: green #6E8B5B calm-dots on the few at the front, honey #DDA63A tags on the crowd behind, soft #6A625A greying on the crowd, paper ground.
- Motif: the surplus is sensible, not junk. The greying does the work; no red spark here. Slate #3C5A6B label: "THE SENSIBLE SURPLUS."
- Small Inter caption: "kept for a someday that rarely comes."

## Beat 4 · The Use Test gate

The chapter's hero device and the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: one object (an umbrella) enters on the left along a slate arrow into a central gate. The gate is a rounded panel #FBF7EF with a 3px slate #3C5A6B border reading, in Fraunces, "When did you last use it?" with a small Inter line beneath, "recently · or cannot say."
- USED branch: a green #6E8B5B path curves up to a pale green zone #E8EFE0 labeled "USED IT RECENTLY," carrying a green calm-dot and the words "necessary" (Fraunces) and "keep it" (Newsreader italic).
- CANNOT REMEMBER branch: a slate #3C5A6B path curves down to a zone #EEF1F3 labeled "CANNOT REMEMBER," carrying the words "unnecessary" (Fraunces) and "the out box" (Newsreader italic).
- Footer, Newsreader italic soft #6A625A: "One question about the past, not the maybe."
- This is the clearest single frame of the necessary-and-unnecessary logic. Everything else echoes it. Do not paraphrase "necessary," "unnecessary," or "the out box."

## Beat 5 · For the hard cases

The tiebreaker that settles the few that will not answer. It hangs off the gate, not a new flow.

- Composition: a small honey #DDA63A side gate branching down off the main question with a dashed honey line. A rounded panel #FBF3E0 with a honey border reads "FOR THE HARD CASES" in Inter and, in Newsreader italic, "If it vanished today, would you buy it again?"
- Palette: honey #DDA63A for the whole side gate, paper ground. Honey because this is a waypoint for a genuinely hard call, not a verdict.
- Motif: it strips away the sunk cost. An easy yes keeps it; a slow no is the answer. Keep the branch visibly secondary to the main gate, a quiet tiebreaker.

## Beat 8 · The out box, and the honest maybe

Where the unnecessary things wait, and how to hold a genuine maybe without pre-empting Chapter 10.

- Composition: a plain slate #3C5A6B out box holding the released items, calm and set out of the flow. A small Inter teaser in soft grey: "where it goes: a later chapter."
- Off to one side, a single honey #DDA63A "maybe," drawn as one tidy item set apart, not a pile, with an Inter teaser "its own system next chapter." Do not build the system here.
- Palette: paper ground, ink items, slate on the out box, honey only on the one maybe.
- Caption idea: "Today you decide. Later you route."
- Fence: no trash, no donation, no sale. The out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination.

## Beat 9 · Letting go is not wasting

The anti-shame center of the chapter. A pause beat, restrained.

- Composition: the book's pull-quote treatment. Panel #FBF7EF, slate #3C5A6B rules above and below, the line in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "Letting go of what you do not use is not wasteful. The money was spent when you bought it, and keeping it unused does not earn that back. It only charges you rent in space and attention."
- Palette: no sparks, no dots. Slate does the work. This beat should feel like the room going quiet.
- Tone note: the point is permission, not shame. Letting go is not a confession; the reason you bought it simply passed. Keep the frame light.

## Beat 10 · The needle moves again

The landmark payoff, and the close of both decks. Rebuild from the HTML SVG, and make the second, smaller step unmistakable.

- Composition: the semicircular friction meter. The arc runs green #6E8B5B (CALM) on the left, honey #DDA63A in the middle, red spark #CB4B36 (FRICTION) on the right.
- The move: the live needle sits a little further toward calm than last chapter, pointing up, labeled NOW in ink #2B2622. Chapter 8's needle position is now a faint dashed ghost in #B9AC96 just behind it, tagged "Ch. 8 was here," so the shorter second step is legible.
- The GOAL marker: a slate #3C5A6B crosshair on the calm side, still ahead of the needle, carrying its slate PURPOSE flag.
- Footer caption, verbatim: "The needle takes a second step, shorter than the first. Sort already removed the big surplus of what did not belong; this pass clears the smaller surplus of what you do not use. Still within the first S, still short of the goal."
- Motif close: the needle moves again because objects moved again, and the step is shorter because this pass cleared a smaller surplus. Honest, not triumphant. Still within the first S, still short of the goal.

## Carrying it across the set

- Open every sequence with the basket tipped open; close every sequence with the moved friction meter taking its second step. The two bookend the second Sort chapter.
- Use Fraunces only for the load-bearing lines: titles, the gate question, the pull quotes, the two column headers. Keep running copy in Newsreader so the slides breathe.
- Never let honey signal correctness. Necessary is green, unnecessary is slate, and that two-color logic should be legible even in a thumbnail. Honey carries only neutral waypoints: the middle of the meter, the just-in-case marks, the hard-case side gate, and the one honest maybe.
- The frozen outcome words "necessary," "unnecessary," and "the out box" appear on the gate, the duplicates sweep, and the two-column figure. Copy them exactly.
- Keep generous paper margins and a single idea per frame. The chapter's voice is calm, concrete, and anti-shame; the visuals should feel the same, decided but never loud, with no force or hype imagery.
