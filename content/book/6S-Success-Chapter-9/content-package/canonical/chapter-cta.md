# Chapter 9 Calls to Action: Necessary vs. Unnecessary

This is the second Sort chapter. Its strongest CTA is running the Use Test on what the first pass left behind, even on a single object. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive, anti-shame tone: letting go of what you do not use is not wasting, and it is not a confession that buying it was a mistake.

## Primary CTA
Run the Use Test. "Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case and ask when you last used it. If you cannot remember, put it in an out box today. Used it recently, or you would replace it, means it stays. One honest release is a full pass. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Everything left after your first Sort belongs here. That is not the same as needing it. Chapter 9 gives you one question that keeps what you use and sets the just-in-case surplus aside, without the guilt. Read it free online, then Chapter 10 handles the things you honestly cannot decide."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Pick up one thing you have been keeping just in case. Ask when you last used it. If you cannot remember, put it in an out box today. One honest release is a full necessary pass.
- **Family Challenge:** Everyone finds one duplicate, something you own several of and need one of, and keeps their favorite. The rest go in the out box. No voting on anyone else's duplicates.

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the brief)
- **The Use Test card:** a printable and phone wallpaper of the one question, "useful is not the same as used," and the "would you buy it again?" follow-up, for quick reference on any space.
- **The Necessary vs. Unnecessary sheet:** a printable with two columns, what you actually use and what you were keeping just in case, so you can run a clean pass on one space.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is running the Use Test, however small. Lead with "ask when you last used it," which is concrete and can be done in minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive and low-stakes. The out box is a set-aside spot, not a destination; the reader decides, and routes later.
- "Useful is not the same as used" and "letting go is not wasting" are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype and any force or violence framing. The whole move is subtraction, done without spending a cent and without deciding anyone was foolish.
