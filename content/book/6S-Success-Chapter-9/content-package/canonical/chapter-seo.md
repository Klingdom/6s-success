## Search intent
Decision-oriented, with an undertone of guilt. Readers arriving here have already cleared the obvious clutter and are now stuck on things that all seem useful and defensible, and many feel that letting go wastes the money they spent. The chapter answers with a single, concrete decision rule (the Use Test, when did you last use it), a tiebreaker for hard cases (would you buy it again), and a sunk-cost reframe that lowers the emotional stakes (letting go is not wasting, the money was already spent). The "useful is not the same as used" framing captures the exact confusion that keeps just-in-case clutter for years.

## Content gaps this page fills (vs typical search results)
- Separates "useful" from "used", the exact distinction most declutter advice blurs
- Gives one repeatable question (when did you last use it) instead of a vague "do you love it" prompt
- Names the just-in-case trap and the duplicates-and-surplus pattern explicitly
- Offers a sunk-cost reframe (letting go is not wasting) that most posts skip, defusing the guilt that stalls a pass
- Separates deciding a thing is unnecessary from deciding its fate (decide today, route later), so a quick pass stays quick
- Treats the honest maybe as normal residue, not a failure, and points it to a later system
