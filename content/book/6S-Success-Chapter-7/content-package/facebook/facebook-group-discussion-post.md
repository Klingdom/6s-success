# Facebook Group Discussion Post (Home and Organizing Groups)

Written for a home-and-organizing Facebook group. Leads with a genuinely useful nugget (a space is allowed to have one job), then asks an open question to get people talking. Friendly group tone, soft CTA at the very end, and written so it does NOT read like an ad.

---

A small organizing idea that finally calmed down my worst clutter spot, and I want to hear yours.

For years I thought my entryway table was just messy and that I was just bad at keeping it clear. Then I read something that flipped it for me: a space is not failing because it is messy. It is messy because it is failing to be any one thing.

My table had quietly taken on five jobs. Mail station, key drop, charging hub, homework pile, and the home of a candle nobody has ever lit. None of those jobs was wrong. The problem was asking one little table to do all five at once. They fight each other. The mail buries the keys, and nothing has room to be done well.

The thing that actually helped was permission, not a product. A space is allowed to have one job. Pick the one that matters most, let the other jobs find homes elsewhere, and suddenly the space starts working. Mine became a launch pad, just keys and a bag basket, and the mail moved to the kitchen where I actually deal with it.

So here is what I am genuinely curious about:

What space in your home is secretly trying to do five jobs at once? And if you had to pick just one real job for it, what would you keep and what would you kick out?

I will go first in the comments with my embarrassing candle table. Tell me yours, the worse the better. 😄

(If anyone wants the "give a space one job" idea written out properly, it is from Chapter 7 of a book called 6S Success: Home Edition, free to read online. Not selling anything, it is just the most useful reframe I have hit in a while and I had to bring it here.)
