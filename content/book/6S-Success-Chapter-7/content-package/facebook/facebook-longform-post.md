# Facebook Longform Post: The Everything-Space and the One-Job Fix

A longer, story-driven post from Chapter 7 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Conversational, like talking to a friend. Ends with a soft CTA to write your space's one job in a sentence. No em dashes.

---

Let me tell you about the most overworked spot in your house.

It is the little drop zone by your door. The console table, or the counter, or the bench where everything lands the second you walk in. And here is the thing nobody ever told it: that spot is doing five full-time jobs at once.

It is the mail station. It is the key depot. It is the phone charging hub. It is the homework landing pad. And it is the home of one decorative object somebody bought with good intentions and nobody has actually looked at in about a year.

Nobody ever decided any of this. It just happened, one reasonable item at a time, the way these things always do. And now those five jobs are quietly at war with each other. The mail buries the keys. The chargers tangle into the bag straps. There is never quite enough room to do any single thing well, because the space is trying to do everything at once.

So we look at that pile and we think, this space is a mess. But that is not really the problem. The space is not failing because it is messy. It is messy because it is failing to be any one thing. You handed it five jobs and then got frustrated that it was not great at all of them.

Here is the fix, and it is gentler than you would expect. You do not need a smarter system for all five jobs. You just need to admit that one small space cannot do five jobs, pick the one that matters most, and let the other four go find homes of their own.

For most entryways, the real job is being a launch pad. The place that gets everyone out the door fast in the morning, and lands them softly on the way back in. Keys belong there. A bag basket per person, maybe. But the mail does not help anybody leave the house, so mail sorting becomes its own little job somewhere else. The charging hub moves to where the phones actually spend the night.

And here is how you lock it in. You write one plain sentence:

This space is for one main job, so that an outcome you actually want.

Like this: This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic.

That sentence does a quiet amount of work. It names the job, and it names the reason the job matters, which keeps you honest. And once it exists, you can hold any object in the world up against it. The keys serve the launch pad. The three weeks of mail do not. The space basically sorts itself, and you have not moved a thing yet.

One small rule: keep it to one primary job, with one supporting job at most. If your sentence needs three "ands," it is still trying to do too much.

So here is your no-pressure homework for the comments, if you want it. Pick the busiest little space in your home and write its one job in a single sentence. This space is for ___, so that ___. That is the whole thing.
