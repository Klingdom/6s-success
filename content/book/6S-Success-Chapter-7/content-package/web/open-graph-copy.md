# Open Graph and Twitter Card Copy: Chapter 7, Define the Purpose of the Area

Two variants. Use the og:image concept noted under each.

## Variant A (the one-job hook)

- og:title: Give a Room One Job Before You Sort It
- og:description: A space cannot be good at everything. Decide its one job in a single honest sentence, and every keep-or-go decision that follows gets fast and calm.
- twitter:title: Give a Room One Job Before You Sort It
- twitter:description: Most messy spaces were never given a job. Write one sentence that names theirs, and the sorting nearly does itself.
- og:image concept: The "One Job Beats Five" split. Left side, a drop zone tagged with five colliding jobs (mail, keys, charging, homework, decor) under the words "Good at nothing." Right side, the same space with one tag, "Launch pad," under "Good at one thing." Caption line: A space cannot be good at everything.

## Variant B (the Purpose Statement tool)

- og:title: One Sentence That Sorts a Space for You
- og:description: The Purpose Statement is a fill-in-the-blank sentence: this space is for one job, so that an outcome you want. Fill the two blanks and the space sorts itself.
- twitter:title: One Sentence That Sorts a Space for You
- twitter:description: This space is for ___, so that ___. Two blanks, five minutes, and every later decision gets easy. From Chapter 7 of 6S Success: Home Edition.
- og:image concept: The Purpose Statement template card. A clean card showing the fill-in-the-blank sentence "This [space] is for [one primary job], so that [an outcome you actually want]," with the worked entryway example below it: "This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic."
