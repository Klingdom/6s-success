# Landing Page Intro: Chapter 7, Define the Purpose of the Area

Stop and try to say, in one sentence, what the messiest spot in your home is actually for. The drop zone by your door, maybe. It was never decided to be anything. It just became the place where mail lands, and keys land, and chargers live, and homework gets dumped, all at once, none of it chosen, all of it competing. That space is not failing because it is messy. It is messy because it is failing to be any one thing.

This is the everything-space, one small surface asked to do five jobs and good at none of them. And here is the quiet reason so much organizing falls apart a few weeks later: people sort the stuff before they decide the job. So every item becomes a fresh judgment call with no yardstick, the decisions wear you out, and the clutter creeps back.

The fix takes about five minutes and you will not move a single object. You give the space one job, written in one honest sentence: this space is for ___, so that ___. That sentence becomes the rule every later keep-or-go decision is measured against, which makes the sorting almost automatic.
