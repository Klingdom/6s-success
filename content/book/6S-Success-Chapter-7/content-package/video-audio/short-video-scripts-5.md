## SHORT 1: Why your space is actually a mess
**Topic: messy = no one job. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Your drop zone is not messy because you are messy.

**Script:**
Your drop zone is not messy because you are messy. It is messy because it is trying to be five things at once. It is the mail station, and the key spot, and the charging hub, and the homework pile, and the home of that bowl nobody has touched in a year. Each of those is a fine job. The problem is one little space doing all five at the same time. They fight. The mail buries the keys. So before you sort anything, ask one question. What is this space actually for?

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Not messy because you're messy"
- 8s: "Mail. Keys. Charging. Homework. Decor."
- 20s: "Five jobs at once = good at none"
- 32s: "A space is messy because it's failing to be any ONE thing"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Open on a cluttered entryway console. Quick cut-ins on each collision as you name the five jobs. End on a slow hold of the whole mess with the closing text over it.

## SHORT 2: One job beats five
**Topic: the one-job rule. Runtime ~35 seconds.**

**Hook line:** A space cannot be good at everything. Stop asking it to be.

**Script:**
A space cannot be good at everything. Stop asking it to be. Excellence at one thing requires not being mediocre at four others. So pick the one job that matters most and let the other four go find spaces of their own. For most entryways, the real job is being a launch pad. The place that gets everyone out the door fast in the morning. Keys, yes. A bag per person, sure. But the mail does not help you leave the house, so mail sorting lives somewhere else. The moment a space specializes, it starts working.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "A space can't be good at everything"
- 10s: "One clear job. One supporting job at most."
- 22s: "Entryway = launch pad"
- 30s: "Specialize, and it finally works"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Split screen. Left side cluttered with five floating job labels and red friction-sparks, captioned "good at nothing." Right side the same space, calm and open, green calm-dots, one label, captioned "good at one thing."

## SHORT 3: Write the one sentence
**Topic: write the Purpose Statement. Runtime ~45 seconds.**

**Hook line:** This one sentence will sort your space before you touch a thing.

**Script:**
This one sentence will sort your whole space before you touch a thing. Here is the shape. This space is for one main job, so that some outcome you actually want. First blank is the job. Second blank is why the job matters. Here is mine for the drop zone. This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so mornings start calm instead of frantic. Now hold any object up to it. Keys serve it. The basket serves it. Three weeks of mail does not serve it at all. The sentence just sorted the space for you. You are not writing poetry. A plain true sentence beats a pretty vague one.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "One sentence. Then sorting gets easy."
- 8s: "This space is for ___, so that ___"
- 22s: "...so mornings start calm instead of frantic"
- 36s: "A plain true sentence beats a pretty vague one"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Hand writing on a small card on the console by the still-cluttered drop zone. Fill the template card on screen as you say it. Hold objects up to the written card, a clear yes, a clear no.

## SHORT 4: Stop organizing for a home you don't have
**Topic: match your real life. Runtime ~40 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Stop organizing for the home you wish you had.

**Script:**
Stop organizing for the home you wish you had. It is so easy, sitting with a pen, to decide your entryway is a serene minimal space with one elegant tray and a little sprig in a vase. Lovely. Also a complete fantasy for four people, a dog, and a sports schedule. A purpose built on that fantasy will be wrong every single morning, because every backpack and muddy shoe becomes an intruder. So define the space for the people who actually live there. Picture your worst day, the frantic Tuesday, the rainy game day. If your purpose only holds when everyone behaves like a catalog model, it is the wrong purpose.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "Stop organizing for a home you don't have"
- 12s: "The minimal vignette is a fantasy"
- 25s: "Define it for your REAL household"
- 34s: "Does it hold on the frantic Tuesday?"

**[VISUAL] direction:** Side by side. Left, an aspirational empty entryway, one tray, a vase. Right, the same space holding four bags and muddy shoes, working hard. Cut to the real one when you say "define it for the people who actually live there."

## SHORT 5: Install a bouncer at the door
**Topic: purpose sets the limits. Runtime ~38 seconds.**

**Hook line:** Here is how to keep a space tidy after you tidy it.

**Script:**
Here is how to keep a space tidy long after you tidy it. Most spaces are not ruined in one dramatic collapse. They erode, one reasonable stray object at a time. A clear purpose fixes that, because once a space has one job, you know exactly how much belongs there. Exactly what the job needs, and not one thing more. The purpose becomes a bouncer at the door. When something new drifts in, the purpose asks it one question. Do you serve the job? If the answer is no, it gets gently turned away before it can move in and start the slow slide back to chaos.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT] beats:**
- 0s: "How to keep a space tidy AFTER you tidy it"
- 10s: "Spaces erode one stray object at a time"
- 22s: "Purpose = a bouncer at the door"
- 32s: "Does this serve the job? No? Turned away."

**[VISUAL] direction:** A clean, working launch pad, keys on a hook, one basket per person, clear empty room. A hand reaches in to set down a random object, then pulls it back. Stamp the "bouncer" text over the doorway.
