# Teleprompter Script: Define the Purpose of the Area

**Chapter 7, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** define-the-purpose-of-the-area
**Length:** about 640 words. Narration only. Short lines for easy reading.

---

[Read at a calm, even pace. Pause at each blank line.]

You have done the prep.

You chose your space.

You put an honest number on it.

You took the before photo.

And right now your hands are itching to start.

I get it.

But there is one last question before we touch a thing.

And skipping it is the quiet reason so much organizing falls apart a few weeks later.

Here it is.

Before you decide what stays and what goes,

you have to decide what the space is for.

That sounds too simple to be a step.

So try it.

Say what your drop zone is for, in one sentence.

It gets uncomfortable fast.

Because most troubled spaces were never given a job.

They just collected a few by default.

The mail lands there.

And the keys.

And the chargers.

And the homework.

And a bowl nobody has looked at in a year.

Here is the part to sit with.

That space is not failing because it is messy.

It is messy because it is failing to be any one thing.

Each of those jobs is fine on its own.

The problem is one small space doing all of them at once.

They fight.

The mail buries the keys.

Nothing has room to be done well.

We call that the everything-space.

And it is the most common failure in any home.

So before we sort anything, we give the space a job.

Here is why that comes first.

Sorting is just a long line of decisions.

Keep this. Move that. Let go of the other thing.

Make every call on instinct, and it is slow and exhausting.

And it comes out inconsistent.

A clear purpose fixes that.

It turns every choice into one fast question.

Does this thing help the space do its job?

The umbrella helps you get out the door.

It stays.

The stack of old magazines does not.

It has a better home elsewhere.

You are not debating each object anymore.

You are checking it against a standard you set in advance.

So here is the rule.

A space cannot be good at everything.

Give it one clear job.

One supporting job at most.

And it will finally be good at that.

For most entryways, the real job is being a launch pad.

The place that gets everyone out the door fast,

and lands them softly on the way back in.

Now write the sentence.

This space is for one main job,

so that some outcome you actually want.

Here is mine for the drop zone.

This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast

and landing softly on the way back in,

so mornings start calm instead of frantic.

Hold any object up to that.

The keys serve it.

The basket serves it.

The three weeks of mail does not.

The sentence just sorted your space for you.

You are not writing poetry.

A plain true sentence beats a pretty vague one.

And aim it at a real feeling.

Calmer mornings. Never hunting for keys again.

Not at tidiness.

Tidiness is not a purpose.

It is a side effect of a space doing its real job well.

One warning.

Do not write the purpose for the home you wish you had.

Write it for the people who actually live there.

Picture your worst day.

The frantic Tuesday. The rainy game day.

If the purpose only holds when everyone behaves like a catalog model,

it is the wrong purpose.

A good purpose works with your life.

A vain one fights it.

And here is the quiet gift.

A clear purpose sets the space's limits.

Once it has one job, you know how much belongs.

Exactly what the job needs, and nothing more.

The purpose becomes a bouncer at the door.

Every new object gets one question.

Do you serve the job?

If not, it is gently turned away.

So here is your five-minute job.

Go to your space.

Ask what it is most for in your real life.

Then write the one sentence.

This space is for one job,

so that some outcome you truly want.

Keep it to one primary job.

Match it to the people who actually live there.

Write it down, and keep it with your before photo.

You did not move a thing today.

But your space knows what it is for now.

And that is everything you needed before the work begins.

Go write your purpose.
