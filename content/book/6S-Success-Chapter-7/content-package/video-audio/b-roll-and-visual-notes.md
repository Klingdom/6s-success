# B-Roll and Visual Notes: Define the Purpose of the Area

**Chapter 7, 6S Success: Home Edition**
**Slug:** define-the-purpose-of-the-area
**Use:** Shot list, on-screen text moments, and lower-third suggestions for the YouTube, short-form, and any companion video cuts.

**Visual vocabulary to keep consistent (from the chapter):**
- Green calm-dots glow around a single-job space that is working.
- Red friction-sparks fire at every collision point in an everything-space.
- The friction meter holds steady this chapter (no objects move). The GOAL crosshair on the calm side now carries a small purpose flag. The NOW marker on the friction side is still framed as a photo.
- Warm light, lived-in homes, real households. Not a styled catalog look.

---

## CORE SHOT LIST

### Shot 1: The purpose card by the cluttered drop zone (hero shot)
- A hand sets a small handwritten card down on the entryway console, right next to the cluttered drop zone. The card reads a few plain words, like "the launch pad."
- Critical detail: the space behind the card is still a mess, completely untouched. The purpose is the first thing being decided, before a single object moves.
- Warm light. Slow, deliberate hand movement.
- Coverage: one wide that shows card plus mess, one macro on the card and the pen, one over-the-shoulder of the hand placing it.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Decide what it's for before you sort it."
- **Lower third:** Chapter 7. Define the Purpose of the Area.

### Shot 2: The everything-space, five jobs at once
- The drop zone doing all five jobs: mail, keys, charging cables, homework, a forgotten decorative bowl.
- Quick cut-ins on the collisions: mail sliding over keys, a cable tangled around a homework folder, a hand searching and not finding the keys.
- This is the "good at nothing" half of the contrast.
- Overlay red friction-sparks at each collision point.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Mail. Keys. Charging. Homework. Decor. All at once." then "Good at nothing."
- **Lower third:** The everything-space.

### Shot 3: One job versus five (the "One Job Beats Five" contrast)
- Split-screen or before/after dissolve of the SAME space.
- Left/before: the cluttered version with five floating job labels and red friction-sparks. Verdict line: good at nothing.
- Right/after: the same console given one clear job, calm and open, holding only keys on a hook and one basket per person, green calm-dots glowing. Verdict line: good at one thing.
- Center caption available: "A space cannot be good at everything."
- **On-screen text moment:** left "5 jobs = good at nothing" / right "1 job = good at one thing."
- **Lower third:** One job beats five.

### Shot 4: A hand writing the Purpose Statement
- Close on a hand writing the sentence on the template card: "This space is for ___, so that ___."
- Then reveal the filled worked example: "This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so mornings start calm instead of frantic."
- Follow with the yes/no test: hold the keys up to the card (clear yes), hold a stack of old mail up to the card (clear no).
- **On-screen text moment:** the template card animating in, then the filled sentence, then "Keys? Yes. Three weeks of mail? No."
- **Lower third:** The Purpose Statement.

### Shot 5: Aspirational vignette versus real working space
- Side by side of the SAME entryway.
- Left: the aspirational, near-empty version. One elegant tray, a sprig in a vase, nothing else. Pristine and unreal.
- Right: the real, working version holding four bags, muddy shoes, a backpack, all of it doing its job well.
- Cut decisively to the real one on the line "define it for the people who actually live there."
- **On-screen text moment:** left "The home you wish you had" / right "The home you actually live in."
- **Lower third:** Match it to your real life.

### Shot 6: Purpose sets the limits (the bouncer)
- A clean, working launch pad: keys on a hook, one basket per person, an umbrella, and clear empty room left over.
- A hand drifts in to set down a random unrelated object (a stray gadget, a piece of junk mail), pauses, then pulls it back out of frame.
- This visualizes the bouncer at the door, turning away what does not serve the job.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Does this serve the job?" then "No. Turned away."
- **Lower third:** Purpose sets the limits.

### Shot 7: The friction meter, holding with the purpose flag (close)
- The recurring friction meter, needle holding exactly where it sat through Part 2.
- The GOAL crosshair on the calm side now carries a small flag or tag that reads "purpose," fully aimed.
- The NOW baseline marker on the friction side is still framed as a photo.
- Emphasize stillness: nothing has moved, because no objects have moved. The goal just gained a name.
- **On-screen text moment:** "Still holding. Nothing has moved yet." then "But now the goal has a name."
- **Lower third:** A number, a picture, and a purpose. Part 3 is where the needle travels.

## EDITING NOTES

- Keep the cut calm and unhurried. This chapter is about deciding, not doing. Avoid fast, frantic montage except briefly in Shot 2 to sell the everything-space chaos, then settle.
- Do not show anything being thrown away or removed in this video. No objects move in Chapter 7. The first real removal belongs to Chapter 8. Keep that promise visually.
- Let the purpose card be the recurring visual anchor. Open on it, return to it, and end near it.
- Color and warmth should match Chapters 1 through 6: warm paper tones, terracotta and honey accents, slate for any data overlays.
