# Brand Voice Check: Chapter 7 Content Package

The voice of *6S Success: Home Edition* is warm, plain-spoken, and quietly confident. Chapter 7 adds a specific job: it gives the reader permission to stop apologizing for the life they actually have, and hands them one calm decision (name the space's one job) that makes everything later easier. The voice must stay encouraging and non-prescriptive. The book supplies the template; the reader writes the answer.

## Voice fingerprint (from the authored final HTML and manuscript)
- **Warm and personal.** Talks to "you," returns to the same scene the reader already knows (the entryway drop zone they scored and photographed).
- **Permission-giving, not prescriptive.** "It gives you permission to stop apologizing for the life you have." The book never tells the reader what their space is for.
- **Plain over clever.** "A plain, slightly clumsy sentence that is true beats a beautiful one that is vague."
- **Concrete and specific.** Mail, keys, chargers, homework competing on one surface; the umbrella that earns its place; the read magazines that do not.
- **Gently witty.** "One honest job or three jobs in a trench coat."
- **Honest about scope.** No objects move in this chapter, so the friction meter holds. The chapter decides; it does not sort.

## Specific voice wins
- The permission thread is preserved everywhere: no asset shames the reader for a multi-purpose mess. It reframes the mess as a missing decision, not a character flaw, which continues the anti-shame line that runs from Chapter 1's "rooms are not messy people."
- "A space cannot be good at everything" survives intact as the chapter's spine across the thread, a quote card, the article, and the infographic.
- "Three jobs in a trench coat" carries the gentle wit without tipping into snark.
- "Organizing without a purpose is just moving things around neatly" lands the chapter's argument in one plain sentence and reads as the book, not as marketing.

## Voice risks to watch
- **Contractions on X.** The book uses few; X uses several. Deliberate platform choice. Revise only if brand guidelines forbid contractions everywhere.
- **CTA sameness.** Rotate the five soft sign-offs in `canonical/chapter-cta.md` so posts do not feel templated.
- **Keep it non-prescriptive.** A few "give your space a job" angles could read as an instruction rather than an invitation. Spot-checked: the assets keep the reader as the author of their own purpose. Keep it that way.
- **Emoji on Facebook.** Used sparingly. Confirm it matches your brand's emoji policy.

## Banned-language audit
- The widened dash scan (em dash, en dash, and spaced-hyphen " - " separators) is clean across all files. No em dashes anywhere, and no en dashes or spaced hyphens used as separators.
- No prohibited hype words. "Transform" does not appear as filler; the chapter's claim is deliberately modest ("one clear job makes the next decisions easy"), and the voice keeps it that way.
- No "in today's fast-paced world" or similar.

## Verdict
Voice is consistent and intact across all twelve packages, and the give-permission, decide-before-you-sort job of Chapter 7 is honored throughout. Approve the voice. The chapter itself still needs author sign-off, since it was authored in the book voice rather than supplied from source.
