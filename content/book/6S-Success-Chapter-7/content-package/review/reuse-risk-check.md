# Reuse Risk Check: Chapter 7 Content Package

When one chapter becomes fifty assets, the risk is overexposure: the same line, hook, or image hitting the same follower too many times until the message feels canned. This check flags where that can happen and how the calendar manages it.

## High-frequency elements (used in many assets)

| Element | Appears in | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A space cannot be good at everything | Web landing intro, LinkedIn post, X thread 1, FB longform, YouTube open, podcast open, quote card, infographic | A follower on several platforms sees the same line repeatedly | Calendar staggers these across Days 1 to 8. Vary the phrasing per platform. |
| The everything-space (five jobs, good at none) | Hero infographic, carousel, deck, LinkedIn post, X post | Medium | This is the chapter's diagnosis, so repetition reinforces. Vary the example surface (drawer, counter, room). |
| Three jobs in a trench coat | LinkedIn post, FB post, short video, quote card | Medium | Funniest, most shareable line. Use across formats, not stacked on the same day. |
| The Purpose Statement template ("this space is for ___, so that ___") | The template graphic, carousel, X posts, short video, worksheet | Low to Medium | Practical tool, meant to repeat. Vary the worked example you fill in. |
| Does this help the space do its job? (the yardstick question) | LinkedIn post, FB post, short video, quote card, discussion question | Medium | Memorable rule. Keep this chapter's framing about deciding, not yet sorting. |
| Write-your-purpose CTA | Most assets (it is the hero action) | Low to Medium | This is the chapter's main conversion event, so frequent use is intended. Vary the wording. |
| Soft-CTA sign-off wording | Most LinkedIn and X posts | Medium | Rotate the five phrasings from the CTA bank. Most likely thing to feel templated. |

## Same-day collision check
The calendar was built to avoid two assets sharing the same hook on the same day. Confirmed clear at the planned spacing. If your LinkedIn and Facebook audiences overlap heavily, push FB post 1 to the next day so "a space cannot be good at everything" does not land twice in one day.

## Platform cross-posting risk
- The LinkedIn carousel outline (`linkedin/`) and the slides carousel copy (`slides/`) cover the same Purpose Statement content. Post only one to LinkedIn. Use the copy-ready version (`slides/linkedin-carousel-copy.md`) and keep the outline as a planning doc.
- The LinkedIn newsletter version and the email newsletter version share structure. Fine for different lists, but do not send both to the same subscriber the same day.

## Duplicate-content / SEO risk
- The web landing intro, ebook description, and LinkedIn article overlap in phrasing. For SEO, the canonical chapter page should be the only indexed full version. Set syndicated copies (LinkedIn article, Medium, etc.) to point back with a canonical link.

## Cross-chapter risk (specific to Chapter 7)
- The entryway drop zone is the running worked example for all of Part 2. Chapter 5 scored it (14 of 30), Chapter 6 photographed it, Chapter 7 gives it a purpose. The repetition is intentional and builds continuity, but when all three chapters' assets are live in the same week, vary which step each post leads with so the same drop zone does not feel overused.
- Chapters 5, 6, and 7 form the Part 2 (Prepare) set: the number, the picture, and the purpose. They are a natural trio, captured in `chapter-quotes.md` as "the number told you where the space stood, the photo recorded it, the purpose is the one that keeps it good." Keep their "prepare your starting point" content from colliding in the same week.
- The yardstick question ("does this help the space do its job?") sets up the Sort decision in Chapter 8. Do not run Chapter 7's "decide the purpose" and Chapter 8's "now sort against it" posts so close that the payoff is spoiled before Part 3 launches.

## Originality and source-fidelity risk
- The chapter itself is a draft authored in the book voice, not supplied source. All derived assets are faithful to that draft. If the author revises the chapter, refresh the assets so they do not drift from the published version.
- Nothing is borrowed from outside the book. All quotes are verbatim or lightly trimmed from the authored chapter (see `canonical/chapter-quotes.md`).

## Recommendations
1. Rotate CTA wording across posts (highest-value, lowest-effort fix).
2. Pick one carousel version for LinkedIn, not both.
3. Set the chapter page as the SEO canonical and mark syndicated copies.
4. Keep the Part 2 trio (Chapters 5, 6, 7) and the entryway drop-zone example from colliding in the same week, and hold the Chapter 8 sorting payoff until Part 3 opens.
5. Re-sync all assets if the authored chapter changes after author review.
