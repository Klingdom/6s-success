# Diagram Ideas: Chapter 7

**"Define the Purpose of the Area"**
Five diagrams that carry the chapter's logic. All use the warm book palette and the two
chapter fonts (Fraunces display, Newsreader body, a clean sans for labels). These are
working specs, not finished art.

---

## 1. The Purpose Statement (the hero tool)

**Purpose:** Show the fill-in-the-blank sentence as a tool the reader can copy, then
prove it works with the filled entryway example.

**What it shows:** The decision rule in two blanks. Fill the job and the outcome, and
every object can be held against the result.

**Rough layout:**
- Top: small sans caps label, soft gray, `THE PURPOSE STATEMENT`.
- Middle: a panel card with a slate border. Fraunces sentence in ink, blanks in
  terracotta: "This **[ space ]** is for **[ one primary job ]**, so that
  **[ an outcome you actually want ]**." Helper line beneath: "Name one primary job,
  and one supporting job at most."
- Below: a white card with a green `FILLED EXAMPLE` corner tab. Newsreader italic:
  "This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the
  way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic."
- Footer italic, soft gray: "A plain sentence that is true beats a beautiful one that is vague."

---

## 2. One Job Beats Five (the same space, twice)

**Purpose:** Show why specializing wins. The same drop zone fails at five jobs and
succeeds at one.

**What it shows:** Five competing job labels colliding versus one clear job with room to breathe.

**Rough layout:**
- A panel split down the middle by a dashed bone line.
- Left, header `FIVE JOBS AT ONCE`: five overlapping label chips (MAIL, KEYS, CHARGING,
  HOMEWORK, DECOR) in white with ink borders, drawn so they crowd and overlap. Two
  spark-red X marks at the worst collisions. A sagging shelf line under them.
  Footer in Fraunces, spark red: "Good at nothing".
- Right, header `ONE CLEAR JOB`: a single green-outlined `LAUNCH PAD` pill, a honey keys
  glyph, a small basket shape, three green calm dots, a firm level shelf line.
  Footer in Fraunces, green: "Good at one thing".
- Bottom center, Newsreader italic, slate: "A space cannot be good at everything."

---

## 3. Purpose as a Bouncer at the Door

**Purpose:** Show how a clear purpose protects a finished space from slow re-cluttering.

**What it shows:** Every new object meets one question at the entrance. Items that serve
the job get in. Items that do not are turned away before they can settle.

**Rough layout:**
- A simple doorway drawn center frame, slate frame, a velvet-rope curve in terracotta.
- Above the door, the gate question in sans caps: `DOES THIS SERVE THE JOB?`
- A short queue of small item glyphs approaching from the right. The keys and a daily
  bag pass through into a calm green-tinted space (green calm dots inside).
- A stack of old mail and a stray charger are stopped at the rope, gently angled away,
  marked with a small spark-red turn-away arrow pointing back out.
- Footer, Newsreader italic: "A defined purpose gives every stray object a bouncer at the door."

---

## 4. Aspirational vs Real Space (honest purpose)

**Purpose:** Warn against writing the purpose for the home you wish you had.

**What it shows:** A side-by-side of the same entryway, the catalog fantasy versus the
real, busy household, and which purpose actually holds on a frantic Tuesday.

**Rough layout:**
- Two framed panels.
- Left, label `THE HOME YOU WISH YOU HAD`: a serene console, one elegant tray, a single
  sprig in a vase, cool and empty. Cool slate tint. A small spark-red footnote tag:
  "wrong every real morning".
- Right, label `THE HOME YOU ACTUALLY LIVE IN`: four bags, four pairs of shoes, an
  umbrella, a basket per person, warm and full. Warm honey and paper tint. A green check
  tag: "a complete success".
- Center divider holds the line, Newsreader italic, slate: "A good purpose works with
  your life. A vain one fights it."

---

## 5. The Friction Meter with the Purpose Flag

**Purpose:** Close the chapter and Part Two. Show that nothing has moved yet, but the
goal now has a name.

**What it shows:** A gauge from CALM (green) to FRICTION (spark red). The needle still
sits on the friction side. The GOAL crosshair on the calm side now carries a small
`PURPOSE` flag. The NOW marker is framed like a photo.

**Rough layout:**
- Half-circle gauge, arc colored green to honey to spark left-to-right reversed so calm
  is the low side. Pale `#EDE3CE` track behind it.
- Needle pivot in ink, needle pointing toward the friction side (unchanged from the
  earlier chapters).
- On the calm side, a slate crosshair labeled `GOAL`, with a short pole and a slate
  `PURPOSE` flag above it.
- On the friction side, a small photo-corner frame around the `NOW` marker, spark-red label.
- Bottom band: `CALM` (green, left) and `FRICTION` (spark, right).
- Footer, Newsreader italic, soft gray: "The goal has a name now. Everything is aimed."
