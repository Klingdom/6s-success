# Visual Storyboard

## Define the Purpose of the Area

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 7. A visual storyboard tying the teaching deck and the LinkedIn carousel to the book's design system.*

---

## Design system reference

Pulled directly from chapter_07_final.html :root variables. Use these exactly so the slides read as part of the book.

- Paper background: #F7F2E9
- Panel / card fill: #FBF7EF
- Ink (primary text): #2B2622
- Soft text: #6A625A
- Terracotta (primary accent, the brackets and the launch-pad card): #BC4B2A
- Honey (secondary accent, art notes and family callouts): #DDA63A
- Slate (chapter labels, definition box border, the GOAL flag): #3C5A6B
- Green calm-dot (the working, one-job side): #6E8B5B
- Friction spark (the failing, five-jobs side): #CB4B36
- Rules / hairlines: #E2D8C4 and #D9CDB8
- Display font: Fraunces (titles, pull quotes, the template sentence)
- Body font: Newsreader (worked example, captions, running text)
- Label font: Inter, uppercase, wide letter-spacing (kickers, tags, axis labels)

**The recurring motif.** Red friction-sparks mark the failing, overloaded side. Green calm-dots mark the working, one-job side. This pairing carries the whole chapter's argument visually. Keep it consistent on every beat: anything overloaded or out of place gets a red spark; anything that serves the one job gets a green dot. Honey stays warm and neutral, never used to signal good or bad.

## Beat 1: The purpose-card opener

The chapter and both decks open the same way. A hand sets a small handwritten card reading "the launch pad" on a console beside a still-cluttered entryway drop zone.

- Composition: full-bleed, warm and quiet, no people beyond the hand. The card is the only crisp, decided thing in an otherwise messy frame.
- Palette: paper #F7F2E9 ground, terracotta #BC4B2A on the card edge or the handwriting, ink #2B2622 for the clutter.
- Motif: hold the sparks and dots back here. This is the question, not the answer. The frame should feel unresolved.
- Caption line: "One question remains before you touch a thing: what is this space for?"

---

## Beat 2: The everything-space

Establish the problem before the split. A single console asked to do five jobs at once.

- Composition: one surface, five white job tags (MAIL, KEYS, CHARGING, HOMEWORK, DECOR) in ink #2B2622 outlines, overlapping and colliding.
- Motif: two red friction-sparks #CB4B36 placed exactly where the tags overlap, the visual of jobs fighting for the same space.
- Palette: panel #FBF7EF card, soft #6A625A for the axis label "FIVE JOBS AT ONCE."
- This is the left half of the next beat, shown alone so the diagnosis lands first.

---

## Beat 3: The One Job Beats Five split

The chapter's secondary hero device, used as the centerpiece of both decks. Rebuild it faithfully from the HTML SVG.

- Layout: one canvas split by a vertical dashed divider in #D9CDB8.
- Left, "FIVE JOBS AT ONCE": the five colliding white job tags, two red friction-sparks #CB4B36 at the collision points, a thin grey shelf line. Headline in Fraunces #CB4B36: "Good at nothing."
- Right, "ONE CLEAR JOB": a single LAUNCH PAD tag, green-outlined #6E8B5B on a pale green fill #E8EFE0, a sturdy ink shelf, a few supporting items (a honey key outline, a basket), and three green calm-dots #6E8B5B. Headline in Fraunces green: "Good at one thing."
- Footer, slate #3C5A6B italic Newsreader: "A space cannot be good at everything."
- This is the clearest single frame of the red-spark / green-dot motif. Everything else echoes it.

---

## Beat 4: The one-job rule (definition box)

A pause beat between the split and the tool.

- Composition: the book's definition box. Panel #FBF7EF fill, 2px slate #3C5A6B border, uppercase slate label "THE ONE-JOB RULE."
- The rule in Fraunces medium #2B2622: "A space cannot be good at everything. Give it one clear job, and at most one supporting job, and it will finally be good at that."
- Palette: restrained, no sparks or dots. Slate does the work.

---

## Beat 5: The Purpose Statement template

The chapter's primary hero device. Two stacked cards, template above, filled example below.

- Template card: slate #3C5A6B border on panel #FBF7EF. Sentence in Fraunces medium #2B2622 with the blanks called out in terracotta #BC4B2A brackets: "This [space] is for [one primary job], so that [an outcome you actually want]." Small Inter note in soft #6A625A: "Name one primary job, and one supporting job at most."
- Filled example card: white #FFFFFF fill, hairline #E2D8C4 border, a green #6E8B5B tab reading "FILLED EXAMPLE." The sentence in Newsreader italic #2B2622: "This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic."
- Footer, soft italic: "A plain sentence that is true beats a beautiful one that is vague."
- Motif: pair the filled example with three small object tests beside it. Keys and a basket pass with green calm-dots; the stack of mail fails with a red friction-spark. The template literally sorting the space.

---

## Beat 6: Aspirational vs real

The "match it to your real life" warning, the kindest beat in the chapter.

- Composition: a side-by-side contrast. Left, a catalog-perfect, empty entryway labeled "the home you wish you had." Right, a lived-in entryway with backpacks, boots, and a dropped bag labeled "the home you actually have."
- Motif: the perfect one carries a faint red friction-spark #CB4B36 (it fails every real morning). The real one carries a steady green calm-dot #6E8B5B (the purpose works with the life).
- Tone note: the real side is honest, not unflattering or comic. Warm light, paper ground. The point is permission, not shame.

---

## Beat 7: Purpose sets the limits (the bouncer)

How a clear purpose defends a space.

- Composition: a doorway or threshold, almost diagrammatic. A short queue of objects approaching.
- Motif: objects that serve the one job pass through, each tagged with a green calm-dot #6E8B5B. One stray object is turned away at the line, tagged with a red friction-spark #CB4B36.
- Palette: slate #3C5A6B for the threshold line, ink for objects, paper ground.
- Caption idea: "A defined purpose gives every stray object a bouncer at the door."

---

## Beat 8: The close, the friction meter fully aimed

The chapter's running instrument, used to close both decks. Rebuild from the HTML SVG.

- Composition: the semicircular friction meter. The arc runs green #6E8B5B (CALM) on the left, honey #DDA63A in the middle, friction spark #CB4B36 (FRICTION) on the right. The needle holds steady, because nothing has moved yet.
- The GOAL marker: a slate #3C5A6B crosshair on the calm side, now carrying a small slate flag reading PURPOSE. This is the payoff, the goal finally has a name.
- The NOW marker: framed as a small photo (corner brackets) on the friction side, labeled in #CB4B36, a callback to Chapter 6's before photo.
- Footer, soft italic Newsreader: "The goal has a name now. Everything is aimed."
- Motif close: the green calm side and the red friction side are the same dot-and-spark language from the One Job split, now mapped onto the whole journey. The reader ends knowing where they are (NOW, red), where they are going (GOAL, green), and what better means (the PURPOSE flag).

---

## Carrying it across the set

- Open every sequence with the purpose-card; close every sequence with the aimed friction meter. The two bookend the "number, picture, and purpose" arc.
- Use Fraunces only for the load-bearing lines (titles, the template sentence, the two split headlines). Keep running copy in Newsreader so the slides breathe.
- Never let honey signal correctness. Good is green, failing is red, and that two-color logic should be legible even in a thumbnail.
- Keep generous paper margins and a single idea per frame. The chapter's voice is calm and non-prescriptive; the visuals should feel the same, decided but never loud.
