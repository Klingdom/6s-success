# LinkedIn Carousel Copy

## Define the Purpose of the Area

*6S Success: Home Edition, Chapter 7. On-slide copy, ready to set. 10 slides.*
Palette: paper #F7F2E9, panel #FBF7EF, ink #2B2622, terracotta #BC4B2A, honey #DDA63A, slate #3C5A6B, green calm-dot #6E8B5B, friction spark #CB4B36. Display Fraunces, body Newsreader.

---

### Slide 1 (hook)

**Your space is not messy.**
**It is failing to be any one thing.**

Most cluttered spots were never given a job. They quietly took on five.

[VISUAL] Purpose-card opener. A hand setting a small "the launch pad" card beside a cluttered entryway console. Warm paper, terracotta accent on the card.

---

### Slide 2

The drop zone by your door is doing five jobs at once.

Mail. Keys. Charging. Homework. Decor.

It is good at none of them.

[VISUAL] One console crowded with five colliding job tags in white ink-outlined boxes. Two red friction-sparks where the jobs overlap.

---

### Slide 3

Here is the part most people skip.

Before you decide what stays and what goes, you have to decide what the space is for.

Decide the job first. Then the sorting has something to be right about.

[VISUAL] A trail of done steps (chose, audited, photographed) and one waiting step marked with a slate question mark.

---

### Slide 4

One clear job beats five competing ones.

Five jobs at once: good at nothing.
One job, a launch pad: good at one thing.

Excellence at one thing means not being mediocre at four others.

[VISUAL] The One Job Beats Five split. Left, five tags with red friction-sparks, "Good at nothing." Right, a green LAUNCH PAD tag with three green calm-dots, "Good at one thing." Dashed divider.

---

### Slide 5

A purpose in your head is a vague intention.

A purpose on paper is a tool.

So write one sentence.

[VISUAL] Pull-quote card on panel. The line set large in Fraunces, slate rules above and below.

---

### Slide 6

The template:

This **[space]** is for **[one primary job]**,
so that **[an outcome you actually want]**.

One primary job. One supporting job at most.

[VISUAL] The Purpose Statement template card. Slate border, the three blanks shown in terracotta brackets, Fraunces type.

---

### Slide 7

Filled in:

"This entryway is for getting everyone out the door fast and landing softly on the way back in, so that mornings start calm instead of frantic."

Now hold any object against it. The keys serve it. The three weeks of mail do not.

[VISUAL] The filled example card in Newsreader italic, green tab. Beside it: keys and a basket pass (green dots), a mail stack fails (red spark).

---

### Slide 8

One warning.

Do not write the purpose for the home you wish you had.

A space built for an imaginary, tidier family is wrong for the real one every single morning.

[VISUAL] Aspirational-vs-real contrast. Left, a catalog entryway with a faint red spark. Right, a lived-in entryway with backpacks and boots, steady green calm-dot.

---

### Slide 9

A clear purpose sets a limit.

Once a space has one job, you know exactly how much belongs there.

That is your defense against the slow re-cluttering that undoes most organizing. A purpose is a bouncer at the door.

[VISUAL] A doorway gatekeeper. Items that serve the job pass (green dots), a stray object is turned away at the threshold (red friction-spark).

---

### Slide 10 (soft CTA)

A space cannot be good at everything.

Give it one clear job, in one honest sentence, and every later decision gets easier.

This is from Chapter 7 of *6S Success: Home Edition*. You can read it free in the online book, then Chapter 8 begins the sorting.

[VISUAL] The friction meter, fully aimed. The GOAL crosshair carries a small slate PURPOSE flag, the NOW marker is framed as a photo. Footer: "The goal has a name now. Everything is aimed."
