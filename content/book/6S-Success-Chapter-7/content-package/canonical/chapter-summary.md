# Chapter 7 Summary: Define the Purpose of the Area

*6S Success: Home Edition · Part Two, Prepare*

## One-line summary

Before you sort anything, decide what the space is actually for, in one written sentence, because that purpose becomes the rule that makes every later keep-or-go decision easy.

## Full summary (about 250 words)

Chapter 7 is the final chapter of Part Two, Prepare. The reader has chosen, audited, and photographed a space. Now they decide what it is for, before moving a thing.

The case for purpose-before-sorting is logical. Sorting is a long series of keep-or-go decisions, and without a fixed standard each one is slow, tiring, and inconsistent. A defined purpose turns every decision into one fast question: does this help the space do its job? It is the yardstick. It also explains why a space can be tidy and still fail, because it was optimized for nothing in particular.

The chapter names the common failure: the everything-space, a surface or drawer asked to do five jobs at once (mail, keys, charging, homework, decor) and therefore good at none. The fix is the one-job rule: pick the single job that matters most, allow one supporting job at most, and let the rest find homes elsewhere.

The hero tool is the Purpose Statement, a fill-in-the-blank sentence: "This space is for one primary job, so that an outcome you actually want." The worked example makes a drop zone a launch pad for getting people out the door, so mornings start calm. The chapter warns against writing the purpose for an aspirational home rather than the real one, and shows the quiet superpower of a clear purpose: it sets the space's capacity, giving every stray object a bouncer at the door and preventing the slow re-cluttering that undoes most organizing. The reader writes their sentence, and Part Two is complete. Next, Part Three begins the sorting.
