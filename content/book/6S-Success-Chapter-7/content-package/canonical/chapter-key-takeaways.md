# Chapter 7 Key Takeaways: Define the Purpose of the Area

The core ideas a reader should carry out of this chapter.

1. **Define the purpose before you sort.** The space's job is the criterion you sort against. Decide it first, and the sorting has something to be right about.

2. **A space is messy because it is failing to be any one thing.** Most troubled spaces were never assigned a job; they accumulated several by default, and the jobs compete.

3. **Purpose turns every keep-or-go decision into one fast question.** Does this thing help the space do its job? Without that yardstick, every item is a slow, tiring, inconsistent debate.

4. **A tidy space can still fail.** If you sort without a purpose, you optimize for nothing in particular, and the result is neat and wrong.

5. **The enemy is the everything-space.** A surface asked to do five jobs at once is good at none of them. The mail buries the keys; nothing has room to be done well.

6. **One job beats five.** Give a space one clear primary job, and at most one supporting job. Excellence at one thing requires not being mediocre at four others.

7. **Write the purpose as one sentence.** "This space is for one primary job, so that an outcome you actually want." A plain true sentence beats a vague pretty one.

8. **Aim the purpose at a real outcome, not at tidiness.** Tidiness is not a purpose; it is a side effect of a space doing its real job well.

9. **Match the purpose to your real life.** A purpose written for the home you wish you had will be wrong every real morning. Define it for the people who actually live there.

10. **Test the purpose at the worst moment.** The frantic Tuesday, the rainy game day. If it only works when everyone behaves like a catalog model, it is the wrong purpose.

11. **Purpose sets the limits.** Once a space has one job, you know exactly how much belongs there, which is your defense against the slow re-cluttering that undoes most organizing.

12. **A clear purpose is a bouncer at the door.** It asks every new object whether it serves the job, and turns away the ones that do not before they can move in.
