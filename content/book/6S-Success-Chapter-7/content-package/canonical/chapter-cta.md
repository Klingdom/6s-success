# Chapter 7 Calls to Action: Define the Purpose of the Area

This chapter decides the space's job; it does not sort. Its strongest CTA is writing the one-sentence purpose. The CTAs match the chapter's calm, non-prescriptive tone.

## Primary CTA
Write your purpose sentence. "Name the one job your space exists to do, then write it as a sentence: this space is for ___, so that ___. Keep it to one primary job, and match it to the people who actually live there. Read the chapter free in the online book."

## Secondary CTA
Read the chapter (or the next one). "Before you sort anything, give the space one clear job. Chapter 7 shows you how to write a one-sentence purpose that makes every later decision easy. Read it free online, then Chapter 8 begins the sorting."

## In-chapter actions a reader can take (from the callouts)
- **Quick Win:** Say your space's job out loud, then name one thing in it that has nothing to do with that job. Do not move it yet, just notice it.
- **Family Challenge:** Each person writes the purpose of their space, reads it aloud, and the family votes on whether it is one honest job or three jobs in a trench coat.

## Soft CTAs for social posts (rotate these)
- "This is from Chapter 7 of 6S Success: Home Edition. Read it free online."
- "A space cannot be good at everything. The full chapter is free in the online book."
- "Write your space's one job in a sentence. The chapter shows you the template."
- "Grab the free Purpose Statement worksheet and give your space a job."
- "Next after the purpose: Chapter 8, where the sorting finally begins."

## Lead-magnet / resource CTAs (planned companion assets, from the signature)
- **The Purpose Statement worksheet:** a printable fill-in-the-blank sentence with room to draft and refine, plus a place to note the primary and optional supporting job.
- **The "One Space, One Job" card:** a printable and phone wallpaper of the one-job rule and the template sentence, for quick reference on any space.

## CTA writing notes
- The action is writing the sentence. Lead with "name the one job and write it down," which is concrete and five minutes.
- Keep it non-prescriptive. The reader defines their own purpose; the book supplies the template, not the answer.
- "A space cannot be good at everything" and "three jobs in a trench coat" are the most memorable, shareable lines. Lead social with them.
- Avoid hype. The claim is modest and true: one clear job makes the next decisions easy.
