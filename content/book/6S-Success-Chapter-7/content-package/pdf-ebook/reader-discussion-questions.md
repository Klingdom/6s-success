# Reader and Book-Club Discussion Questions: Define the Purpose of the Area

*Chapter 7, 6S Success: Home Edition. Use these for personal reflection or a group discussion. They are open-ended, with no single right answer.*

1. The chapter says a space is not failing because it is messy, it is messy because it is failing to be any one thing. Which space in your home fits that description, and what jobs has it quietly taken on?

2. Think of a space you have organized before that drifted back into clutter. Looking back, did it ever have a defined job? How might naming one have changed the outcome?

3. The chapter argues that purpose has to come before sorting because it is the yardstick you sort against. Does that order match how you have organized in the past, or have you usually sorted first and asked the purpose later?

4. The everything-space is described as a surface or drawer asked to do five jobs at once and therefore good at none. Where is your everything-space, and which of its competing jobs actually matters most?

5. The one-job rule allows one primary job and at most one supporting job. For a space you have in mind, what would you choose as the primary job, and what would you let go to another home?

6. The chapter offers the Purpose Statement template: this space is for one primary job, so that an outcome you actually want. Try writing one out loud now. What did you notice while trying to keep it to a single sentence?

7. The worked example turns an entryway into a launch pad, so mornings start calm instead of frantic. The purpose aims at a feeling, not at tidiness. What real outcome, not just neatness, do you want from your space?

8. The chapter warns against writing a purpose for the home you wish you had rather than the one you actually live in. Where have you been organizing for an ideal family instead of the real one, and what would change if you stopped?

9. The chapter suggests testing a purpose at the worst moment, like a frantic Tuesday or a rainy game day. How does your draft purpose hold up on your hardest day, and what would you adjust?

10. A clear purpose is described as a bouncer at the door that sets the space's limit and turns away clutter that does not serve the job. What stray object in your space would that bouncer turn away first, and what makes it so easy to let creep back in?
