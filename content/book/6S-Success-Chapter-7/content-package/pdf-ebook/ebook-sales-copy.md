# 6S Success: Home Edition (Sales Page Copy)

## You sort and sort, and the space still fails

You have organized that drawer before. The entryway, the kitchen counter, the spot by the door. You sorted it, you arranged it, it looked good for a week, and then it drifted right back into the same mess.

The problem is not that you are bad at organizing. The problem is that the space was never given a job. It became an everything-space, asked to handle mail and keys and charging and homework and a decorative bowl all at once. A space asked to do five jobs is good at none of them. The mail buries the keys, and nothing has room to be done well.

## One sentence makes every decision easy

Chapter 7 of *6S Success: Home Edition*, "Define the Purpose of the Area," fixes this before you sort a thing. You decide what the space is actually for, and you write it as one plain sentence: this space is for one primary job, so that an outcome you actually want.

That sentence becomes your yardstick. Sorting stops being a thousand separate judgment calls and becomes one fast question you can answer about any object: does this help the space do its job? Keep the ones that do. The rest have a better home elsewhere.

## What you get in this chapter

- The reason purpose has to come before sorting, so your work actually holds
- How to spot the everything-space, the most common failure in a home
- The Purpose Statement template, with a real entryway worked through end to end
- How to write the purpose for your real life, not an ideal one, and test it on your worst day
- How a clear purpose sets a space's limits and stops the slow re-cluttering that undoes most organizing

It takes about five minutes and one honest sentence. No new bins, no system to maintain.

Read the chapter, write your one sentence, and start the sorting with something to be right about.
