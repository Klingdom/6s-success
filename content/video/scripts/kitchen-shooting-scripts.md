# The Kitchen: Seven Shooting Scripts

**6S Success, Home Edition, Video Companion. Room 02 of 20.**
Episodes MZ-006 to MZ-012. Seven episodes, forty two Shorts, one room.

Full production scripts for the Kitchen's seven micro zones: verbatim voiceover, a synced shot
sequence with 9:16 framing notes, on screen text, and six Short cuts per episode. Headless
throughout. No presenter on camera at any point.

The Entryway pilot proved the format on the easiest room in the house. The Kitchen is the test that
matters, because it is the busiest room, the dirtiest room, and the only room where getting the
Safety pass wrong can actually hurt somebody.

---

## How to read a script

- Every episode is the same shape: cold open, then six segments in canonical order, then the close.
  Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain. Safety is the fourth pass, never the last.
- The voiceover boxes are the exact words to record. Timings are targets, not measurements. Let the
  edit breathe.
- The shot table syncs each setup to the line of voiceover it plays under. The 9:16 column is not
  optional. Every one of these frames gets cropped to vertical for a Short, and a frame composed
  without that in mind cannot be rescued later.
- Each segment ends with its Short cut. A Short cannot lean on anything shown earlier in the episode,
  so each one carries its own hook, its own re establishing line, and its own end card.
- Anything in square brackets is swapped at record time.

## The framing rule, in one line

Shoot 4K 16:9 and compose every shot inside a centred 9:16 safe box, keep on screen text inside that
box, and grab one deliberate vertical take of each segment opener. The vertical crop discards roughly
half the width of the frame. Hands that drift wide are hands that vanish.

## The fixed opener

Beat 1 is written per zone and lives in each episode below. Beats 2 and 3 are identical across all
114 episodes and are recorded once as a reusable asset. Only the closing line changes, where the zone
name and session length are swapped in. The full asset lives in the Entryway pilot. It is repeated
here so nobody has to open two files on set.

**Beat 2, about 20 seconds. What 6S is.**

> 6S is six steps, always in the same order. Sort, keep what earns its place and route out the rest.
> Straighten, give what stays a home you can reach. Shine, clean closely enough to catch the small
> problems early. Safety, the fourth step, make the space safe before you make it pretty.
> Standardize, set the state you want so anyone can find it again. And Sustain, keep it with a habit
> rather than a burst of willpower.

**Beat 3, about 20 seconds. The solution, the Micro Zone Room Reset.**

> Most home projects fail because a room is too big to finish in one sitting, so you stop halfway and
> the mess wins. A micro zone is small enough to finish. One shelf, one drawer, one corner. Six
> steps, one sitting, and a change you can see from the doorway. That is the 6S Success Home Edition
> Micro Zone Room Reset. Today we reset the *[zone name]*, and it takes about *[session length]*.

## Room shoot notes, all seven episodes

Shoot the Kitchen across two days on mid morning side light. It is the only light that shows crumbs,
a grease film, and the dust band at the back of a shelf. Kill the ceiling fixture. Its colour will
not match the window and it drops a hard tripod shadow straight across the work surface.

All overheads come off one tripod with the centre column inverted, or a lateral arm fitted, set
outside the walkway so the strip of floor between sink, hob and refrigerator stays clear. This is a
one person shoot, so every overhead is locked off and framed before hands enter, and no shot may need
a second operator. Nothing above the wrists enters frame at any point, so push sleeves back and
rehearse every hand move once before rolling. Where a second pair of hands is genuinely needed, it is
a household member's hands only, wrists down, entering a locked off frame.

Light cabinet interiors and the under sink cabinet with a bounce card. Never put a lamp inside a
cabinet. It reads as fake instantly.

**On set safety is real in this room and it is not a formality.** Knives and the mandoline stay
sheathed between takes and never rest on a towel edge. The hob stays cold except for the few beats
where heat is genuinely needed, and somebody stays with it while it is on. Cleaner bottles in the
sink sequence stay capped, are never placed in the basin together, and are never decanted into an
unlabelled container for the camera. Wet crisper drawers get carried two handed, because wet plastic
on tile is the fall risk of the day. Unplug any appliance before it is lifted, wiped or opened.

Record room tone twice, once with everything off and once with the refrigerator running, or the
compressor will cut in and out underneath the voiceover.

## Shooting order, and one conflict worth knowing

Publish in tracker order, MZ-006 through MZ-012, because that is how the site and the book read.

Shoot in a different order. The Micro Zone Manual is explicit that the real work starts at the sink,
because a sink with dishes in it makes every counter decision look impossible. So stage and shoot
**MZ-008, the Sink and Dishwashing Zone, first**, hold that zone empty, and shoot the other six
against it. Every other zone in this room eventually drains into that basin, and a full sink in the
background of a prep counter shot will undercut the episode you are trying to make.

The other room level fact to hold across all seven scripts: **grease first, then germs.** Disinfectant
cannot reach through a film of cooking oil. Wash food surfaces, the hood and the splash wall with warm
water and dish soap, or with a food area degreaser, before any disinfectant goes on. Then give the
disinfectant the full time its own label asks for.

# MZ-006 . R02.01 . The Primary Prep Counter

**Runtime 9 to 12 min. Session 45 to 75 minutes, most of it in Sort, deciding what has earned the
surface. Thumbnail: "One board, nothing else".**

**Title: How to Reset a Kitchen Prep Counter with 6S**

**Zone purpose:** the stretch of counter where food is actually cut, mixed and plated.

### Shoot notes

One locked off overhead on the full counter run, framed before anything moves, reused for the before
and the after so the two cut together cleanly. A three quarter tripod at counter height carries every
placement and reach move. Keep the sink out of the deep background, or empty it first.

The Sort sweep is the shot of the episode and it is one continuous move, so clear a landing surface
on the dining table before you roll and rehearse the path once. Unplug the toaster and the kettle
before the Shine lifts, and show the plug leaving the socket, because otherwise half the audience
will copy the lift with the appliance live. Keep every blade sheathed between takes. For the knife
beats, carry the edge away from the lens, and never rest a knife on a towel edge where it can slide.
Stage the drying rack knife shot with the hob cold and the sink empty and dry.

Check the counter for a reflection of the ceiling and of you before every overhead. A polished stone
or laminate run will hold both.

**Beat 1 continues, about 10 seconds.**

> comes off, and only three things earn their way back.

### Sort, about 90 seconds

> Everything comes off. Sweep the whole run onto the dining table in one move, so you can see how
> wide this counter actually is. Most people have not seen it bare in years. Now fix the count before
> you start deciding, because deciding first is how everything creeps back. Three things earn this
> surface. What you cut on. What you cut with. What you season with. That is the entire list. Bring
> those three back and stop. Everything else gets handled once, one item at a time. The vitamins. The
> second cutting board. The bread bag. The pile of receipts. Each one gets a home somewhere else, or
> it leaves. What none of them gets is a spot at the back. Then there is the hard one. The appliance
> you genuinely love and rarely use. The mixer, the bread machine, the espresso setup. Count real
> uses over the past month, not the ones you intended. Less than a couple of times a week and it
> comes off the counter into a low cabinet, ideally on a pull out. Not switched on in a year and it
> leaves the kitchen. And if your honest reason for keeping it out is that lifting it from a cabinet
> would stop you using it, you have just proved it is not part of your cooking. It is worth more sold
> than stored.

| 2 | Overhead | The bare run with a cutting board laid across it, showing how many boards wide it really is. | Board centred, a board width of clear counter visible above and below it. | so you can see how wide this counter actually is | ~8s |

### Straighten, about 65 seconds

> Now start where you already stand. Put a hand down where it lands when you begin chopping, without
> thinking about it. That is where the board goes. Not where the counter looks tidiest. Then build
> outward from the board. Knives go on a wall strip or in a block on your dominant side, so the reach
> mid cut is short and you are never crossing your body with a blade in your hand. Oil and salt move
> from beside the board to behind it, because beside the board is really on the board once you are
> working. Then open the cabinet directly underneath and lift a mixing bowl out without turning
> around. That is the reason the board sits here and not two steps along. Last, the kettle and the
> toaster stand back against the wall with their cords coiled, at the far end from the sink.

**Short cut, Straighten, 30 to 45 seconds**

- **Hook:** "Two ways to fetch a mixing bowl while you are chopping. One of them costs you a turn and a step, every single time."
- **Stands alone:** say this is the prep counter, and that the board's position is decided by where your hands land, not by how the run looks. Cut the two reaches side by side.
- **End card:** "That is Straighten. Five more passes in the full prep counter reset."

### Shine, about 95 seconds

> Clean this counter to inspect it, not to impress anyone. Unplug the toaster and the kettle first,
> then lift them straight up off the counter instead of wiping around them. Under each one is a ring
> of crumbs in the shape of its own feet. Wiping around them has never once moved that. While the
> toaster is off the counter, pull the crumb tray out and shake it over the bin, then wipe it dry.
> Most people have never opened that tray. Now take the seam where the counter meets the backsplash.
> Work top to bottom, so whatever you loosen falls onto counter you have not cleaned yet. Spray the
> backsplash, wipe it down, then lay a damp cloth into the seam and drag it the full length. What
> comes off a joint that looked clean from standing height is the grease line. For everyday food
> residue, a neutral pH cleaner on a microfiber cloth is enough. Where oil has built up, switch to a
> food area degreaser, give it the dwell time its label asks for, then follow with a clean damp cloth
> so no film is left where food will touch. Now look at what you have uncovered, because this is the
> part that pays. A scorch mark or a heat bloom on the wall behind the toaster. A sealant bead along
> the seam that is lifting or gone dark, so water can get behind it. A soft or swollen patch in the
> counter near that seam, which means water already has. And the cords you just coiled. A cord that
> is cracked, brittle or fraying takes that appliance out of service today. You replace it. You do
> not tape it.

| 2 | Macro | The toaster lifted straight up out of frame, revealing a crumb ring in the exact shape of its feet. | Crumb ring on the vertical centre, appliance exiting upward. | Under each one is a ring of crumbs in the shape of its own feet. | ~12s |

- **Unplug, then lift** over shot 1

- **End card:** "That is Shine. The full prep counter reset is in the episode."

### Safety, about 80 seconds. This is the fourth S.

> Safety is the fourth pass, and it comes before the counter is made pretty. Two things on this run
> can genuinely hurt somebody. First, blades. A chef's knife left blade up in the drying rack, or slid
> under the suds in a full sink, is the most common serious cut in a home kitchen, and it lands on a
> hand that was reaching for something else. So every blade gets a home. A block, a magnetic strip, or
> a bladed sleeve in a drawer. Never loose among the utensils. Never blade up in the rack. Never in
> water you cannot see through. Move them one at a time, edge turned away from you, and if you have to
> set one down on the way, set it flat with the edge facing the wall. Second, heat. A toaster pushed
> under a low cabinet, or standing against the paper towel roll, puts heat and flame exactly where
> nothing can escape it. Slide it out so it has clear air above it and beside it, and move anything
> paper off that end of the counter. Then trace both cords. The kettle and the toaster cords stay off
> the sink side of the run, because that is the end where a wet hand meets them.

| 2 | Macro | A blade sliding into a bladed sleeve, then the sleeve laid into a drawer tray. | Sleeve enters from the bottom of frame and travels up. | or a bladed sleeve in a drawer | ~12s |
| 3 | Overhead | The toaster slid out from under the low cabinet and away from the paper towel roll, clear air around it. | Hold the cabinet edge at the top of the box and the toaster below it, so the clearance reads. | Slide it out so it has clear air above it and beside it | ~14s |

### Standardize, about 45 seconds

> Right now the clear run is your opinion, and an opinion does not survive a busy Tuesday. Give it an
> edge you can see. Run a short strip of tape along the underside of the counter lip, marking where
> the clear run starts and where it ends. If you would rather not tape the lip, trace the footprint of
> the board itself instead. Either way, the mark decides. Board, knife block and salt inside the line.
> The mixer, the bread bag and the post go outside it. Nobody has to be reminded and nobody has to be
> nagged, because the line is doing the telling. See Chapter 14, visual controls.

### Sustain, about 50 seconds

> The trigger already exists in your house, so use that one rather than inventing a new one. You press
> start on the dishwasher most nights. That press is the signal. Counter clears, and the board goes
> back down onto bare surface. In that order, as one linked action, not as a job you schedule for
> later. And the board is your gauge. Set it down. If it will not sit flat, something has arrived
> underneath it that does not live here. That wobble is not a nuisance, it is the check reporting in.
> Deal with the one thing under the board, and the counter is done for the night.

### Close

**Done state reveal.** Final overhead in the same frame as the opening shot. One clear run of counter,
at least as wide as your largest cutting board, holding the board, the knife block or strip, and the
salt. The kettle and the toaster standing back against the wall with their cords coiled. No post, no
fruit bowl, no charging phone anywhere on the surface.

> Here is the run you got back. One board, one knife block, one seasoning within reach. Nothing parked
> on the work surface overnight. That is the standard, and it is short enough that everyone in the
> house can hold it.

**Sustain trigger, shown once more.** The dishwasher start button pressed, and the counter clearing
behind it in the same frame, so the standard and the moment that holds it land together.

> When you press start on the dishwasher, the counter clears.

**Next teaser:** One step to the left, to the burners, where the hood filter has been quietly giving
up.

### Consolidated shot list

- Overhead locked off on the full counter run, framed once and reused for the before, the sweep, the bare run, the board width, the taped boundary and the final after.
- Three quarter tripod at counter height for placement moves, the cord routing, and the carry tray leaving frame.
- Macro at counter height for the crumb ring, the crumb tray, the backsplash seam, the tape end and the rocking board.
- Close on the plug leaving the socket, shot before any appliance is lifted.
- Close on knife handling: the drying rack, the magnetic strip, the block and the sleeve. Dedicated vertical take on the drying rack frame.
- Low three quarter on the cabinet directly under the board for the one motion bowl lift.
- Macro sequence on the uncovered findings: heat bloom, lifting sealant bead, cracked cord. One per frame.
- Insert on the dishwasher start button.

**Description hook:** The prep counter is the surface a kitchen loses first, because it is flat and it
accepts anything. Six passes, Sort through Sustain, to get one clear run back and keep it.
