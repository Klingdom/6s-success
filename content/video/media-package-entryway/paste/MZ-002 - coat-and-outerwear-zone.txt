VIDEO TITLE
Entryway Coat Rail Reset: Count Coats by Weather, Not by Number

DESCRIPTION
Somewhere on your coat rail is one good coat that cost real money and goes outside almost never. It survives every cull, while the coats you actually wear come down without a thought.

This is the coat rail just inside the front door, the one that fills up faster than any other spot in the house. In this episode you strip it bare, put back only the coats that earned their place through a full cold season of real outdoor use, and rebuild the whole zone around one idea. You count coats by weather, not by number. Each person gets one coat for each kind of weather your climate genuinely produces, and a second coat in the same category has to beat the first on a specific day you can name.
The rail matters because it is the first thing your hands reach for on the way out, and the first thing they drop on the way in. When it is overloaded, the coat you need is buried behind three you do not, and the floor below fills with bags nobody put away. The genuinely hard part here is that one expensive coat you almost never wear. Instead of giving it a guilty pass, you give it one honest trial on the front hook for two weeks, and let the answer settle itself.
You move through all six passes on this one rail: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety as the fourth pass, then Standardize and Sustain. Safety here is real and physical. A soaked winter coat is far heavier than the dry one you casually hang, so you hang your own weight on every hook, fit a proper wall anchor before one lets go, bring weight down low, and keep scarves and straps off the low hooks where a loop sits at a small child's neck height.
Finished looks like this. One coat per person for each kind of weather, wool on hangers and almost everything else on hooks, hats and gloves in one labeled bin you can read from the floor, umbrellas standing in the stand, and two hooks by the door left deliberately empty. Those two empty hooks are the whole standard. When every hook is full, one coat is in the wrong room, and the rail is quietly telling you to walk it back.

The idea that changed this zone for me is the two empty hooks by the door. They are not wasted space, they are a gauge. If the whole rail is full, one coat is in the wrong room and it is time to walk it back. Now the honest question: which coat is your one expensive coat that survives every cull but almost never goes outside? What is stopping you from giving it the two week trial on the front hook?
