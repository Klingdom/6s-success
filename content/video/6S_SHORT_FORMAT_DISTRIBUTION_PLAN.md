## The one decision that cannot be fixed later

**The 114 long videos must be framed for 9:16 at shoot time, or all 684 Shorts are compromised.**

Cropping a 16:9 frame to 9:16 discards about 56% of the picture. For a headless, hands-only format
that is usually survivable, because the subject is centred and small. But it is only survivable if the
camera was placed knowing it would happen. Framed without that knowledge, hands drift out of the safe
area, on-screen text sits in the crop zone, and the reveal shot loses its context.

**The fix costs nothing now and is impossible later.** Three rules, added to the shoot spec before
episode one:

1. **Shoot 4K 16:9 and compose every shot inside a centred 9:16 safe box.** The 4K resolution means a
   9:16 crop still delivers full vertical HD, so one shoot serves both outputs with no quality loss.
2. **Keep all on-screen text inside the 9:16 safe box**, or add it in post per output rather than
   in-camera.
3. **For the six segment-opening shots in every episode, capture a deliberate vertical take.** Six
   extra takes per episode is a small cost and it gives every Short a purpose-framed opening frame,
   which is the frame that decides whether it is watched at all.

The headless format is an unusual advantage here, and it is worth saying plainly: no presenter means
no talent scheduling, no continuity problem, and infinite reshoots. It is also the dominant aesthetic
in cleaning and organizing short-form. The format is already right; only the framing needs deciding.

**The cause is chronological, not careless.** The video plan is dated 2026-07-21 and the Part Nine
chapters were written from 2026-07-22 onward, so the plan predates the frozen names.

**Why it matters more here than anywhere else.** A Short's on-screen title *is* the zone name, and it
is often the only text a viewer reads. If the video says "Landing Zone" and the book says "the landing
spot", a viewer who buys the book cannot find the chapter they just watched. That is a discoverability
failure at the exact moment of conversion, which is the most expensive place to have one.

The two nightstand names are worse than cosmetic. "Nightstand Left" and "Nightstand Right" are purely
spatial and carry no meaning, where **"your own nightstand"** and **"the other sleeper's nightstand"**
carry the chapter's single most distinctive idea: you clean, they cull. A Short titled "Nightstand
Right" throws away the reason anyone would watch it.

**Action: reconcile the tracker to the book's frozen names before any script is written.** Twelve
edits, one file, and the twelve remaining rooms should be checked as their chapters are finished.

## Platform strategy

The three platforms are not one audience and should not receive one file.

| | YouTube Shorts | Instagram Reels | TikTok |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Funnel into the long video | Reach and saving | Discovery and volume |
| **Why it fits this content** | The parent long video already exists, so a Short has somewhere to send people | Home and organizing content over-indexes here, and saves are the strongest signal | Highest reach ceiling, lowest intent |
| **Duration ceiling that matters** | Comfortably takes the longest cuts | Shorter is stronger | Shortest is strongest |
| **The 60 to 90 second band** | Fine as-is | Needs a tighter alternate | Needs a tighter alternate |
| **Primary CTA** | The parent video | The resource page | The resource page |

**The practical consequence: the 60 to 90 second cuts need a second, tighter version.** The plan's
three duration bands were written for one platform. The long band works on YouTube and underperforms
on the other two. Cut the tight alternate at the same time as the main cut, not months later.

**Captions are not optional.** A headless, voiceover-driven format is entirely dependent on text when
watched with sound off, which is most of the time. Burn them in rather than relying on platform
auto-captions, because auto-captions will not spell the frozen zone names correctly and those names
are the thing that has to survive.

## Release sequencing: probe, then pour

684 assets need an order. The instinct is to work room by room, finishing the Entryway's thirty before
starting the Kitchen's forty-two. That is the wrong first move, because it takes weeks before you
learn anything.

**Phase 1, the probe.** Publish a deliberate spread rather than a block: a handful of rooms crossed
with all six S's. The purpose is to answer two questions before committing production capacity.

- **Which S travels?** Sort and Shine are the visually satisfying ones and should out-perform. Safety
  is the book's differentiator and the least proven in short-form. If Safety travels, that is a strong
  signal for the whole brand, not just the channel.
- **Which rooms travel?** The catalogue analysis in the affiliate plan showed Garage and Workshop
  carry the most product references. Whether they also carry the most *attention* is a separate
  question and worth knowing early.

**Phase 2, the pour.** Once the probe has a signal, produce in the order the signal indicates, and use
room-complete blocks for the rooms that won so playlists and the book's chapter structure line up.

**Cadence.** The constraint is not ideas, it is shooting days. One zone shoot yields one long video
plus six Shorts, so **production is measured in zones, not posts**. Set the cadence from how many zone
shoots per week are realistic, then divide the six Shorts across the week rather than dumping them.

## The funnel, and where it now points

This connects directly to work completed earlier today.

A Short's job is not to teach the whole method. It is to make one zone look solvable and hand the
viewer somewhere to go. There are exactly three destinations and they should not compete:

1. **The parent long video** — the natural YouTube CTA.
2. **The chapter resource page** — the compliant surface built in the affiliate plan. This is where a
   viewer who wants the kit goes, and it is the only place affiliate links are permitted.
3. **The book** — the destination for a viewer who wants the method rather than the room.

**One CTA per Short, chosen by platform**, not three stacked at the end.

**Affiliate disclosure applies to video.** If a Short points at a page carrying affiliate links, the
disclosure obligation follows it. Spoken and on-screen, not a description-box line. This is the same
Safety pass finding from the affiliate plan and it is easy to forget in a format with no presenter.

## Measurement: the one number that decides what to make more of

Views are the wrong metric for this content because the goal is not reach, it is a reader.

**Track saves and shares over views.** For a how-to format, a save means "I am going to do this",
which is the only proxy for intent that short-form gives you. A Short with a tenth of the views and
triple the save rate is the better asset and should decide the next production block.

Secondary, in order: watch-through on the tight cuts, click-through to the resource page, and
resource-page conversion. The tracker already has status columns per episode; add per-Short outcome
columns to the same file rather than starting a second one.
