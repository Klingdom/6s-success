1 · The concept

A dedicated distribution center is premature for one metro; a storefront alone can’t fulfill or dispatch. The launch answer is a combination micro-hub: ~3,000 sq ft of flex/industrial-condo space that holds inventory, ships online orders, welcomes walk-in shoppers into a live showroom, and launches service crews, all under one modest lease. It proves the unit economics of a whole metro before any second location.

4 · Selling every product from the center
  • Walk-in showroom. Live room vignettes (a real Entryway, Kitchen, etc.) let people touch the kits, decks, and books, then buy on the spot.
  • Buy-online / pickup (BOPIS). Order on 6s-success.com, pick up at the counter same day.
  • Local delivery. Same- or next-day van delivery across the Treasure Valley; kits arrive with the service crew when a reset is booked.
  • Ship anywhere. National e-commerce fulfillment (books, games, decks) picked and packed from the same racks.
  • Service attach. Every in-home reset and consult sells the kits, decks, and app that keep the room reset, the highest-margin cross-sell.
5 · The scalable service center — crews sized to demographics

Service capacity is not fixed to the building; it is a dial. One crew of two handles roughly 20 room-resets a month. As penetration of the serviceable market (SAM, ~130k Treasure Valley homeowner households over $75k income + recent movers) rises, you simply add crews, and eventually certified affiliates, without changing the footprint.
