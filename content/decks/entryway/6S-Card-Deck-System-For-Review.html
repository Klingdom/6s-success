By S (the header stripe): the chaos-to-calm ramp, so you can sort the whole home by activity.

2 · The card taxonomy · what is in every deck
System cards (4 per deck)
Deck Cover
The room, its signature colour, and the deck count. The card that fronts the box.
How to Use · The Ways to Play
The modes: full room reset, whole-home S-pass, one zone in one sitting, daily draw, family challenge, audit night, safety blitz.
The 6S Colour Legend
The chaos-to-calm ramp and what each S means, in canon order: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain (Safety is the 4th).
The Room Kit
The exact cleaning and organization products this room needs, grouped by the shared core plus room-specific add-ons, from the 123-product library.
Room activity cards (7 per deck · not micro-zone based)
Primary Purpose
The room's one job, in a sentence. What it is for, and what must always work. Read this first, every reset points back to it.
Family Values Sharing
A household conversation card: what should this room feel like, who uses it, what everyone agrees to keep true here.
Before Photos
How and what to photograph before you start: the honest starting point, the angles, the drawers to open. The record you will beat.
6S Audit
Score the room against the six S's, a quick, kind check-in (not an inspection). Catch drift while it is small.
Safety Blitz
A room-wide hazard hunt using the five safety questions (trip, burn or fire, poison-choke-strangle, fall-cut-crush, water and electricity). Find it, fix it now.
Room Signature · After
Take the ideal-state photo of the finished room. That photo becomes the room's standard, the picture of what right looks like.
Keeping Rhythm
The three cadences for this room: the daily touch, the weekly check-in, and the seasonal 6S event. Anchored to moments that already happen.
Micro-zone cards (per zone)
Zone Card
Names the micro-zone, its purpose, what a reset zone looks like, and the specific products it needs. The anchor for that zone's six S cards.

Then one card per S, for that zone (explicit instructions + the exact products):

S Sort
Decide what stays. Touch each item once; keep what earns its place; route the rest out the Five Doors (Donate, Sell, Store, Recycle, Throw). Red-tag the maybes with a date.
S Straighten
Give everything a home where it is used. Group like with like, set the point-of-use spot, add the organizer, label the home so anyone can return it.
S Shine
Clean to inspect, with the exact inputs and the nine-rule method (top to bottom, dry before wet, mist the cloth, two cloths, match the cleaner, dwell time, clean-to-inspect-and-flag).
S Safety
Ask the five questions of this zone and fix the hazard on the spot: anchor, cover, move up high, unplug, or clear the path.
S Standardize
Write the one-line standard and take the zone's ideal-state photo, so the reset is repeatable and anyone can see what right looks like.
S Sustain
Bolt this zone's upkeep to a moment that already happens, the daily touch that keeps it from sliding back.

6 · Sample card descriptions · the explicit-instruction format
PRIMARY PURPOSEEntryway

The launch pad. The entryway's one job: everything you reach for on the way out, and set down on the way in, has a home, and nothing lives on the floor.

  • Read this before any reset. Every zone card below serves this purpose.
  • The test: can a family member leave in under a minute without hunting for keys, a bag, or shoes?
SORT · Coat & OuterwearEntryway

Decide what stays on the hooks and what leaves the entryway.

  • Inputs: 3 sorting bins or the Five Doors labels; red tags; a season box.
  • 1. Touch each coat, bag, and scarf once. Keep only what this household wears this season.
  • 2. Route the rest out the Five Doors: Donate, Sell, Store (off-season), Recycle, Throw.
  • 3. Off-season outerwear goes to the season box, not the daily hooks.
  • 4. Not sure? Red-tag it with a date; if it is not worn by then, it goes.
  • Flag: more coats than hooks means the zone is over capacity, decide, do not add hooks.
SHINE · Landing ZoneEntryway

Clean the landing tray and surface to inspect, and reveal what hides underneath.

  • Inputs: 2 microfiber cloths (one damp, one dry), all-purpose cleaner, a washable tray liner.
  • 1. Clear the tray. Work top to bottom, dry dust before any wet wipe.
  • 2. Mist the cloth, not the surface. Wipe the tray and the console top.
  • 3. Follow with the dry cloth. Fresh liner in the tray.
  • Inspect & flag: a sticky ring (a leak), grit (tracked-in dirt, check the mat), or a wobble (a safety fix).
SAFETY BLITZEntryway

A two-minute hazard hunt across the whole entryway, using the five questions. Find it, fix it now.

  • Trip: is the floor and path clear of shoes, bags, and cords? Clear it.
  • Burn or fire: nothing blocking the door as an exit? Keep it clear.
  • Poison, choke, strangle: keys, batteries, small items up and away from small children?
  • Fall, cut, crush: is a tall shelf or hook rail anchored? Anchor it.
  • Water & electricity: chargers and outlets away from wet, tracked-in floors?
7 · Products & next steps

Cleaning & organization products are built in. Every Room Kit card and every zone card lists exact inputs drawn from the 123-product, 7-family library (cleaning supplies, cleaning tools, storage & organization, safety, visual controls, sort & routing). The shared core (cloths, cleaners, bins, labels, safety) recurs across zones; room-specific add-ons finish each one, the same overlap that powers the kits and the shop.
