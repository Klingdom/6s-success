# Entryway Deck — Game-Theory & Incentive-Design Review

*Reviewer lens: mechanism design, incentive structures, cooperative vs. competitive dynamics, engagement/retention loops, gamification of real-world behavior. Builds on the four-expert Improvement Plan; does not repeat it. Canon respected: Sort · Straighten · Shine · **Safety (4th)** · Standardize · Sustain. Metrics respected: **seconds-to-launch (<60, zero hunting)**, **items-on-surface (≤3)**, **empty-hooks (=2)**.*

---

## 1. Diagnosis — what the current "game" actually is

Read as a game, the deck today is a **checklist with theme paint**, not a game. That is not an insult; it is the specific problem to fix. Here is the honest read:

- **No real decisions.** Every card tells you the one correct action. A game needs *meaningful choices under scarcity* — where to spend limited time/energy, what to skip, what to defend. The deck has zero scarcity model, so there is nothing to be clever about. Engagement in chores comes from **agency over sequence and stakes**, and there is none.
- **The six "modes" (Card 2) are labels, not rules.** "Family challenge · deal and race, or cooperate" is a *category*, not a playable spec — no win condition, no turn structure, no scoring, no end. A player cannot sit down and play it. Same for "daily draw" and "audit night."
- **Scoring is invisible and unlinked to progress.** The Master Black Belt's three metrics are excellent — they are countable, honest, hard to fake — but they live only on the audit/purpose cards. **A game needs its score on the table the whole time.** Right now the metrics are a post-hoc grade, not a live scoreboard driving play.
- **The boring-but-critical steps are the ones most likely to be skipped, and nothing protects them.** Shine (the deepest step, per the deck's own framing) and Safety (the 4th-S differentiator, the *entire brand claim*) carry the highest effort and the lowest visible payoff. In any incentive system, the high-effort/low-visible-reward step is the one that gets dropped. The deck currently relies on virtue to hold them. Virtue is not a mechanic.
- **The one genuine engagement asset is under-used: the before/after photo.** The signature photo (Cards 7 & 10) is a beautiful *loss-aversion + progress-made-visible* device and it is treated as a documentation step, not a reward. This is the single biggest missed opportunity.
- **Uncertainty exists in the sibling board games ("life happens" events) but not here.** The deck has no draw randomness that matters, so the "daily draw" mode is just a shuffled to-do list — which risks the worst chore-gamification anti-pattern: **guilt on a miss**.

**Net:** the deck reliably *instructs* the reset. It does not yet *drive* it, because it never converts effort into a legible, escalating, defendable reward — and it never gives the household a reason to come back tomorrow. Below are mechanics to fix that, keyed to the mode or card where they live.

---

## 2. Mechanic & incentive improvements

### 2.1 Put a live score on the table — the "Calm Score" (all modes; anchor on Cards 5, 8, 10)

Convert the three metrics into a single **10-point Calm Score that is always visible**, not just graded at the end:

| Metric | Points | How it's counted (honest, hard to fake) |
|---|---|---|
| Seconds-to-launch | 0–4 | Actually time one person leaving. <60s & zero hunting = 4; <90s = 2; hunted = 0. |
| Items on any surface (worst surface in the zone/room) | 0–3 | ≤3 = 3; 4–6 = 1; 7+ = 0. Count the *worst* surface, not the average — prevents hiding a pile. |
| Empty hooks | 0–3 | Exactly 2 empty = 3; 1 or 3+ = 1; 0 = 0. Note it is **=2, not "more is better"** — over-emptying is not a win. |

- **Why on the table the whole time:** progress you can *see climbing* is the core retention loop. Print a **Calm Track** (0–10) on the box insert or a punch-out dial (reuse the board-game friction-dial art — same manufacturing) and move a token as you finish zones. The reset stops being "a chore list" and becomes "walk the needle from 3 to 10."
- **Card 5 (Primary Purpose)** becomes the scoreboard-definition card. **Card 8 (Audit)** already scores 0–2 per S — keep that as the *diagnostic* score, but add the Calm Score as the *outcome* score so effort and result are separated (you can do all six S's and still measure whether launch actually got faster).
- **Anti-gaming guardrail (print on Card 8):** *"Count the worst surface, not your best one. The score is for the house, not the household — a low score is a system to fix, never a person to blame."* This preserves the deck's existing non-blame voice while closing the metric-gaming hole.

### 2.2 Introduce scarcity: the **Time-Token** economy (Full reset, One-zone, Family modes)

Give players a fixed budget of **Time Tokens** (1 token ≈ 5 minutes) drawn for the session. Each S-step on a zone card has a token cost printed in a small footer pill (the Improvement Plan already proposes a time/effort footer — put a *number* on it):

- Sort 1 · Straighten 1 · Shine 2 · Safety 1 · Standardize ½ · Sustain (free — it's a habit, not a task).

Now there is a **real decision**: with 8 tokens you cannot deep-clean all five zones. You must choose *which zones get the full loop and which get a lighter pass* — exactly the real-world triage a busy household does anyway. This is the difference between a checklist and a game: **the deck stops pretending you have infinite time and starts rewarding good triage.** The Landing Spot's "highest-leverage square foot" framing (Card 1) becomes a genuine *opening-move* decision rather than a suggestion.

### 2.3 Reward the boring-but-critical steps so they are never skipped (Shine → Cards 15/22/29/36/43; Safety → Cards 9/16/23/30/37/44)

This is the central incentive problem. Three complementary mechanics:

1. **Gate the reward behind the boring step (interlock, not exhortation).** A zone only counts toward the Calm Score and only earns its **Standard** if Shine *and* Safety are done — because the deck's own logic is that "cleaning inspects" and Safety acts on what Shine flags. Print on every Standardize card: *"No Shine, no photo. No Safety, no lock. A zone isn't standard until it's clean and safe."* This makes the two skippable steps **structurally load-bearing** rather than optional virtue.
2. **Make Safety the highest-value single action, and make it feel like a *catch, not a chore*.** Reframe each Safety card as a **"Hazard Bounty."** Every hazard the Shine pass flags and you fix is worth a bonus Calm point (cap +2/room so it can't be farmed). The five-question Safety Blitz (Card 9) becomes a **scored mini-round** — "how many of the five did you find and fix?" — which turns the deck's differentiator into the most *satisfying* two minutes in the game, not the most forgettable. Give it the warm-alert colour/shield the Graphic Artist already specced, so it is visually the *jackpot* step, not the grey one.
3. **Shine gets a visible-payoff kicker.** Shine's problem is that its result (clean) photographs identically to "wiped-ish." Fix with the **grey-film reveal**: the deck already notes the film under the tray "tells you how long since anyone did this properly." Make that a scored observation — *"Wipe once, look at the cloth. A dirty cloth is points earned, not shame — it's proof the Shine was real."* Rewarding the *evidence of effort* (the dirty cloth, the flagged hazard) is how you pay out an invisible step.

### 2.4 Useful uncertainty without punishing chores (Daily-draw & Family modes; new small "Life Happens" sub-deck)

Randomness makes a game replayable, but the anti-pattern in chore-games is **randomness that adds work and triggers guilt on a bad day.** Design the uncertainty so it is *always survivable*:

- **Daily draw = one card, one small win, floor of zero.** The daily task must be completable in ≤5 minutes and the *only* failure state is "didn't draw today" — never "drew and failed." Print on Card 11 (Keeping Rhythm) or a new daily-draw card: *"Draw one. Do the one thing. That's a full day's win. Miss a day? The streak forgives one — just draw tomorrow."* (See streak design, 2.6.)
- **"Life Happens" events, borrowed from the board games but de-fanged for real life.** A tiny event sub-deck (guests coming, mail flood, muddy-boot day, new-shoes-arrived) that *names the real disruption and hands you the counter-move* — e.g., "Muddy day → run the boot-tray Shine (Card 29), +1 Calm." The event never *removes* points you earned by cleaning; it only offers a **bonus objective**. Uncertainty should feel like an opportunity draw, not a penalty die. This keeps the honest tie to real life (life *does* keep arriving — the deck says so on Card 1) without ever making the game a source of guilt.

### 2.5 Pick the right social frame — and it is **mostly co-op, with a light-competitive skin for kids** (Family challenge, Card 2/6)

Game-theory read of this audience (a household sharing one physical space):

- **The reset is a common-pool resource / collective-action problem**, not a zero-sum contest. If you make adults *compete* to reset the shared entryway, you get the classic failure: people optimize their score, not the room (someone "wins" the coats while the floor stays filthy). **Co-op is the correct default** and the sibling flagship agrees. Keep the shared Calm Track as the single team objective.
- **But pure co-op has its own failure mode: the "quarterback / one-adult-does-everything" problem**, where one person directs and others disengage. Counter it with **secret-role or hidden-assignment light structure**: each player privately draws a zone they are personally responsible for, and the *team* only maxes the Calm Score if *every* zone clears. This forces distributed effort — you can't win by carrying.
- **Light competition works only as a within-family, handicapped skin for kids** (see 2.7), never adult-vs-adult on the shared room. Frame it as **"beat the clock / beat last time,"** not "beat each other." The opponent is entropy and yesterday's score — the deck's own "the picture the door has to match from now on" (Card 10) is already a *beat-your-past-self* mechanic; make it explicit.

### 2.6 Loss-aversion & streaks for the Sustain loop (Cards 11, 18, 25, 32, 39, 46 — every Sustain card)

Sustain is where resets die, and it is a pure retention problem. Loss aversion (people hate losing a thing ~2× more than they like gaining it) is the strongest lever here — but it must be tuned to *motivate, not shame*:

- **The "Standard Photo" is the thing you can lose.** Once a zone is Standardized (photo taped inside the cupboard, Card 10/17), it holds a **"Kept" token**. The nightly reset *defends* the token. This converts abstract "keep it nice" into a concrete loss-averse stake: *don't lose the token you earned.* Print on Sustain cards: *"You earned the Standard. The nightly pass is how you keep it — one minute defends a whole reset."*
- **Streaks with a built-in forgiveness rule (this is critical).** A streak counter for the daily reset is the retention engine, **but an un-forgiving streak is a guilt machine** — one missed day and people quit entirely (the "broken streak → abandon" cliff). Design in a **"grace day": the streak survives one miss per week.** This is the single most important anti-guilt mechanic in the whole deck. The goal is a home that stays reset, not a perfect record.
- **Anchor the streak to an existing event, not a clock** — the deck's own best idea (Card 11: "bolt upkeep to a moment that already happens"). The streak advances on "coat off → tray" at the door, so it needs no separate remembering. The habit *is* the game action.

### 2.7 Fair kid-vs-adult handicapping (Family mode; Cards 6, 21, 28)

To make family play fair and to actually recruit kids into the habit:

- **Handicap by role, not by scoring the same task differently.** Give kids **zone-appropriate, high-visibility jobs** (shoes paired soles-down, two hooks left empty, mat squared) that are *genuinely* worth full points — the deck already sets kid-height hooks/shelves (Cards 21, 28), so lean in: kids own the low zones, adults own the high/heavy/safety zones.
- **The "empty-hooks = 2" metric is a perfect kid job** — it's a countable, binary, satisfying check a six-year-old can own and audit. Assign it as a permanent kid role.
- **Kids get a shorter loop.** Children run **Sort → Straighten → Sustain** (find it a home, put it away, keep it) and are *not* scored on Shine chemistry or Safety hazard-fixing (which are adult jobs for real safety reasons — button batteries, anchoring, cleaners). This is honest handicapping: kids play the sub-game that suits them and still contribute to the shared Calm Score.
- **Print a picture-label variant** (Improvement Plan already notes picture labels) so pre-readers can play the audit — the *game* teaches the *labels* that make the system hold.

### 2.8 Win states & session length (per mode) — a reset must *end* with a payoff

Every mode needs an explicit finish line and a **celebration beat**, or effort feels bottomless (the "grinding" anti-pattern):

- **Every mode ends on the after-photo + Calm Score reveal.** The win *is* the matched-pair reveal (before → after) plus the number climbing. This is the deck's best asset (2.1) used as the confetti moment. Print: *"You're done when the photo's taken and the score's read aloud."*
- **Define the win as *reaching a target, then stopping* — not maximizing.** Because the metrics are `=2 hooks` and `≤3 items`, **over-doing is explicitly not more winning.** Say so: *"10 Calm is a win. There is no 11. Stop and enjoy the room."* This kills the over-optimization / "clean forever" anti-pattern and respects that the goal is a livable room, not a showroom.

### 2.9 Anti-patterns to design against (call-outs for the How-to card and Card 8)

| Anti-pattern | Where it bites | Guardrail (print it) |
|---|---|---|
| **Guilt/shame on a miss** | Daily draw, streaks | Grace day (2.6); "a low score is a system, not a person" (Card 8). |
| **Gaming the metric** (hide the pile in a drawer, over-empty hooks) | Calm Score | Score the *worst* surface; hooks = *exactly* 2; drawers count as surfaces (Card 34's ≤3 rule extends inside). |
| **Grinding / clean-forever** | Full reset | Hard win cap at 10; "there is no 11" (2.8). |
| **One person carries** (co-op quarterback) | Family mode | Secret zone-assignment; every zone must clear (2.5). |
| **Skipping the boring step** | Shine, Safety | Interlock: no Shine → no photo; no Safety → no lock (2.3). |
| **Randomness that adds work** | Life Happens | Events only add *bonus* objectives, never remove earned points (2.4). |

## 3. Print-ready mode specs (drop-in for the How-to card, Card 2)

Three fully specified modes covering the three real play occasions: a big satisfying together-session, a daily keep-it-going loop, and a fair family race. Each fits on the How-to card as a titled block.

---

### MODE A — **The Reset Run** (the flagship: full room, together)
> **Players** 1–5 · **Time** 45–60 min · **Feel** co-op, satisfying, once-a-season
>
> **Setup.** Take the Before photo (Card 7) from the marked spot. Count your start: time one person leaving, count items on the worst surface, count empty hooks → set the **Calm token** on the Track (usually 2–4). Draw your **Time Tokens** (2 per 15 min you have).
>
> **Play.** Each player secretly takes a zone (Cards 12/19/26/33/40) they're responsible for. On your turn, spend tokens to run the loop **in order — Sort · Straighten · Shine · Safety · Standardize** — on your zone. **A zone only locks (earns its Standard photo) when Shine *and* Safety are both done.** Every hazard you find and fix from the Shine flags = **+1 Calm (Hazard Bounty, max +2 per zone).**
>
> **Win.** All five zones locked, or tokens run out. Retake the After photo from the same spot, re-count the three metrics, read the **Calm Score** aloud. **10 is a win — there is no 11.** Tape the photo inside the cupboard: that's the target the door has to match from now on.

---

### MODE B — **The Daily Draw** (keep-it loop: 5 minutes, every day)
> **Players** 1+ · **Time** ≤5 min · **Feel** solo, low-stakes, streak-building
>
> **Play.** Draw one card. Do the one small thing it names (a Sustain card = tonight's reset; a zone card = one quick pass). That is a **full day's win** — floor of zero, you cannot "fail" a draw.
>
> **Streak.** Bolt it to walking in the door (coat off → tray). Each day done advances your **streak**. **The streak forgives one miss per week** — miss a day, just draw the next. The nightly pass **defends** every Standard photo you've earned: keep the Kept tokens, don't lose the reset.
>
> **Life Happens (optional).** Some days, flip a Life-Happens card (guests, muddy boots, mail flood). It names the disruption and hands you the counter-move for a **bonus** Calm point. It never takes points away — a bad day can only offer extra credit, never a penalty.

---

### MODE C — **Family Race the Clock** (fair, handicapped, 20 minutes)
> **Players** 2–6, mixed ages · **Time** 15–20 min · **Feel** co-op with a friendly timer, kid-fair
>
> **Setup.** Set a 15-min sand timer. Deal zones by role: **adults** take the high/heavy/chemical zones (Shine + Safety); **kids** take the low zones and run the short loop **Sort · Straighten · Sustain** (find a home, put it away, keep it). The **empty-hooks = 2** check is a permanent kid job — countable and satisfying.
>
> **Play.** Everyone works at once against the timer. You're not racing *each other* — you're racing **last time's Calm Score and the clock.** Kids earn full points for their zones; nobody is scored on a step that isn't their job.
>
> **Win.** When the timer runs out, count the three metrics together and compare to last time's number. **Beat your own record = the whole family wins.** Take the after-photo; read the score aloud; the winner is the room.
