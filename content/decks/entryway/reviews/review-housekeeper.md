# Entryway Deck v2 - Housekeeper's Review

*Professional cleaning tips and tricks keyed to card numbers. Product TYPES only, no brands. Nothing unsafe: never mix cleaning chemicals.*

---

## Top cleaning gaps in the deck

The deck has an excellent method skeleton (top-to-bottom, dry-before-wet, mist the cloth, two-cloth, colour-code). What it lacks is the professional *how* for the specific soils an entryway actually gets. The biggest gaps:

1. **Road salt / white salt haze.** The single hardest entryway soil in winter, and the deck never says how a pro removes it. Salt is alkaline mineral crust; it needs an acidic cleaner (diluted white vinegar solution) to dissolve, not a neutral cleaner, and never a wet scrub that just spreads the brine. This is a real omission on the boot tray, mat, and floor cards.
2. **Dwell time is vague.** "A few seconds" is repeated, but real soils (salt film, dried mud, sticky ring, door glass) need concrete dwell windows and the reason you never scrub dry.
3. **Scuff marks** on walls, doors, and skirting get "lift the scuff" with no technique. Pros reach for a melamine foam pad (used barely-damp, light pressure, spot-tested) for rubber/shoe scuffs.
4. **Door-glass / sidelight streak-free method** is under-specified. Direction-crossing is mentioned once; the deck misses the low-light rule, the dry-buff finish, and why you never clean glass in direct sun.
5. **Odor control.** Entryways are the smelliest room for their size (wet wool, damp boots, mildew mat) and the deck barely addresses deodorizing at the source.
6. **Microfiber laundering and cloth-life** get one line (no softener). Missing: wash warm, no bleach, air or low-heat dry, no fabric softener AND no dryer sheets, and when a cloth is dead.
7. **Realistic time per zone** is only given for the landing spot. Each zone needs an honest estimate so people plan the session.
8. **Grit management** (the whole point of an entryway) is treated as "vacuum," but the sequence pros use — dry-remove ALL grit first, work the mat/tray outdoors, never wet grit into a slurry — deserves emphasis.
9. **Mat depth of clean.** Rubber-backed vs. woven vs. coir each launder differently; the deck says "wash if washable" without the how, the dry-flat rule, or the two-mat (coarse-outside / absorbent-inside) logic that makes a mat system actually work.
10. **Chemical safety with the products themselves** — ventilation, never mixing, glove use, spot-testing, and the one rule that matters most: never combine an acid (vinegar) with bleach or with an ammonia glass cleaner.

## Tips keyed to card numbers

**Card 4 (Room Kit).** Add to the kit list, because these are what actually beat entryway soils: (a) plain **white vinegar** to dilute into a mild acidic solution for salt haze and hard-water/mineral film — the one cleaner a neutral pH product cannot handle; (b) a **melamine foam pad** for scuffs on painted walls, doors, and skirting; (c) a **grout/detail brush with stiffer bristles** for boot-tray corners; (d) **baking soda** as a dry deodorizer for trays, bins, and mats. Add the rule in bold: **never mix products** — vinegar with bleach or with an ammonia-based glass cleaner makes toxic gas; use one cleaner, rinse, then the next. Note microfiber care fully: wash warm, no bleach, no softener AND no dryer sheets, air-dry or low heat.

**Card 4 / two-cloth rule.** Reinforce the pro version: the "clean" cloth is *damp, not wet* — wrung until no drip. A dripping cloth is why doorway floors streak and stay slick. Fold the cloth in quarters so you get eight clean faces; flip to a fresh face the moment the one you are using looks grey (the "fold-and-flip" technique). One grey face smears soil instead of lifting it.

**Card 9 / 16 / 23 / 30 / 37 / 44 (Safety cards).** Add a chemical-safety line to the room's safety thinking, since the deck is cleaning-heavy: work with the door cracked for ventilation when using vinegar or glass cleaner in a tight entry, wear gloves for repeated salt/vinegar work, and spot-test any acidic or melamine treatment on a hidden corner first (vinegar can dull natural stone and some sealed finishes; melamine can burn matte/satin paint if pressed hard).

**Card 12 / 15 (Landing Spot Shine).** For the **grey key/coin film** in the tray and under it: that film is metal oxide plus skin oil, not just dust. A neutral cleaner lifts it, but let it dwell ~30 seconds before wiping so you are not burnishing it into the finish. If the tray is unsealed wood or leather, damp-wipe only — never soak. Realistic time: **15–20 min** done properly.

**Card 15 (Landing Spot - mirror).** Streak-free mirror method in full: clean glass in **indirect light** (never direct sun — it flash-dries the cleaner into streaks before you can buff). Mist cloth, wipe side-to-side, then **dry-buff with a separate dedicated glass/dry cloth** in a top-to-bottom pass so a missed streak shows against the light. For stubborn spray-fleck (hairspray, water spots) let the cleaner dwell 10 seconds first. A dry glass cloth used only on glass — never on greasy surfaces — is what gives the shine.

**Card 15 (dock & cable).** Good call keeping it dry-only. Add: for grime on plastic, use a cloth barely damp with neutral cleaner and dry immediately; never spray directly at ports. A dry soft detail brush or a bit of compressed air clears port lint better than a cloth.

**Card 22 (Coats & Outerwear Shine).** **Odor is the real job here.** Wet wool and damp bin liners go musty. Before re-boxing: let anything damp fully air-dry, and sprinkle a little **baking soda in the hat/glove bin, leave 15 min, then shake/vacuum out** to kill musty smell at the source rather than masking it. For the umbrella stand, dry it fully **inverted** — standing water is what breeds the smell and the rust you flag. Realistic time: **20–30 min.**

**Card 22 (wall behind hooks - scuff).** The shoulder/bag scuff is usually rubber and shoe-black transfer. Reach for the **melamine foam pad, barely damp, light circular pressure**, spot-test first — it erases scuffs a cloth cannot. Follow with a dry wipe. Do not press hard on matte paint or it burnishes a shiny patch.

**Card 26 / 29 (Shoes & Boots Shine) — the salt card.** This is the deck's biggest missing trick. **White salt haze and road-salt crust need an acid, not a neutral cleaner.** Method a pro uses on the boot tray, the rack, and the salt-marked floor: mix a **mild solution of white vinegar in warm water (roughly 1 part vinegar to 4 parts water)**, apply, let it **dwell 2–3 minutes** to dissolve the salt crust, then wipe and rinse with clean water. Never dry-scrub salt — it is abrasive and grinds micro-scratches into floor sealant. For **dried mud, always work it dry first**: let mud fully dry, knock/brush it off outdoors, THEN wet-clean the residue. Wetting mud makes slurry that spreads. Realistic time: **20–25 min**, more with heavy winter salt.

**Card 29 (boot tray).** After the vinegar-and-rinse, dry the tray fully before it returns — a wet tray re-wets the floor (deck says this, good). Add a **thin layer of baking soda or a few pebbles/tray liner** to absorb drips and odor between deep cleans. Scrub corners with the stiffer detail brush where salt cakes hardest.

**Card 29 (floor under/around rack - salt-etched finish).** Where you flag "finish worn dull/grey under the tray": that is salt etching. Clean it with the diluted vinegar solution to stop ongoing damage, but note that a **neutral pH floor cleaner is what you use day-to-day** — repeated acid dulls sealant too, so reserve vinegar for actual salt/mineral film, not routine mopping.

**Card 33 / 36 (Bench/Console - upholstered seat).** For a padded seat that has taken outdoor grit and body oil: vacuum seams first (deck says this), then for spots use an **upholstery-safe cleaner matched to the fabric code** and blot — never rub, which drives soil into the pad and can leave a ring. Work from the outside of a spot inward to avoid haloing. Let it dry fully; sitting on a damp seat re-soils it. Realistic time: **20–30 min.**

**Card 36 (drawer/runner grit).** Wipe runners dry after cleaning and, if they drag, a **dry PTFE/silicone or a rub of candle wax** frees a sticky wooden runner — do not oil it, oil grabs grit. (Flag only per deck rule; this is the fix for the safety pass.)

**Card 40 / 43 (Door, Mat & Floor Shine) — door glass & mat depth.** Door glass/sidelight: same streak-free rule as the mirror — **indirect light, mist the cloth, wipe one way, dry-buff the crossing way.** For fingerprints around the handle and letterbox, let neutral cleaner dwell 10–15 seconds. The **threshold grit line** the mat never reaches: vacuum the crevice first with the crevice tool, then damp-wipe — do not wet-drag the grit.

**Card 43 (mats — the core of the room).** Full mat method by type: **Rubber-backed:** hose both sides, scrub the top with neutral cleaner (vinegar solution if salt-crusted), rinse, and **hang or stand on edge to dry — never lay a wet rubber mat back down**, trapped moisture rots the backing and marks the floor. **Woven/washable:** vacuum both faces, machine-wash cool/gentle if the label allows, **air-dry flat — heat shrinks and curls the backing.** **Coir/coarse outside mat:** it does not wash well; **beat and vacuum only**, never soak (coir holds water and rots). Return only bone-dry. Realistic time: **25–35 min** including mat drying (start the mats first so they dry while you do the door).

**Card 43 / 44 (mat SYSTEM logic).** Make explicit why two mats: a **coarse scraper mat OUTSIDE** to knock grit off, an **absorbent mat INSIDE** to catch moisture. That pairing removes the most grit and water before it reaches the house — the whole point of the zone. A single mat, or two of the same type, does half the job.

**Card 44 (mat replacement test).** Excellent test already. Add the pro tell: when the fibres stay **matted flat and no longer spring back**, or the rubber backing **crumbles/sheds when flexed**, the mat is finished regardless of looks — it is now abrasive drag on the floor, not protection.

**Card 46 / 11 / 32 (Sustain / seasonal).** Add weather-cued upkeep: **Winter** — wipe salt off the tray and floor weekly with the vinegar solution before it etches; shake mats more often. **Autumn** — leaves and their tannin stain wet floors; sweep the threshold more and dry-remove before mopping. **Rain** — dry the umbrella stand after every wet day so it never sits in water. Anchor these to weather, not the calendar, the way the deck anchors dailies to events.

---

*Standing rules across every Shine card: dwell, do not scrub dry; match pH to soil (neutral for everyday, acid only for salt/mineral film); dry-remove grit before any water touches it; one damp cloth to clean, one dry to finish; fold-and-flip to a clean face; never mix chemicals; ventilate and spot-test.*
