# TCG Mechanics for Cleaning Events — the complete 6S card line

Reviewer role: published tabletop designer, continuing from the Entryway deck review (`review-game-designer.md`). This applies **Pokémon TCG** and **Magic: The Gathering** systems to run real **cleaning events** across the complete three-tier set (HOME · MICRO-ZONE · STEP) plus the leaner Family Edition.

Canon held throughout: **Sort · Straighten · Shine · Safety (4th) · Standardize · Sustain.** Safety is a first-class type, not a keyword.

Design stance carried over from the first review: **a mechanic ships only if a player cannot "win" it without the room actually being reset.** Every event below terminates in the same honesty interlock I specced — the **6S Audit as the scorecard** and the **After-photo-matches-Standard** gate. TCG systems are the *engine that motivates and paces* the real work; the Audit + photo is the *referee* that keeps it true.

---

## 0. Shared foundation — mapping the three tiers to TCG roles, and one resource

Before either game's mapping, three decisions make both coherent and make the physical product simple:

**Tier → TCG role (same for both games):**
- **HOME cards → the board state & the referee.** Purpose = the win-condition card. 6S Audit = the scorecard/prize track. Before/After = the photo interlock. Room Kit = your resource base (see below). Family Values / Keeping Rhythm / Safety Blitz = special encounter cards.
- **MICRO-ZONE cards (~114) → the "creatures" you play and evolve.** Each zone is the unit that changes state: Messy → Cleaned → Standardized → Sustained. FRONT = the win-state target; BACK = inputs + ordered steps + flags (from my template spec).
- **STEP cards (6 universal method cards) → your shared toolbox / rules reference.** Sort, Straighten, Shine (nine rules), Safety (five questions), Standardize, Sustain. In Pokémon these behave like **Trainer/Supporter** cards; in MTG like the **rules of the stack** you resolve in order. One small shared set applies to every zone — this is what stops 114 zones needing 114× the text.

**ONE resource economy for both games — the Time/Effort token.** Pokémon calls its resource *Energy*; MTG calls its resource *mana*. **Unify them into a single physical punchboard token: the ⏱/💪 Time-Effort token (TE).** One token = one ~5-minute unit of real focused effort. This means the two game modes share the same components and teach the same honest lesson: **a reset costs real time, and you must budget it.** The Room Kit "produces" TE (it is the *land* that lets you do work — no cleaner, no Shine).

**Step costs (the TE economy, shared by both games):**
| S-type | TE cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sort | 1 | decisions, fast |
| Straighten | 1 | placement |
| **Shine** | **3** | the deep one (nine rules) — costs the most, honestly |
| Safety | 1 (+instant) | act on flags on the spot |
| Standardize | 1 | photograph & tape |
| Sustain | 0 upkeep + streak | free daily, paid in consistency |

This TE table is the spine both games plug into.

## PART 1A — POKÉMON TCG mapped to the 6S line

Pokémon's genius is **collect → level → beat gyms → earn badges**, powered by **type-matched energy** and **evolution**. It maps almost 1:1 onto "reset a home room by room."

| Pokémon system | 6S mapping | Concrete rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Types** | **The six S's** | Every action has an S-type. STEP cards are the type reference; MICRO-ZONE backs list which types (steps) that zone needs and in what order. |
| **Energy (attach 1/turn)** | **Time-Effort tokens** | To perform an S on a zone you must "attach" its TE cost (see table). You have a limited TE pool per session — this forces triage, the Lean heart of the game. |
| **Evolution (Basic→Stage1→Stage2)** | **A zone's state ladder** | **Messy (Basic) → Cleaned (Stage 1, after Sort+Straighten+Shine+Safety) → Standardized (Stage 2, after the after-photo is taped) → Sustained ("shiny", after a streak of upkeep).** You physically flip/advance the MICRO-ZONE card up the ladder. |
| **Evolution requirement** | **Do the real steps in order** | You cannot evolve Messy→Cleaned without paying the TE **and** completing the actual tasks; Standardize is gated by a real photo; Sustained by real streak days. Evolution = proof of work, not a card you just play. |
| **Gyms & regions** | **Rooms** | Each room is a Gym. Its zones are the gym's line-up. |
| **Badges** | **HOME room crests** | Beat a gym = **all its zones reach Standardized AND the room passes its 6S Audit** → earn the **Room Badge** (a HOME-tier crest / passport stamp). This is the honesty gate: no badge without an audited, photographed room. |
| **Prize cards / win** | **The reset milestone track** | Instead of KO prizes, your "prizes" are the badges. Collect the house's badges = you have reset the home. |
| **Trainer / Supporter / Item cards** | **STEP cards + one-shot helpers** | STEP cards are reusable "Supporters" (play the Shine method to any zone). One-shot **Helper cards** = "a family member joins (+2 TE this session)", "Power Hour (double TE for 60 min)", "Caddy packed (Shine costs 2, not 3)". These are earned by real conditions, never bought for power. |
| **Professor (onboarding)** | **Purpose + How-to-Play HOME cards** | The "Professor" walks a new player through the first gym: **Landing Spot, ~20 min** (the tutorial from Card 1). Learn the type wheel, evolve one zone, earn your first badge. |
| **Pokédex / collect loop** | **The House Passport** | Track every zone evolved and every room badged across the whole product line — the collection is *rooms actually reset*, not cards owned. |

**The core Pokémon loop, in one line:** *attach Time-Effort → perform the S-steps → evolve the zone → clear the gym → earn the badge.* Every rung is a real task; the "level up" is a real state change you can photograph.

## PART 1B — MAGIC: THE GATHERING mapped to the 6S line

MTG's genius is **resource curve (lands→mana→spells)**, **the ordered stack**, **permanents vs one-shots**, **deck-building**, and **synergy/combo**. It maps onto "**plan and execute** a reset" — the strategic, adult-facing layer.

| MTG system | 6S mapping | Concrete rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Colors (WUBRG)** | **The six S disciplines** | Each S is a color of action. A "reset plan" that leans one color (e.g. all Sort) is fragile; balanced plans win — mirrors real resets. |
| **Lands → mana** | **The Room Kit / product cards → Time-Effort** | Kit cards are your **lands**: tap them to produce TE. **No matched cleaner = you cannot cast Shine.** Missing a boot-tray brush = a Shine step you can't pay for until you acquire the land. Honest: tools gate work. |
| **The stack / turn order** | **The fixed six-S loop order** | The stack resolves in order: you cannot resolve **Standardize** while **Sort** is unresolved. The MICRO-ZONE back is the stack for that zone; STEP cards are the resolution rules. This encodes "don't photograph a mess." |
| **Permanents** | **Standardize & Sustain outputs** | The **Standard photo is a permanent** — it stays "in play" as the target. A **Sustain habit is an enchantment** that keeps working each turn (each day). |
| **Sorceries (one-shot, your turn)** | **Sort & Shine** | Done once, this session, then gone. |
| **Instants (react anytime)** | **Safety fixes** | When Shine "triggers" a flag, you may cast a **Safety instant** on the spot (anchor the console, pull the frayed cable). Safety interrupts the normal order — exactly right for the 4th S. |
| **Deck-building (Constructed)** | **Assembling a reset plan** | Before an event, build your "deck": choose which MICRO-ZONE cards + STEP cards + Kit you'll bring, matched to your TE budget and time. This is planning the reset. |
| **Limited / Booster draft** | **Randomized zone draw for variety** | Draw zones in random order from a room (a "pack") and reset in the order drawn — variety without buying anything (the pack is *your own cards, shuffled*). |
| **Synergy / combo** | **Kit + point-of-use combos** | Colour-coded microfiber + the two-cloth rule + a packed caddy is a **combo engine**: assembled, it *reduces the TE cost* of Shine (3→2). Point-of-use storage reduces Sustain cost. Good "combos" = real efficiency, not exploits. |
| **Rarity** | **Scope/difficulty label only** | A "rare" zone = big/hard (garage bay, deep pantry); "common" = a single drawer. Rarity describes **effort**, never scarcity — every card ships in the base set (de-fanged in Part 3). |
| **Formats / events** | **The cleaning events** | Constructed = planned full reset; Limited = draft campaign; Commander/raid = family co-op; boss = Move-Out (Part 2). |

**The core MTG loop, in one line:** *tap the Kit for Time-Effort → resolve the six-S stack in order → leave permanents (standard + habit) on the board.* The resource curve teaches that you cannot Shine what you have no tools for, and the stack teaches you cannot standardize a mess.

## PART 2 — Four playable CLEANING EVENTS

Each event states: cards used, win condition, session length, **how the TCG mechanic DRIVES real cleaning**, and **the honesty interlock**.

### EVENT 1 — "Guests in an Hour" (Pokémon speed / energy-limited triage)
- **Flavour & mechanic:** a **60-minute timer** and a **hard-capped TE pool (12 tokens ≈ 60 min)**. You cannot afford everything — you must triage. This is Pokémon's "limited energy, choose your attacks" pressure.
- **Cards used:** HOME **Purpose** (sets the guest-facing target) + **Safety Blitz** (2-min hazard sweep). MICRO-ZONE: only the **high-visibility zones** you choose to spend TE on. STEP: **Straighten + Shine** primarily (skip deep Sort — you triage to surface reset). Helper cards allowed.
- **How the mechanic drives cleaning:** the TE cap forces Lean prioritization — you spend tokens on the zones a guest sees, evolve them Messy→Cleaned only (no time to Standardize), and consciously **defer** the rest as a visible **"debt" pile** of un-played zone cards.
- **Win condition:** the **Guest Test** passes — seconds-to-launch < 60, ≤3 items on visible surfaces, hazards cleared. A **10-second visual audit** (a stripped-down Audit) of the visible zones only.
- **Length:** 60 minutes, solo or family.
- **Stays honest:** it is explicitly a **triage event, not a reset** — the deferred-zone debt pile is the honesty. You "win" a genuine surface-clean; the un-played cards are literally the work you still owe, carried into a full reset. Nothing is faked because the target itself is "presentable in an hour," and it is really met.

### EVENT 2 — "Spring Whole-Home Reset" (MTG Limited draft × Pokémon gym tour — the flagship campaign)
- **Flavour & mechanic:** a **season-long campaign**. Each session you **"open a pack"** = shuffle one room's MICRO-ZONE cards and draw your reset order (MTG Limited variety, no purchase — your own cards). You **tour the gyms** (rooms) earning badges (Pokémon).
- **Cards used:** the **whole line.** HOME per room (Purpose/Before/Audit/After/Standardize/Rhythm), all MICRO-ZONE cards, all STEP cards, the House Passport, the Tracker Mat.
- **How the mechanic drives cleaning:** the draft keeps a long campaign fresh (you don't do zones in the same order twice); the gym-tour badge chase gives a multi-week reset a visible arc and a reason to return; the TE curve paces you so you don't burn out.
- **Win condition:** **every room badged** (each room = all zones Standardized + room Audit passed) → the **House crest** on the Passport, plus a **whole-home before/after photo album**.
- **Length:** weeks (one room / a few zones per session).
- **Stays honest:** each badge is gated by that room's **Audit pass + After-photo match** — you cannot stamp a room you didn't finish. The photo album is objective proof of the campaign.

### EVENT 3 — "Move-Out" (MTG boss encounter — phased, high-stakes)
- **Flavour & mechanic:** the **boss fight.** Like a phased planeswalker/raid boss, the home must be beaten in **four ordered phases**, and you cannot advance a phase until the prior resolves (the stack, room-scale):
  - **Phase 1 — Declutter (Sort, house-wide):** one-in-one-out to *zero*; every room emptied of non-movers.
  - **Phase 2 — Deep Shine:** the nine-rule method, every zone, top to bottom.
  - **Phase 3 — Repair & Safety (instants):** act on every flag — patch, anchor, replace.
  - **Phase 4 — Standardize-to-empty:** photograph each room at broom-clean / deposit-back standard.
- **Cards used:** all tiers, run at maximum rigor; the Kit "lands" matter most here (you must have the tools to cast deep Shine).
- **How the mechanic drives cleaning:** the phase-lock enforces correct sequence (no painting over grime, no cleaning around clutter) and the boss's real deadline (move date) supplies genuine pressure without a fake timer.
- **Win condition:** **every room passes a stringent move-out audit** and a **landlord/buyer-ready photo set** exists.
- **Length:** days to a couple of weeks; the highest-stakes format.
- **Stays honest:** the move-out audit *is* someone else's real inspection (deposit, sale) — the ultimate external referee. The photo set is the proof.

### EVENT 4 — Family Co-op "Raid" (Pokémon raid × MTG Two-Headed Giant × Family Edition)
- **Flavour & mechanic:** a **cooperative boss raid** on one room, built on the **Family Edition** (fewer, bigger, picture-first cards). Shared TE pool / shared timer. The room is the **raid boss with HP = number of zone-cells to clear.** The family cooperatively "damages" it by completing real tasks.
- **Roles (kid-facing S-type specialists):** **the Sorter, the Straightener, the Shine Captain, the Safety Scout, the Photographer (Standardize), the Streak-Keeper (Sustain).** Kids pick a role/type. The **Safety Scout runs the Safety Blitz as a hazard scavenger hunt** — hazard-triangle tokens are the kid's "damage dealt."
- **Cards used:** Family Edition HOME + MICRO-ZONE (picture-first), STEP cards as simple role cards, token punchboard.
- **How the mechanic drives cleaning:** roles give every family member a real, non-overlapping job; the shared HP bar makes it visibly cooperative (no one "loses"); the scavenger hunt makes Safety genuinely fun for kids.
- **Win condition:** **raid defeated** — all cells cleared before the timer / before dinner — **and** a quick **family audit** by a "Quality Captain" (a parent) passes.
- **Length:** 20–40 minutes, one room.
- **Stays honest:** a cell only clears on **real task completion**, and the Quality-Captain audit is the gate — a rushed cell that fails the audit goes back on the board (regains HP). Co-op means the incentive is quality-together, not speed-against.

## PART 3 — Fun-but-counterproductive mechanics, and how to de-fang them

TCGs carry monetization and engagement patterns that are *toxic in a household chore product*. Flagged and neutralized:

1. **Gambling / booster-pack rarity / FOMO — CUT the paid-random layer entirely.**
   - *The danger:* loot-box boosters, chase rares, and duplicate-for-value trading turn a cleaning tool into a spend-to-collect treadmill and gate real work behind chance.
   - *De-fang:* **no randomized paid packs, ever.** The "booster draft" in Spring Reset is a **shuffle of cards you already own** (draw-order variety, zero purchase). **Rarity = scope/difficulty label only** — every card ships in the base set; nothing is scarce, nothing is chased, no secondary market is designed in.

2. **Pay-to-win — structurally impossible, keep it that way.**
   - *The danger:* buying power / shortcuts.
   - *De-fang:* **money cannot fake the Audit or the After-photo.** The work is the win. Selling additional *room decks* is fine (more scope/content); selling *power* is not. Consumables sold are only refills (Tracker pads, token punchboards) — never anything that makes the real task optional. More kit is a real *land*, not a cheat.

3. **Grinding / infinite levels / over-cleaning — cap the loop at the real cadence.**
   - *The danger:* XP grind pushing busywork and over-cleaning; "one more level" detached from need.
   - *De-fang:* **Sustain streaks plateau** at the real cadence (daily/weekly/seasonal). There is **no infinite XP** — a Sustained zone is *maintained*, not "maxed." The game's ceiling is a clean, upkept home; there is nothing to grind past it.

4. **PvP racing → rushing, low quality, and family shaming.**
   - *The danger:* competition incentivizes fast-and-dirty and blame.
   - *De-fang:* the **Audit quality-gate** means a rushed zone doesn't score / regains HP; **co-op is the family default**, competition optional. Carry Card 8's rule verbatim: **"check the home, not the household"** — score the room, never a person. No leaderboards that rank people.

5. **Abstract points / fake progress.**
   - *The danger:* score inflation untethered from reality.
   - *De-fang:* **the only currencies are real** — Time-Effort spent, cells cleared (real tasks), badges (audited rooms), photos. No score exists that isn't backed by a real state change.

6. **Completionism / "collect all rooms" sunk-cost pressure.**
   - *The danger:* guilt-driven FOMO to buy every room deck.
   - *De-fang:* **every room deck is standalone and complete alone.** The House Passport **celebrates what you did** and never penalizes gaps — a partial passport is a real accomplishment, not an unfinished set nagging to be completed.

## SUMMARY (for the coordinator)

1. **One resource unifies both games:** the **Time-Effort token** serves as Pokémon *energy* and MTG *mana*, produced by the Room Kit ("lands"), with a fixed per-S cost table (Shine = 3, the deep one). This keeps components simple and teaches the honest lesson that a reset costs budgeted real time.
2. **Pokémon maps to "level up a room":** the six S's are **types**, a zone **evolves** Messy → Cleaned → Standardized → Sustained (each rung a real, photographable task), rooms are **gyms**, and completed+audited rooms earn **badges** on a House Passport — the collect-and-level loop where the collection is rooms actually reset.
3. **MTG maps to "plan and execute a reset":** Kit = **lands** (no tools, no Shine), the fixed loop order = **the stack** (can't Standardize an unresolved mess), Standardize/Sustain leave **permanents**, Safety fixes are **instants**, and Kit combos legitimately lower Shine's cost — the strategic adult layer.
4. **Four events, all set on the honesty interlock:** "Guests in an Hour" (TE-capped triage, deferred-work debt pile), "Spring Whole-Home Reset" (draft × gym-tour campaign, badge per audited room), "Move-Out" (phased MTG boss gated by a real external inspection), and a Family Co-op "Raid" (Family Edition, S-type kid roles, Safety Blitz as scavenger hunt).
5. **Every win terminates in the Audit scorecard + After-photo match** I specced earlier — rushing cannot fake a clean; a failed-audit cell goes back on the board.
6. **De-fanged the toxic TCG patterns:** no paid random boosters or rarity chase (rarity = difficulty label only, all cards in-base), pay-to-win is structurally impossible (money can't fake the photo/audit), grinding capped at the real upkeep cadence, PvP defaulted to co-op with a "score the room, not the person" rule, and completionism celebrated-not-punished.
