## CARD 19 — Coats & Outerwear (Zone intro) [Zone header]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A wide, friendly hero shot of the whole coats-and-outerwear zone at a Scandinavian-modern entryway wall, read top to bottom: a shelf up high, a rail with hooks below it, a labelled hat-and-glove bin on the shelf, and an umbrella stand on the clean floor. Riley stands looking up at the wall while the helper gestures a downward sweep, showing the zone is worked "from the shelf down."
- Learning moment: This is the map of the zone and the order you attack it. Reset looks like: one coat per person on the rail, two hooks visibly empty, hats and gloves in one labelled bin, umbrella stand clean and dry.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): the shelf-over-rail wall with hooks; a labelled "HATS · GLOVES" bin on the shelf; one coat per hook with two hooks clearly empty; a clean dry umbrella stand; a top-to-bottom terracotta "work from the shelf down" arrow.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, cutaway-lite elevation of the wall (we see the whole zone flat-on like a labelled diagram, but with real characters in front of it).
- Character approach: Riley center, chin up, taking in the wall; helper (warm woman, mid-30s, short auburn hair, olive-green cardigan) beside her sweeping a hand downward. Small friendly dog sitting near the umbrella stand.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the whole zone as "look here / this is Zone 2"; green check tags on the two empty hooks to say "this is the good way"; terracotta down-arrow for order.
- Callout/arrow style: one big soft terracotta arrow running shelf → rail → floor; tiny 1-3 word chips: "SHELF", "RAIL", "BIN", "STAND".
- Composition notes: wall fills upper two-thirds; calm lower third clear for caption; Zone badge top corner in terracotta zone-header tone.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): This whole wall is one zone, and you clean it from the shelf down.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Look up at the shelf.
     - Kids: Find the shelf, the rail, the bin, and the umbrella stand.
     - Caregivers: Name the six surfaces aloud so the zone has edges.
     - Look for: everything from the top shelf down to the floor.
  2. Start high, work down.
     - Kids: Always begin at the top so crumbs fall to places you clean later.
     - Caregivers: Model the shelf-to-floor order once.
     - Look for: the top-to-bottom arrow.
  3. Picture "done."
     - Kids: One coat per person, two hooks empty.
     - Caregivers: Point at the two empty hooks as the target.
     - Look for: hats and gloves in one labelled bin.
  4. Check the umbrella stand.
     - Kids: It should be clean and dry.
     - Caregivers: Tip and dry it before it goes back.
     - Look for: no puddle underneath.
- Common mistake: Starting at the floor first, so dust from the shelf lands on the floor you just cleaned.
- Encouragement line: You just learned to read a whole wall like a map. Nice.

## CARD 20 — Coats & Outerwear · Sort [Sort]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The rail is emptied; coats are laid on a clean surface and Riley sorts them by weather with the helper. A small "weather line" of icons (sun, rain, snow) shows one coat per kind of weather. A never-worn coat still has nothing on it; a red-tag hangs off the "maybe" pile alongside odd single gloves and a broken umbrella heading to the out box.
- Learning moment: Coats are counted by weather, not by number. A second coat must beat the first on a nameable day. Odd gloves and broken umbrellas go.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a weather row (sun / rain / snow) with one coat under each; a "maybe" coat wearing a dated red tag; odd single gloves and a broken umbrella in an out box; "the money is spent either way" idea shown gently by an untouched fancy coat.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with a light infographic strip (weather icons across the top).
- Character approach: Riley holding up a coat to a weather icon to test it; helper (grandfather figure, warm tan skin, silver beard, blue apron) holding the out box. Same Riley design as Card 19.
- Color legend use: green check under coats that earn their weather slot; red tag chip on the undecided coat; honey ring on the weather row as the deciding rule.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrows from coats into either "KEEP" or "OUT"; tiny chips "SUN", "RAIN", "SNOW", "OUT".
- Composition notes: weather strip up top, sorting action mid, out box lower left; lower third clear.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Count coats by weather, not by number.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Empty the rail.
     - Kids: Take every coat off and lay it on a clean surface.
     - Caregivers: Give nothing a way back without a verdict.
     - Look for: an empty rail to decide against.
  2. Name the weather.
     - Kids: Sun, rain, snow. Your real weather.
     - Caregivers: Match one coat to each kind of weather your climate makes.
     - Look for: one coat per weather.
  3. Make the second coat earn it.
     - Kids: A second coat must beat the first on a real day.
     - Caregivers: If you can name that day, keep it; if not, it goes.
     - Look for: doubles that do the same job.
  4. Tag the maybes.
     - Kids: Write today's date on a red tag.
     - Caregivers: If it is not used by then, it goes; leaving it is not an answer.
     - Look for: the dated red tag.
  5. Out with the broken.
     - Kids: Odd single gloves and broken umbrellas go.
     - Caregivers: Both act and recycle are answers.
     - Look for: the out box filling up.
- Common mistake: Keeping the expensive never-worn coat out of guilt. The money is already spent either way.
- Encouragement line: You are deciding, not just tidying. That is the hard part done.

## CARD 21 — Coats & Outerwear · Straighten [Straighten]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The signature empty-hook card. A tidy rail of named hooks, one daily coat per person, with two hooks at the end left visibly empty for visitors and glowing green. A height guide shows the lowest hook set right at the shortest person's reach: a small child (or Riley's younger sibling) hangs their own coat on the low hook with ease. A labelled "HATS · GLOVES" bin sits on the shelf.
- Learning moment: One hook per person for the daily coat; the lowest hook at the shortest person's height; two hooks always empty; label the home, not the thing.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): named hooks (name chips); two clearly empty hooks glowing green; a dashed height line from the shortest person to the lowest hook; the shortest person reaching their own hook comfortably; labelled hat-and-glove bin.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with a subtle measurement-diagram overlay (dashed height guideline).
- Character approach: Riley hanging her coat on her own named hook; helper (dad figure, dark brown skin, glasses, rolled-sleeve shirt) crouching to set the low hook for a smaller child who reaches it happily. Same Riley design.
- Color legend use: green glow on the two empty hooks (the good way / room for a visitor); honey ring on the low hook + height line as the focus; terracotta not needed for steps here but small numbered chips optional.
- Callout/arrow style: dashed height line with a tiny "LOWEST" chip; name chips over each hook; "2 EMPTY" chip.
- Composition notes: rail across the middle; height line on the left; bin on the shelf top; lower third clear.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): A hook everyone can reach, and two always kept empty.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Give each person a hook.
     - Kids: One hook is yours, for your daily coat.
     - Caregivers: Name each hook so it belongs to someone.
     - Look for: one coat per hook, not three.
  2. Set the lowest hook low.
     - Kids: The bottom hook is at the shortest person's height.
     - Caregivers: Measure to the smallest reacher and mount there.
     - Look for: the little one hanging their own coat.
  3. Keep two hooks empty.
     - Kids: Two hooks stay bare for visitors.
     - Caregivers: The empty hook does the asking when a stray shows up.
     - Look for: two hooks glowing green.
  4. Bin the hats and gloves.
     - Kids: One labelled bin holds them all.
     - Caregivers: Label the home, not just the thing.
     - Look for: the "HATS GLOVES" label.
- Common mistake: Mounting every hook at adult height, so kids drape coats on chairs instead.
- Encouragement line: Now the littlest person can hang their own coat. That is a real fix.

## CARD 22 — Coats & Outerwear · Shine [Shine]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The deep card. A numbered storyboard of the ordered Shine sequence around the coat wall: (1) bin down and emptied onto a clean surface, (2) dust then wipe the shelf, (3) clean the bin in and out, (4) brush the coats, (5) wipe the rail and hooks, (6) wipe the wall behind, (7) scrub the umbrella stand outside, (8) floor last. A small inspect-and-flag magnifier badge shows Riley spotting a loose hook plate without stopping to fix it.
- Learning moment: Shine runs in a fixed order, top to bottom, floor last, and you inspect-and-flag as you go without stopping to fix.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): eight terracotta-numbered steps around the wall in order; dry-then-damp cloth cue on the shelf; bin cleaned in and out; soft upholstery brush on coat shoulders; umbrella stand tipped and scrubbed outside; floor vacuumed and mopped last; a magnifier "FLAG" badge on a loose hook plate.
- Note: "wool put away damp is what turns musty" -> show the bin being dried fully. Do not stop to fix flagged items.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard comic panels arranged as a numbered ring around the coat wall (or a clean 8-step vertical/loop layout), each mini-panel one action.
- Character approach: Riley active in the key panels (brushing coats, scrubbing stand); helper (mom figure, light skin, red headscarf, apron) handling the shelf and rail. Same Riley design across the batch.
- Color legend use: terracotta numbers 1-8 for the exact order; honey ring on the current focus; a small red flag/magnifier for inspect-and-flag; green check on finished floor.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta numbered circles; tiny verb chips "DUST", "WIPE", "BRUSH", "SCRUB", "FLOOR"; a magnifier "FLAG" chip.
- Composition notes: keep panels legible at card size (favor 6-8 simple icons over cramming); lower third clear.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Clean the coat wall in order, top to bottom, floor last, and flag what needs fixing.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Take the bin down and empty it.
     - Kids: Shake it out, set the contents on a clean surface, not the floor.
     - Caregivers: Give yourself a clear shelf to work on.
     - Look for: an empty bin and a clean landing spot.
  2. Dust the shelf, then wipe it.
     - Kids: Dry cloth first, then a barely-damp one.
     - Caregivers: Reach the back edge and the front lip's grey line.
     - Look for: the grey line gone.
  3. Clean the bin in and out.
     - Kids: Damp cloth, then dry it all the way.
     - Caregivers: Dry fully; wool put away damp is what turns musty.
     - Look for: a bin that is dry inside.
  4. Brush the coats.
     - Kids: Brush shoulders and collar downward.
     - Caregivers: Use the soft upholstery tool, start with this week's pieces.
     - Look for: dust falling away from shoulders.
  5. Wipe the rail and hooks.
     - Kids: Slide coats along and wipe the whole rail and every hook.
     - Caregivers: Dry the base plates so nothing rusts.
     - Look for: each hook and base plate wiped.
  6. Wipe the wall behind.
     - Kids: Barely damp, lift the scuff marks.
     - Caregivers: Gentle on paint, dry after.
     - Look for: the shoulder-and-bag scuff gone.
  7. Empty and scrub the umbrella stand.
     - Kids: Tip the water and grit out outside, scrub inside, rinse.
     - Caregivers: Invert it to dry.
     - Look for: a clean dry stand.
  8. Floor last.
     - Kids: Vacuum under the coats and behind the stand, then mop.
     - Caregivers: Let the sealed floor dry.
     - Look for: grit gone, floor drying.
- Common mistake: Stopping to fix a loose hook mid-clean. Flag it and keep cleaning; fixing happens on the Safety card.
- Encouragement line: Eight steps, top to bottom. You are cleaning like a pro.

## CARD 23 — Coats & Outerwear · Safety [Safety — the 4th S]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Act-on-the-flags card. - Learning moment: Safety is the fourth S. You act now on everything the clean turned up: strangle risk from low straps, and hooks or brackets about to let go under a coat's weight.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): long scarf/strap being lifted off a low hook (red triangle before, green check after); a hook base plate / rail bracket being tightened; a small "FROM SHINE" flag list; helper steadying while Riley helps; no scary imagery, calm and matter-of-fact.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: line-art + accent, two-beat before/after within one scene (hazard marked, then fixed).
- Character approach: Riley handing up a scarf to a higher hook; helper (young adult, wheelchair user, warm medium skin, short curly hair, tool in hand) tightening the base plate at reachable height. Same Riley design.
- Color legend use: red warning triangle on the low strap at neck height; green check once the strap is moved and the plate is snug; honey ring on the flag list as the source of today's actions.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrow moving the strap from low hook to high; small chips "FROM SHINE", "TIGHTEN", "MOVED".
- Composition notes: hazard on one side, fix on the other; flag list as a small chip cluster; lower third clear.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Fix now what the cleaning flagged, before it can hurt someone.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Read your flags.
     - Kids: Look at what the clean found.
     - Caregivers: Bring the Shine flags here and act on each.
     - Look for: the "FROM SHINE" list.
  2. Lift straps off low hooks.
     - Kids: Long scarves and bag straps go up high, away from little necks.
     - Caregivers: Anything at a child's neck height moves up.
     - Look for: no loop dangling at neck height.
  3. Tighten the hooks and brackets.
     - Kids: A wobbly hook gets fixed before a coat hangs on it.
     - Caregivers: Act on any base plate pulling out, a proud screw, or a bracket backing out.
     - Look for: a snug plate, a straight rail.
  4. Check the damp flag.
     - Kids: A brown bloom behind wet coats means it needs airing and drying.
     - Caregivers: Address any soft patch behind soaked coats.
     - Look for: no dark damp bloom.
- Common mistake: Leaving a long scarf on a low hook because it looks tidy. A loop at neck height is a strangle risk.
- Encouragement line: You did not just find problems, you fixed them. That is the fourth S.
