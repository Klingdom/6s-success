## CARD 12 — Landing Spot (Zone intro) [ZONE 1 · terracotta]

### IMAGE SUMMARY
- **Scene idea:** A single hero shot of the Landing Spot itself — the console against the wall with a mirror above it, a landing tray, a key hook, an upright folder of live paper, and a charging dock — shown mid-reset, half messy on one side and calm-and-clear on the other, so you can see the target at a glance. Riley points up at a honey highlight ring around the whole zone; the helper sets a portable caddy of inputs on the floor.
- **Learning moment:** This is the map of the zone — these are the surfaces you will work (tray, console top, mirror, wall + switch plate, charging dock, floor beneath) and this is the caddy of inputs you gather before you start.
- **Key elements to keep (3-6):** the console + mirror + tray + key hook + upright folder + dock; a visible "grey film" ring under where the tray sat; the loaded portable caddy (cloths, spray bottles, small brush, compact vacuum); Riley pointing; a calm cleared half vs a slightly cluttered half.

### STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- **Chosen style:** friendly flat vector (single wide establishing view of the zone).
- **Character approach:** Riley front-and-center gesturing up at the zone; grown-up helper (this card: a bearded dad with glasses, short curly hair, olive henley) crouching to set down the caddy. Small friendly dog optional at Riley's feet.
- **Color legend use:** honey highlight ring around the whole Landing Spot (this is the focus zone); one small green check on the clear/calm side; the caddy items color-coded (glass = slate blue cloth, surface = green cloth, dry buff = honey cloth, floor = terracotta cloth).
- **Callout/arrow style:** three or four tiny terracotta label chips pinned to parts of the scene — "tray", "mirror", "dock", "floor" — 1-2 words each. No numbered arrows here (this is the map, not the sequence).
- **Composition notes:** the console + mirror fill the upper two thirds against the wall; Riley and the caddy occupy the mid-left; lower third kept calm and open for the caption; Sort-red badge? No — this is a ZONE card, so the badge is the terracotta ROOM/zone marker in the top corner.

### TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- **Big idea (1 line):** This little spot by the door is the highest-value square foot in the house, so we clean and set it as one zone.
- **Steps:**
  1. Meet the zone. **Kids:** Find the tray, the mirror, the dock, and the floor under it. **Caregivers:** Name the six surfaces out loud together. **Look for:** the grey film ring under the tray — that shows how long since anyone did this.
  2. Picture the reset. **Kids:** Keys and phone in one tray, top wiped clear, mirror bright. **Caregivers:** Point at what "done" looks like. **Look for:** one upright folder of live paper only.
  3. Gather your inputs. **Kids:** Load the caddy so nothing sends you walking off. **Caregivers:** Check each color-coded cloth is in. **Look for:** glass cleaner, surface cleaner, floor cleaner, four cloths, small brush, compact vacuum, caddy.
- **Common mistake:** Starting to wipe before you have gathered the caddy, then wandering off mid-clean.
- **Encouragement line:** Twenty minutes here changes how the whole house feels.

## CARD 13 — Landing Spot · Sort [SORT · #CB4B36]

### IMAGE SUMMARY
- **Scene idea:** Riley empties the tray and sorts what comes out, giving every item a verdict. A clear tray sits empty; three labeled destinations wait — a KEEP tray (keys, phone, wallet, sunglasses), a RECYCLE bin for old paper, and an OUT box for dead batteries, odd keys, dead pens. The helper holds up a single sheet of paper with a small "14 days" red-tag hanging off it.
- **Learning moment:** Nothing goes back in the tray without a verdict; paper older than 14 days is a decision, not paperwork; "maybe" gets a dated red tag, and leaving it is not an answer.
- **Key elements to keep (3-6):** empty tray tipped out; three sorted piles/bins labeled KEEP / RECYCLE / OUT; true daily-carry (keys, phone, wallet, sunglasses) going to KEEP; dead batteries + odd keys + dead pens going OUT; a dated red tag on a "maybe" item; Riley deciding, helper offering the tag.

### STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- **Chosen style:** friendly flat vector with a light sorting-flow feel (items fanning toward three destinations).
- **Character approach:** Riley holds an item mid-decision over the three bins; helper (this card: a mom with a headscarf, warm tan skin, denim apron) hands Riley a red date-tag. Keep both identical to the rest of the batch.
- **Color legend use:** green check on the KEEP tray; red mark on the OUT box; honey highlight ring around the item being decided right now.
- **Callout/arrow style:** three terracotta arrows fanning from the tipped tray to KEEP / RECYCLE / OUT; small rounded chips label the three bins (1 word each).
- **Composition notes:** tipped tray upper-center, three destinations across the mid-band, Riley deciding at center; Sort-red badge top corner; lower third clear for caption.

### TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- **Big idea (1 line):** Empty the tray, then let nothing back in without a verdict.
- **Steps:**
  1. Empty the zone. **Kids:** Tip everything out of the tray. **Caregivers:** Resist re-tidying; get it all out first. **Look for:** the true bottom of the tray.
  2. Give paper a verdict. **Kids:** Act, file, or recycle — pick one. **Caregivers:** A sheet older than 14 days is a decision tonight. **Look for:** anything that has just been sitting.
  3. Keep only daily-carry. **Kids:** Keys, phone, wallet, sunglasses stay. **Caregivers:** Everything else earns its spot or leaves. **Look for:** the four things you actually grab.
  4. Send the dead stuff out. **Kids:** Dead batteries, odd keys, dead pens to the OUT box. **Caregivers:** Batteries to proper disposal, not the bin. **Look for:** anything broken or orphaned.
  5. Red-tag the maybes. **Kids:** Not sure? Put a date on it. **Caregivers:** If it is not used by the date, it goes. **Look for:** items you keep "just in case."
- **Common mistake:** Leaving a "maybe" untagged — leaving it is not an answer.
- **Encouragement line:** Both keep and recycle are wins; only leaving it is a loss.

## CARD 14 — Landing Spot · Straighten [STRAIGHTEN · #BC4B2A]

### IMAGE SUMMARY
- **Scene idea:** Everything that survived Sort gets a chosen home. Riley places the tray exactly where a hand naturally opens on entering; a key hook (or key dish) goes on the wall; one upright folder stands sectioned Action / File / Toss; the dock sits at a single outlet with its cable neatly coiled. Small labels sit under each home, not on the objects. The helper presses a label under the folder slot.
- **Learning moment:** Give each thing a home chosen by how you use it, and label the home (not the thing) so anyone can put it back without asking.
- **Key elements to keep (3-6):** the tray placed where the hand opens (a little dashed "reach" arc); key hook/dish; one upright folder with three tabs Action / File / Toss; the dock at one outlet, cable coiled; small under-home labels; Riley placing, helper labeling.

### STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- **Chosen style:** friendly flat vector with light cutaway-overlay hints (a dashed reach-arc showing where the hand lands).
- **Character approach:** Riley setting the tray into its natural spot; helper (this card: a grandpa with silver hair, glasses, warm brown skin, cardigan) pressing a small label under the folder. Keep characters consistent.
- **Color legend use:** honey highlight ring around each "home" as it is set; green check appears once the label is on; terracotta arrow (dashed reach-arc) from the door to where the tray lands.
- **Callout/arrow style:** small rounded label chips under each home — "tray", "keys", "paper", "dock" — plus the three folder tabs (Action / File / Toss). Keep every label 1-3 words.
- **Composition notes:** console top across the mid-band with the four homes lined up; the reach-arc sweeps in from the card edge (the door); Straighten-terracotta badge top corner; caption space clear below.

### TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- **Big idea (1 line):** Give what survived a home chosen by how you actually use it.
- **Steps:**
  1. Set the tray where your hand opens. **Kids:** Put the tray where your hand goes first. **Caregivers:** Match it to the natural entry motion. **Look for:** the spot you reach without thinking.
  2. Hang the keys. **Kids:** Keys to a hook or a dish, every time. **Caregivers:** One obvious key home only. **Look for:** where keys currently get dropped.
  3. Stand one folder for live paper. **Kids:** One upright folder: Action, File, Toss. **Caregivers:** Only live paper lives here. **Look for:** loose sheets that need a slot.
  4. Dock at one outlet, coil the cable. **Kids:** Put the dock at one plug and coil the cord. **Caregivers:** Keep it to a single outlet. **Look for:** tangled or trailing cable.
  5. Label the home, not the thing. **Kids:** Put the label under the spot. **Caregivers:** Labeled homes hold without asking. **Look for:** any home a guest could not guess.
- **Common mistake:** Labeling the object instead of the spot, so it drifts back to a pile.
- **Encouragement line:** A labeled home means anyone can put it back — that is what makes it last.

## CARD 15 — Landing Spot · Shine [SHINE · #D98A2B]  ← the deep one

### IMAGE SUMMARY
- **Scene idea:** An eight-step visual clean sequence flowing around the Landing Spot, numbered 1 to 8 with terracotta arrows, so the ordered method reads even with the words gone: (1) clear & unplug, (2) dust high & dry, (3) mirror one direction, (4) wall & switch plate, (5) console top, (6) wash the tray, (7) dock dry-only, (8) floor last. A separate honey "inspect & flag" thought-bubble shows Riley spotting a problem and dropping a flag chip rather than stopping to fix it.
- **Learning moment:** Shine follows a fixed order — top to bottom, dry before damp, glass one-direction, dock unplugged and dry only, floor last — and you inspect and flag as you go without stopping to fix.
- **Key elements to keep (3-6):** the numbered 1-8 path with terracotta arrows around the zone; the unplugged dock (cord pulled from the outlet) before any damp cloth; mirror wiped one direction (motion lines); the color-coded cloth changing per surface; the floor done last (vacuum + mop); an "inspect & flag" bubble with a little flag on a spotted water ring / frayed cable.

### STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- **Chosen style:** storyboard comic panels laid out as a flowing numbered path (mini vignettes 1-8 around one console scene), the batch's deep, step-flow card.
- **Character approach:** Riley is the actor in each mini-step; the helper (this card: an aunt with locs, deep brown skin, rolled-sleeve plaid shirt) hands the right cloth at step 5 and points at the flag bubble. Keep both consistent with the batch.
- **Color legend use:** honey highlight ring on the current surface in each step; terracotta numbered arrows 1 to 8 for order; a small green check when a surface is done; a red mark only inside the inspect-and-flag bubble (frayed cable / water ring).
- **Callout/arrow style:** eight small terracotta numbered arrows chaining the steps; tiny 1-3 word chips per step ("unplug", "dust dry", "one way", "switch", "top", "wash tray", "dock dry", "floor"); one honey "flag it" chip on the inspect bubble.
- **Composition notes:** the numbered path curves from top (dust high) down to bottom (floor last), literally teaching top-to-bottom order; inspect-and-flag bubble tucked to one side; Shine-honey badge top corner; caption band kept clear at the very bottom.

### TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- **Big idea (1 line):** Clean to a fixed order — top to bottom, dry before damp, floor last — and flag problems as you pass them.
- **Steps:**
  1. Clear & unplug. **Kids:** Everything off, tray and folder out, unplug the dock. **Caregivers:** Unplug before any damp cloth touches the zone. **Look for:** the dock still plugged in.
  2. Dust high & dry first. **Kids:** Dry buff the mirror-frame top and the console back seam. **Caregivers:** Dry cloth before any wet one. **Look for:** the dust line where top meets wall.
  3. Mirror / glass, one direction. **Kids:** Mist the cloth (never the glass), wipe one way, buff dry crossing. **Caregivers:** Spray the cloth, not the mirror. **Look for:** streaks left by circles.
  4. Wall & switch plate. **Kids:** Barely-damp cloth, lift finger marks round the switch and the door-side scuff. **Caregivers:** Dry behind you. **Look for:** grubby prints by the switch.
  5. Console top. **Kids:** Mist surface cleaner on the cloth, wipe back edge to front. **Caregivers:** Let sticky rings soften, then buff dry. **Look for:** rings and dust at the back edge.
  6. Wash the tray. **Kids:** Wipe the grey film ring, wash the tray in and out, brush the corners. **Caregivers:** Dry fully before keys return. **Look for:** the grey film ring.
  7. Dock & cable, dry only. **Kids:** Still unplugged, wipe body and cradle dry, brush the port, coil loosely. **Caregivers:** Never damp on the dock. **Look for:** dust in the port.
  8. Floor last. **Kids:** Vacuum grit under and around the legs, then mop and let it dry. **Caregivers:** Floor is always last. **Look for:** the dark grit trap under the console.
  - Inspect & flag as you go. **Kids:** See a problem? Drop a flag, keep cleaning. **Caregivers:** Do not stop the clean to fix. **Look for:** a brown water ring under the hooks; a cracked, frayed, or hardened cable; a scorched or warm plug.
- **Common mistake:** Spraying the mirror directly, or doing the floor before the surfaces so grit lands on clean tops.
- **Encouragement line:** Follow the order and the whole spot comes up bright — no going back over it.

## CARD 16 — Landing Spot · Safety [SAFETY · #DDA63A]  ← the 4th S

### IMAGE SUMMARY
- **Scene idea:** Riley and the helper act on the exact flags Shine turned up — fixing them now, not later. Three clear fixes: move the dock and power strip out of the drip line beneath wet umbrellas/sleeves; keep it to one dock at one outlet with nothing chained to a lamp or heater; and drop button batteries, coins, and magnets into a lidded tin. A small "from the Shine flags" side note shows the frayed cable / warm plug / yellowed strip being retired.
- **Learning moment:** Safety is the fourth S — you act on everything the clean flagged: water-and-electricity, burn/fire, and poison/choke, fixed on the spot.
- **Key elements to keep (3-6):** dock + power strip moved clear of a drip line under a wet umbrella; a "one dock, one outlet" idea with a red X over a chained lamp-heater-strip; a lidded tin taking button batteries, coins, magnets; a retired frayed/warm cable with a red mark; helper anchoring or moving with Riley; the "act now" feel.

### STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- **Chosen style:** line-art + accent (clean home scene with a few strong red/green safety accents), so hazards pop.
- **Character approach:** Riley slides the power strip out of the drip line; helper (this card: a young uncle, short beard, wheelchair user, warm brown skin, teal polo) holds the lidded tin open for the small items. Show ability diversity here per the Style Bible.
- **Color legend use:** red warning marks on the three hazards being fixed; green check as each is made safe; honey highlight ring on the drip-line danger zone under the wet umbrella.
- **Callout/arrow style:** small terracotta arrow showing the dock moving OUT of the drip line; red X chip over the chained strip; 1-3 word chips ("drip line", "one outlet", "lidded tin"). No scary imagery — hazards shown calmly.
- **Composition notes:** three tidy fix-vignettes stacked; the retired-cable side note small in a corner; Safety-honey badge top corner (the 4th S); caption band clear.

### TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- **Big idea (1 line):** Act now on everything the clean turned up — Safety is the fourth S.
- **Steps:**
  1. Move dock and strip out of the drip line. **Kids:** Slide the dock and power strip away from under wet umbrellas and sleeves. **Caregivers:** Water and electricity must not meet. **Look for:** drips landing near a plug.
  2. One dock, one outlet. **Kids:** Never chain a dock, a lamp, and a heater to one strip. **Caregivers:** Keep the door clear as an exit too. **Look for:** an overloaded, warm, or yellowed strip.
  3. Lock away the small dangers. **Kids:** Button batteries, coins, and magnets into a lidded tin. **Caregivers:** Poison, choke, and swallow risks off open surfaces. **Look for:** loose button batteries and magnets.
  4. Retire what Shine flagged. **Kids:** A cracked or frayed cable or a warm plug gets pulled from use. **Caregivers:** Replace, do not tape over. **Look for:** a scorched or warm plug, a yellowed socket.
- **Common mistake:** Noting a hazard during Shine and then forgetting to actually fix it — Safety is where you act.
- **Encouragement line:** Fixing it the moment you find it is what keeps everyone safe.
