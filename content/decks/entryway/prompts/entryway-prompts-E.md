# Entryway Deck — Illustration Plans + Image Prompts, Batch E

**Batch E: Cards 33–39 — ZONE 4, BENCH / CONSOLE (top / seat / drawer / legs)**
Source of truth: `Entryway-Deck-v2-CardText.md`. Visual system: `Entryway-Art-StyleBible.md` (frozen).
Signature ideas: the surface rule **"top to three items or fewer"** (the whole-room glance-test number) featured on Cards 34 and 39; and **the seat collects the same outdoor grit as the floor**, because people sit on it in outdoor clothes.

Canon: the six S's are exactly, in order — Sort · Straighten · Shine · **Safety (the 4th S)** · Standardize · Sustain. Never a five-step model, never drop or move Safety, never "Set in Order." No QR codes, no logos, no misspelled text in art, nothing scary, no em dashes.

## CARD 33 — Zone 4: Bench / Console (Zone intro) [Zone / terracotta]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A wide, welcoming portrait of the whole bench-console unit in the entryway — Riley and the grown-up helper standing beside it, gesturing at its parts. A honey spotlight sweeps the four teaching surfaces: the top, the seat, the pulled-out drawer, and the legs with the floor beneath. A small friendly dog sits on the clear floor for warmth.
- Learning moment: This zone is one piece of furniture with several surfaces that each collect dirt, and the seat gets the same outdoor grit as the floor because people sit on it in their coats and boots.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): the console/bench with a clear top (three items only), the padded/hard seat, one drawer pulled partway out to show the cavity, legs meeting a clean floor, a honey highlight ring on the seat with a tiny grit motif, Riley plus helper.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, cutaway-lite (drawer eased open to reveal the runners and cavity).
- Character approach: Riley front-left pointing at the top; grown-up helper (this card: a warm bearded dad, medium-tan skin, short dark hair, olive henley, rolled sleeves) crouched by the seat brushing grit — keep this helper design consistent within the batch as "the helper on the intro."
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring on the seat (the card focus: seat = grit magnet); small green check floating over the cleared three-item top.
- Callout/arrow style: three tiny terracotta pointer chips labeling "TOP", "SEAT", "DRAWER" (1-word each); one honey ring.
- Composition notes: furniture centered, occupying the middle band; calm lower third clear for the caption; Zone badge top corner in terracotta.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The bench-console is one piece of furniture with four surfaces that each catch dirt — and the seat is a floor you sit on.
- Steps:
  1. Look at the whole unit. Kids: Point to the top, the seat, the drawer, the legs. Caregivers: Name each surface out loud. Look for: which parts your family actually touches most.
  2. Notice the seat. Kids: Rub a finger on the seat and check for grit. Caregivers: Explain people sit here in outdoor clothes. Look for: sandy, gritty crumbs like on the floor.
  3. Pull the drawer out. Kids: Ease the drawer open to peek at the tracks. Caregivers: Show the hidden cavity behind it. Look for: sand hiding in the back corners.
  4. Picture the reset. Kids: Imagine only three things on top. Caregivers: Describe an empty seat you can actually sit on. Look for: a top that passes the glance test.
  5. Gather the caddy. Kids: Load the cloths and vacuum tools. Caregivers: Check you have the upholstery and crevice tools. Look for: a detail brush for drawer corners.
- Common mistake: treating the console like a shelf and never lifting the drawer out — the grit lives behind it.
- Encouragement line: One piece of furniture, four easy wins. You've got this!

## CARD 34 — Sort · Bench / Console [Sort #CB4B36]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley clears the console top down to exactly THREE keeper items while the helper holds an "OUT" box. A big honey "3" badge floats over the top — the whole-room glance-test number. Everything non-entry gets a verdict, not a pile.
- Learning moment: The top holds three items or fewer, and only entry-useful things stay (library returns, gym bag, umbrella); everything else gets a decision.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): three keeper items lined up under a green check, a big honey "3" number, an OUT/verdict box the helper holds, a red-tag chip on a "maybe" item, Riley actively clearing, no leftover pile.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with one bold infographic number.
- Character approach: Riley placing the third keeper down; helper (this card: a warm auntie, deep brown skin, hair in a headwrap, teal apron) holding the OUT box — vary her from the intro helper.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring + honey "3" on the three keepers; green check over the trio; red-tag chip on one uncertain object.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrow sweeping clutter off the top toward the OUT box; small "3" chip; red date-tag chip.
- Composition notes: cleared top across the mid-band, the "3" large and legible; lower third calm; Sort badge top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Clear the top to three items or fewer — that's the glance-test number for the whole room.
- Steps:
  1. Empty the top. Kids: Take everything off the console top. Caregivers: Set it all on a clean surface, not the floor. Look for: how much was really living up there.
  2. Pick the entry-useful few. Kids: Choose things you use leaving the house. Caregivers: Think library returns, gym bag, umbrella. Look for: items that belong at the door.
  3. Count to three. Kids: Put back three or fewer. Caregivers: Confirm the top passes the glance test. Look for: a top you can take in at one look.
  4. Give the rest a verdict. Kids: Send each leftover somewhere real. Caregivers: Keep, relocate, or recycle — no pile. Look for: a decision on every single item.
  5. Red-tag the maybes. Kids: Stick a dated tag on "not sure" things. Caregivers: If unused by the date, it goes. Look for: both act and recycle are answers; leaving it is not.
- Common mistake: making a "deal with it later" pile — a pile is a delayed decision, not a home.
- Encouragement line: Three things on top and the whole room already looks calmer!

## CARD 35 — Straighten · Bench / Console [Straighten #BC4B2A]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley labels three baskets — Returns, Gym, Dog — one basket per purpose, while the helper fits sections into the open drawer so small things stop roaming. The seat stays clear enough to sit on (shown by the dog hopping up onto the clear seat).
- Learning moment: Give what survives a home chosen by use: one basket per purpose, sections in the drawer, and the seat kept clear to sit on. Label the home, not just the thing.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): three labeled baskets (Returns / Gym / Dog), a sectioned drawer with small items sorted into compartments, a clear seat, a label chip being applied, Riley + helper, small dog on the seat.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with clear labeled-container infographic.
- Character approach: Riley pressing a label onto a basket; helper (this card: a young grown-up sibling/cousin, light skin, red-framed glasses, green cardigan) placing drawer dividers.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the three labeled baskets; green check on the sectioned drawer; green glow on the clear seat.
- Callout/arrow style: three small label chips reading "RETURNS", "GYM", "DOG"; a terracotta arrow from a loose small item into its drawer section.
- Composition notes: baskets in a row along the console base, open sectioned drawer in the mid-band; clear seat with the dog; lower third calm; Straighten badge top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): One basket per purpose, sections in the drawer, and a seat you keep clear to sit on.
- Steps:
  1. Set one basket per job. Kids: Line up a basket for Returns, Gym, and Dog. Caregivers: One purpose per basket, no mixing. Look for: a clear job for each container.
  2. Label each home. Kids: Stick the name on the basket. Caregivers: Label the home, not just the thing. Look for: names anyone can read and follow.
  3. Section the drawer. Kids: Drop dividers in so small things stop rolling. Caregivers: Give each small item a compartment. Look for: no loose stuff roaming the drawer.
  4. Keep the seat clear. Kids: Leave the seat empty enough to sit. Caregivers: The seat is for sitting, not stashing. Look for: room for a person (or the dog).
  5. Test the put-back. Kids: Put something away without asking. Caregivers: If a label makes it obvious, it will hold. Look for: anyone can return an item unaided.
- Common mistake: labeling only the object and not its spot — a labeled home is what makes the system stick.
- Encouragement line: A named home for everything means no more "where does this go?"

## CARD 36 — Shine · Bench / Console [Shine #D98A2B]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The deep card. A numbered storyboard of the full Shine sequence wrapping the console, terracotta 1-to-8 arrows: (1) clear top and take the drawer fully out, (2) wipe wall and back edge, (3) clean the top, (4) vacuum and wipe the seat, (5) clean the drawer and sections, (6) wipe runners and vacuum the cavity, (7) wipe fronts and legs, (8) floor last. A honey "inspect & flag" magnifier notes problems without stopping to fix.
- Learning moment: Shine follows one fixed order — high and dry to low and wet, floor last — and you inspect and flag as you go rather than stopping to repair.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): the drawer pulled fully off its runners, the seat being vacuumed with the upholstery tool for outdoor grit, terracotta step numbers 1-8, floor-last at the bottom, an "inspect & flag" honey magnifier with a small flag, Riley + helper sharing the steps.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard comic panels / numbered process ring around one console (the deep multi-step card).
- Character approach: Riley vacuuming the seat with the upholstery tool; helper (this card: a warm grandmother, silver locs, warm brown skin, mustard cardigan) wiping the runners and back edge. Keep both identical to their base designs.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring on the current focus (the seat, step 4); terracotta numbered arrows for order 1 to 8; a small honey magnifier + flag icon for inspect-and-flag (not red — nothing is being fixed yet).
- Callout/arrow style: eight terracotta numbered step chips arranged in the working order around the furniture; one honey magnifier chip labeled "FLAG".
- Composition notes: console centered; the eight steps read clockwise ending at the floor; "floor last" clearly lowest; lower third calm; Shine badge top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Shine the console in one fixed order — top and drawer out first, floor last — and flag problems as you find them.
- Steps:
  1. Clear the top and take the drawer out. Kids: Top to three, then pull the drawer fully off its runners and lift the divider. Caregivers: Get the drawer right out so the cavity opens up. Look for: sand already showing at the back.
  2. Wipe the wall and back edge. Kids: Barely-damp cloth along where the top meets the wall. Caregivers: Clear the dust line at the back edge. Look for: a grey seam of built-up dust.
  3. Clean the top. Kids: Dust or vacuum, then damp multi-surface, then dry it off. Caregivers: Back edge to front, buff dry. Look for: sticky rings that need to soften.
  4. Do the seat. Kids: Vacuum the seat and seams with the upholstery tool for outdoor grit, then wipe. Caregivers: Wipe a hard seat or spot-clean a padded one, dry before sitting. Look for: the same grit here as on the floor.
  5. Clean the drawer. Kids: Tip sand and crumbs from the back corners, wipe the base and sections. Caregivers: Detail-brush the corners and wipe the divider. Look for: crumbs hiding in the joins.
  6. Wipe runners and vacuum the cavity. Kids: Wipe both runner tracks, then vacuum the empty cavity. Caregivers: Get the grit that drops behind the drawer. Look for: the hidden dirt trap behind the drawer.
  7. Wipe fronts and legs. Kids: Wipe the handles and the kick line where shoes scuff. Caregivers: Follow the legs down. Look for: scuff marks near the floor.
  8. Floor last. Kids: Vacuum under and around the legs, then mop the sealed floor. Caregivers: Always the last step so grit falls onto a floor you clean after. Look for: grit that dropped during the whole job.
- Common mistake: stopping mid-clean to fix a wobble or a loose screw — inspect and FLAG it, then act in the Safety pass. Also: cleaning the floor before the furniture, so falling grit re-dirties it.
- Encouragement line: Eight steps, one smooth flow — and you spotted every problem for later!

## CARD 37 — Safety · Bench / Console [Safety #DDA63A — the 4th S]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Act on what the clean turned up. Riley and the helper anchor a tippy top-heavy console to the wall with a bracket/strap, and move button batteries, coins and magnets out of a low drawer into a lidded tin. Red warning marks sit on the hazards; green checks confirm each fix.
- Learning moment: Safety is the fourth S — the moment you act on everything the Shine pass flagged: anchor the tippy console, and get small swallowable items out of a low drawer into a lidded tin.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a console being strapped/bracketed to the wall (anti-tip), a lidded tin catching button batteries + coins + magnets from a low drawer, a red warning triangle on the tip hazard turning to green when fixed, "the 4th S" cue, Riley + helper.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, hazard-and-fix pairing (red flag then green check).
- Character approach: Riley holding the anti-tip strap taut; helper (this card: a warm dad in a wheelchair, tan skin, short beard, blue flannel — show a kid of/adult of different abilities per the Bible) tipping small items into the lidded tin at an accessible height.
- Color legend use: red warning triangle on the tipping console and on the low drawer of small parts; green check appearing over the anchored unit and the closed tin.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrow from the wobbling console to its wall anchor; terracotta arrow from the low drawer to the lidded tin; small "4th S" chip near the badge.
- Composition notes: two clear fix vignettes (anchor left, lidded tin right); lower third calm; Safety badge top corner in honey (#DDA63A).

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Safety is the 4th S — now act on everything the clean flagged.
- Steps:
  1. Re-read your flags. Kids: Look at the problems you marked while cleaning. Caregivers: Gather every flag from the Shine pass. Look for: a rock, a tip, a loose screw, small swallowables.
  2. Anchor the console. Kids: Help hold the strap while it's fixed to the wall. Caregivers: Bracket a tippy or top-heavy console so it cannot tip toward you. Look for: no rock when you lean on it.
  3. Clear small swallowables. Kids: Move button batteries, coins and magnets out of the low drawer. Caregivers: Put them in a lidded tin up out of reach. Look for: choke and poison hazards at kid height.
  4. Fix the flagged hardware. Kids: Point out the loose or split parts. Caregivers: Tighten a backing-out runner screw; note a split seat or lifting splinter. Look for: a drawer that drops or drags.
  5. Confirm each fix. Kids: Give a thumbs-up when it's safe. Caregivers: Turn every red flag into a green check. Look for: nothing left unhandled.
- Common mistake: leaving a "later" hazard — Safety is the pass where flags become fixes, not more flags.
- Encouragement line: You didn't just spot the danger, you fixed it. That's the 4th S!

## CARD 38 — Standardize · Bench / Console [Standardize #6E8B5B]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley photographs the finished console — seat clear, one basket per job, drawer sectioned — and the helper tapes that printed photo inside a cupboard door as the target. The photo shows what "right" looks like at a glance.
- Learning moment: Standardize by photographing the finished state (seat clear, one basket per job, drawer sectioned) and posting it — a written standard tells you what to do; the ideal-state photo shows what right looks like.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a phone/camera framing the tidy console, a printed photo being taped inside a cupboard door, the reset shown in the photo (clear seat, labeled baskets, sectioned drawer), a green check on the matching real console, Riley + helper.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, before/after-as-target (photo-within-the-card).
- Character approach: Riley holding up the camera/phone framing the console; helper (this card: a warm mum, olive skin, dark ponytail, rust sweater) taping the printed photo inside the cupboard.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the printed target photo; green check where the real console matches the photo.
- Callout/arrow style: a terracotta arrow from the camera view to the taped-up printed photo; a small "TARGET" chip on the photo.
- Composition notes: real console on one side, the framed/taped target photo on the other, clearly the same scene; lower third calm; Standardize badge top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Photograph the finished console and post it — the photo is the target anyone can match.
- Steps:
  1. Finish the reset. Kids: Make the seat clear, baskets in place, drawer sectioned. Caregivers: Get it to the ideal state first. Look for: seat clear, one basket per job, drawer sectioned.
