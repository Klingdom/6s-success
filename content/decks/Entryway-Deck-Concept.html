6S Success · the Entryway deck · expanded concept

A room you can play.

The Entryway deck turns a real home reset into a loop you can play, learn, and finish together: face the room’s problems, equip the tools that end them, build the habits that hold them, and prove it with the actual before and after. Do the work, win the game — you cannot win without doing the work.

Today the deck ships three card types (Zones, Problems, Tools). It refers to six more — Habits, Skills, Upgrades, Wins, Events, Experts — that were never built. This is the whole nine-type system, defined.

The nine card types

Every card is one of nine kinds. Three describe the room (Zone, Problem, Tool); the other six make it a game and a curriculum (Habit, Skill, Upgrade, Win, Event, Expert). Each has a job in the story and a job in the rules.

EM
Micro Zone
the WHERE
A single place in the room to reset - the tray, the hooks, the mat.
The board spaces. Completing a zone (for real) advances one of the six S-tracks.
e.g. EM-002 Landing Zone
EP
Problem
the PAIN
The recurring failure a neglected zone breeds - the pile, the hunt, the mud.
Face-up tension. Its Impact both rewards solving it and penalizes the household each turn it festers.
e.g. EP-012 Morning Rush
ET
Tool
the FIX
The product that solves a problem for good - a bowl, hooks, a boot tray.
Equip to a zone (costs Momentum); grants a Permanent Bonus that lowers that zone's upkeep.
e.g. ET-001 Key Bowl
EH
Habit
the RITUAL
The small daily, weekly, or seasonal action that holds the reset.
Attach to a zone to convert a one-time win into a maintained streak - and to block Decay.
e.g. EH Coat-off-on-entry
ES
Skill
the ABILITY
A transferable 6S skill you learn once and use in every room.
Six Skills = the six S's. Unlock one and you reset any zone of that S faster and cheaper.
e.g. ES Shine to a method
EU
Upgrade
the LEVEL-UP
A bigger, aspirational build - the mudroom bench, the command wall.
Spend banked Momentum for a permanent, once-only zone upgrade (a stronger bonus).
e.g. EU Built-in bench
EW
Win
the PROOF
A milestone worth celebrating - the 60-second launch, the clean-floor month.
Earned only by hitting a real, measured target. Scores points and unlocks the next tier.
e.g. EW 60-Second Launch
EE
Event
the TEST
A real-life curveball - a rainy week, unexpected guests, sports season.
Drawn each round; spikes Impact on the zones it touches, forcing you to reprioritize.
e.g. EE Rainy Week
EX
Expert
the HELP
A coach you can call in - the organizer, the housekeeper, the safety officer.
Spend Momentum to pull an Expert for a one-shot bonus or to cancel an Event.
e.g. EX The Organizer

The economy & how you win

Momentum

The one currency. Earn it by solving Problems and completing Zones for real; spend it to equip Tools, buy Upgrades, and call Experts. A score you can only ever bank isn’t an economy — Momentum has a sink and a target.

The six S-tracks

Each completed Zone advances one of the six S’s. Filling all six — and holding a verified 7-day streak on every Zone — wins the Entryway.

SortStraightenShineSafetyStandardizeSustain

Decay

A Zone you stop maintaining flips back to its Problem side. Gains must be held, which is what turns a one-night win into an ongoing household habit.

The reality gate. You score the before / after delta of each card’s own Common-Symptoms list — a quick 0–5 audit before, the reset, then 0–5 after. Points are the change in the real room, never a self-ticked box, so the game is impossible to fake and finally cashes in the book’s long-promised audit baseline.

Ways to play

Daily Draw

One card is today's small task. Two minutes, done.

Zone Run

Grab one zone's cards and reset it start to finish (~20 min).

Family Co-op

Beat the whole entryway together before the Events pile up.

Campaign

Fill all six S-tracks, hold every streak, then unlock the next room.

Head to Head

In a bigger house, race to Habit-Established on your assigned zones.

The collectible layer

The same set powers a trading-card game. The six S’s become types (energy); a shared Time-Effort token is the resource a Tool-kit “land” produces; a Zone evolves Messy → Cleaned → Standardized → Sustained, each rung a real photographable task; rooms are gyms that earn badges on a House Passport; Experts are trainer cards and Events are the opposition. Deck-building a reset plan is the strategy — and every match still ends at the audit-and-after-photo gate, so a rush can never fake a clean. Rarity is a difficulty label, not a paywall: every card ships in the base box.

Beyond the Entryway

One room, then the house. The Entryway is set one (its EM-012 already hands off to ER-002 Living Room). Each of the 20 rooms becomes its own boxed set on the same nine-type grammar, and the House Passport is the campaign spine that strings them into a season.

A Family Edition cuts a leaner, sturdier, game-first version: fewer, bigger, picture-first cards, kid-simple roles, and the Safety hunt played as a scavenger game.

It plugs into the whole system: the deck is the playable face of the book’s method, the app’s tracker, and the micro-zone product kits — each Tool card is a real kit you can buy.

Anatomy of a card

Front — the hook

  • Code + card-type gauge (Difficulty / Impact / Bonus)
  • The S-tag badge (which of the six S’s)
  • A clean photoreal hero with numbered callouts
  • One-line Objective + a 30-second Quick Win
  • The Game Effect (what it does in play)

Back — the depth

  • Why It Matters + Common Symptoms (the audit rubric)
  • Best Practices + the 7-day Home Quest streak
  • One Did-You-Know stat, sourced
  • Next Card + 2–3 verified related links
  • A slim tracker: 3 checkboxes, no dead codes
