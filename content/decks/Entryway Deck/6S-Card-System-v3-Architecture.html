1 · Apply the six S's to the card system

The method that organizes a home also organizes its deck. Each S removes a specific waste from the 1,000-card set.

Sort
Cut the redundancy
The 1000+ set reprints the same six-S how-to on ~684 near-identical step cards. Sort keeps one universal copy of each method and deletes the rest, the overproduction / inventory waste the deck was carrying.
Straighten
A home for every card
Give every card one obvious place by splitting the set into three tiers: HOMES (where), MICRO ZONES (what), STEPS (how). Nothing floats; anyone can find or refile a card in one move.
Shine
One clean template
Standardize the card so it reads at a glance: the game designer's double-sided tarot (front = play face, back = method). Same anatomy on every card, so a mixed pile still scans.
Safety
Make the 4th S impossible to skip
Give Safety its own warm-alert colour + shield, and protect it with an interlock (no Safety, no lock) and a Hazard Bounty, so the step that keeps a home safe can't quietly vanish into the pile.
Standardize
Lock the system
One template per tier, one Tracker Mat, one House Passport. The set has a documented standard anyone can reprint, extend to a new room, or hand to a manufacturer.
Sustain
A set that keeps itself
The shared core + the House Passport + streaks mean adding room 21 is a few new zone cards, not a whole new deck. The architecture scales without bloating back to 1000+.
2 · The v3 set, in three tiers (plus the game layer)

1 · HOMES

the WHERE

Per room, the room-level cards: Primary Purpose, Family Values (with one-in-one-out), Before Photos, the 6S Audit (now the scorecard / Calm Track), Safety Blitz (a hazard-hunt board), Signature & After (a frame for your own photos), Keeping Rhythm, and the Room Kit.

160 · 8 cards x 20 rooms

2 · MICRO ZONES

the WHAT

One double-sided card per micro-zone. FRONT = the win-state target (the reset zone, at a glance) + one imperative + a time/effort pill + a loop-rail showing your place in the S-order. BACK = the exact inputs (the kit) and the ordered STEPS TO COMPLETE that specific zone, plus its inspect-and-flag list.

114 · 114 zones, 1 card each (steps live on the back)

3 · STEPS

the HOW

The universal six-S method, once: how to Sort (the Five Doors + red tag), Straighten (a home by use), Shine (the nine rules + fold-and-flip + the salt fix), Safety (the five questions + Hazard Bounty), Standardize (photo the ideal state), Sustain (bolt it to a habit). Learn these once; they apply to every zone in every room.

12 · ~12 shared cards (was ~684 reprinted)

+ SYSTEM

the GAME

The whole-home game layer: the Calm Track, Time-Tokens, the three modes (Reset Run / Daily Draw / Family Race), the Master how-to, and the House Passport line spine. Included once, powers every room.

12 · ~12 shared cards

5 · The Family Edition

A leaner, sturdier, game-first cut of the set (~~130 cards) built for kids at the table, not a shrunk-down manual.

Fewer, bigger, sturdier cards

Picture-first, rounded, wipe-clean; readable across the table by a 6-year-old.

Kid-simple S-order

Four kid steps, Sort, Home, Shine, Safe; grown-ups own Standardize & Sustain. Same canon, less to hold.

Game front-and-centre

The Calm Track board, the token punchboard, and the three modes ship in the box, not as an afterthought.

Kid roles & the hazard hunt

Kids own the low zones and the empty-hooks check, and the Safety Blitz is played as a scavenger hunt.

Picture labels & toss-in bins

Aligning pairs is an adult skill; a labelled toss-in bin is a kid skill, so the systems are ones kids can actually run.

Curated scope

Starts with the six highest-traffic family rooms (Entryway, Kitchen, Living/Family, Kids Bedroom, Bathroom, Mudroom); the rest are expansions.

6 · Cleaning events · Pokemon & Magic mechanics

One resource ties both games together: the Time-Effort token (Pokemon energy = Magic mana), produced by the Room Kit (the "lands"), spent on a fixed per-S cost table (Shine = 3, the deep one). Play teaches the truest lesson in the deck: a reset costs budgeted real time, so you must choose.

Pokemon · level up a room

Types → the six S's

Evolution → a zone goes Messy → Cleaned → Standardized → Sustained, each rung a real, photographable task

Gyms & badges → rooms are gyms; an audited room earns a badge on the House Passport

Energy → Time-Effort tokens, produced by the Kit

Professor → the how-to / onboarding card

Magic · plan & execute a reset

Lands → the Room Kit, no tools means no Shine

The stack / turn order → the fixed six-S loop, you cannot Standardize a mess

Permanents → Standardize & Sustain stay in play and keep scoring

Instants → Safety fixes, played the moment a hazard appears

Synergy / combos → shared-core products legitimately lower Shine's cost

De-fanged for a household

No paid random boosters or rarity chase (rarity is only a difficulty label, every card ships in the base set); pay-to-win is structurally impossible because money cannot fake the photo or the audit; grinding is capped at the real upkeep cadence; competitive play defaults to co-op with "score the room, not the person"; and finishing everything is celebrated, not punished.

Every event terminates in the Audit scorecard + after-photo match, so a rush can't fake a clean; a failed cell goes straight back on the board. Full design in reviews/review-tcg-mechanics.md.
