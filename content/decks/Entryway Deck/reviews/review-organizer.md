# Entryway Deck v2 — Professional Organizer Review

Reviewer lens: NAPO-level professional home organizer specializing in entryways, drop zones, and busy-family systems (KonMari-informed). The 6S cleaning method in this deck is genuinely strong — better than most. What it is thin on is the *organizing* layer that decides whether the room stays clean between cleans. Below are the deck-wide gaps, then concrete, card-keyed additions. I only list cards where I can add real value; method-only cards (Shine, most Safety) I leave alone.

---

## Top organizing gaps in the deck

1. **No incoming-mail/paper *system*, only a "verdict" rule.** The deck tells you to give paper a verdict (act/file/recycle) but never builds the physical thing that makes verdicts happen: a labeled mail sorter with defined slots and an *outbound* tray. "Verdict" without a slot to put the verdict in becomes a pile. This is the single biggest gap.

2. **No outbound / "leaves the house" flow.** Returns, library books, dry cleaning, signed permission slips, outgoing mail, packages to ship, the donation bag — the entryway is the last surface before the car, and the deck almost never uses it that way. A launch pad without an *out-box* only solves half the door.

3. **Sentimental / guilt items handled once (the coat) and nowhere else.** No rule for gift items, "someday it'll fit," kids' outgrown coats and shoes, or the memento/kids-art paper that lands on the console. Guilt is what stalls every Sort.

4. **Containment strategy is under-specified.** The deck names bins, baskets, trays but rarely says *how*: contain-by-category, one-basket-one-job, drawer dividers, clear vs. opaque bins, a cord/charging box, and toss-in bins for kids (aligning pairs is an adult skill; a bin is a kid skill).

5. **Labeling is a slogan, not a method.** "Label the home" is repeated on all five Straighten cards but never says how: label-maker vs. removable, *picture* labels for pre-readers, and where the label physically goes so it actually gets used.

6. **Almost nothing for renters / small spaces / no-entryway homes.** Everything assumes a rail, a console, a rack, a cupboard. No damage-free options (command hooks/strips, over-the-door racks, freestanding units, a tension rail) — which is where a huge share of readers actually live.

7. **Seasonal rotation is named but not built.** "Swap by weather" appears, but not *where* off-season goes (labeled bin up high / under the bench / vacuum bag), or the anchor that triggers the swap twice a year.

8. **Backpacks, bags, and sports gear are missing.** For families this is half the entryway chaos and it has no zone, no hook rule, no launch-pad basket.

9. **The Straighten and Sustain cards under-teach friction reduction.** Sustain leans entirely on "anchor to a habit," which is correct but incomplete. The other half is *making the right thing the easy thing*: hooks beat hangers, open bins beat lids, a bench where you shed shoes, one-motion storage. This belongs on the light cards.

## Card-keyed additions

**Card 4 — Room Kit.** The "Organization" list names containers but omits the tools that make them work. Add: a **label maker or a pack of removable/write-on labels**; **drawer dividers or small open bins** to section the console drawer; a **cord/charging box** to hide the dock tangle; **command hooks + an over-the-door hook rack** as the renter/small-space swap for a drilled rail; a **mail sorter with defined slots**; and a **standing donation/out-box bin** by the door. Tip: choose **opaque bins for ugly-but-necessary stuff (hats, gloves, dog gear) and clear bins only where seeing in helps** — clear bins broadcast their own clutter.

**Card 5 — Primary Purpose.** The launch pad is only half a launch pad if nothing *leaves*. Add to the definition: **an outbound spot for things that must go with you** (returns, library books, signed forms, dry cleaning) so they physically block the door instead of being forgotten on the counter. Tip: **the test cuts both ways — can anyone leave in under a minute *with the thing they were supposed to take*?** Add a labeled spare-key hook so a lost key is a 5-second fix, not a morning meltdown.

**Card 6 — Family Values.** Add a third household agreement that does more work than any cleaning rule: **one-in-one-out — a new coat, bag, or pair of shoes means an old one leaves that day.** It is the only rule that keeps a finished entryway finished. Also agree on a **"landing pad per person"** (a hook + a shelf/cubby + a basket) so every arrival has one obvious place to unload — shared space with no owner is what breeds the pile.

**Card 7 — Before Photos.** Add: **also photograph the mail pile and any junk-drawer/basket, and count the coats and shoes.** A number ("we owned 34 pairs of shoes for 4 people") is a sharper before-and-after than any photo and makes the one-in-one-out rule land. Sentimental note: shooting the *before* is also how you let go — you keep the picture, not the pile.

**Card 8 — 6S Audit.** Add one family-system question to the check: **"Does everyone — including the kids — actually know where their stuff goes without asking?"** A system only one person can run is a chore, not a system; that is the real thing to score.

**Card 11 — Keeping Rhythm.** The seasonal clock needs a *where* and a *when*. Add: **off-season coats and shoes go into one labeled bin (up high, under the bench, or a vacuum bag), swapped at the two clock changes** (daylight-saving weekends are the memorable anchor). And to the daily clock add the **outbound check** — glance at the out-box on the way out so returns and mail actually leave.

**Card 12 — Zone 1 / Landing Spot (Purpose).** Reframe this zone as the household's **command center**, because it is where mail lands whether you plan for it or not. Add to its purpose: **a mail sorter with named slots and an outbound tray**, so incoming paper has somewhere to go the moment it crosses the threshold instead of drifting across the console.

**Card 13 — Sort / Landing Spot.** Strong paper rules; add the *handling* and the *guilt*. **Open mail standing over the recycling bin — junk and envelopes never even reach the surface.** Sentimental paper (kids' art, cards, keepsakes) does **not** live on the landing spot: **photograph it or send it to a memory box elsewhere in the house** — the door is a transit zone, not a shrine. For mystery keys, **label with a date; if nothing's unlocked by then, it goes.**

**Card 14 — Straighten / Landing Spot (light card, needs the most).** Build the actual system: **a vertical mail sorter with three labeled slots — Action / To File / To Shred-Recycle — plus a shallow outbound tray for things leaving with you.** Give the dock a **cord box or a small charging drawer** so the tangle is hidden and the top stays clear. Put the drop-tray at the **exact spot your hand naturally opens as you walk in** (measure it — friction of even one extra step is why keys land elsewhere). Renter/small-space version: **a wall-mounted file pocket + key rail on command strips, or an over-the-door pocket organizer** — no console required.

**Card 17 — Standardize / Landing Spot.** Add: **label the sorter slots themselves** (and use picture/color labels if kids sort mail), so the standard is enforced by the slot, not by memory of the photo.

**Card 18 — Sustain / Landing Spot (light card).** Add the ritual and the friction fix: a **60-second nightly "clear to zero"** — every flat surface back to empty, anchored to lights-off. **Touch mail once:** it does not get set down without going into a slot. Anchor the actual paperwork to a recurring moment — a **"Sunday bill-and-file session" tied to a show you already watch**. And teach the family the sorter; a command center only one person understands isn't sustained, it's just delegated.

**Card 19 — Zone 2 / Coats (Purpose).** Two categories are missing from this zone and belong here: **daily bags/backpacks (each needs its own hook — bags on the floor is where the pile starts)** and **seasonal rotation** (current-season coats at the front/reachable, off-season boxed or pushed high). Accessories: **one bin per person, or clear bins, for hats-gloves-scarves** so the shelf doesn't become a jumble.

**Card 20 — Sort / Coats.** Excellent "weather not number" rule; extend the guilt handling beyond the expensive coat. Add: **the gift coat, the "someday it'll fit" coat, and the kids' outgrown coats get the same test — would you acquire it again today? If not, it warms someone else this winter.** Kids grow out yearly; **size-out at each seasonal swap.** Crucial behavioral step: **bag donations immediately and put them in the car** — a donation bag that lingers in the entryway just becomes new clutter.

**Card 21 — Straighten / Coats (light card).** The organizing content the card lacks: **hooks beat hangers for daily coats — one motion means kids and tired adults actually use them** (reserve the rail/hangers for guests and long coats). **Add a second low rail or a row of low hooks under the adult rail for kids** — the whole "shortest person's height" rule needs a place to exist. Renter version: **an over-the-door hook rack or a freestanding coat tree** instead of drilling. Labeling: **name or photo labels on each hook**, current-season coats to the reachable front, off-season pushed to the ends or up top.

**Card 24 — Standardize / Coats.** Add: **picture labels on the accessory bins** (a mitten icon, a hat icon) so pre-readers put things back — the label is only a standard if the user can read it.

**Card 25 — Sustain / Coats (light card).** Add the friction and rotation mechanics: the **one-coat rule is enforced by the empty hook and by one-in-one-out** — a new coat in means an old coat out that day. Anchor the **seasonal coat swap to the daylight-saving clock change** so it never gets forgotten. And a **Friday backpack empty-out** (papers, lunchboxes, permission slips) before they compost in the bag over the weekend.

**Card 26 — Zone 3 / Shoes (Purpose).** Add a boundary and a limit: **a "shoes-off here" line at the door with a bench to sit on and a basket right there** — the single biggest lever for keeping outside dirt out of the whole house. And a **per-person daily limit on the rack (e.g., 2–3 pairs); the rest are stored or off-season**, or the rack overflows within a week.

**Card 27 — Sort / Shoes.** Add the sentimental and single-shoe rules: **kids' outgrown shoes move on fast — they won't remember them, and holding them just shrinks the rack.** The "worn once" fancy pair and the expensive-mistake pair get the honest test. **Donate or bin orphan singles** — a lone shoe is not a pair-in-waiting. Same rule as coats: **bag the donations and get them to the car.**

**Card 28 — Straighten / Shoes (light card).** Real containment guidance: **a labeled cubby or open bin per person, not a perfectly aligned row** — a bin is a toss-in target a child can actually hit; aligned pairs is an adult standard nobody sustains. Renter/small-space: **an over-the-door shoe pocket, a shoe rack on the back of the coat-closet door, or a slim vertical spinner.** Keep the **daily rack to the per-person limit**, and put **name/picture labels at kid height** on each cubby.

**Card 32 — Sustain / Shoes (light card).** Add the friction reduction the card is missing: **shoes-off actually happens when there's a bench to sit on and a bin within arm's reach — build the ease, don't rely on discipline.** Add a **weekly "rehome the orphan shoes" sweep with the family**, a **seasonal shoe swap** alongside the coats, and **one-in-one-out on the rack.** Teach kids the toss-in bin so it's their system, not your enforcement.

**Card 33 — Zone 4 / Bench-Console (Purpose).** Name the trap: **an entryway drawer becomes a junk drawer within weeks unless it is divided and every section is labeled.** Add to the zone: **a charging drawer** (dock and cords hidden inside) and, under an open bench, **a labeled launch-pad basket per family member** for tomorrow's stuff.

**Card 34 — Sort / Bench-Console.** Add the drawer discipline: **categorize what's allowed (sunglasses, pet gear, mail supplies, spare keys) and evict anything unrelated from the entry entirely** — the console drawer is the black hole every other room's clutter migrates toward. If it doesn't earn a spot at the door, it doesn't live at the door.

**Card 35 — Straighten / Bench-Console (light card).** The containment specifics it lacks: **drawer dividers or small open bins to zone the drawer by category, each labeled** ("keys," "sunglasses," "dog"). Under an open bench, **a labeled basket per person — the launch pad** — one basket, one owner, one job. Enforce **one-basket-one-category** so nothing roams. Renter tip: **stacking clear bins or a rolling cart** turns any bench into contained storage without built-ins.

**Card 39 — Sustain / Bench-Console (light card).** Add the single highest-value family habit the deck is missing: **the nightly launch-pad load — before bed, each person packs tomorrow's bag/shoes/items into their basket by the door.** It converts the morning scramble into a grab-and-go and is the behavior that actually keeps the surface clear. Pair it with **one-in-one-out (full basket = something leaves)** and anchor the **Returns-basket run to a weekly errand** already on the calendar.

**Card 40 — Zone 5 / Door-Mat-Floor (Purpose).** Add the delivery/outbound layer the door itself provides: **a package/delivery drop spot just inside (not in the swing), a clip or wall pocket for outgoing mail, and a hook for the donation bag and reusable shopping bags** — the door is the last chance to grab what's leaving.

**Card 42 — Straighten / Door-Mat-Floor (light card).** Build the **outgoing station** the deck never places anywhere: **an outbound tray or wall pocket for letters to post, a clip on the door for the day's must-take papers, and a hook for the growing donation bag.** Hang the **reusable bags and dog leash right at the exit** so grab-and-go is one motion. Renter version: **an over-the-door pocket organizer** carries all of this with zero holes. Keep it all out of the door's swing.

**Card 46 — Sustain / Door-Mat-Floor (light card).** Add the leave-the-house behaviors: a **reusable-bag hook and a small "don't forget" spot** by the door so nothing gets left behind; **anchor outgoing mail and the donation-bag drop to bin night** (bag goes to the car when full — it never sits and re-clutters); and a **daily hands-free-entry pass** — everything to its owned hook, basket, and cubby in one trip through the door.

---

*Notes:* The friction-anchoring idea on the Sustain cards ("bolt upkeep to a moment that already happens") is genuinely excellent and should stay — I've added its missing other half (making the right thing physically easiest) rather than replacing it. The one rule I'd add across the whole deck if I could add only one: **one-in-one-out**, stated in Family Values (Card 6) and echoed on every zone's Sustain card. It is what separates a room that was organized once from a room that stays organized.
