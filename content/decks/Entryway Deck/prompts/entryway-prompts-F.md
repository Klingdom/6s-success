# Entryway Deck — Batch F Art Prompts (Cards 40–46)

**Zone 5 · Door, Mat & Floor.** Source of truth: `Entryway-Deck-v2-CardText.md` (Cards 40–46) and the frozen `Entryway-Art-StyleBible.md`. Six S's, in order, always: Sort · Straighten · Shine · Safety (4th) · Standardize · Sustain. No QR codes, no logos, no misspelled text, no em dashes, nothing scary.

RILEY is identical on every card: a kid about 8, warm brown skin, curly dark hair, mustard striped tee, teal shorts, orange sneakers. The grown-up helper varies in appearance across cards but is always warm and helps alongside, never instead of Riley. Optional small friendly dog for warmth.

## CARD 40 — Door, Mat & Floor (Zone intro) [Zone / intro]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A cutaway hero of the whole Zone 5 anatomy at the front door. Riley stands at the open threshold pointing out each part while the helper crouches beside the flat, gripped inside mat. The one big teaching device is a honey spotlight on the inside mat with a small chip label "MOST OF THE JOB," and a bold terracotta line showing that everything outside meets the mat before any other floor.
- Learning moment: The mat is the single thing standing between the outside and every other floor in the house, so this zone protects the whole home.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): open door face + frame + handle + letterbox + glass panel; inside mat glowing under a honey spotlight; coarse outside mat on the step; a clear stride of floor inside the door swing (green check); the caddy of inputs at Riley's feet.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: cutaway overlay (labelled anatomy of the doorway) in friendly flat vector.
- Character approach: Riley presenter pose, arm sweeping across the doorway; helper crouched at the mat, thumb-up. Same outfits as every card.
- Color legend use: honey spotlight on the mat = look here; green check on the clear floor stride; terracotta arrow from outside step to the inside mat showing dirt's path.
- Callout/arrow style: 4-6 small rounded label chips (1-3 words) pinned to parts; one terracotta path arrow.
- Composition notes: door and frame fill the card; calm cream lower third clear for the caption; Zone badge top corner. Warm daylight spilling through the open door.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The doorway and its mats catch the outside so the rest of the house stays clean.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Meet the zone. Kids: point to the door, the mats, and the floor. Caregivers: name each part out loud together. Look for: the door face, handle, glass, threshold, both mats, and the floor inside the swing.
  2. Find the two mats. Kids: spot the soft inside mat and the rough outside mat. Caregivers: explain each mat's job. Look for: one coarse mat outside, one flat mat inside.
  3. See the mat's big job. Kids: trace the dirt path from outside to the mat. Caregivers: "the mat catches it first." Look for: grit stopping at the mat, not spreading.
  4. Check the swing. Kids: walk the clear stride inside the door. Caregivers: make sure the door opens all the way. Look for: nothing on the floor in the door's path.
  5. Gather the caddy. Kids: help carry the tools. Caregivers: set outside space to beat and wash the mats. Look for: cleaner, glass cleaner, floor cleaner, colour-coded cloths, vacuum, caddy.
- Common mistake: Skipping the mat because it "looks fine." The mat does the most work, so it needs the most care.
- Encouragement line: Clean this one zone and you protect every floor you own.

## CARD 41 — Door, Mat & Floor · Sort [Sort]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley and the helper clear everything stored against the door and out of its swing. The floor inside the swing becomes a glowing green clear zone. A stray propped umbrella, a leaning box, and a shoe pile get lifted out along a terracotta arrow toward an out-of-swing spot, while a red arc shows the door's swing path that must stay empty.
- Learning moment: Nothing lives against the door or inside its swing path; that floor stays completely clear.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a red dashed swing arc from the door; items being lifted out of the arc (box, umbrella, shoe); green glow on the now-clear floor; terracotta "out" arrow; a small red-tag chip on a "maybe" item.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with a top-down swing-arc overlay motif.
- Character approach: Riley lifting a box out of the arc; helper steadying the door open. Same outfits.
- Color legend use: red dashed arc = keep clear; green glow = cleared floor; honey ring on the one item being decided; terracotta arrow = where it goes.
- Callout/arrow style: 2-3 label chips ("CLEAR," "KEEP OUT," a dated red tag); one terracotta out-arrow.
- Composition notes: door hinged at one side with the arc sweeping across the lower-middle; cleared cream floor in the lower third for caption.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The door needs an empty runway, so nothing gets stored in its path.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Open the door wide. Kids: swing it slowly all the way. Caregivers: watch what it bumps. Look for: anything the door hits or scrapes.
  2. Find the swing path. Kids: trace the arc the door draws on the floor. Caregivers: mark the invisible arc together. Look for: the whole curve the door travels.
  3. Lift out the blockers. Kids: carry the box, umbrella, and stray shoe out of the arc. Caregivers: give each a real home elsewhere. Look for: nothing leaning on the door.
  4. Give each a verdict. Kids: keep, move, or let it go. Caregivers: no "leave it" pile. Look for: an answer for every item.
  5. Red-tag the maybes. Kids: stick a dated tag on the not-sure item. Caregivers: set the go-date. Look for: if it is not used by the date, it goes.
  6. Clear the floor fully. Kids: sweep your eyes across the swing floor. Caregivers: confirm it is empty. Look for: a completely clear stride inside the door.
- Common mistake: Parking "just for now" items behind the door; they always stay and block the exit.
- Encouragement line: An empty runway means the door opens easy and everyone gets out fast.

## CARD 42 — Door, Mat & Floor · Straighten [Straighten]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The poka-yoke card. Riley and the helper lay a thin taped outline exactly where the inside mat belongs and mark the clear stride inside the door swing. On the left the mat sits squared inside its taped outline (green check); on the right a curled, shoved mat pokes outside the tape so the mismatch is instantly visible (red mark). Two mats and no more, plus an umbrella stand so nothing leans.
- Learning moment: Tape a thin outline where the mat sits so a curled or shoved mat shows instantly; give the mat a visible home.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): taped rectangle outline on the floor; mat squared inside it (good) vs mat shoved past the tape (bad); "2 MATS" chip; the umbrella stand; clear floor stride marked; terracotta step arrows.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard comic split (good vs. off) with a floor-outline overlay.
- Character approach: Riley pressing tape down along the outline; helper squaring the mat to the door. Same outfits.
- Color legend use: green check on the mat inside the tape; red mark on the mat spilling over the tape; honey ring on the taped outline; terracotta arrows numbering the tape-then-place order.
- Callout/arrow style: chips "TAPE OUTLINE," "SQUARED," "2 MATS"; terracotta 1 to 2 arrows.
- Composition notes: floor-forward, near top-down on the mat area so the outline reads clearly; left panel tidy, right panel a shoved mat; caption band clear below.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Give the mat a taped home so a shoved or curled mat is obvious at a glance.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Square the mat to the door. Kids: line the mat up straight with the doorway. Caregivers: set it flat and gripped. Look for: edges parallel to the threshold.
  2. Tape a thin outline. Kids: press tape along each mat edge. Caregivers: use a thin, low-profile line. Look for: a clean rectangle the mat lives inside.
  3. Mark the clear stride. Kids: outline the walking path inside the swing. Caregivers: keep it inside the door's arc. Look for: a marked, empty stride.
  4. Keep two mats only. Kids: count them, one inside, one outside. Caregivers: no extra layered mats. Look for: exactly two, no more.
  5. Stand the umbrella up. Kids: put wet umbrellas in the stand. Caregivers: so nothing leans on the door or wall. Look for: an umbrella stand doing its job.
  6. Test the tell. Kids: nudge the mat and see it poke past the tape. Caregivers: "now you know to fix it." Look for: any curl or shove showing outside the outline.
- Common mistake: No outline, so a slowly creeping or curling mat goes unnoticed until someone trips.
- Encouragement line: When the mat has a taped home, anyone can put it right in one second.

## CARD 43 — Door, Mat & Floor · Shine [Shine]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The deep card, told as a numbered top-to-bottom sequence around the doorway. Terracotta numbered arrows walk the eye through the ordered clean: dust the top rail and frame corners, wipe the door face top-to-bottom, wipe the frame and architrave, do the handle/letterbox/glass, then the bottom edge and threshold, beat and wash the inside mat, shake the outside mat, and vacuum-and-mop the floor inside the swing last. Riley and helper each work a step; a small honey "inspect and flag" magnifier catches a curled mat corner without stopping to fix it.
- Learning moment: Shine follows a fixed high-to-low order and you inspect and flag problems as you go, without stopping to fix them.
- Key elements to keep (4-6): terracotta numbers 1 through 8 around the door in order; the two-cloth idea (mist onto the cloth, not the glass); the inside mat being beaten/vacuumed outdoors; floor inside the swing done last (green); a small honey magnifier flagging a curled mat corner.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard sequence overlay on one hero doorway (numbered path).
- Character approach: Riley wiping the door face top-to-bottom; helper at the threshold/mat step. Same outfits.
- Color legend use: terracotta numbered arrows = the exact order; honey ring/magnifier = inspect focus; green check on the floor done last; a small red flag icon on the found defect (not being fixed).
- Callout/arrow style: tiny numbered chips 1-8; one "FLAG IT" chip by the magnifier; keep labels 1-3 words.
- Composition notes: whole doorway visible so the high-to-low path reads; the mat-beating happens just outside the open door; caption band clear below.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Clean the doorway top to bottom in order, and flag anything wrong as you pass it.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Dust the top rail and corners. Kids: reach the top of the door and clear cobwebs from the frame corners. Caregivers: hold the step stool. Look for: dust and webs up high, done first.
  2. Wipe the door face, top to bottom. Kids: wipe downward, extra on the panels and kick line. Caregivers: barely-damp cloth. Look for: streak-free face, clean base.
  3. Wipe the frame and architrave. Kids: get the top ledge and the strike-side edge hands push. Caregivers: dry behind. Look for: the edge where fingers grab.
  4. Do handle, letterbox and glass. Kids: mist cleaner onto the cloth, never the glass, wipe one way, buff dry the other. Caregivers: check the flap. Look for: bright glass, clean handle.
  5. Clean the bottom edge and threshold. Kids: open the door and wipe the very bottom edge and threshold. Caregivers: dry after. Look for: the grit line the mat never reaches.
  6. Beat and wash the inside mat. Kids: vacuum the top, flip and vacuum under, beat it outdoors. Caregivers: wash if washable. Look for: return it only fully dry.
  7. Shake the outside mat. Kids: beat out grit and salt. Caregivers: hose a rubber-backed one, stand it to dry. Look for: no loose grit left.
  8. Floor inside the swing, last. Kids: both mats up, vacuum the patch, then mop. Caregivers: dry before anyone steps on it. Look for: clean, dry, clear floor.
- Common mistake: Doing the floor before the door, so dust and grit fall onto floor you just cleaned.
- Encouragement line: Top to bottom, mat then floor last, and the whole doorway shines.

## CARD 44 — Door, Mat & Floor · Safety [Safety (4th)]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The mat test. Riley wipes their feet on the mat the way people really do, then the helper lifts the mat corner to look underneath. A honey magnifier shows grit that rode straight over the fibres onto the floor, meaning the mat is worn out and gets a red "REPLACE" verdict. A second beat shows the good case: a flat, gripped mat with the door swinging fully open as a clear exit (green check).
- Learning moment: Do the mat test; if grit rides over the fibres onto the floor, the mat has failed and gets replaced. Keep the mat flat and gripped, and the door must open fully as an exit.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): Riley wiping feet on the mat; helper lifting the mat corner; honey magnifier on grit that carried through onto the floor; red "REPLACE" chip on the worn mat; green check on a flat gripped mat and a fully open door as exit.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: line-art-plus-accent close-up of the mat test, with a small good/bad beat.
- Character approach: Riley mid feet-wipe; helper lifting the corner to inspect. Same outfits.
- Color legend use: honey magnifier = the test focus; red mark and "REPLACE" chip = failed mat; green check = flat gripped mat and clear open exit door.
- Callout/arrow style: chips "MAT TEST," "REPLACE," "FLAT + GRIPPED"; a small terracotta arrow tracing grit riding over the fibres.
- Composition notes: big readable close-up of the mat and floor under it; the open door shown as an unobstructed exit; caption band clear below.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): A worn mat lets grit ride straight through, so test it and replace it when it fails.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Pick a dry, gritty day. Kids: wait for dry weather. Caregivers: choose a real, normal day. Look for: dry grit on shoes, not mud.
  2. Wipe your feet for real. Kids: scuff your feet on the mat the way you always do. Caregivers: no extra-careful wiping. Look for: a normal, honest wipe.
  3. Lift the mat and look. Kids: peek at the floor under the mat. Caregivers: lift the corner. Look for: grit that made it onto the floor.
  4. Read the result. Kids: did the dirt ride over the fibres? Caregivers: name the verdict. Look for: if grit rode through, replace it this month.
  5. Keep the mat flat and gripped. Kids: press the corners down. Caregivers: check the backing still grips. Look for: no curl, no slide.
  6. Test the exit. Kids: open the door all the way. Caregivers: make sure nothing blocks it. Look for: the door opens fully as an exit.
- Common mistake: Judging a mat by how it looks on top; a mat can look fine and still let all the grit through.
- Encouragement line: A mat that passes the test keeps the whole house's floors safe.

## CARD 45 — Door, Mat & Floor · Standardize [Standardize]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley photographs the finished, clear threshold with a phone (no logos on screen), capturing both mats flat, the floor clear, and the door open. The helper tapes the printed photo inside the coat cupboard as the target. A honey ring frames the photo and its taped copy; small check chips confirm "MATS FLAT," "FLOOR CLEAR," "SWINGS FREE."
- Learning moment: Photograph the clear threshold; that photo becomes the target the doorway has to match.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): Riley taking the standard photo of the clear threshold; the printed photo being taped inside the cupboard; both mats flat and squared; clear floor; door swinging free and latching; three tiny check chips.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with a photo-frame overlay (the standard image).
- Character approach: Riley holding the phone level to shoot; helper taping the printout up. Same outfits.
- Color legend use: honey ring around the photo = this is the target; green checks on the flat mats, clear floor, and free-swinging door.
- Callout/arrow style: chips "STANDARD PHOTO," "MATS FLAT," "FLOOR CLEAR"; a small terracotta arrow from the phone to the taped printout.
- Composition notes: clean, finished doorway as the subject inside a photo frame; the taped printout small in a cupboard at the side; caption band clear below.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): A photo of the perfect threshold becomes the target anyone can match at a glance.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Finish the reset. Kids: check both mats are flat and the floor is clear. Caregivers: confirm the door latches. Look for: mats flat, floor clear, door swinging free.
