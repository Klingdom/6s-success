# Entryway Deck — Batch D Art Direction & Image Prompts

Cards 26–32 · ZONE 3: SHOES & BOOTS (rack / boot tray / clear stride)
Source of truth: Entryway-Deck-v2-CardText.md · Style: Entryway-Art-StyleBible.md (frozen)

Signature idea running through the whole zone: **the boot tray is the whole point — it catches salt and grit at the door so it never scratches every floor in the house. Control at the source.**

Canon check for this batch: the six S's are exactly Sort · Straighten · Shine · Safety (4th) · Standardize · Sustain. No five-step model, Safety never dropped or moved, never "Set in Order." No QR codes, no logos, no misspelled art text, nothing scary, no em dashes.

Character continuity (identical on every card): RILEY — kid about 8, warm brown skin, curly dark hair, mustard striped tee, teal shorts, orange sneakers. GROWN-UP HELPER — warm caregiver, appearance varied per card as noted, always helping alongside, never doing it for Riley. Optional small friendly dog for warmth.

## CARD 26 — Shoes & Boots (Zone intro) [Zone intro / terracotta]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A wide, welcoming view of the whole shoe zone by the door — a low shoe rack with pairs lined soles-down, a boot tray holding wet boots front and centre with a honey spotlight on it, and a clean clear stride of floor between the door and the rack. Riley sets a pair onto the rack while the helper gestures to the boot tray.
- Learning moment: This is the map of the zone, and the boot tray is the hero — it catches the salt and grit that would otherwise scratch every floor in the house.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): low shoe rack with paired shoes soles-down; boot tray with wet boots under a honey highlight ring; clear floor strip door-to-rack; the caddy of inputs; Riley plus helper; small "control at the source" honey chip on the tray.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, gentle isometric room view so the whole zone reads as a map.
- Character approach: Riley mid-action placing a pair; helper (warm, tall, short curly grey hair, olive-green cardigan) pointing to the tray.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring on the boot tray (this is the focus); green check on the neatly paired rack; the clear floor strip left calm and empty.
- Callout/arrow style: one terracotta arrow tracing the clear stride from door to rack; small rounded honey chip reading "AT THE SOURCE".
- Composition notes: rack and tray fill the upper two thirds; clear floor strip and lower third kept calm for the caption; Zone badge top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The boot tray stops salt and grit at the door, so it never scratches the rest of the house.
- Steps:
  1. Look at the zone. Kids: find the rack, the tray, and the floor path. Caregivers: name each part out loud. Look for: a clear walking strip.
  2. Pair the shoes. Kids: line pairs soles-down on the rack. Caregivers: give each person a shelf. Look for: no shoes on the floor.
  3. Feed the tray. Kids: put wet boots in the tray, not on the floor. Caregivers: keep the tray dry and clean. Look for: grit caught in the tray, not on the tile.
  4. Keep the stride clear. Kids: nothing in the door-to-rack path. Caregivers: check the swing of the door. Look for: an open walking lane.
- Common mistake: leaving wet boots straight on the floor so the grit spreads everywhere.
- Encouragement line: One little tray protects every floor in your home.

## CARD 27 — Sort · Shoes & Boots [Sort #CB4B36]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley and the helper have emptied the rack onto a clean floor and are giving every pair a verdict — this-week pairs go back on the rack (green), split-soled or broken trainers go to a "GO" bin (red), off-season pairs go into a labelled box, and a lone pair with no owner heads out.
- Learning moment: Nothing goes back on the rack without a verdict; only this week's real pairs earn a slot.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): emptied rack; a keep pile with green check; a broken split-soled trainer with red mark going to a GO bin; a labelled off-season box; a red-tag date on a "maybe" pair.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, close sorting scene.
- Character approach: Riley holding up a shoe deciding; helper (warm, brown skin, short black hair, denim apron) holding the GO bin.
- Color legend use: green glow on the keep pairs; red mark on the broken trainer; honey ring on a red-tagged maybe pair.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrows splitting the pile into KEEP, GO, and OFF-SEASON; small honey date-tag chip.
- Composition notes: emptied rack to one side, three destinations clearly separated; lower third calm.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Only this week's real pairs earn a slot on the rack.
- Steps:
  1. Empty the rack. Kids: take every pair off. Caregivers: clear a floor space to sort on. Look for: an empty rack.
  2. Keep this week's pairs. Kids: set pairs you wear back to keep. Caregivers: match them to who is home. Look for: real, worn pairs.
  3. Retire the broken. Kids: split-soled or broken trainers into the GO bin. Caregivers: recycle where you can. Look for: cracked soles.
  4. Box the off-season. Kids: snow boots or sandals into the labelled box. Caregivers: label the box. Look for: nothing off-season on the daily rack.
  5. Send off the ownerless. Kids: a pair with no wearer here, out. Caregivers: donate them. Look for: strays with no owner.
- Common mistake: keeping a pair "just in case" instead of giving it a verdict.
- Encouragement line: Every pair gets an answer, and that is what makes room.

## CARD 28 — Straighten · Shoes & Boots [Straighten #BC4B2A]

- Scene idea: The kept pairs get proper homes — a slot per pair soles-down on the rack, a boot tray waiting for anything wet, one basket for daily slip-ons, and named shelves per person. Riley slides a pair into a labelled slot while the helper labels a shelf.
- Learning moment: A home chosen by how you use it, and a labelled spot means anyone can put it back without asking.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): rack with one slot per pair, soles down; a labelled boot tray for wet; a slip-on basket; named shelves (small name chips); honey ring on the label being applied.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector, tidy front-on rack view.
- Character approach: Riley placing a pair soles-down; helper (warm, light skin, red-brown ponytail, mustard jumper) sticking a name label on a shelf.
- Color legend use: green checks on filled labelled slots; honey highlight ring on the label being placed.
- Callout/arrow style: small rounded name chips ("RILEY", "MOM") on shelves; a "SOLES DOWN" chip; terracotta arrow showing a pair going into its slot.
- Composition notes: rack fills upper two thirds with clearly separated homes; boot tray and slip-on basket at the base; lower third calm.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Give every pair a labelled home so anyone can put it back without asking.
- Steps:
  1. One slot per pair. Kids: stand each pair soles-down in its slot. Caregivers: space the rack so pairs do not touch. Look for: no piling.
  2. Set the wet tray. Kids: put the boot tray where wet boots land. Caregivers: place it by the door. Look for: a clear spot for wet.
  3. Make a slip-on basket. Kids: daily slip-ons in one basket. Caregivers: pick a basket that fits. Look for: quick shoes grab-and-go.
  4. Name the shelves. Kids: find your named shelf. Caregivers: label a shelf per person. Look for: a name on every home.
- Common mistake: labelling the shoe instead of the shelf home.
- Encouragement line: A named shelf means no more "where do these go?"

## CARD 29 — Shine · Shoes & Boots [Shine #D98A2B]  (the deep card)

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A numbered storyboard of the ordered Shine sequence around the shoe zone, ending with the boot tray being washed outside and the floor strip mopped last. Riley and the helper move through the steps together, with an inspect-and-flag magnifier at the end.
- Learning moment: Shine runs in a fixed order — clean from high and dry toward the floor, wash the tray outside, do the walking strip last — and you inspect and flag as you go without stopping to fix.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): step 1 clearing the rack and knocking mud off outside; wiping kept pairs; wiping shelves; taking the boot tray outside and hosing it; wiping wall and skirting; vacuuming under the rack; the clear floor strip mopped last; a magnifier "inspect and flag" corner.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard comic panels — a numbered sequence so the order teaches itself.
- Character approach: Riley in every panel doing the task; helper (warm, dark brown skin, closely cropped hair, slate-blue apron) assisting; both hold matching action across panels.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring on the boot-tray wash panel (the signature moment); green checks appear as each panel completes; red flag markers appear only in the inspect-and-flag corner.
- Callout/arrow style: bold terracotta step numbers 1 to 7 driving the order; a magnifier icon with small red flag chips for the inspect step (no scary imagery).
- Composition notes: seven small clean panels in reading order (grid), the boot-tray-outside panel enlarged and honey-ringed as the hero; a slim inspect-and-flag strip at the base with a calm caption band below.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Clean top-down and door-last, wash the tray outside, and flag what you find without stopping to fix.
- Steps:
  1. Clear the rack and knock off mud. Kids: take footwear off; outside, knock soles together and brush the welts with the stiff dry brush. Caregivers: do the mud outside, not indoors. Look for: caked mud gone before wiping.
  2. Wipe the pairs you keep. Kids: wipe the uppers; shake and wipe slipper soles; set them aside. Caregivers: keep cleaned pairs off the floor. Look for: clean uppers.
  3. Wipe the rack shelves. Kids: knock dried mud into the bin, then wipe multi-surface front to back. Caregivers: dry after so mud does not cake back on. Look for: dry, grit-free shelves.
  4. Take the boot tray outside and wash it. Kids: hose it, scrub the salt film and grit from the corners, rinse. Caregivers: dry it fully — a wet tray just re-wets the floor. Look for: corners free of grit and salt.
  5. Wipe the wall and skirting. Kids: lift the mud spray and scuff behind the rack, barely damp. Caregivers: dry after. Look for: clean skirting behind the rack.
  6. Vacuum under the rack. Kids: reach the dark grit trap underneath, then wipe the sealed floor there. Caregivers: use the crevice tool. Look for: no hidden grit line.
  7. Clear the floor strip, last. Kids: vacuum the full stride door-to-rack, then mop. Caregivers: let it dry before anyone crosses it wet. Look for: a clean, dry walking lane.
  Inspect and flag as you go (do not stop to fix): a hairline split in the boot tray leaking (note any dark stain under it); a sagging shelf or loose joint that makes the rack rock; rust on the frame where salt water pools; a damp stain or soft patch.
- Common mistake: putting a wet boot tray straight back, so it just re-wets the floor.
- Encouragement line: Wash the tray, and you have protected every floor in the house.

## CARD 30 — Safety · Shoes & Boots [Safety #DDA63A] (the 4th S)

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: Riley and the helper act on the three hazards the clean turned up — keep the stride of floor inside the door clear (fall), wet boots on a tray so the floor is not slippery (slip), and anchor a top-heavy rack to the wall (crush). A clear "4th S" badge marks Safety.
- Learning moment: Safety is the fourth S — you act now on everything the cleaning flagged.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): clear floor stride with a green check; wet boots on the tray (slip prevented); a wall anchor bracket being fitted to a top-heavy rack; three small From-the-Shine-flags chips; honey ring on the anchor being fixed.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector with three clear hazard-fix vignettes.
- Character approach: Riley steadying the rack; helper (warm, medium brown skin, glasses, teal shirt) fixing the wall anchor. Calm, capable, never alarmed.
- Color legend use: green checks on each fixed hazard; a single small red watch-out mark on the un-anchored rack before it is fixed; honey ring on the anchor point.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrow from the tippy rack to the wall anchor; three small rounded chips: "CLEAR PATH", "WET ON TRAY", "ANCHOR IT".
- Composition notes: three tidy vignettes; Safety-yellow "4th S" badge prominent in the top corner; lower third calm.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Safety is the fourth S — fix now what the clean turned up.
- Steps:
  1. Keep the stride clear. Kids: nothing left in the door-to-rack path. Caregivers: check the door swing too. Look for: an open, uncluttered lane (plain fall).
  2. Wet boots on the tray. Kids: put dripping boots in the tray, not the floor. Caregivers: keep the tray dry underneath. Look for: no wet, slippery patches (slip).
  3. Anchor the rack. Kids: hold the rack steady. Caregivers: fix a top-heavy rack to the wall. Look for: a rack that cannot tip (crush).
  Act on the Shine flags: a hairline split in the boot tray leaking (note any dark stain under it); a sagging shelf or loose joint that makes the rack rock; rust on the frame where salt water pools.
- Common mistake: noticing a wobbly rack during cleaning and never coming back to anchor it.
- Encouragement line: You found it, you fixed it — that is the fourth S.

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: The finished shoe zone is photographed as the target: named shelves, wet boots in the tray, path clear. The helper takes the standard photo on a phone while Riley points proudly at the named shelves; the printed photo is being taped up as the standard.
