# Entryway Deck — Illustration Plans & Image Prompts (Batch A: Cards 1–11)

*The deck-system + whole-room cards. Frozen against `Entryway-Art-StyleBible.md`. Source text from `Entryway-Deck-v2-CardText.md`. Six S's are always Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety (4th), Standardize, Sustain. RILEY and the grown-up helper are visually identical on every card. ## CARD 1 — The Entryway (Deck cover) [Deck]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A hero portrait of the whole entryway as a warm, inviting little stage, with RILEY stepping through the front door and the grown-up helper welcoming them. The one room where the outside comes in, shown at its calm, reset best so the cover promises the payoff.
- Learning moment: This tiny room sets the tone for the whole house and pays back a 6S loop faster than any other; start here.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): the open front door with daylight spilling in; a tidy landing spot (tray + key hook); a coat rail with two empty hooks; a shoe rack with a boot tray; the small friendly dog greeting Riley.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: isometric room (a warm, dollhouse-style cutaway of the whole entryway so the cover reads as the "stage" for the deck).
- Character approach: RILEY mid-step through the doorway, backpack on; grown-up helper (this card: a warm-faced dad with short beard, olive skin, rolled sleeves) holding the door, welcoming, not doing the work for Riley. Dog at their feet.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring softly around the Landing Spot to say "start here"; everything else calm and green-ish/settled to signal "the good way."
- Callout/arrow style: a single terracotta "Start here" arrow pointing to the landing spot; no numbered steps on the cover.
- Composition notes: room fills the card; door and Riley slightly left-of-center; lower third kept calm and uncluttered for the title caption; terracotta room-border feel at the card edge; S-type badge corner uses a neutral 6S/deck mark (top corner) since this is a Deck card, not one S.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The entryway is the smallest room and the biggest head start — begin the whole house here.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Look at the door. Kids: notice this is where outside comes in. Caregivers: name it the "in-between" of house and world. Look for: dirt tracked just inside.
  2. Meet the room. Kids: spot the tray, the hooks, the shoes. Caregivers: point out each zone. Look for: five little zones hiding in one small room.
  3. Pick one zone. Kids: choose the landing spot to start. Caregivers: promise it's about 20 minutes. Look for: the honey ring marking where to begin.
  4. Follow the six S's. Kids: get ready to Sort first. Caregivers: preview the loop. Look for: the order Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain.
  5. Notice the payoff. Kids: see how calm a reset room feels. Caregivers: connect calm entry to calm days. Look for: two empty hooks waiting for you.
- Common mistake (if the scene suggests one): trying to do the whole house at once instead of one small zone.
- Encouragement line: The tiniest room gives you the biggest win first.

## CARD 2 — The ways to play (How to use) [Deck]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A friendly "menu of games" tableau: RILEY and the grown-up helper at a low table fanning out the deck, with small vignette bubbles floating around them showing each way to play (full reset, S-pass, one zone, daily draw, family challenge/race, audit-values-safety night).
- Learning moment: There are many ways to use the deck; run zones in any order, but within a zone the loop is fixed, and the deep part is Shine.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): the fanned deck in hand; a few floating mode-bubbles (a full-room icon, one colored S-card being pulled, a single "today" card, two kids racing, a photo/values/safety trio); a small terracotta color-swatch strip hinting cards are color-coded.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: storyboard comic panels (small labeled vignette bubbles around a central pair — a visual "pick your game" board).
- Character approach: RILEY holding the fanned deck; grown-up helper (this card: a mom with warm tan skin, glasses, hair in a bun) kneeling alongside, pointing at a bubble to invite choice.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the "Start / Daily draw" bubble as the easiest on-ramp; each S-pass bubble tinted with its own S color as a preview of the color-coding system.
- Callout/arrow style: tiny rounded label chips under each bubble (1-3 words: Full reset, S-pass, One zone, Daily draw, Family race, Room night); no numbered arrows since order is a choice here.
- Composition notes: central pair low and slightly forward; bubbles orbit the upper two thirds; lower third calm for caption; Deck-type badge in top corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): One deck, many games — pick the way that fits today.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Fan the deck. Kids: spread the cards like a hand. Caregivers: show it's a toolkit, not a rulebook. Look for: the terracotta room-color on every card edge.
  2. Choose full reset. Kids: go zone by zone, start to finish. Caregivers: best for a big tidy day. Look for: the whole-room bubble.
  3. Try an S-pass. Kids: pull one color and sweep the room. Caregivers: great when time is short. Look for: one colored S-card lifted out.
  4. Grab one zone. Kids: take just a zone's cards for a short session. Caregivers: bite-size wins. Look for: a small stack labeled with a zone.
  5. Do a daily draw. Kids: one card is today's small task. Caregivers: keeps momentum. Look for: the honey-ringed "today" card.
  6. Play a family challenge. Kids: deal and race, or team up. Caregivers: make it cooperative some nights. Look for: two players and a smile.
  7. Hold a room night. Kids: use audit, values, and safety cards together. Caregivers: schedule it. Look for: the photo-values-safety trio.
- Common mistake (if the scene suggests one): thinking you must do everything at once instead of choosing one mode.
- Encouragement line: Any way you play, you win a calmer doorway.

## CARD 3 — The six S's, in order (6S Legend) [Deck]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A clean left-to-right journey strip: a chaotic entryway on the left transforming across six labeled stepping-stones into a calm entryway on the right, with RILEY walking the path and the grown-up helper cheering. Each stepping-stone is one S, in its exact color, in order.
- Learning moment: The six S's run in a fixed order, and Safety is the fourth S — this book's difference.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): chaos on the left, calm on the right; six color-coded stepping-stones labeled Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain; a special honey glow on the 4th stone (Safety); a big terracotta 1→2→3 arrow flow along the path.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: line-art + accent (a crisp infographic path where the six S-stones carry the accent colors).
- Character approach: RILEY mid-journey stepping onto the Shine stone; grown-up helper (this card: a grandparent with light-brown skin, silver curls, cardigan) at the calm end giving a thumbs-up.
- Color legend use: each stone in its exact S color (Sort #CB4B36, Straighten #BC4B2A, Shine #D98A2B, Safety #DDA63A, Standardize #6E8B5B, Sustain #4E7A57); green calm end = done; honey highlight ring specifically around Safety, the 4th.
- Callout/arrow style: terracotta arrows chaining 1 through 6 along the path; one-word chip on each stone.
- Composition notes: horizontal path reads left(chaos)-to-right(calm) across the middle band; badge corner marks this as the Legend/Deck card; lower third calm for the caption.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Six steps, always in this order, and Safety is the fourth.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Sort. Kids: empty the zone; nothing comes back without a verdict. Caregivers: decide keep, act, or let go. Look for: the empty-it stone, first.
  2. Straighten. Kids: give each thing a home chosen by how you use it. Caregivers: a home you pick on purpose. Look for: labeled homes.
  3. Shine. Kids: clean to a method and inspect as you go. Caregivers: this is the deep part. Look for: the shining surface.
  4. Safety (the 4th S). Kids: act on everything the cleaning turned up. Caregivers: this is the book's difference — never skip or move it. Look for: the honey-ringed 4th stone.
  5. Standardize. Kids: photograph it; that photo is the target. Caregivers: the picture becomes the standard. Look for: the camera.
  6. Sustain. Kids: hang upkeep on things that already happen. Caregivers: bolt habits to routines. Look for: the calm, kept end.
- Common mistake (if the scene suggests one): treating it as five steps or dropping Safety — never do that.
- Encouragement line: Follow the stones in order and the room almost tidies itself.

## CARD 4 — What the entryway needs (Room Kit) [Room]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A neat "flat-lay + caddy" tableau: RILEY packing a portable caddy while the grown-up helper holds up the color-coded microfiber set, everything laid out in tidy labeled groups — cleaners, cloths and tools, and organization gear (tray, hooks, rack, boot tray, mats, bins).
- Learning moment: Buy for the job in types not brands; use the two-cloth rule and color-code by surface so grit never travels.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a portable caddy being loaded; three color-coded microfiber cloths (glass, surfaces, floor/boot-tray) shown as distinct colors; two cloths paired (one to clean, one to dry); spray bottles labeled by type (no brands); organization items grouped (tray, key hook, shoe rack, boot tray, two mats).

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector (an organized knolling-style flat-lay with a caddy hero).
- Character approach: RILEY loading the caddy; grown-up helper (this card: an uncle with dark brown skin, close-cropped hair, apron) holding up the colored cloth set for the "color-code" lesson.
- Color legend use: each cloth in a clearly different color to teach color-coding; a green check by the correctly paired two cloths; honey highlight ring around the caddy as the thing to gather into.
- Callout/arrow style: small 1-3 word type chips only (Glass, Surface, Floor, Two cloths); a small red "no softener" cross chip near a fabric-softener bottle to teach the anti-rule without being scary.
- Composition notes: caddy hero center; grouped items around it; badge corner is Room/Deck type; lower third calm for caption.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Gather the right types into one caddy — buy for the job, not the brand.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Grab the caddy. Kids: pick a caddy you can carry. Caregivers: mobility is the point. Look for: a handle you can hold.
  2. Add the cleaners. Kids: neutral multi-surface, streak-free glass, a floor-matched one. Caregivers: match cleaner to your real floor. Look for: type labels, no brand names.
  3. Color-code the cloths. Kids: give glass, surfaces, and floor each their own color. Caregivers: stops grit traveling. Look for: three different cloth colors.
  4. Pair two cloths. Kids: one to clean, one to dry. Caregivers: a wet doorway streaks and a wet floor slips. Look for: the clean-and-dry pair.
  5. Add the tools. Kids: a small vacuum, a soft detail brush, a stiff boot-tray brush. Caregivers: the right tool per job. Look for: brushes matched to surfaces.
  6. Set the organization gear. Kids: tray, key hook, rail, shelf, bins, shoe rack, boot tray, two mats. Caregivers: the homes things will live in. Look for: labeled bins.
- Common mistake (if the scene suggests one): washing microfiber with fabric softener, which kills the grab.
- Encouragement line: One packed caddy and you can reset any zone in minutes.

## CARD 5 — The launch pad (Primary Purpose) [Room]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: RILEY leaving for the day in a smooth "launch" — grabbing keys and phone from one tray, coat and shoes from their easy homes, in under a minute, while the grown-up helper glances approvingly at a little clock. Two hooks stand visibly empty for the next person.
- Learning moment: Everyone can land and launch in seconds; the test is leaving in under a minute without hunting.
- Key elements to keep (3-6): one landing tray holding keys and phone; today's coat and shoes with an easy home; wet things on the mat; a small clock showing under a minute; two empty hooks; a clear floor inside the door's swing.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector (a single clear action moment).
- Character approach: RILEY in motion toward the door, one hand scooping keys/phone from the tray; grown-up helper (this card: a mom with deep brown skin, box braids, denim shirt) by the door pointing at a small wall clock, pleased.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the single tray (the launch pad focus); green glow on the two empty hooks (the good, ready state); a subtle green check by the clear door-swing floor.
- Callout/arrow style: one terracotta motion arrow tracing Riley's quick path tray-to-door; tiny chips: One tray, Under a minute, Two empty.
- Composition notes: action sweeps lower-left to door upper-right; lower third calm for caption; Room/Deck badge in corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): The entryway's one job is to let anyone land and launch in seconds.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):

## CARD 6 — Share it as a household (Family Values) [Room]

IMAGE SUMMARY
- Scene idea: A warm family huddle at the entryway: RILEY, the grown-up helper, and one or two more family members talking it through and writing answers on a small shared list, with the entryway behind them and two named, empty hooks on the wall doing the asking.
- Learning moment: Decide together what the room should feel like and its one job; two agreements end the nagging (coats counted by weather; everyone gets a named hook and shelf with two left empty).
- Key elements to keep (3-6): a family talking together; a written shared list/agreement; named hooks with little name tags; two visibly empty hooks; a small weather icon cluster (sun/rain/snow) hinting "coats by weather"; warm, equal, kind poses.

STYLE CHOICE + VISUAL RULES
- Chosen style: friendly flat vector (a warm group scene).
- Character approach: RILEY holding a marker at the list; grown-up helper (this card: a dad using a wheelchair, warm beige skin, glasses, plaid shirt — show ability diversity) alongside; a younger sibling adds a sticker. Everyone contributing, nobody bossing.
- Color legend use: honey highlight ring around the shared written list (the focus); green check beside each named, filled hook and beside the two empty hooks (the good, agreed state).
- Callout/arrow style: tiny chips only: Our values, By weather, Two empty; small name tags on hooks (use plain shapes/blank tags, not risky text).
- Composition notes: family group fills the middle, wall of hooks behind them; lower third calm for caption; Room/Deck badge corner.

TEACHING INSTRUCTION SET
- Big idea (1 line): Agree together how the entryway should feel and work, so the room does the reminding.
- Steps (5-9, verbs first, kid-readable):
  1. Gather everyone. Kids: sit together at the door. Caregivers: make it a short, kind talk. Look for: everyone included.
  2. Ask the feeling. Kids: what should it feel like walking in? Caregivers: name the vibe you want. Look for: calm, welcoming words.
  3. Name the one job. Kids: what must this room always do? Caregivers: pick a single core purpose. Look for: one clear job.
  4. Make your promises. Kids: what will each of us keep true here? Caregivers: agree on shared rules. Look for: each person's promise.
  5. Write it down. Kids: put the answers on one shared list. Caregivers: written beats remembered. Look for: the family list.
