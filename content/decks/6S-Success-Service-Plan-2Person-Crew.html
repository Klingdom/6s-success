7 · Two-person choreography
Lead

runs the audit and the Sort verdicts, makes the Straighten calls, handles the client, shoots the Standardize photo, and does the final quality check.

Tech

carries the Shine (surfaces, floors, bathrooms, kitchen), the hauling and disposal, and the organizing-hardware installs.

Tandem — both on one zone

Best for heavy soil and decision-dense first passes: the Lead sorts while the Tech shines right behind. Use it on the messiest room first.

Divide — one zone each

Best for recurring cleans and large, low-decision homes: each person owns whole rooms and they meet in the middle.

Pipeline handoff

The flow trick: the Lead sorts Zone 1, hands it to the Tech to Shine, and moves straight to Zone 2’s Sort — nobody waits, which is Lean flow applied to the crew.

9 · Add-ons, minimums & policies
  • 2-hour minimum. every visit; 1-hour minimum for a Micro-Zone Rapid Reset
  • Travel. free inside the ~20-minute core radius; $25 / $45 trip fee beyond
  • Same-day / rush. +15% on the crew-hour rate
  • Heavy, hazard & hauling. billed at the $160 deep rate + a disposal fee by load
  • Products & kits. sold at retail; installed free during any reset
  • Membership. recurring clients get the $120 rate, priority scheduling, and a locked calendar slot
  • Guarantee. a 24-hour touch-up on anything that misses the standard photo
10 · The estimating formula
Crew-hours = Base (room or size) × Condition factor  |  Price = Crew-hours × Rate + Trip fee

Condition factor: Light 1.0 · Moderate 1.4 · Heavy 1.9.   Example: a moderate kitchen reset = 2.0 base × 1.4 = ~2.8 crew-hours × $140 ≈ $390. A 2-hour minimum applies to every visit.

All rates, outputs, and time estimates are planning figures for a Treasure Valley launch; calibrate them against the first 20–30 real jobs and adjust the productivity table and condition factors from logged actuals.
