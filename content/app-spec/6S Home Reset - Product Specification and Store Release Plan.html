6S Home Reset is a guided home-transformation application that turns the 6S Success methodology into a room-by-room, zone-by-zone working system. It breaks a whole-home reset into 114 bounded micro zones across 20 room types, each engineered to finish in 15 to 90 minutes and end in a photographable, auditable change of state. In a single flow it acts as a work-session guide, a photographic evidence system, an AI coach that builds a plan from the household's own photos, and a sourced shopping companion, running on a professional standard operating procedure library rather than a generic checklist.

This document has two parts. Part I is the current-state specification for the validated v2.4 artifact: the single-file application already running and gate-tested as a Claude artifact. 01Product Overview

What it is

6S Home Reset is a guided home-transformation application for families and individuals. It operationalizes the 6S Success methodology (Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain) at the micro-zone level: 114 bounded zones across 20 room types, each engineered to complete in 15 to 90 minutes and end in a photographable, auditable change of state. The app is simultaneously a work-session guide, a photographic evidence system, an AI coach, and a sourced shopping companion.

Current platform

A single-file React application (about 277 KB, roughly 4,300 lines including embedded datasets) executing as a Claude artifact. Persistence uses the artifact key-value storage API; AI vision features call the Anthropic Messages API through the artifact's built-in proxy. There is no external backend, no build system, and no third-party service dependency.

Who it serves

Primary: a household adult driving a home reset. Secondary: family members executing shared standards. The design assumes standards must survive children, tired Tuesdays, and 7:40 a.m.

Core value loop

  • Pick a zone.
  • Photograph the honest starting state.
  • Receive an AI Sort plan built from those photos.
  • Execute six guided steps with checkable sub-procedures and one-tap product sourcing.
  • Photograph the finished result.
  • Receive a coach's scored review.
  • Sustain through anchored triggers and two-minute photo audits.

Reliability layer

The AI path is wrapped in a reliability layer, every element of which was validated in harness.

  • Image shrink: photos are re-shrunk to 640 px before transport, cutting the image tokens each request sends versus the source photo.
  • Timeout: each request is bounded by a timeout so a hung call cannot stall the loop.
  • Retry: one automatic retry with backoff on failure.
  • Tolerant JSON extraction: parsing succeeds even when the model wraps the JSON in code fences, preambles, or postambles.
  • Truncation repair: if a reply is cut off, the parser backtracks to the last structurally complete value and recovers a usable object.
  • Errors are surfaced verbatim to the user rather than swallowed.
Validated in harness

The truncation-repair logic achieved 100 percent recovery in a sweep that truncated a reference reply at every third character. Combined with tolerant extraction, this means a partial or messily framed model response still yields a valid Sort Plan or Coach Review rather than a failed screen.

5.2 Hardened photo pipeline

This pipeline was hardened against real storage failures encountered during development. It is the reference design for the mobile port's local layer, even though the port replaces the chunking mechanism with a file system.

StageBehavior
Capture / uploadNative camera (capture=environment) or library upload. Six photos per gallery per side.
DecodecreateImageBitmap with an img fallback, a 40 MB guard, and explicit format-failure messaging.
CompressMulti-pass quality-then-dimension step-down to a hard budget of about 175 KB, verified against 48 MP sources.
SaveA single write is attempted first. On rejection, a tiny self-test distinguishes 'too big' from 'storage down,' then chunk sizes are probed downward (96 to 48 to 16 to 6 KB), verified by read-back, with the working size cached. Every write retries with backoff.
FallbackIf storage refuses photos entirely, photos run session-only with an honest one-time notice. Galleries, AI analysis, and zone completion all continue unblocked.
MigrationVersion 1 single-photo keys auto-migrate into version 2 galleries.
Proven under failure

The save path was proven in harness against simulated backends capped at 5 MB, 100 KB, 30 KB, and 10 KB, under transient rate-limiting, and fully broken. In every case the app degraded honestly rather than losing the user's work.

06Design System

The app is continuous with the 6S Success book identity. A warm paper ground carries the interface, with a single terracotta accent for action and emphasis. Fraunces sets the display type over a Newsreader serif body, with a monospace utility face reserved for times, minute estimates, and codes.

The six step colors are the product's semantic spine. Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, and Sustain each own a fixed hue that recurs everywhere a step is represented, so a user learns the palette once and reads progress at a glance across every screen.

Signature components

  • The step spine: a proportional six-segment time and progress bar, rendered at both zone-card scale and full runner scale.
  • Progress rings for per-room and program-level completion.
  • Level-coded input chips that map each product to its role (Core, Recommended, Conditional).
  • The before and after diptych for paired evidence photos.
  • Bottom-sheet product sourcing surfaced from any input chip or kit row.

Layout is mobile-first, a single column in a 560 px shell, so the artifact reads and behaves like the phone product it is destined to become.

11Go-to-Market & the Content Funnel new

6S Home Reset does not launch into a cold market. It launches into an ecosystem built alongside it and structured to feed it. The book teaches the method, the Micro Zone Manual is the source of the app's SOP dataset, the Product Library powers its Zone Kit, and the video series is a purpose-built acquisition engine. The strategic point is that these are not four separate marketing efforts. They are one funnel whose shape is set by a single fact: the content and the app share the same skeleton, the 114 micro zones.

The one-to-one funnel geometry

The video series is planned as 114 headless YouTube episodes plus 684 Shorts. That count is not arbitrary. The app has 114 micro zones, and the six-S sequence run across all of them accounts for 684 distinct activities, exactly the number of Shorts. The series therefore maps one-to-one onto the product. Each long episode is the reset of one zone. Each Short is one activity within a zone, for example resetting a single junk drawer or wiping down one glass surface.

Because of that alignment, every piece of content has a precise destination inside the app. A Short about resetting a drawer is not generic inspiration; it is a direct funnel to the exact app screen for that drawer. The link in the video description deep-links to that zone, so the viewer arrives already knowing what they are about to do and finds the professional plan for it waiting. The content generates demand and the app fulfills it, for the same task and at the same level of granularity.

12Competitive Landscape & Moat new

6S Home Reset competes in the home organization, cleaning, and decluttering app category, alongside general-purpose checklist and habit trackers that users bend toward the same goal. The category is real and populated, but it is fragmented by function. Each incumbent solves one slice of the problem well and leaves the rest to the user's own discipline. The opening is that no widely used app in this space ships a complete professional method. They ship containers, schedules, or inventories that the user must fill with expertise they do not have.

None of them tells a tired adult standing in front of a cluttered drawer what professional sequence to run, what the drawer should look like when finished, or what to buy to hold the result.

How 6S Home Reset differs

The difference is categorical, not incremental. 6S Home Reset is a system, not a checklist. Three capabilities combine to make that claim real, and no incumbent ships all three:

  • A complete professional SOP library. Every one of the 114 zones carries authored, expert-cued instructions for all six steps, expanded into checkable procedure sub-steps that encode real technique, for example dwell-time chemistry before wiping in wet zones, degrease before sanitize at cooking surfaces, and child-height sweeps in kids' spaces. This is know-how, not a to-do list.
  • AI plans built from the user's own photos. The Sort Plan reads up to two before photos of the actual space and returns a tailored verdict on what belongs and what to relocate, donate, discard, or keep, plus visible hazards, and the Coach Review scores the after photos against the target state. The guidance is grounded in the user's real space, not a generic template.
  • Three-way sourced product recommendations. The Zone Kit resolves the exact products a zone needs and offers three sourced picks per product, a top name brand, a best generic, and a best overall, each with a real retail listing and a direct link, surfaced at the moment of need.
The checklist trap

A checklist assumes the user already knows how to do each item. That assumption is why home-organization attempts stall. The professional SOP closes the expertise gap the incumbents leave open, which is the difference between a list that reminds you to clean the fridge and a system that tells you to watch for thermal shock while you do it.

The durable moat

Differentiation only matters if it is hard to copy. Any competitor can bolt an AI feature onto a schedule or a checklist, and some will. What they cannot cheaply reproduce is the depth of authored assets underneath, built over the course of this project and kept current on a fixed cadence. The moat is three compounding assets:

  • The authored SOP corpus. The zone-by-zone manual behind the app, spanning 114 micro zones across 20 rooms, the per-zone expert cues, and the branching sub-step engine represent a large body of domain authoring. This is the asset a well-funded competitor would have to sit down and write from scratch, zone by zone and surface by surface, and the one an AI cannot convincingly fake, because it is specific professional technique.
  • The sourced product catalog. Keeping it accurate is ongoing labor that deepens the moat each quarter rather than letting it decay.
  • The content funnel. The one-to-one mapping of 114 episodes and 684 Shorts onto the app's zones and activities is a proprietary acquisition machine a competitor cannot replicate without first building an equivalently structured product and then producing the matching content library against it.
Honest read on defensibility

The individual features are copyable in isolation; the combination and the corpus behind it are not. An incumbent could add photo-based AI in a quarter. Reproducing an authored, zone-by-zone professional SOP, plus a maintained three-way sourced catalog, plus a one-to-one content funnel is a multi-year program, and by the time a competitor finished, the catalog refresh cadence and the growing back catalog of share cards and Shorts would have moved further ahead.

Positioning line to defend

Hold the line stated in the product definition: the only home-organization app that combines a complete professional SOP library, AI plans built from your actual photos, and three-way sourced product recommendations. It is a system, not a checklist. Every competitive conversation should return to the assets the category cannot match, not to feature parity on any single one.

Ownership and row-level security

The governing rule is household tenancy. Every record that a household creates carries a household_id, and row-level security restricts reads and writes to members of that household. A member's identity comes from the auth provider (Apple, Google, or email) and is resolved to a member row, which anchors every household-scoped query.

Zone ownership is the retention mechanism made concrete. The Sustain step's named owner becomes owner_member_id on zone_progress, constrained to a member of the same household, and audit-due notifications are addressed to that member. Reference data (zone, product, catalog_version) is globally readable but immutable to clients, so the 90-day sourcing refresh ships as a new catalog_version without an app update. Sensitive and financial records are write-protected from clients entirely: photos are reached only via signed URLs and removed on account deletion, subscription rows are written solely by the receipt-validation function, and ai_usage is written solely by the metered AI proxy.

Fields and constraints are a design proposal, not an implemented schema. The Pro tier (client households, reports, white-label) is out of v1.0 scope, but the household and member entities are shaped to accommodate it later.

16Accessibility new

Accessibility is treated as both a store expectation and a product value. Apple and Google both weight accessibility in review and in featuring decisions, and the audience for a home-reset system includes people managing physical limits, low vision, and fatigue. The commitment for v1.0 is that a full zone loop, from picking a zone through the Coach's Review, can be completed with a screen reader, at large text sizes, and without depending on color to carry meaning.

Screen readers

Every interactive element ships with an explicit VoiceOver (iOS) and TalkBack (Android) label and role, not a label inferred from an icon. The load-bearing controls are the step spine, checkable substeps, input chips, the I own this toggle, the before/after galleries, and the camera and upload actions. Progress is announced meaningfully, for example "Shine, 4 of 7 substeps done," rather than as a bare percentage, and photo thumbnails carry descriptive labels ("before photo 2 of 6") instead of a filename.

Scalable text

The interface honors Dynamic Type on iOS and the system font scale on Android across the full supported range. The Georgia-family serif display, system-sans body, and monospace utility type all scale, and layouts reflow rather than clip or truncate. The 560 px single-column shell is verified to remain usable at the largest accessibility text sizes, including the mission card, product sheet, and substep lists.

Color and contrast

The six step colors are the product's semantic spine, so meaning must never rest on them alone. Each step also carries its name, an icon, and its position in the sequence, and status is conveyed with text and shape, not just hue. Text and essential controls meet WCAG AA contrast against the warm paper ground, and the design is verified against common color-vision deficiencies so the Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain progression stays legible without color perception.

Touch targets and motion

Interactive targets, especially the dense substep checkboxes and input chips, meet the platform minimum tap size (roughly 44 pt on iOS, 48 dp on Android) with adequate spacing, so a reset can be run one-handed or with limited dexterity. The app respects the Reduce Motion setting: progress-ring fills, step-spine transitions, and share-card animations degrade to instant or simple state changes when motion is reduced.

Captions

Any onboarding or zone-loop video, including the optional App Store preview and any in-app clips drawn from the 6S Micro Zone Reset series, ships with accurate captions and does not rely on audio alone to convey instructions.

Value, not just compliance

The same choices that pass accessibility review, clear labels, legible type at any size, and meaning carried by more than color, make the product easier for everyone to run on a tired weekday morning. Accessibility and the everyday-usability goal are the same work.

Verification

Accessibility is a build gate, not a final polish pass. A screen-reader run of one complete zone loop and a largest-text-size layout check are added to the phased roadmap's exit criteria, alongside the existing engine and pipeline gates.

29The Two Decoupling Workstreams new

These two pieces of work turn the artifact into an app. They are the critical path; nothing else in the beta can be tested until they land.

1. Persistence, off the artifact host

  • Keep the existing storage interface (the get, set, delete wrappers and the save-photo call) so the screens do not change.
  • Back it with device storage: structured data (home, progress, owned gear) in SQLite or a key-value store, and photos written to the file system as files, with the record holding only a file URI.
  • Delete the chunking, size-probing, and session-only fallback layer. Native storage has no artifact quota, so that entire subsystem retires.
  • Collapse the current split (photo ids in progress, data-urls in local state, session photos in memory) into a single photo record with a URI. This removes the app's most fragile code.

2. AI, through a thin server proxy

  • Stand up a small backend endpoint that holds the API key and forwards the Sort Plan and Coach Review calls. The app calls the proxy, never the model directly. No key ships in the binary.
  • Use an API configuration that is zero-retention and not used for training, and document it. This is both a compliance gate and a marketing asset.
  • Meter per user or per device so a free beta cannot run up unbounded cost (a hard cap on analyses per tester is enough for the beta).
  • Confirm the current model and raise the token cap at build time; keep the JSON contracts and the tolerant parser, which port as-is.
  • Keep AI optional: the app already works with AI disabled, so if the proxy is down the loop still completes on the static plan.
What ports for free

The content datasets, the instruction engines, the AI prompts and JSON contracts, the truncation-repair parser, and all component logic carry over with little change. The rebuild is concentrated in storage, the AI transport, camera and image handling, styling, and navigation.

32Beta Compliance & Privacy Gates new

The sensitive asset is photos of the inside of people's homes, which can contain faces, children, mail, and medication, and which are sent to an AI provider on request. These gates are non-negotiable before a real tester captures a real photo.

P0, before any real home photo enters the beta

  • Remove the client-side unauthenticated model call; route AI through a server proxy on a zero-retention, no-training configuration. No key in the binary.
  • Ship a privacy policy and terms, linked in-app and in both store listings, naming the AI provider, stating that photos leave the device on request, retention is none or short, and data is never used for training.
  • Add first-run consent and a pre-send notice on the AI buttons (your photo will be sent to our AI provider for analysis). The artifact has no privacy copy at all today.
  • Add native camera and photo-library permission usage strings with honest, specific text.
  • Encrypt photos at rest in the native shell; the AI call is already HTTPS.

P1, required to pass store review

  • A data-deletion path that also covers any server-retained copy; keep the existing local wipe and label it clearly.
  • A data-safety form and privacy nutrition label declaring Photos and User Content, shared with a third party for AI processing, no tracking (only if that stays true).
  • Liability disclaimers for the cleaning-chemistry guidance and for the AI hazards-spotted list: informational only, not a safety inspection, follow product labels.
  • Runtime validation of AI JSON (clamp the score and clutter values, default the arrays) before render.
Chemical and hazard content carries liability

The content gives specific home-chemistry instructions (never mix cleaners, chlorine away from ammonia, degrease before sanitize) and the AI emits a hazards-spotted list. Both are responsible, but if the AI misses a real hazard a user may rely on it. Prominent disclaimers are required, and the app must not present itself as a safety inspection.
