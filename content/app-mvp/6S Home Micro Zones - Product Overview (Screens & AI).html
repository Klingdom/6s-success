Section 1What the app is

6S Home Micro Zones turns a whole-home declutter, which is overwhelming, into a series of tiny, finishable wins. A home is broken into micro zones (a landing surface, a single drawer, one shelf). Each zone is run through the six S's, once, start to finish:

SortStraighten ShineSafety StandardizeSustain

A before photo and an after photo bracket the work. The after photo becomes the zone's standard: what "done" looks like next time. The app is a self-contained, offline-first mobile app; photos and progress stay on the device. AI coaching is optional and has three modes (off, on-device, cloud), covered in sections 14 and 15.

The core loop in one linePick one small zone → before photo → work the six steps → after photo → keep it with a light recurring audit. Repeat, zone by zone, until the home is done.

The zone runner: six accordions

  • Each S is one collapsible step, color-coded, with a short tagline. You expand one, work it, mark it done, move on.
  • Adaptive guidance. The "You'll need" product chips and the "How to do it" substeps are generated for the specific zone, not generic. Owned products show a check.
  • Sort has a tally for items relocated, donated, and discarded. This feeds the "items released" north-star stat.
  • Safety is a full step, not a footnote (the fourth S), so hazards get found on every zone.
  • "Done when" gives an objective finish line per step; the zone is not done until all six are marked and both photos exist.

9

The step most apps skip

  • A cadence you choose (weekly, every two weeks, monthly) drives when the dashboard nudges you to re-check the zone.
  • An owner and a trigger. Naming who keeps it, and hooking the check to an existing habit ("after dinner"), is what makes a standard actually hold.
  • Holding or drift, in two taps. The audit compares against the after photo standard.
  • Two drifts in a row tells you the standard is wrong, not you, and prompts a redesign ("Run this zone again").

Three AI modes, privacy first

  • Off is the default: pure built-in guidance, zero data anywhere.
  • On-device runs the coaching on the phone. Photos never leave the device, it works offline, and no consent prompt is needed. Shows "Ready on this device" when the hardware supports it.
  • Cloud uses a key-holding proxy (no key stored in the app) and asks for photo-send consent once.
  • Support without seeing photos: a diagnostic report carries counts and reliability data only, no images, names, or URLs.

Section 15Cost-effective AI for before-and-after photos

The two AI jobs are both vision tasks: read a before photo (clutter, plan, hazards) and review an after photo (score, coaching). This section reviews how to do that well for the least money. Dollar figures are directional, based on late-2025 / early-2026 list prices; verify current pricing, which keeps falling.

What actually drives the cost

  • Image resolution is the biggest lever. Vision models bill by image tokens, which scale with pixels. A photo downscaled to 768 to 1024 px costs a small fraction of a full-resolution capture and is more than enough to judge clutter and spot obvious hazards.
  • Output length. The app's JSON schemas are small; capping output at roughly 500 to 700 tokens keeps the response cheap.
  • Model tier. A "flash" or "mini" or "haiku" class model costs a fraction of a flagship and is well matched to this task.
  • Call frequency. AI fires only when the user taps, once per action, and results are stored, so there are no background or repeat calls.

On-device users cost zero regardless of scale. Cloud users on the right model cost pennies for an entire home. The wrong model choice raises that by 10x to 20x for no real gain on the everyday clutter-and-plan task.

The recommended configuration

Recommendation On-device first, cheap-cloud fallback, flagship only on demand. Concretely:
  1. Default to on-device where the phone supports it. It is free, private, offline, and good enough for clutter, planning, and coaching. This is the single biggest cost saver.
  2. For the cloud tier, use a flash-lite / mini class vision model, not a flagship, for the routine before-and-after calls.
  3. Downscale to 768 to 1024 px before sending (the app already downscales), and cap output near 600 tokens.
  4. Prompt-cache the fixed system instructions at the proxy, so only the image and a small delta are billed per call.
  5. One call per user action, on demand, results stored. No background analysis, no auto-retries beyond one.
  6. Reserve a flagship model for one optional "deep safety check" that a user can trigger deliberately, rather than paying flagship prices on every routine photo.
  7. Rate-limit at the proxy to cap worst-case spend and abuse.

Most of this is already true in the build: AI is on-demand only, results are cached in the zone, images are downscaled, output is capped, and the on-device tier (free) is the default path. The remaining cost work is a proxy configuration choice: point the cloud tier at a flash-lite / mini model and enable prompt caching. That combination keeps a whole-home reset in the range of a few cents for cloud users, and free for everyone on a supported device.
