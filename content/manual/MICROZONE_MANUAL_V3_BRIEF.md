# Micro Zone Manual v3 — Canonical Strings and Brief

*Companion volume to "6S Success: Home Edition." Frozen brief for the v3 rewrite. Every room agent writes against this document.*

## What this book is

A standalone companion to the 40-chapter book. The book teaches why and how; this manual is the field reference you carry to one specific shelf. It does not re-teach the method. It cross-references it.

**Title:** The Micro Zone Manual
**Subtitle:** One shelf, one drawer, one corner at a time.
**Series line:** 6S Success · Home Edition · Companion Volume

## The frozen canon (never vary)

The six S's, always in this order, always these names:

1. **Sort** — keep what serves the zone, route out the rest
2. **Straighten** — a home for everything, chosen by how it is used
3. **Shine** — clean to inspect, not just to impress
4. **Safety** — the 4th S. Never 6th. Never omitted. Never renamed.
5. **Standardize** — make the right state obvious at a glance
6. **Sustain** — design the habit, do not rely on willpower

**This is the single most important check in the document.** The book's chapter art has repeatedly printed five-S systems with Safety missing or demoted. This manual must never do that. Safety is the 4th S in every list, every diagram, every card.

**Frozen rule lines** (quote verbatim where used):
- "A standard is the best way you know today, written down." (Ch 23)
- "An audit checks the home, not the household. What it finds is always a system to fix, never a person to blame." (Ch 27)
- "You do not finish 6S. You keep it." (Ch 26)
- "Find it, and fix it now." (Ch 21)

**The five safety questions** (Ch 21) — frame every hazard note as one of these:
fall · burn or fire · poison, choke, or strangle · fall, cut, or crush · water and electricity

## Voice

Warm, plain, confident. Talks to a person standing in their own home holding one object, deciding.

**Banned vocabulary** — this is the failure mode of v2:
workcell · throughput · production capacity · fire lane · ergonomic · lean practice · auditor (as a role the reader plays) · SOP · deploy · optimize · leverage · KPI · cadence (use "rhythm") · real estate (as metaphor for shelf space)

**Write like this:** "The kitchen is the busiest room in the house, and the one 6S pays back fastest."
**Not like this:** "The kitchen is the highest-throughput workcell in the home."

**Hard rules:**
- **Zero em dashes.** Zero. Use a comma, a full stop, or a spaced hyphen if you truly need a break. This is the book's quality floor and it is checked mechanically.
- No en dashes except in number ranges (45–75 min).
- Second person. "You," not "one" or "the homeowner."
- No invented statistics, no percentages, no research citations.
- Never claim a household will feel a specific emotion. Describe what they will see.

## Time

Ranges only. `45–75 min` style. Add a short note on where the time actually goes, e.g. "most of it in Sort."

**Never** print per-step minute splits (no "Sort 12′, Straighten 15′"). **Never** print a grand total for the whole house. v2 claimed "98h 00m" and "684 activities"; both were invented and both are gone.

Session sizes, pick the honest one: `15–30 min` (a drawer) · `30–45 min` · `45–75 min` · `60–90 min` (a garage bay).

## The zone card — six required elements

Every one of the 114 zones gets genuinely specific content. If a sentence would read identically for a different zone, it is wrong and must be rewritten.

1. **What it is for** — one line, the zone's actual job.
2. **Done looks like** — concrete enough to photograph. This is Ch 25's ideal-state photo in words. Not "tidy and organized." Instead: "Two coats per person on the rail, the rest in the closet, and a clear hook at kid height."
3. **The six passes** — Sort, Straighten, Shine, Safety, Standardize, Sustain, written for *this* zone. One to two sentences each. Specific nouns from this zone, never generic ones.
4. **The call that matters here** — the single genuinely hard decision unique to this zone, and how to make it. The nursery's outgrown-clothes call is nothing like the garage's third-hammer call. This element is what makes the manual worth owning; write it with real judgment, not encouragement.
5. **Watch for** — hazards specific to this zone, framed as the five safety questions. Real and physical. If a zone has a genuine hazard (medicine cabinet, workshop finishes, nursery cords, garage chemicals), say the specific thing plainly.
6. **Leave behind** — the standard that holds it, plus a Sustain trigger tied to something that already happens in the house ("when the coffee brews," "before the school run"), never a calendar reminder or an app.
