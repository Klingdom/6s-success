Stop buying bins

We have been solving the wrong problem. 6S Success does not have a traffic problem. It has a distribution problem, and those are a completely different kind of thing: traffic is something you beg for, distribution is something you build.

A complete book with a retail ready cover. A companion manual. A working offline app. A free printable deck. Twenty room playbooks. A hundred and fourteen micro zones, each with its desired function, its likely root causes, its hazards, its standard and its sustain trigger. And a Master Black Belt with real shop floor pilots behind all of it.

Every single one of those assets is currently pointed at one channel: people typing into Google and landing on 6s-success.com.

Roughly three of them do that a day. So I have been reporting three as though it were a verdict on the work. It is not. It is a verdict on the one door we have opened.

The book has been finished and unsellable

Until an hour ago, ops/build_epub.py wrote [AUTHOR NAME] into the book's metadata, with a comment saying it was deliberately unfilled pending the owner. You filled it. Your answers have been sitting in ops/front-matter.json the whole time and the builder never read the file.

Amazon rejects a book whose metadata does not name an author. So one unread line kept a fifty chapter book off the largest bookstore on earth. It now reads Philip Kling, Nova Consulting, thirty two of thirty two checks pass, and the cover was already at 1600 by 2560 on a 1.60 ratio, which is exactly what KDP asks for.

The book can go up this week. Amazon brings its own buyers. We do not have to win at SEO first.

Five doors, and only one of them is open

01

Amazon KDPReady now

Kindle plus print on demand paperback. No inventory, no upfront cost, and Amazon does the discovery it takes us a year of SEO to approximate. At $8.99 the 70 percent royalty band pays about $6.29 a copy.

The book is not really the revenue though. It is the widest possible top of funnel and the strongest credibility asset we own. Every reader arrives at the site already understanding the method, and a fraction of them have a house they would rather pay somebody to fix.

Effort LowAudience ~300M buyersBlocked on Nothing

02

Etsy, for the printablesNear ready

The 155 packs we wired to Stripe today are exactly what Etsy's buyers already search for: printable checklists, cleaning cards, organizing systems. Our $4 to $16 band sits precisely in that market's sweet spot, and digital downloads cost nothing to fulfil.

The difference from our own shop is that Etsy shoppers arrive already intending to buy a printable. We currently ask a stranger to discover us, trust us, and then buy. Etsy collapses that to the last step.

Effort LowAudience ~90M buyersBlocked on Listing work

03

The professional channelHighest value

This is where twenty thousand a month actually lives, and it is the one we have barely touched. Professional organizers improvise their method. We have one, written down, with a taxonomy nobody else has built.

Organizers, senior move managers, listing agents, stagers, property managers and short term rental hosts all need a repeatable standard and client facing materials. The rental handover kit already exists as a product. It was built for a homeowner; it is arguably better suited to somebody turning over twelve units.

Nobody hires a stranger to reorganise their house. They hire the person who wrote the book, whose method they have already tried for free, whose cards are on their own kitchen counter. Seventeen reset days a month is a real business. Five thousand four dollar downloads is not.

The wedge nobody else is standing on

The organizing industry sells containers. Every competitor's answer to a messy room is a product that holds the mess more attractively.

6S asks a different first question, and it is a genuinely better one: what is this space supposed to do, what is stopping it, and what is the root cause? Often the answer is that something has no home, or the home is in the wrong place, or there are simply too many of the thing. None of those are solved by a bin, and a customer who has bought three bins already knows it.

That is a real position, it is defensible, and it makes an arresting hook in a market where everybody else sounds the same.

What I would do first

  1. Put the book on Amazon

    It is ready. The only genuinely irreversible decision in it is the list price, and that is yours. Everything else I can do.

  2. Switch on the post engine

    1,325 written posts and no schedule is the cheapest unforced error on this list. It costs nothing and it starts the compounding.

  3. Open the Etsy shop with the 20 room packs

    Not all 155. Twenty listings is enough to learn whether that market wants this, without spending a week on listings before the first signal.

  4. Write the professional offer

    One page, aimed at organizers and senior move managers. This is the route to the actual target, and it is the one thing on this list that needs you rather than me, because it ends in a conversation.
