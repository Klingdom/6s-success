# Retrospective, cycle 27 (2026-08-31)

## What this cycle did

Set out to improve the Quest's finish experience. Found it did not need
improving, and found something much larger on the way.

**The Quest's full loop is now verified end to end.** Driving six more cards
reaches the finish view, which recaps the session as seven coloured dots plus
the words "1 sort, 2 straighten, 1 shine, 1 safety, 1 standardize, 1 sustain",
offers a way to keep going, and a reload then meets the returning visitor at
1 of 114 zones. The finish screen is well built: it withholds the commercial
offer entirely on somebody's first finished zone, by rule, because that is the
moment the method lands and an offer there reads as an interruption.

**The outage is much bigger than "6 dead payment links".** Chasing where the
earned offer sends people, I found the live checker reads six HTML pages and
**no JavaScript at all**. That matters because `data.js` is the catalogue the
shop and cart are built from and carries 155 payment links by itself.

Scanning it turned up more than a link count. **The live `data.js` is 4,115
bytes holding 10 products. The repository's is 74,599 bytes holding 159.**
Checked against the exact fingerprinted URL the live shop page requests, not a
guess at the path. Production is not the same shop with broken buttons. It is a
different, much smaller shop, missing **149 things we sell**.

Both the outage report and the deck's most prominent sentence now say so.

## What went well

**Following a small question into a large answer.** The cycle's question was
"where does the offer at the end of a zone send someone". That led to the
checker, which led to the catalogue. None of it was on the plan, and the
catalogue finding is the most consequential thing I have measured in a week.

**The lesson existed and had not travelled.** `audit_catalog.py`'s own docstring
says, in its own words, that a dead payment link hiding in a `.js` file is
invisible to every check around it. That was learned about the local repository
and never carried across to the checker watching production. Finding a written
lesson that had not been applied is more useful than finding a new one.

**I checked before believing a defect, for the third cycle running.** The finish
test reported "the recap is empty". It is empty by design: `#f-recap` is a strip
of decorative, aria-hidden colour dots, and the words live in `#f-recap-note`
so nobody has to tell six similar hues apart. My assertion was on the wrong
element. The page was right.

## What did not go well

**Eight days of reporting a number I had never checked the scope of.** "6
payment links are deactivated" has been in every status email and every deck
since the outage began. It was true and it was the wrong size, because the
checker's page list was six URLs I chose once and never revisited. I have spent
this fortnight insisting that a check state what it did not look at, and this
one never did.

**The catalogue gap was visible the whole time.** `deploy_freshness` has been
reporting "4 of 4 assets differ" for days, and one of those assets is `data.js`.
"An asset differs" and "the shop is missing 149 products" are the same fact at
different resolutions, and I read the first one many times without once asking
what the difference actually was.

**Still nothing that moves revenue by itself.** Everything this cycle sharpens
what the redeploy is worth. It does not perform the redeploy.

## What to change next cycle

1. **Audit the scope of every checker's input list, not just its logic.** Three
   have now been found looking at too little: the sitemap URLs, the narrow
   `site/*.html` globs, and now the live link checker's six pages. In every case
   the logic was fine and the list was short.

2. **When a check says "something differs", ask what.** `deploy_freshness` knew
   for days. It reports a difference and never a diff.

3. **Read the deck cold, as Phil would, once per cycle.** Item 2 from cycle 22,
   still not done, and it is exactly what would have caught this.
